## Published forecast grades carried no refused-outcome coverage

- Severity: medium (selection bias invisible on every published grade).
- Where: `forecast/grading.py` (no audit role existed); Record surface
  (`site/refresh_record.py`, `site/record/`) showed no abstention figures.
- What was missing: engines record abstentions in the forecast ledger
  (absence states), but nothing resolved what prices did on the refused
  attempts, so a grade could be flattered by the attempts an engine
  refused and no reader could see it. Adopted fix per the abstention
  coverage audit design (research analysis sections 6.5 and 8.2 item 1).
- Fix: `grading.abstention_coverage_audit(rows, prices)` as a separate,
  read-only audit role. It resolves refused and taken outcomes with the
  same `resolve_outcome` and price source the grading runner uses,
  publishes per-engine taken/refused counts and per-outcome-kind
  refused distributions (count graded, mean, median, favourable share)
  beside the taken distributions, applies the thin-cell rule (fewer
  than 30 graded outcomes in a cell publish counts only), and names
  per engine, with reasons, any abstention it cannot resolve. It never
  appends a grade and never alters a forecast row or a published
  figure. The Record refresh computes the audit into
  `site/record/data.json` and the Record page renders it as a panel.
- Regression blocker: `forecast/tests/test_abstention_audit.py`
  (8 tests) in the existing forecast test setup
  (`python3 -m pytest tests/ -q` from `forecast/`; 18 passed after the
  fix). Fail direction proven: all 8 audit tests fail against the
  pre-fix `grading.py` (no audit function) and pass after. The fixture
  ledger test asserts the ledger rows and `recompute_figures` output
  are byte-identical before and after an audit run.
- Live state at fix time (audit over the live ledger, prices ending
  2026-10-23): 8,753 taken, 8 refused, all 8 refused on engine_12
  (absence_state no_data), refused outcomes graded 0 because those
  rows are `binary_event` outcomes with no realised value recorded
  and no price route derives one. Named, not skipped.

## Grading PriceSource loaded daily bars with no payload integrity check

- Severity: medium (silent data corruption risk in every grade).
- Where: `forecast/grading.py`, `PriceSource.__init__`.
- What was wrong: the price source behind all grading loaded its
  daily history file row by row into a dict. A duplicate date was
  silently overwritten by the later row, non finite or non positive
  prices were accepted, and nothing checked the payload as a whole,
  so a corrupted or truncated history file would grade forecasts
  against bars nobody validated. Adopted fix per DEEP-PASS 3.1
  item 2 (the new-head Trade Brain bars payload integrity gate,
  Section 2.3 point 10), whose desk side is logged in the desk
  BUGLOG of the same date (BARS-INTEGRITY-20261029).
- Fix: `validate_bars_payload` runs at load and refuses the payload
  whole as daily_history_invalid_or_incomplete (BarsIntegrityError)
  on more than 1000 bars for one symbol, a malformed row, a non
  finite or non positive OHLC value, or a duplicate or non
  increasing date. `PriceSource` also accepts `expected_sessions`,
  a per symbol expected latest completed session derived by the
  caller from the trading calendar: a named symbol whose history
  does not end on that session is refused, with no tolerance
  window; unnamed symbols are not session checked, so a delisted
  name's shorter history stays loadable.
- Regression blocker: `forecast/tests/test_bars_integrity_gate.py`
  (7 tests). Fail direction proven: the 6 refusal tests failed on
  the pre-cure loader (it loaded every bad payload without
  complaint) and pass post-cure; the clean load test is a contract
  pin passing both ways. Final state proof: the shipped history
  file (1,833 symbols, max 399 bars per symbol, last session
  2026-10-23) loads clean under the gate, loads again with
  expected_sessions set to its real last sessions, and refuses a
  fabricated expected session with the named message.

## CSV intakes carried no anti fixture rejection and no evidence intake contract

- Severity: medium (fixture or sample rows could enter as market
  input at the exact seam where fixture and live data share our
  surfaces; human supplied evidence CSVs had no schema, caps or
  per row accounting at all).
- Where: the terminal's only CSV loader (site/refresh_pinorescape.py,
  the VIX daily file) read with a bare csv.DictReader; no evidence
  CSV intake existed anywhere on the terminal.
- Fix: site/intake.py, one source of truth for both adopted
  contracts (DEEP-PASS.md 3.1 items 4 and 5). Anti fixture: any row
  whose source field (source, data source, source name, side source
  or provenance, any casing) tokenises to synthetic, sample or
  fixture is refused as market input; generic intakes raise naming
  the row line. Evidence contract: a port of the parseEvidenceCsv
  semantics with hard caps (10 MiB, 50,000 rows, 8,192 characters
  per field), the fixed three clock schema (required ObservedAt,
  Entity, Source, Metric, Value; optional PublicAt, ReceivedAt,
  Unit, Method, Confidence, EventType, SourceURL), unknown or
  duplicate header columns refusing the whole file, and per row
  rejection reasons with line numbers (rows are never repaired and
  never silently dropped; absent optional fields stay None).
  refresh_pinorescape.py now reads the VIX file through the guarded
  reader and fails closed if the guard module is unavailable.
- Regression blocker: forecast/tests/test_intake_contract.py
  (18 tests) in the existing forecast test setup. Fail direction:
  the file imports site/intake.py, which did not exist pre-fix, so
  all 18 fail pre-fix and pass post-fix. Live proof: the wired
  refresh_pinorescape.py run after the fix exits 0 (self-test
  passed, premium over 362 sessions). Full forecast suite after
  the fix: 71 passed, 2 failed, both the pre-existing named open
  items (refresh_engines stamp wiring, stale asset manifest).
