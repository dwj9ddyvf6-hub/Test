The golden sweep rule, in plain words

A print is a golden sweep when all three of these hold at once: it is a sweep, an order split across exchanges to fill at once; its size is at least the contract's whole open interest, the number of contracts of that kind already open, so this one print is as big as every open position in the contract combined; and the money in it is at least $1 million in premium.

That is a deliberately hard bar. Most trading days have no golden sweep at all, and long runs with none are normal. This wire lists only prints that clear the full bar, exactly as they were recorded, and never fills the space with lesser prints.

How to read a direction: a bought call or a sold put points up, a bought put or a sold call points down. Which side of the market a print hit is judged against the latest available quote, not the quote at the exact moment of the print, so on a fast-moving contract the direction read can be wrong. Treat it as a read, not a fact.

A golden sweep means a print was extraordinary by the rule above. It does not mean the buyer knew something. Big prints can be hedges, spreads, or positions being closed as easily as new bets.

Every golden print on this wire is kept on the public record and graded in public once the verdict date arrives: see The Record. The full day's tape, golden or not, is on the Flow Tape.
