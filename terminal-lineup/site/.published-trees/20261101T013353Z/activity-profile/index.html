How the comparison works: a name's usual level is the average of its own 20 prior recorded trading days, whole days compared with whole days. The current day never counts toward its own usual level. Until 20 prior days are on record, no comparison is shown at all: a ratio built on a baseline that does not exist yet would be a made-up number, and this page does not make up numbers.

How to read the net premium figures: every print is read for direction (a bought call or a sold put leans up; a bought put or a sold call leans down), and the net premium is the up-leaning premium minus the down-leaning premium, calls and puts counted apart. Prints that were part of a multi-part trade count in the totals but not in the net premium, and cancelled prints never count at all. Which side of the market a print hit is judged against the latest available quote, not the quote at the exact moment of the print, so the direction read can be wrong on a fast-moving contract.

A day can only be counted here if its full tape was recorded. A day with no recording is not a quiet day and is never counted as zero activity: it is simply not on record. The full day's biggest prints are on the Flow Tape, and every tool here is graded in public on The Record.
