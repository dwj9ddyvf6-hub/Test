What counts: a wall is a price level the map published before a trading session, the upper wall above the price and the lower wall below it; a wall was respected if price never traded through it during that session, and it broke if price traded through it. A wall counts as reached if price came within 0.2% of it. The flip level was respected if the session closed on the same side of it as the price stood when the card was published.

How the grading works: each day's last published card for a name is the card on record, and each card on record is one test. A card published before the close grades against its own session; a card published after the close grades against the next session. A wall that price never came near counts as respected, exactly as the definition above says, and the reached counts are printed next to the held counts so a wall that was never tested is not dressed up as a wall that turned price back.

Why some rows show no percentage: a count built on fewer than 30 tests shows its counts only. A percentage on a handful of tests reads as far more certain than it is, so it is left off until the count grows. Every published call and count from these tools is graded in public on The Record.
