The scoring rule, in plain words

Every options print is given a heat score for how unusual it is for its own contract. It earns points for: the money in the print compared with that contract's average print so far that day (at the average earns 1 point, 3 times it earns 2, 5 times earns 3, 10 times earns 4); how soon the contract expires (under 35 days earns 3 points, 35 to 98 days earns 2, longer earns 1); being a sweep, an order split across exchanges to fill at once (2 points); its size (5,000 contracts or more earns 1 point, 10,000 or more earns 2); how far its strike price sits from the current price (2% away earns 1.5 points, rising step by step to 7.5 points at 30% away or more); its size compared with the contract's open interest, the number of contracts already open (three quarters of it earns 1 point, up to 4 points at 5 times it); its size compared with the contract's average print size (4 times earns 1 point, up to 4 points at 10 times); and the contract's open interest compared with the average contract's (5 times earns 1 point, 10 times earns 2).

A print becomes an alert in one of two bands. High heat: its heat score reaches 15. Golden sweep: it is a sweep, its size is at least the contract's whole open interest, and the money in it is at least $1 million. A print is also tagged unusual when its strike sits at least 10% away from the current price and it expires within 35 days. Only the 25 largest alerts for each index are published here, biggest money first.

How to read a direction: a bought call or a sold put points up, a bought put or a sold call points down. Which side of the market a print hit is judged against the latest available quote, not the quote at the exact moment of the print, so on a fast-moving contract the direction read can be wrong. Treat it as a read, not a fact.

An alert means a print was unusual by the rule above. It does not mean the buyer knew something. Big prints can be hedges, spreads, or positions being closed as easily as new bets.

Every published call from the morning tools is kept and graded in public at 1, 5 and 21 trading days, losers included: see The Record. The tape's own alerts join that grading as it is extended to them; the line above says exactly where that stands.
