How this range is built

This page reproduces a published method: the 15.87 expected-move construction, published as a free brief by Azrael Options. It is reproduced faithfully here, rounding steps included; it is not our invention.

Take the name's last closing price and the last closing value of the volatility index (the VIX, which is itself built from options prices). Divide the volatility close by 15.87: that is the percent move priced for the coming session. Multiply that percent by the closing price and round to the nearest tenth of a point: that is the expected move in points. The session band is the closing price plus and minus that move. The week band widens the same rounded move by the square root of five, for the five trading days of a week, rounded the same way.

What the band means: it is where the options market prices the move, the range the market's own pricing treats as the expected reach for the session and the week. What it does not mean: it is not a forecast. Prices can close outside the band, and often travel only part of it.

Three readings from the same map are shown per name. The regime names the day's pace from the volatility close: CALM below 15, MIXED from 15 to 18.5, FAST above 18.5. The walls are the session band edges, with a half move line inside each wall. The funded curve shows how much of the session move is funded as time passes: the funded part grows with the square root of the elapsed share of the session, so half the move is funded early and the last part takes the rest of the day. Type any price into a name's checker and it is classified against that name's walls: beyond a wall, at a wall, in an outer half, or in the central zone between the half moves.

Each band is shown exactly as it was drawn and filed at the time, from the closing price and the volatility close named in its receipt. Nothing is redrawn after the fact and nothing is carried forward to a newer session than the records support.

The percent is the same for every name on a given day, because it comes from the one volatility index; what differs per name is the closing price it is measured from, so the bands differ in points.
