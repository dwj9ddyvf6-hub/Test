Short interest share: the shares sold short in a company, as a percentage of the company's shares outstanding. It comes from the twice-monthly short interest readings published by Nasdaq out of the market-wide short interest collection; the shares outstanding come from each company's own public filings.

Days to cover: how many days of that stock's usual trading volume it would take for every short seller to buy back what they borrowed, as reported alongside the same readings.

Change in shares sold short: how the count of shares sold short moved between the two settlements on record, shown only for names that carry a reading at both.

The 15% bar: the published squeeze formula this watch is built from only admits names whose short interest reaches 15% of the shares outstanding. Names at or above that bar are flagged; the flag marks that one bar and nothing more.

How the readings work: short interest is counted on settlement dates twice a month and published days later, so every figure on this page carries the settlement date it belongs to. The freshest settlement on record is the one listed; a newer settlement is added when its reading is published.

Why there is no ranked score: the published squeeze score ranks each name against the others inside the group that passes the full set of gates, using each name's recent price swings and trading volume. That full group is not assembled on record, and only 2 names clear the 15% short interest bar, so a ranking built from them would be two names compared with each other and nothing more. This page shows the readings themselves instead.

Why the headline shows counts only: a count with fewer than 30 names behind it shows its counts and no percentage. A percentage built on a handful of names reads as far more certain than it is, so it is left off until the count grows.
