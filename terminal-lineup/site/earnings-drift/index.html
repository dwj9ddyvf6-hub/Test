What counts: an earnings event is the day a company put its earnings results into its required public filing with the Securities and Exchange Commission. The move counted for each event is how much the stock's closing price changed from the close just before the report to the close of the first trading day on or after the filing.

How the count works: for each of the three names, the count takes up to the 12 most recent earnings reports in the price history on record and measures the report day's own move for each one, then pools all the reports together. A report whose stock closed exactly where it had closed before is counted as not ending higher.

What is not in this count yet: the count measures only each report day's own move. Moves over the 5 and 21 trading days after a report, a split of the results by each separate company, and a list of which companies report tonight or reported last night are not in this count, so none of them is shown.

Why a count might show no percentage: a count with fewer than 30 events shows its counts only. A percentage built on a handful of events reads as far more certain than it is, so it is left off until the count grows. Every published call and count from these tools is graded in public on The Record.
