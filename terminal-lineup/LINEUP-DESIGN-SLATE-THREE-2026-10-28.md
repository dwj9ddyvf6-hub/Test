# The Lineup, Slate Three: 10 Forecast Services + 10 Builds on Unbacked Teardowns (41 to 60)

Designed 2026-10-28 at the owner's direction. This slate deliberately takes the two territories the corpus analysis fenced off, and designs into them honestly instead of pretending the fences are not there.

## How this slate stays honest

- **Forecast services** ship only what the Forecast Factory's own discipline produced: cells and forecasters that survived, or are explicitly labelled provisional, every forecast written to the ledger before display and graded at 1, 5, and 21 sessions in public. A forecast service here never says "this will happen." It says "this forecaster said this, here is its record, here is how its kind has graded." The record is the product, exactly as with The Record in Slate One.
- **Builds on unbacked teardowns** are designed together with the backing they lack. Each one names the evidence that must be created before it can ship as a normal tool: fixture tests from the vendor's own published examples, a working replica or calculation proof, and a forward-grading cohort on the ledger. Until that backing exists, the tool may publish only in a visibly marked proving state, or not at all. Designing them is how the backing gets built; the analysis's warning stands until each one earns its way past it.

## Part A: the 10 forecast services (41 to 50)

### 41. Replication Wire
- **Job:** the daily research wire of the forecast program: what replicated, what died, what changed grade, in plain language.
- **Built from:** the Forecast Factory's rerun grades and registry state. This is the "research note" the analysis allowed, promoted to a standing service at the owner's direction.
- **Honesty rule:** every item links to the cell or forecaster it describes and its full grade history.

### 42. Grade-A Board
- **Job:** the surviving forecast cells, live: the 63 of 1,217 promoted cells that graded A under the pre-registered replication rule, shown as forecast cards with their cell definitions and records.
- **Moment:** the only forecast list on the terminal that has already survived an attempt to kill it.
- **Honesty rule:** the card header states the denominator forever: 63 of 1,217. The 1,154 that did not survive are one tap away (tool 50).

### 43. Fear-Day Forecaster
- **Job:** on fear days, the fear-day forecaster's read: which names its evidence supports, with its cross-era record (information coefficients +0.026 and +0.031 at 20 days across two eras) printed on the card.
- **Relation to tool 16:** the Fear Day List shows the day and the names; this service is the forecaster itself, its parameters and its live grading, a researcher's view versus a reader's view.
- **Honesty rule:** calm days it publishes nothing but the regime read and its own waiting record.

### 44. The Router
- **Job:** one verdict per name from the forecast ensemble: Buy, Wait, Avoid, or Abstain, with Abstain as the default and the reason for any non-abstain verdict shown.
- **Built from:** the factory's router design (BUY/WAIT/AVOID with ABSTAIN default) and the promoted book.
- **Honesty rule:** its record is 7 sessions and provisional, and the card says so in words, not a footnote, until the cohort matures.

### 45. Promoted Book
- **Job:** the 388-entry promoted book as a living list: every promoted forecast, its age, its current grade, its decay state.
- **Moment:** for anyone following the factory's output as a portfolio of claims rather than a feed of new ones.
- **Honesty rule:** entries leave the book only by grading out, never by being quietly dropped.

### 46. Odds Cards
- **Job:** for the named setup families, the historical odds: base rate, average outcome, and the distribution, computed from the replay corpus (296,093 cases in the picker replay).
- **Moment:** when a setup appears and you want its past, not a prediction. **Question:** when this has happened before, what followed?
- **Honesty rule:** odds are history with n printed, labelled as history. The replay's own verdict ships on the card: no setup family passed the go-live gate, and the card says so.

### 47. Decay Watch
- **Job:** which live forecasters are decaying: forecasters whose recent grades are falling against their own history, flagged before they fail outright.
- **Built from:** the record-extension decay machinery (windowed decay state) from the factory's dependency surface.
- **Honesty rule:** decay is a statement about grades, computed from the ledger, never an opinion.

### 48. Horizon Grades
- **Job:** one page that answers, per forecaster family: at which horizon, if any, has this kind of forecast actually graded well: 1, 5, or 21 sessions.
- **Moment:** before trusting any forecast on this terminal, including ours. **Question:** what is this kind of call worth, and at what distance?
- **Honesty rule:** families with no horizon in positive territory are listed as such, in the same size type as the winners.

### 49. Survivors Wire
- **Job:** the cross-check survivor candidates as a graded forecast feed, led by pullback depth versus the stock's own norm at 60 days (IC +0.062, t 9.8 in the 2023+ era), each candidate publishing its calls to the ledger from day one.
- **Honesty rule:** labelled unproven candidates on their face. This wire is how they either earn promotion or die in public; Slate One's Pullback Norm (tool 17) is the reader's scanner, this is the candidates' own feed with full statistics.

### 50. The Graveyard
- **Job:** the dead forecasters, kept readable forever: every forecaster and cell that failed replication or graded out, what it claimed, what the record showed, and when it died.
- **Moment:** whenever a shiny forecast claim appears anywhere. **Question:** what does the pile of dead forecasts teach about the live ones?
- **Honesty rule:** nothing leaves the Graveyard. It is the forecast program's memory and the strongest honesty surface in this slate.

## Part B: the 10 builds on unbacked teardowns (51 to 60)

The unbacked set, per the analysis: thestrat (3,615 entries, zero backing), and the big bundle dissections (nansen, tapeboard, gexradar, tradeecho, optionscartel, flashalpha at 0.1 to 0.7 percent backed). Each build below names its backing-to-create. The shared engine for all ten is tool 60.

### 51. Strat Scanner
- **Job:** live bar-scenario labels (1, 2-up, 2-down, 3, hammers, shooters, failed 2s) for the index and sector ETFs on 30-minute and daily bars, from the published rule system.
- **Backing status:** the strongest of this slate. The 71-rule spec is source-cited and a working replica passes 21 of 21 tests including the vendor's own golden cases; what lacks backing is the teardown's index mass, not the rules.
- **Backing to create:** the replica's event fixture extended to a live forward cohort: every live label graded on The Record. Ships as a labelling tool (it names patterns; it claims no edge) once the live cohort is running.

### 52. Strat Combos Board
- **Job:** the named combination setups (simultaneous breaks, FTFC alignment, failed-2 follow-throughs) watched across the same ETF set, firing only when a full combo is present.
- **Backing to create:** combo-level golden fixtures from the vendor's published examples, plus the claim-audit method (replay the vendor's own posted calls on SIP bars) run forward weekly. The 2026-10-27 audit method is the template: bar-level true, minute-level checked.

### 53. Smart Money Tracker (Nansen class)
- **Job:** labelled-wallet and smart-money flows for the names and tokens the teardown covered: who accumulated, who distributed.
- **Backing status:** dissection only, 0.7 percent backed.
- **Backing to create:** a labelled-wallet fixture set verified against known public attributions, and a forward cohort grading whether flagged accumulation preceded anything. Ships in proving state only, with the cohort's record on its face, until it matures.

### 54. Gamma Ladder (GexRadar class)
- **Job:** gamma by strike as a ladder, per index, every strike's contribution visible, next to the Map's summary figures.
- **Relation to tool 1:** the Map summarizes; the Ladder shows the whole distribution. A different job at a different moment (diagnosis versus glance).
- **Backing to create:** known-answer fixtures for the gamma calculation at ladder depth (the Map's worked examples extended per strike) and a parity test: ladder sums must equal the Map's net figure, always, or neither publishes.

### 55. Tape Heatmap (TapeBoard class)
- **Job:** the market as a heatmap: sectors and large names colored by flow and positioning readings, one screen, no tables.
- **Backing status:** dissection only, 0.2 percent backed. Note the Squeeze formula from this family is separately fixture-backed and already lives in tool 15; the heatmap's own readings are what lack backing.
- **Backing to create:** per-cell source definitions frozen in writing, each cell's reading reproducible from a named input, and the whole board graded as a cohort (did red cells and green cells actually diverge afterward).

### 56. Flow Board (OptionsCartel class)
- **Job:** a curated flow board in the cartel style: the day's notable options flow, ranked, with the poster's-style annotations replaced by our receipts.
- **Backing status:** dissection only, 0.1 percent backed.
- **Backing to create:** the ranking rule written down and frozen, then graded: does the board's top decile actually differ from its bottom, forward, on the ledger. Until that cohort answers, it publishes in proving state.

### 57. Signal Feed (FlashAlpha class)
- **Job:** an AI-style signal feed in the flash style: short, timestamped signal cards with confidence language replaced by the signal's own graded record.
- **Backing status:** dissection only, 0.1 percent backed. This is the slate's most dangerous design, because the genre's whole appeal is ungraded confidence; ours ships only with the grade attached to every card.
- **Backing to create:** the signal definitions frozen, a fixture set from the vendor's public examples, and a minimum 30-session proving cohort before any card may drop its proving banner.

### 58. Echo Board (TradeEcho class)
- **Job:** tracked public traders' calls, echoed with timestamps and graded against the market from the moment of the call.
- **Backing status:** dissection only, 0.2 percent backed.
- **Backing to create:** call-capture integrity first (a call counts only if captured before its outcome could be known, hash-timestamped at capture), then per-trader records on the ledger. A trader's card shows their full graded record or does not appear.

### 59. Flow Engine (InsiderFinance class)
- **Job:** the client-side flow-scoring engine itself, rebuilt as a tool: paste a ticker, get the engine's flow score with every input shown.
- **Relation to tool 21:** Insider Wire is transactions by people; this is the vendor's flow-scoring machinery, a different artefact recovered from the same family's teardown.
- **Backing to create:** the engine's calculations fixture-checked against the vendor's published worked values where they exist, and its score graded forward as a cohort before the word score appears without a proving banner.

### 60. The Proving Ground
- **Job:** the meta-tool this slate stands on: every unbacked mechanism in the corpus gets a public proving page: its claim, its fixture status, its forward cohort, its current grade, and its verdict (promoted, still proving, or dead).
- **Moment:** any time anyone asks what any of tools 51 to 59 is worth. **Question:** what is the evidence, right now, in one place?
- **Built from:** novel mechanisms N1 and N4 (ledger-first grading, fail-closed eligibility) applied to the unbacked corpus itself. The Proving Ground is what turns "built on unbacked teardowns" from a warning into a program: the backing is created in public, on schedule, and the failures stay readable in the Graveyard's spirit.
- **Ships first in this part,** before 51 to 59 open their doors, because it is the door they open through.

## Build sequence for this slate

Part B's Proving Ground (60) first among this slate, then Strat Scanner (51, closest to backed), then the rest of Part B in order of backing distance. Part A's Graveyard (50) and Replication Wire (41) first among the forecast services, because they define the honesty surface the other eight publish through; then Grade-A Board, Horizon Grades, Promoted Book, Router, Fear-Day Forecaster, Odds Cards, Decay Watch, Survivors Wire. All of Slate Three queues behind Slates One and Two except the two honesty surfaces (41, 50, 60), which should be built early because every other tool in this slate publishes through them.
