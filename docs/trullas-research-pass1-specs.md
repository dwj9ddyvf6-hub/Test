# David Trullás — "MACD Triple Divergence" Strategy: Source-Cited Reconstruction

Researched 2026-10-25. Sources prioritized: (1) his own words in the Rankia Markets Experience Valencia 2026 talk (primary, verified live on YouTube), (2) the official World Cup Trading Championships historical standings (primary for results), (3) Spanish trading press (Rankia) as secondary corroboration.

**Source-quality tags used throughout:** PRIMARY = Trullás stated it himself (or the official organizer's standings for results). SECONDARY = someone else's summary/reconstruction. UNVERIFIED = claim found only in social-media-style retellings or not found in any accessible source.

## Summary

- David Trullás Vila is a real, verified World Cup Trading Championships (Robbins) competitor from Andorra (originally Manresa, Catalonia; ex-physiotherapist; author of the 2015 book *Bienvenidos al mundo real de la bolsa*). His official record is **four consecutive quarterly Futures Day Trading titles** (Q2 2025 → Q1 2026), preceded by a 2nd place in Q1 2025 and a 2nd place in the 2024–2025 Global Cup of Futures Trading. **The X claim of "five consecutive championships" is not supported by the official standings**, and **no 2,200% / 22× quarter exists in the official record** — his best official quarter is **930.70%** (Q4 2025, ≈10.3×). Trullás himself markets a "1,321%" quarterly figure that also does not match any official final; he said on stage in May 2026 that he was at 1,221% in the then-running quarter. These three numbers must not be conflated.
- The May 2026 conference video **exists**: "MACD, RSI, Volumen y Fibonacci: el Sistema del Campeón Mundial | David Trullas," Rankia Markets Experience Valencia 2026 (Valencia, Spain, 22 May 2026), YouTube, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0W7EYXrDRBc (20:51). It is the single detailed primary source for the method.
- What he actually teaches is **not** a mechanical "triple divergence" pattern in the usual sense (three successive price pushes against MACD). He never uses the phrase "triple divergence" in the talk. His system: detect **regular or hidden divergence** between price and one or more of **MACD (lead), RSI, and volume**; project a **Fibonacci** retracement over the divergence swing; enter near the **23.6%** level; stop just beyond the swing extreme; targets at **38.2%** and toward **61.8% ("the second third," ~2/3 per Dow theory)**; and size the trade as **one contract per divergence present (MACD = 1, +RSI = 2, +volume = 3)**. Execution is manual and discretionary — he is explicit that it is not an algorithm.
- Several specifics the secondary reconstruction asserts (exact short-side Fib anchor "lowest point between the two highs as 0," "30-second to 5-minute" timeframes, "avoid the open," breakeven stops, daily limits) are **not stated by him** in any source found; some are partially consistent with what he says (1-tick offsets; entry ~23–25%).

## 3. Exact Specs

### 3.1 What the "divergence" is, mechanically

- **The four pillars, his words (PRIMARY, ~4:08):** "Fibonacci, volume, MACD, RSI… The basis of the championships and the percentages you've seen is this." Roles (~5:14): **MACD is the lead** — "This guy's my friend. He's my DNA. If MACD doesn't give me the image I want to see, I don't trade." **RSI** "gives me very fast reactions." **Volume** is strength: "if it has volume, I go; if it doesn't have volume, I think about it." **Fibonacci**: "Using Fibonacci I have a very clear entry, a very clear stop and some targets."
- **Divergence types (PRIMARY):** regular and hidden divergences (per Rankia description and the talk). His on-stage **hidden-divergence** definition: price makes a low, then a **higher** low, while the indicators are instead **lower** = hidden bullish divergence.
- **"Triple divergence" (terminology):** the phrase is **not used by Trullás** in the talk. What he does is **count divergences across three indicators** — one divergence may be present in MACD, another in RSI, another in volume — and that count sets position size (§3.6). The "MACD triple divergence" label is therefore **SECONDARY framing**; the mechanically accurate description of his own presentation is "divergences on up to three tools (MACD/RSI/volume) between two price extremes."
- **Number of pushes / pivots:** In all worked examples the comparison is between **two** price extremes (two highs or two lows). No three-push/wedge definition, no pivot/swing algorithm (no N-bar fractal, no minimum separation) is stated anywhere — **GAP**.
- **What is compared on MACD (PRIMARY, partial):** in the NASDAQ demo he points to the **MACD lines** ("the two lines") making a falling bearish divergence while price makes new highs. He does not specify line-vs-signal-vs-histogram formally, and **never states MACD settings** — **GAP** (no 12/26/9 or other parameters in any source found). RSI settings likewise never stated — **GAP**.

### 3.2 Timeframes and instruments

- **Timeframes (PRIMARY):** he is explicitly timeframe-indifferent: "in a timeframe that you want, I don't care… minutes, five minutes, one hour, daily, I don't care." The stage demos used a **5-second** NASDAQ chart — chosen, he explains, so several trades could appear within the 10-minute stage slot, **not** stated as his normal timeframe — and a **1-minute** gold chart (his requested timeframe for that demo). The secondary claim of "30 seconds to 5 minutes" is **UNVERIFIED** and is narrower than what he actually says.
- **Instruments (PRIMARY, from demos):** NASDAQ (index futures context), **gold futures** — "For gold I always like the regulated market. I don't like XAU/USD. Always go to the future. Always because it's a regulated market" — and Nvidia (single stock, stage demo). All his championship results are in **futures** day trading. **Oil and bonds are not mentioned** in any primary material found — UNVERIFIED as instruments of this method.

### 3.3 Role of each tool

- **MACD (lead, PRIMARY):** gatekeeper — no MACD divergence image, no trade (quote in §3.1). A MACD divergence alone = 1 contract.
- **RSI (PRIMARY, partial):** fast-reaction confirmation; an RSI divergence adds a contract; in the sizing passage he also mentions "MACD, RSI out of overbought… another contract" (auto-dub partially garbled — the exact RSI-overbought condition is **uncertain**). No RSI period, no overbought/oversold thresholds stated — GAP.
- **Volume divergence (PRIMARY, defined by him, ~6:29–8:01):** "If price makes a new high and then another high, the volume at the new high **has to be higher**; if it is lower, volume is not following price" = **bearish volume divergence**. Mirror at lows: a new low must carry higher volume; lower volume = **bullish volume divergence**. He calls this "the basis of what I do in this championship." It is measured as the volume printed at the successive price extremes on exchange (futures) volume — consistent with his insistence on regulated futures markets rather than CFD spot (XAU/USD). A volume divergence adds a contract.
- **Fibonacci (PRIMARY):** supplies entry, stop, and targets (§3.4–3.5). Anchoring as demonstrated: over the divergence swing — in a student demo, the Fib was thrown **"from the highest to the lowest part of the divergence"**; for the hidden-divergence case, the protocol is to identify **"the maximum price between those two minimums"** and trace the Fib from there. Target doctrine (~5:26, Dow theory): "Any rebound or correction usually goes through **2–3rds of that range**. Not always." On his own site, the HLIT method is described as based on Fibonacci proportions/psychological levels (SECONDARY-to-primary brand material; no levels published).

### 3.4 Entry trigger — verifying the secondary reconstruction

Secondary claim: *for shorts, anchor Fib from the lowest point between the two highs to the new high as 0; enter 1 tick below the 23.6 level; stop 1 tick above 0.*

What Trullás himself states (PRIMARY, ~12:0x):
- **"My entry is usually around 23–25% of this section [the Fib]"** — i.e., entry ≈ the **23.6%** retracement level. ✔ consistent with the claim's entry level.
- **"The entry is a tick on break"** — entry is offset **1 tick** (on the break of the level). ✔ consistent in substance with "1 tick below 23.6" for a short.
- **"The exit [stop] is one tick below [beyond]"** and **"the other exit in take profit is one tick before"** — stop 1 tick beyond the reference extreme; take-profit 1 tick before the target level. ✔ the 1-tick-offset structure is his.
- **The specific anchor construction ("lowest point between the two highs," with the new high as 0) is NOT stated verbatim by him** in the talk. His demonstrated anchors are the extremes of the divergence swing ("highest to lowest part of the divergence"; "maximum price between those two minimums" for the hidden case). For a regular *bearish* divergence the secondary anchor is geometrically plausible and not contradicted, but as an exact rule it remains **SECONDARY/UNVERIFIED**.

**Verdict:** entry at ~23.6 with 1-tick offsets = PRIMARY; the precise short-side anchor recipe = UNVERIFIED.

### 3.5 Stops and targets

- **Stop (PRIMARY):** 1 tick beyond the swing extreme (the 0 end of the Fib) — "a very clear stop" via Fibonacci; per §3.4, "the exit is one tick [beyond]."
- **TP1 (PRIMARY):** **38.2%**. The beginner pattern he teaches his students is **"from 23 to 38"** — enter at 23.6, take profit at 38.2 — because, he says, price often turns after 38 when traders are aiming for 50, and "there's always a profit there."
- **TP2 (PRIMARY in substance):** toward **61.8%** — "launch the operation to 60 / 61.8, to the second third" (student demo, auto-dub partially garbled) — grounded in his Dow-theory statement that corrections "usually go through 2–3rds of that range." The secondary claim "TP2 ~66% of the larger swing Fib" matches this 2/3 ≈ 61.8–66% doctrine; the "larger swing" qualifier is not his wording (the Fib in his demos is drawn on the divergence swing). Take-profits are placed **1 tick before** the level.
- **Ancillary targets/confirmation (PRIMARY):** "big trade" (footprint) markers usually sit at these Fib levels and serve as price targets / extra confirmation; he uses order-flow information but the pillars remain the four simple tools.
- **Stop-to-breakeven rule:** **not mentioned** in any primary source found — GAP. Partial exits vs. all-at-TP1/TP2 split: not specified — GAP.

### 3.6 Position sizing and risk

- **Core rule (PRIMARY, ~10:5x–11:5x and Rankia description):** size = **1 contract per divergence detected**, summed across tools. His words: "David always risks the same amount? No, no — I risk the money based on what I see. If I have three divergences — one in volume, [one in] MACD, another in RSI — three contracts. If I have MACD [only], one; if I have MACD [and] RSI, two; if I have MACD, RSI out of overbought… another contract. If I have volume, another contract." Gold demo confirms: MACD+RSI divergence = 2 contracts, plus much lower volume at the highs = **3 contracts**. So the secondary phrase "adding size for RSI/volume confirmation" means, concretely, **+1 futures contract per additional confirming divergence, max 3 in all his examples**.
- **Not found (GAPS):** no risk-%-per-trade figure, no account-size scaling rule beyond the contract count, no daily loss limit, no pyramiding/adding-to-winners rule (contracts are set at entry by the divergence count, not added later, in everything he demonstrates).

### 3.7 Context / session rules

- **Not stated by him in the talk (GAPS):** higher-timeframe support/resistance usage; avoiding the open; news rules; trades per day. The only session-related primary-adjacent item is the Negocios TV interview title quoting him as working "one hour at maximum intensity" — title only, content UNVERIFIED.
- What *is* primary context: the method is presented as timeframe-agnostic and instrument-agnostic in structure ("I don't care" re timeframe), executed manually/discretionarily, and restricted by his own preference to **regulated futures markets** (explicit for gold).
