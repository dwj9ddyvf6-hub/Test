## What this strategy is

A social signal swing: it buys a stock when @EchoAnalysis posts that he bought it, or adds when he adds at support, with a 2 percent stop and a 5 percent target, and then lets the desk's swing manager run the position (trims, a rail flip add, and the crumb rule). Posts are swept every 3 minutes from RSS mirrors of the account.

## Full algorithm

Evaluation order, from the code in this repo: the classifier and gate in `sleeves/[family]/echo.py` (classify_echo, echo_paper_gate, parse_rss), the execution sleeve EchoSwing and its feed job in the main family module in `sleeves/[family]/` (filename carries the blocked name; not reproduced), the ticket and exit wire in `sleeves/[family]/wire.py`, and the paper rules in `sleeves/[family]/paper.py` (PAPER_LAW, paper_shares, paper_stop, paper_target, exit_math, read_exit).

1. Feed sweep, every 3 minutes (180 seconds, the sidecar's POLL_MS): the job tries the RSS mirrors in order (the three Nitter mirrors listed in echo.py, then the two more in the family module: nitter.poast.org and xcancel.com) and the first whose RSS carries posts wins. parse_rss drops repost items (title starting "RT by"), drops items whose creator is not EchoAnalysis, and builds each item's text from its description or title with HTML stripped. A malformed publication date fails the whole sweep, as in the source. The feed is written to the sleeve's cache file and read by the sleeve.
2. First sweep rule: the sidecar's first sweep only seeds the seen ledger (backfill); no post read in the first sweep is traded. Later, each post whose id is new is classified once; the seen ledger keeps the last 2,000 ids, and the paper ledger keeps the last 80 paper rows.
3. Classification (classify_echo), in this order: an empty text or a repost is SKIP. A recap (at least 6 distinct dollar tickers, or a recap phrase such as "another great week" or "every buy posted") is WATCH, not a live buy. A "never bought" or "did not buy" text is SKIP. If the text says he bought ("I bought", "we bought", "publicly bought") or a starter ("starter position", "starter here"), the call is BUY for the named buys (the "I bought TICKER" names if any, else the first 2 dollar tickers), unless a history phrase ("on Monday", "this week", "last week", "yesterday") makes it WATCH as an old fill. If the text says he added ("I added", "I have added", "I am adding", "adding to my position"), the call is ADD for the first 2 tickers; it counts as filled only for the past tense "added" forms. Holding phrases give HOLD; future or patience phrases ("I will buy", "plan to buy", "do not rush", "staying patient") give WATCH; a setup phrase ("buying opportunity", "buy zone", "near a buy") with tickers gives WATCH; any other text with tickers gives WATCH as a ticker note; no ticker gives SKIP. Noise tickers (common words and terms such as EMA, RSI, SUPPORT, TARGET, PLAN, PRICE) are filtered from ticker extraction. Only BUY and ADD calls with paper true (a filled buy with at least one name, or a filled add with at least one name) become pending trades.
4. Pending handling: a pending post waits for its name's live quote; if no quote arrives within 180 seconds of the read it is judged without one and held for "no quote". The vs21 and ret5 reads are computed from the name's daily closes plus today's developing bar from the quote: ret5 is the last close over the close 5 sessions earlier minus 1 (needs at least 6 closes); vs21 is the last close over the 21 day EMA minus 1 (EMA seeded with the mean of the first 21 closes, smoothing 2 divided by 22; needs at least 21 closes).
5. Paper gate (echo_paper_gate), before the ticket judge: a weekend rejects everything. The classifier's own session check in the gate code allows 04:00 to 15:45 ET on a weekday; the ticket judge below then holds any premarket Echo ticket (before 09:35) as observe only, so a traded buy in practice reads 09:35 to 15:45 ET on a weekday, which is the window the strategy card records. For an ADD: it must be at support (step 6), else held. For a BUY: the name must not have been bought today by this sleeve and must not be held (the sleeve passes hasPosition false at this gate and enforces the held check again in the ticket judge), and it must not be a chase: ret5 over 8 percent, or vs21 over 4 percent, rejects.
6. At support, for an ADD: price at or under 1.01 times the position entry price, or vs21 between minus 5 percent and plus 2 percent, or ret5 at or under 0.
7. Ticket judge for an Echo ticket (judge_paper in paper.py, called through wire.py ticket): premarket holds; weekend holds; after 15:45 holds ("no new buys after 15:45"); a held name holds for a BUY (an ADD may proceed); a non ADD buy holds at 6 swings open, at 10 open names, or at 16 new buys that day. A passing ticket becomes a buy intent: market order in the regular session, or a resting GTC limit at the buy price in extended hours, with engine held exit rules for the stop and the target.
8. Stop, target and sizing (paper.py): the ticket is called with stop paper_stop(price, 0) and target paper_target(price). paper_stop with no card stop is the pop stop: entry times (1 minus 0.02), a 2 percent stop. paper_target is entry times 1.05, a 5 percent target, rounded to cents (to 4 decimals under 1 dollar). Sizing for a swing ticket (paper_shares): shares are the floor of the minimum of (equity times 1.5 percent divided by the risk per share, where risk is buy minus stop) and (equity times 8 percent divided by buy). With the 2 percent stop that minimum is the 8 percent cap, so the position is 8 percent of equity in whole shares, exactly as the strategy card records: 1.5 percent of equity at risk to the 2 percent stop, capped at 8 percent of equity. A size that rounds to zero shares holds the ticket.
9. Exits, each pass on trading signal sessions (wire.py exits calling exit_math and read_exit in paper.py), in this order: (a) price at or under the stop sells ("card stop" at the wire; read_exit phrases the same print as a sell under its Fib label, and the card note below governs which sells confirm); (b) price at or over the target sells in profit; (c) the swing manager, read_exit: a Fib broke, retested and rejected pattern sells; a rejection at the first overhead Fib (the first of the 38.2, 50 and 61.8 percent marks of the 90 day range that sits over 1.01 times the buy) after a run of at least 35 percent sells in full as a Wave B rejection; a trim of half fires, once per position, after a gain of at least 50 percent within 8 days, or at a weekly RSI of at least 68 with a daily bearish divergence after a gain of at least 80 percent; a trim of 20 percent fires, once per position, on a rejection at the first overhead Fib (the strategy card records the gain under 35 percent condition for this trim; in code the full sell in this step takes the at least 35 percent cases first, so the 20 percent trim is the under 35 percent remainder); one add fires, once per position, when price is back within 2 percent of the 50 or 200 day EMA after at least 5 of the prior 16 closes (the code reads bars minus 18 to minus 2) were below that EMA, and that add goes through the Echo ADD ticket path; a failed 61.8 percent Fib with price under the 50 day EMA blocks the add and holds. (d) Crumb rule: a position worth under 75 dollars in total is sold flat.
10. Sell confirmation note, from the strategy card and the family module header: the swing manager's other sells (a broken then rejected level, a Wave B rejection) are re judged without daily bars at the ticket layer, so only a print through the stop confirms them; the card target remains the bracket's target. The wire code implements this by routing non stop, non target manager sells back through judge_paper.
11. Trim repeat note, from the strategy card: at the card's pin, the plus 50 percent in a week trim was not recorded as a trim, so it repeated each pass while that held, down to one share; the master desk commit of 2026-10-26 recorded in the card (6d6ae54e, short form as in the card) makes every trim fire once per position. The code in this repo tracks alreadyTrimmed from its fills ledger, which is the once per position reading.
