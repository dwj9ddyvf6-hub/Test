## 1. Adam Grimes — The Art and Science of Technical Analysis + MarketLife free course

**Sources**
- Book: *The Art and Science of Technical Analysis: Market Structure, Price Action, and Trading Strategies* (Wiley, 2012). Publisher/review summaries LISTED.
- Blog post "What works?" — https://www.adamhgrimes.com/what-works/ — READ
- CXO Advisory notes on the book — https://www.cxoadvisory.com/technical-trading/a-few-notes-on-the-art-and-science-of-technical-analysis/ — LISTED (search excerpt read)
- MarketLife / *The Art and Science of Trading* course workbook TOC — via course mirrors, LISTED. Grimes states the MarketLife course "still is, and will always be, completely free" at MarketLifeTrading.com. 9 modules: 1 Chartreading 101, 2 Chartreading Going Deeper, 3 Market Structure & Price Action, 4 The Pullback, 5 The Anti, 6 The Failure Test, 7 Breakouts, 8 Pattern Failures, 9 Practical Trading Psychology.

**What it teaches / distinctive usable rules**
- Core ontology: "Every edge we have, as technical traders, comes from an imbalance of buying and selling pressure. We do not trade patterns — we trade the underlying imbalances that create those patterns."
- Two forces model (READ verbatim): price is shaped by mean reversion vs momentum; most of the time they balance and price is near-random, so the job is waiting. Mean-reversion tells: fading large single bars, fading N-day runs, fading breakouts of N-day highs/lows, fading large excursions from average price (Keltner/Bollinger). Momentum tells: pullbacks, nested pullbacks, breakouts in some cases, volatility compression.
- The Four Trades / market cycle (Ch.2): trend continuation, trend termination, etc.; strongest buying pressure often marks late trend, decisive seller victory sets up reversal.
- Reading protocol: relative-strength screen first, then individual patterns — only patterns "truly driven by large-scale institutional buying and selling pressure" are reliable.
- Named setups taught as complete modules: Pullback (and nested pullbacks), The Anti (a pullback variant Grimes borrowed the name from Raschke's Anti — see §3), Failure Test (price probes beyond a level, fails, reverses — trade the failure), Breakouts, and Pattern Failures as a trade in themselves.
- Support/resistance are zones/areas, not exact lines; stops placed beyond the zone, sized by normal vs extraordinary risk distinction (Ch.9).
- Trade management is daily, without fail (Ch.8).

**Limits:** Book is paid; full course video is on MarketLife (free per author, but the live site was not opened this pass — mirrors only). CXO notes the book offers no statistical validation of its rules; the method is discretionary and complex to codify.

## 2. Peter Brandt — Classical Charting (Factor)

**Sources**
- Factor site tag archive — https://www.peterlbrandt.com/tag/qqq/ — LISTED (member-gated excerpts visible in search)
- "How To Invest Like Peter Brandt" — https://pictureperfectportfolios.com/how-to-invest-like-peter-brandt-commodities-trader-and-chartist/ — READ
- Brandt X post on BTC "bull flag" rejection, reported at https://cryptonews.net/news/bitcoin/32720507/ and https://u.today/veteran-chartist-brandt-rejects-bitcoin-bull-flag-narrative — LISTED (search excerpt, quotes captured)
- Book: *Diary of a Professional Commodity Trader* (referenced in READ source).

**What it teaches / distinctive usable rules**
- Lineage rule: the rules are already written — Schabacker (1934) and Edwards & Magee, *Technical Analysis of Stock Trends* (1948). Do not invent rules to fit a bias. This is Brandt's most repeated public teaching act: publicly rejecting mis-labelled patterns.
- Flag/pennant duration rule (from the 2026 X post): a dependable flag/pennant must complete and break out within ~4 weeks; it is a brief, high-momentum pause "at half-mast" after an aggressive move. A 7–10 week upward-sloping channel that looks like a flag is NOT a flag and should not be expected to behave like one.
- Head & Shoulders validity rules (Factor post): three distinct peaks, right shoulder must fail / be lower than head, roughly symmetric in time and height, neckline connects two reaction lows (horizontal or slightly sloped — a rising support line is wedge logic, not a neckline), and volume must be greatest on left shoulder/head and lightest on right shoulder. The widely advertised index H&S top was rejected by Brandt because the largest volume was in the right shoulder.
- Drawing standard: price-only, clean charts — horizontal lines, trendlines, classical shapes (H&S, triangles, rectangles, double tops/bottoms, flags/pennants), measured moves, breakout/throwback logic. No indicator soup; indicators are derivatives of price.
- Timeframe: weekly/monthly charts, multi-month to multi-year structures; "the longer the base, the higher the space" — trapped capital at a boundary break fuels the move.
- Significance of closes: most significant price of the day is the close; of the week, Friday's close.
- Risk routine: defined invalidation before entry, hard stops in the system (never mental), ~1% risk per trade in the secondary summary (Brandt's own reported win rate 30–40%; expectancy from wins several times average loss; ~80% of profits from 15–20% of trades), take the cleanest of correlated breakouts rather than stacking correlated bets.

**Limits:** Factor Report proper is a paid membership; most peterlbrandt.com posts are members-only beyond excerpts. The detailed secondary profile is a third-party synthesis, not Brandt's own text — rules above that come from his quoted X/Factor excerpts are flagged as such; the 1% figure is the profile's summary.

## 3. Linda Raschke (with Larry Connors) — Street Smarts pattern library

**Sources**
- *Street Smarts: High Probability Short-Term Trading Strategies* (1995/96) pattern taxonomy via Trade2Win review — LISTED
- Turtle Soup deep rule comparison — https://proea.app/en/blog/turtle-soup — READ (quotes LuxAlgo, MQL5, TradingView, Oxford Strat renderings of the book rule; the page itself states it has not read the book — treat tick numbers as consensus of secondary ports, flagged below)
- Holy Grail — https://tradersmastermind.com/linda-raschke-trading-strategy/ — READ; corroborated by Gate/LiteFinance/useThinkScript summaries LISTED
- RoboForex summary of 80-20s / Turtle Soup / Anti — LISTED

**What it teaches / distinctive usable rules**
- Organisation of the whole library: patterns grouped by how support/resistance forms — Tests (Turtle Soup, Turtle Soup +1, 80-20s, Momentum Pinball, 2-period ROC), Retracements (The Anti, Holy Grail, ADX Gapper), Climax reversals (Whiplash, 3-day unfilled gap reversals, Spike and Ledge, Wolfe Waves).
- **Turtle Soup (original):** Fade a new 20-day high/low where the prior 20-day extreme is at least 4 sessions old (some ports say 3+ days — sources split, record both). Buy stop 5–10 ticks back inside the old low (mirror for highs), good for the day only; stop 1 tick beyond the day's new extreme. Turtle Soup +1 waits for the breakout day to close outside, then places the stop at the old extreme next day with a 2-day extreme stop. Key discipline: the soup fills BEFORE the close names the move a sweep or a real breakout — decide which reader you are in advance. ICT's later version drops the 20-day count and age rule entirely; it is not the original.
- **Holy Grail (retracement):** 14-period ADX > 30 AND rising (not extreme — very high ADX, e.g. >60, warns of exhaustion). Wait for first retracement to the 20-period EMA/SMA. Buy stop above the high of the bar that touches the MA (mirror short). Stop under the new swing low; trail as profits accrue; exit part at the most recent swing high. If stopped, one re-entry at the original entry price is allowed. For a second trade, ADX must again turn up above 30. Raschke herself has said in recent webinars many Street Smarts patterns have limited edge now; she singles out the Holy Grail as still working (per TradersMastermind).
- **The Anti:** Stochastic (7,10,3) — enter when the fast line hooks back in the direction of the slow line after a correction, joining the trend; stop behind the signal candle (per RoboForex summary).
- **80-20s:** Day-1 candle whose body is ≥80% of its range closing near its extreme; next day, trade the reversal after price overshoots the close (secondary summaries vary on the overshoot distance — book needed for the canonical number).
- Routine element: named, rule-complete setups with entry stop, protective stop, and re-entry rule all specified before the trade — the opposite of pattern-spotting without a plan.

**Limits:** The book is paid and out-of-print-priced; no full primary text was opened. Several numbers (Turtle Soup tick offsets, 80-20 overshoot) come from secondary ports that disagree in detail — flagged inline. LBR Group class transcripts exist (doczz mirror LISTED, not opened).

## 4. Brian Shannon — Anchored VWAP (AVWAP)

**Sources**
- Books: *Technical Analysis Using Multiple Timeframes*; *Maximum Trading Gains With Anchored VWAP: The Perfect Combination of Price, Time, and Volume*
- TrendSpider AVWAP help (attributes AVWAP to Shannon/AlphaTrends) — https://help.trendspider.com/kb/anchored-indicators/anchored-vwap-volume-weighted-average-price — LISTED
- Financial Wisdom guide to the AVWAP book — https://www.financialwisdomtv.com/post/maximum-trading-gains-using-price-time-volume — LISTED (search excerpt read)
- TradingView Multi-VWAP script notes summarising Shannon — LISTED; Optuma scripting/AVWAP article — LISTED
- Origin note: AVWAP was first developed by Paul Levine (1995–97, MIDAS); Shannon popularised and codified it for modern swing trading. Record the attribution correctly.

**What it teaches / distinctive usable rules**
- Definition: AVWAP = Σ(typical price × volume) / Σ(volume) starting not at the session open but at a trader-chosen anchor bar. It is the average cost basis of everyone who traded since the anchor: price above AVWAP = buyers since the anchor in control / in profit; below = sellers in control / buyers underwater. That is why it acts as dynamic support/resistance.
- **Anchor selection is the skill.** Valid anchors are events that change perception and reset cost basis: earnings gaps and major news, high-volume days (≥ ~1.5× normal volume, breakouts/breakdowns), swing highs and swing lows (price-based anchors, known with a lag but effective), breakaway/continuation gaps, FOMC/Fed announcements, FDA decisions, IPO day (often from the second intraday candle), year-to-date / month-to-date starts.
- Slope + position read: price above a rising 5-day AVWAP is bullish and is Shannon's recommended precondition for longs; price below a falling 5-day AVWAP is very bearish. The 5-day AVWAP proxies the last trading week.
- Advanced forms named in the book: Handoff AVWAP and Pinch AVWAP (two AVWAPs converging/passing control) — names and existence confirmed via summaries; mechanics require the book.
- Practitioner routine: start on the higher timeframe for trend and key AVWAPs, drill down for entries; pullback to a rising AVWAP from a significant low/gap is the entry location, stop below the most recent higher low; multiple anchors (e.g. YTD low + earnings gap) holding together = confluence.
- Anchoring bias rationale (via Optuma): participants anchor decisions to the price of the salient event, so the average price paid since that event is behaviourally real, not just arithmetic.

**Limits:** The definitive text is the paid book; public video summaries (YouTube/Vimeo) were listed, not transcribed in full. AlphaTrends community content is subscription. Anchor choice remains discretionary — two chartists can legitimately anchor differently.

## 5. Mark Minervini & David Ryan — CANSLIM lineage, VCP, pivot discipline

**Sources**
- *Momentum Masters* roundtable (Minervini, Ryan, Zanger, Ritchie II) flip-book pp.51–100 — https://pubhtml5.com/pklzi/jywe/basic/51-100 — LISTED (excerpts read in search)
- Super Trader Tactics webinar transcript (Minervini & Ryan, 2015) — LISTED
- TraderLion "Mastering the Volatility Contraction Pattern" — https://traderlion.com/technical-analysis/volatility-contraction-pattern/ — READ
- TradingSim / QuantStrategy / Makecade VCP guides — LISTED (consistent numbers cross-checked)

**What it teaches / distinctive usable rules**
- **VCP (Minervini's signature):** In a Stage 2 uptrend, a base made of 2–4 contractions (range 2–6), each roughly half the depth of the prior (e.g. −25% → −12% → −5%), tightening left to right, each contraction on lower volume, ending in a tight, quiet area on volume dry-up (multi-week lows) — the "line of least resistance." The footprint (number of Ts, depth of largest correction, tightness of the rightmost pullback, volume at each point) IS the identification method — Minervini explicitly built VCP as an overlay to judge whether a cup-with-handle or other base is genuinely constructive.
- **Pivot and entry:** Pivot = high of the final, tightest contraction. Buy the breakout through the pivot; Minervini generally waits for price to trade 5–20 cents above the pivot rather than a penny (Momentum Masters). Breakout volume: at least +25% (Ryan's minimum), best +100% or more; Minervini guides commonly cite +40–50% vs average as confirmation. Do not chase extended moves.
- **Ryan's simplification:** Two entries only — breakouts and pullbacks. "You don't have to look for cup with handles, or saucers, or W formations. Draw a line across the top of where most of the stock's trading has taken place. Then buy as it moves through that line." Base ≥ 4 weeks; always want a very tight price pattern before new highs.
- **Trend Template / stage gate:** Only buy in Stage 2 (advancing). Minervini's trend template (price above rising 50/150/200-day averages, near highs, RS strong) is the widely published form; the VCP READ source confirms the Stage 2 prerequisite.
- **Risk rules:** Stop 7–8% maximum, ideally 3–5% in a tight VCP because the pivot entry makes the stop structurally small; sell into strength as ranges widen late in a move (volatility expansion after contraction = distribution of the move, exit on the whip up, not the whip down); wall rules in Minervini's office: "No forced trades. No big losses." "Losers average losers."
- Base depth guide: healthy cup corrections ~10–35%; a 50%+ base usually signals too much overhead supply (via study flashcards of *Trade Like a Stock Market Wizard* — secondary).

**Limits:** Master Trader Program and Minervini Private Access are paid; the full Trend Template checklist and workbook (400 pages cited in the webinar) are course material, not public. Numbers above are the consistently repeated public versions.

## 6. William O'Neil CANSLIM — the shared ancestor (included because Minervini/Ryan teach inside it)

**Sources**
- AAII cup-with-handle article — LISTED; IBD Q&A with O'Neil (archived) — LISTED (excerpts read); TraderLion O'Neil summary — LISTED

**Rules captured:** Prior uptrend ≥ +30% before the base; cup-with-handle needs ≥ 7–8 weeks, U-shaped (not V) cup, handle in the upper half drifting slightly down on low volume; pivot = peak of the handle (+1/8 in O'Neil's original wording); buy only within 5% above the pivot; breakout volume ≥ +50% above the 50-day average (best +100–200%); cut every loss at 7–8% below purchase price, no exceptions; volume dry at cup bottom and in handle, expansion on the right side with more up-volume than down-volume.

**Limits:** CANSLIM's fundamental letters (C, A, N, etc.) are outside this TA census except where they gate the technical entry (earnings ≥ +25% is Ryan/Minervini's usual leader screen).

## 7. Stan Weinstein — Stage Analysis

**Sources**
- *Secrets for Profiting in Bull and Bear Markets* (1988)
- AlphaMethodX "Stan Weinstein's Book: Key Rules" — https://alphamethodx.com/blog/secrets-for-profiting-review/ — READ
- Legend AI / Quantum-Algo stage summaries — LISTED (consistent)

**What it teaches / distinctive usable rules**
- One chart, one line: weekly chart + 30-week simple moving average (30 weekly closes; ≈150-day on daily). Classify by price vs the MA and the MA's slope: Stage 1 basing (sideways after a decline, MA flat), Stage 2 advancing (price above a rising MA), Stage 3 topping (sideways after an advance, MA flattening), Stage 4 declining (price below a falling MA). Stages are conditions, not a fixed sequence — a base can fail, a top can re-enter Stage 2.
- Buy rule: weekly close breaking above the top of the Stage 1 base (the level where earlier rallies stopped) with the 30-week MA flattening or turning up. Prefer breakouts with no old peaks just above (overhead resistance check). Secondary entry: first pullback to the breakout level.
- Confirmation: breakout-week volume ~2× normal (one-week surge or multi-week build-up counts); Mansfield relative strength above zero or rising toward it.
- Reading order — "forest to trees": market's stage first (major averages, advance-decline line), then Stage 2 groups, then stocks inside them. Never buy a stock below a declining 30-week MA no matter how cheap.
- Sell/stop rules: initial stop under the low of the base — under support, NOT a percentage. Raise it under each successive pullback low and the rising MA; never lower it. Tighten when the MA flattens (Stage 3 onset). Place stops just under round numbers where order clusters sit. Do not hold for taxes, dividends, low P/E, or company quality — none changes the chart.
- Short side is the mirror: Stage 3 → Stage 4 breakdowns.

**Limits:** Book is paid/copyrighted (the READ source warns free PDFs in search are unauthorised scans — not used). Weinstein's later institutional work (Global Trend Alert) is subscription.

## 9. Thomas Bulkowski — Encyclopedia of Chart Patterns + ThePatternSite

**Sources**
- *Encyclopedia of Chart Patterns* (3rd ed.) — identification-guideline format sampled via publisher excerpt (Big M chapter) LISTED
- ThePatternSite, "Downside Weekly Reversals" — https://thepatternsite.com/WeeklyRevsDownside.html — READ in full. The site is the book's free public companion: one page per pattern, same structure as the book.
- QuantStrategy guide to Bulkowski's statistical method — LISTED

**What it teaches / distinctive usable rules — the identification approach itself is the lesson**
- Every pattern gets a fixed template: (1) **Identification Guidelines table** — characteristic-by-characteristic, each row a checkable visual rule; (2) performance statistics split by bull/bear market and breakout direction; (3) failure rates; (4) measure rule and how often it is met; (5) trading tactics; (6) worked examples.
- Worked example of the template (Downside Weekly Reversal, READ): weekly data only; inbound price trend up; exactly 2 bars; second bar is an outside week (higher high AND lower low than prior week); last bar closes below the prior bar's low. Breakout = close above the pattern high or below its low; wait for it before trading. Measure rule: pattern height added to/subtracted from the breakout extreme (met 52–72% depending on regime/direction in his sample). Failure defined precisely: failure to move ≥5% in the breakout direction (break-even failure rate 24% for bull-market down breakouts; average drop 12%; performance rank 22/23 — i.e. the template also teaches you which patterns are weak).
- Worked example 2 (Big M, from the publisher excerpt): steep, near-straight-line rise into the first peak (a rounded rise disqualifies it — that is a plain double top); rise height often ~2× the peak-to-valley depth; twin peaks within ~1% of each other on average; valid only on a close below the valley low; a close above the highest peak first kills the pattern.
- Method rules that generalise: define the pattern so a computer (or a disciplined eye) can find it without judgement calls; measure from breakout to the nearest 5-bar peak/valley; report throwback/pullback frequency because it changes entries; rank patterns against each other instead of treating all as equal; split every statistic by market regime.
- Practitioner use: ThePatternSite is free and current (pages carry dated corrections, e.g. "Fixed hold time errors… 3/3/25"), making it the desk's cheapest pattern-identification reference.

**Limits:** Book statistics rest on his samples (e.g. 1,234 stocks, Dec 1989–Dec 2012 for the page read; min price $5) and "perfect trades" assumptions — dated market regimes. Some patterns exist in only one edition (Downside Weekly Reversal is first-edition only). Bulkowski measures US equities on daily/weekly scales; intraday transfer is on the reader.

## Cross-teacher synthesis (for the mastery program, not a verdict on any teacher)

- **Drawing standards converge:** horizontal levels from repeated reaction points (Brandt necklines, Ryan's "line across the top," Weinstein's base top, O'Neil's pivot) beat freehand trendlines everywhere; closes, especially weekly/Friday closes, confirm breaks (Brandt, Weinstein, Bulkowski's close-based breakouts).
- **Volume has three distinct jobs:** validity of a pattern (Brandt's H&S shoulder volume), confirmation of a break (Weinstein ~2×, O'Neil +50%, Ryan +25% minimum), and exhaustion of supply before the break (Minervini's dry-up, O'Neil's handle dry-up).
- **Time is an identification rule, not a detail:** Brandt's 4-week flag limit, Raschke's 4-session age rule for the 20-day extreme, O'Neil's 7–8-week minimum base, Weinstein's 30 weeks, Bulkowski's exact bar counts. Most mis-labelling in public charting is a duration violation.
- **Every teacher specifies the failure trade or the failure exit:** Grimes trades pattern failures directly; Raschke's Turtle Soup IS a failed-breakout trade; Brandt/Bulkowski define invalidation by close beyond the pattern; Minervini/Weinstein place stops under structure, never at arbitrary percentages (O'Neil's 7–8% is the exception, and it is portfolio-level, paired with the pivot entry that keeps structural risk near it).
