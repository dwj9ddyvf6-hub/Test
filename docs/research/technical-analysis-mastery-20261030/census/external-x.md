## 1. Echo Analysis — @EchoAnalysis

- URL: https://x.com/EchoAnalysis ; newsletter republication read: https://echoanalysis.kit.com/posts/how-to-use-fibonacci ; mirror seen: https://xstalk.com/profile/EchoAnalysis
- What it teaches: Elliott Wave counting plus Fibonacci confluence as a complete swing-chart language: where the buy zone is, what would invalidate the count, and which moving average must be reclaimed before the next wave is "unlocked."
- Annotation style: Templated and highly repeatable. Ticker, a short verdict line, a numbered wave count drawn on the chart (circled numerals for primary degree, A-B-C for corrections), shaded Fibonacci buy zones, a trendline, and 50 / 200 DMA (and 50 / 200 WMA on weekly views) conditionals printed in the text. Upside is stated as a percentage to a named wave target ("Wave 3 target: +429% upside" in mirror-sampled posts).
- What is marked: Wave labels 1-5 and A-B-C, Fib retracement zones (not single lines), trendline support, 50-DMA, 200-DMA, 200-WMA, horizontal support that held a prior wave low.
- Identification / drawing rules (from the newsletter, read in full; this is the most explicit drawing standard found in this pass):
  - Two different Fib tools for two different jobs. Retracement (two clicks, swing low then swing high) measures a pullback: use for Wave 2, Wave 4, and the pullback after a completed five. Trend-based extension (three clicks: start of Wave 1, end of Wave 1, end of Wave 2) projects targets: use for every Wave 3, Wave 5 and Wave C target. Reading the 1.618 off the retracement tool instead is, in his teaching, the common expensive mistake (worked example: retracement 1.618 at 180.90 vs extension target 200 on the same structure).
  - Anchor wick to wick: "Price traded at the wick, so the wick is the true high or low, not the candle body."
  - Draw in the direction of the move (uptrend low to high, downtrend high to low); use only meaningful swings; draw the higher timeframe first (weekly before daily, daily before 4-hour); when timeframes disagree the higher one wins.
  - Log scale on weekly / monthly charts or any move of several hundred percent before drawing.
  - Levels are zones, not lines. A wick through a level does not kill it; invalidation is a close through the bottom of the zone. Wave 2 zone: 0.5 to 0.786, most often 0.618 to 0.786; 0.886 still possible but low conviction; 1.0 retracement is invalidation ("there was no wave 1"). Wave 4 zone: 0.236 to 0.5, most often 0.382 to 0.5. Wave 3 targets: 1.272 is a stall point, 1.618 is the primary target, 2.0 to 2.618 means Wave 3 is extending and the degree should be rechecked.
  - Confluence rule: minimum two Fib levels in the same zone before calling it high probability; a moving average, trendline or old support on top makes it stronger. Worked $COIN example: 0.618 retracement of the whole impulse at $154.72, horizontal support $140-$160 (the prior Wave 4 low area), and the 200-WMA through the same zone, with an RSI higher-low divergence as the reaction trigger.
- Recurring heuristic in his own words: "I do not buy the level. I buy the reaction to it. The zone tells you where to watch. Price tells you when to act." Also, from mirror-sampled X posts: reclaim language as the trigger ("reclaim both MAs and Wave 3 is unlocked"; "flip that to support, break the trendline, game on").
- Cadence: Daily X posts in templated form; a free newsletter series that teaches the drawing rules in numbered issues (the Fibonacci issue is email six of the sequence). Mirror showed about 33K followers at crawl.
- Limits: X posts are the compressed cut; the drawing standards live in the newsletter, not the posts. Wave counting is degree-sensitive and his own rules require rechecking the degree when extensions run past 2.0. Mentorship is sold at the foot of the newsletter. No sign-in read; X evidence is via Xstalk mirror (crawl dated about 305 days before this pass in search metadata, so treat the sampled posts as style evidence, not as current calls).

## 2. Alex Desjardins — @PrimeTrading_

- URL: https://x.com/PrimeTrading_ ; playbook read in full: https://primetrading.substack.com/p/alexs-technical-setups-playbook- ; method republication: https://qullamaggie.net/alex-desjardinss-method/
- What it teaches: A named, finite playbook of swing entries built almost entirely from two drawn objects: a down trend line (DTL) over a pullback and a pivot (the prior swing / breakout high). Liquidity and relative-strength screening first, then a tactical drawn entry.
- Annotation style: Clean daily charts with the DTL drawn solid, higher lows marked with a dashed up trend line (UTL), the pivot price written on the chart (for example FUTU 59.27, BNTX 222.18, NEX 6.63, DOCN 87.49 in the playbook), and the setup given a code name. X posts add portfolio state: entries, exits with P and L, exposure percentage.
- What is marked: DTL, dashed UTL through higher lows, pivot / breakout level, 10 EMA and 21 EMA pullback levels, low of day (LOD) as the stop reference.
- Identification / drawing rules (playbook, read in full):
  - Seven setups only: Wedge Break (WB), Wedge Break Pullback (WBPB), Breakout Retest Short (BORS, as labelled in the playbook text for the retest family), Breakout Retest Long (BORL), Breakout 10 EMA Pullback (BO10PB), Breakout 21 EMA Pullback (BO21PB), plus the bonus Second Entry / Higher Lows.
  - Wedge: an orderly pullback contained by a DTL, low volume inside the wedge, small down-swings. Entry is the DTL break, or the breakout day's high, or the H1 high intraday, ideally on above-average volume. Stop: LOD or the recent swing low. The same three-trigger, same-stop template repeats for every setup in the book.
  - Retest: after a base breakout, price returns to the breakout pivot on ideally low volume and reverses on higher volume; entry is the retest day's high.
  - EMA pullbacks are the consolation entry when a powerful breakout gives no retest: wait for the pullback to the 10 or 21 DMA to form, then use the same DTL / swing-high triggers.
  - The shared tell across all setups: higher lows forming inside the base right before the break. "I always mark them in dashed Up Trend Line (UTL) on my charts."
  - Screening funnel (from the method republication): UniverseList about 500 names, WatchList 10-100, FocusList 0-10, then execute. WatchList technical filters: above the 200 DMA, above the 50 DMA, 20 DMA above 50 DMA, plus relative-strength rankings at 1, 3 and 6 months. Full position at entry, no scaling in; alerts set on the drawn levels in TradingView.
- Recurring heuristics in his own words: "That's the kind of action you want to see before a breakout. Selling pressure is out, so once the buyers come back, the path of least resistance is up." Current X / Prime Report language adds a market-first gate: read the index against its "21dma-structure" first; no market confirmation, no portfolio risk.
- Cadence: Daily. Daily Prime Report, daily swing portfolio updates with dated entries and exits including losses (seed file verified 2026-10-29 update closing five losers by name and percent), focus lists, and replies. That dated loss disclosure is the strongest honesty signal in the seed roster.
- Limits: The full room, live executions and the wiki are a paid community; the free playbook article is from 2022 and the terminology has since grown (21dma-structure, stretch / sigma language) beyond it. X evidence via TwStalker mirror.

## 3. Oliver Kell — @OliverKell_

- URL: https://x.com/OliverKell_ ; framework republication read in full: https://cyclesedge.substack.com/p/follow-the-cycle-of-price-action ; book: Victory in Stock Trading (kelltrading.com)
- What it teaches: The Cycle of Price Action: before any pattern talk, name which of six stages the stock is in relative to its 10 and 20 EMAs, because the correct action (buy, add, trim, exit, do nothing) is dictated by the stage.
- Annotation style: Sparse by design. Price plus the 10 EMA (red) and 20 EMA (blue) and a stage label. X posts are short and level-specific: a key level to hold, a zone to build through, a level below which he will not hold through earnings. Levels often sit in replies, not only top-level posts (seed file note).
- What is marked: 10 EMA, 20 EMA (he adjusts to 8 / 21 EMA when that is what the market is respecting, and says so), stage boundaries, prior swing highs as "build through" zones.
- Identification rules (six-stage cycle, bullish direction):
  1. Reversal Extension: price stretched sharply below the EMAs into a capitulation low. Watch for basing; "do not short the hole."
  2. Wedge Pop: break out of a downtrend wedge or base, often gapping above the 10 and 20 EMAs. Aggressive early long.
  3. EMA Crossback: price touches or briefly breaks, then reclaims the 10 / 20 EMAs. Add; trend confirmed.
  4. Base 'n Break: tight consolidation (flag, pennant, rectangle) along rising EMAs, then a break to a new high when the pattern high breaks. Repeatable continuation buy.
  5. Exhaustion Extension: price stretched dramatically above the 10 EMA, climactic / parabolic. Trim; risk-reward has flipped.
  6. Wedge Drop: rollover and break down through the 10 EMA. Exit remaining longs. The bearish cycle is the same six stages mirrored, used mostly for exits and risk management rather than aggressive shorting.
  - Operating rules: identify the current stage first; trade with the cycle, not against it; rising EMAs validate longs, declining EMAs validate shorts; size up early in the cycle, size down late; the cycle is fractal across weekly, daily and intraday charts; align weekly context, daily management, and a 65-minute chart for execution (conference-summary republication).
- Recurring heuristics: "Simplicity is most powerful" (the republication's framing of his book). In X practice the heuristic is the single named level: hold above it or the trade is over.
- Cadence: Frequent short X posts and replies around live positions; 2020 U.S. Investing Champion, 941 percent (stated in his book material and the seed file). About 118K followers per the seed file.
- Limits: The stage labels are judgment calls on extension distance, not measured thresholds; two readers can disagree on Reversal vs Exhaustion Extension on the same chart. Full teaching is in the paid book / courses. X evidence is partly via republication and the seed file's 2026-10-30 crawl.

## 4. Pradeep Bonde (Stockbee) — @PradeepBonde

- URL: https://x.com/PradeepBonde ; mirrors read via search: https://instalker.org/PradeepBonde and https://twstalker.com/PradeepBonde ; EP9 exposition: https://breakoutshappen.com/stock-news/ep9-million-method-how-the-stockbee-framework-works
- What it teaches: Momentum bursts and the Episodic Pivot (EP) family: a neglected stock re-priced in one session by a real catalyst on enormous volume. The chart craft is mostly in the qualification checklist and in knowing the difference between the entry day and a delayed entry, rather than in line drawing.
- Annotation style: Minimal drawing, maximal labelling. X is mostly short Q and A replies naming the setup type (EP, EP9, Delayed EP), the volume figure, and whether the name is holding an EMA. The annotated-chart work lives in his Stockbee material, scans and videos rather than in X images.
- What is marked: The catalyst-day range (gap, high, low of day), the 9-million-share volume print, EMA support on a delayed entry, the opening-range high as the trigger.
- Identification rules (assembled from his public material as republished; treat the numbers as his published framework, not as independently audited rules):
  - Momentum burst premise: stocks move in bursts of about 3 to 5 days gaining roughly 8 to 40 percent; the work is to catch range expansion at the start of the burst. His own expectancy framing, quoted in the seed file: singles at 8 to 20 percent are the base strategy, mini home runs at 30 to 50 percent are "the cream on top."
  - EP: a real surprise (earnings-type catalyst, not an upgrade or hype), a stock neglected sideways for months to years, a gap that clears all moving averages and the recent range ("free air", no trapped sellers overhead), and volume so large that a normal day's volume can trade in the first 5 to 15 minutes. Enter on the gap day through the opening-range high; stop at the low of the day; skip if that stop is wider than about one average daily range. Sell a third to a half into strength after 3 to 5 days, trail the rest on the 10 or 20 day average (Qullamaggie-stream codification of the same teaching).
  - EP9 / EP9M: the volume-qualified variant. Published scan line as republished: volume at or above about 8.9 to 9 million shares on the day and price at or above $3, which cuts a roughly 12,000-name universe to a handful of candidates, then qualified by trigger and catalyst type.
  - Delayed EP (DEP): the catalyst is real but the stock has already moved too far to chase. Wait for an orderly pullback holding EMA support, pre-stage the order, enter in the first minutes of the renewed breakout with a very tight stop; three stopped attempts on the same name means walk away; a real DEP breaks out within about 3 to 5 days.
  - Ant / tightness scans in his screener tradition: Trend Intensity (7-day average close at least 5 percent above the 65-day average), the +4 percent range-expansion flag after quiet narrow days, and tight-tight-tight bars (three bars within about 1.5 percent, today within about 0.3 percent) as accumulation footprints.
  - Every setup is qualified with a written checklist, and his decision framework is market breadth first ("My framework is based on market breadth", X reply as mirrored).
- Recurring heuristics in his own words (as quoted in republications): "EP is what people know me for. In reality it is my least important setup today." Ranking as republished from his podcast material: EP9M for most returns, DEP for most setups, classic EP least frequent.
- Cadence: Daily, but conversational: many short replies to followers' ticker questions rather than a daily chart drop. About 93K followers per the seed file.
- Limits: The least chart-visual teacher in this census; a student learns his craft from the checklist, the scans and the long-form Stockbee material, not from X annotations. Some EP9 numbers above come from third-party expositions of his framework (Breakouts Happen, TradingView screener ports) and may drift from his current paid material. Biotech catalyst risk is a named exception in his own replies.

## 5. Stage Analysis — @stageanalysis

- URL: https://x.com/stageanalysis ; method source: Stan Weinstein, Secrets for Profiting in Bull and Bear Markets (1988); guide read via search: https://traderlion.com/trading-strategies/stage-analysis/ and https://legendscanner.com/legends/weinstein
- What it teaches: Weinstein stage classification applied as a weekly watchlist discipline, blended with Wyckoff re-accumulation language: classify first (Stage 1 basing, 2 advancing, 3 topping, 4 declining), and only Stage 2 breakouts are buys.
- Annotation style: Weekly-chart watchlists. Each name carries a stage label, the moving average being tested (typically the 30-week / 150-day and the 200-day), a relative-strength note, and the breakout condition. Seed-file example, 2026-10-16 post: $HUBS late Stage 1, testing the 200-day moving average, approaching a Stage 2 breakout; 2026-10-28 U.S. watchlist listed 18 names from the stage scans.
- What is marked: 30-week moving average (slope and price position against it), the base's resistance line, Mansfield relative strength versus the market, volume on the breakout week, Wyckoff / VCP base labels inside Stage 1.
- Identification rules (Weinstein's published rules, which this account operationalises):
  - Classify every chart first: Stage 1 base, Stage 2 advance, Stage 3 top, Stage 4 decline, read off the weekly chart.
  - Buy only a Stage 2 breakout: price through a well-defined resistance level, above a rising 30-week MA, on expanded volume (TraderLion's guide states ideally 2 to 3 times average), with relative strength confirming and the sector / market supportive.
  - Never buy below a declining 30-week MA, no matter how cheap it looks.
  - After the breakout, the Stage 2 signature is higher highs and higher lows holding above the rising 50-day, 200-day and 30-week averages; a light-volume retest that holds at or just above the breakout point strengthens it.
  - Stage 3 tells: failed breakouts on low volume and a flattening 30-week / 200-day average with price whipping around it. Stage 4 is untouchable for longs.
  - The account's own refinement (via republication): the account holder often prefers a Wyckoff buy point slightly earlier inside late Stage 1 rather than waiting for the exact Stage 1 to 2 boundary print.
- Cadence: Weekly watchlists (dated), plus single-name stage calls between them. About 39K followers per the seed file.
- Limits: A watchlist source, not an entry source by design; stage boundaries are weekly-close judgments and lag turns by construction. The account applies Weinstein, so the primary text is a 1988 book, not X. Direct x.com open was blocked in the seed pass; evidence is from StageAnalysis.net dated posts and search excerpts.

## 6. Brian Shannon (Alphatrends) — @alphatrends

- URL: https://x.com/alphatrends ; books: Technical Analysis Using Multiple Timeframes; Maximum Trading Gains With Anchored VWAP. Supporting reads via search: TrendSpider AVWAP documentation https://help.trendspider.com/kb/anchored-indicators/anchored-vwap-volume-weighted-average-price and Shannon's Trade Ideas summit slides.
- What it teaches: Anchored VWAP (AVWAP) as the central drawn object, inside a multiple-timeframe trend routine. Where Minervini draws a base and Brandt draws a pattern boundary, Shannon draws the average price paid since a meaningful event and asks who is in control from that anchor.
- Annotation style: Price with several AVWAPs from different anchors (earnings, gap, swing high / low, year-to-date, IPO), moving averages for trend context, and standard-deviation bands around key AVWAPs. X text posts name confluent levels precisely (seed file: BTC testing AVWAP confluence near $80K with a possible undercut to $78K; three confluent levels at the $GOOGL 275 area with the year-to-date low at 272.11).
- What is marked: AVWAP lines per anchor event, the 5-day VWAP slope, year-to-date and earnings anchors, volume-by-price shelves (in the TrendSpider tradition he influenced), prior highs / lows.
- Identification / drawing rules:
  - An anchor is a point where psychology reset: an earnings report, a major gap, a significant swing high or low, the start of a year, an IPO. Anchor choice is, in his own slide language, more art than science; the calculation from the anchor is objective (sum of price times volume over volume).
  - Control rule: price above an AVWAP means buyers since that anchor are in profit and in control; below it, sellers are. A rising 5-day VWAP with price above it is the bullish short-term condition; price below a declining 5-day VWAP is "very bearish" in the TrendSpider codification of his teaching.
  - Direction before location: a move above a declining VWAP will often fail; a move below an advancing VWAP will often fail. Rising VWAP equals positive money flow for that timeframe, declining equals negative, flat equals neutral.
  - The AVWAP hand-off: when price taps a base AVWAP for the last time, anchor a new AVWAP there; a chain of hand-offs traces the trend's pivots. If the AVWAPs look messy, no trend has started.
  - Multi-timeframe routine: define trend on the higher timeframe first, then drop down for a low-risk entry near an AVWAP, sizing off the distance to the stop (his interview arithmetic: a $1,000 risk unit with a 20-cent stop is 5,000 shares; with a $2 stop it is 500).
  - Heuristic from the seed file's sampled post: "buy strength after the dip, not the dip" where confluence sits below.
- Recurring phrase: "Only Price Pays" (his long-running motto, in his X bio as mirrored).
- Cadence: Near-daily market and level posts on X, daily video / subscriber analysis off X. About 426-431K followers on mirrors at crawl, the largest free chart-craft audience in this census.
- Limits: The most specific daily levels sit in the paid Alphatrends subscription; free X posts are levels of interest, not full trade plans. AVWAP anchor selection is discretionary, so two students can draw different "correct" lines on the same chart and both be following the method.

## 7. Kristjan Kullamägi — @Qullamaggie

- URL: https://x.com/Qullamaggie ; free method site: https://qullamaggie.com ; secondary study read in full: https://www.geckomode.ai/blog/qullamaggie-modern-breakout-master ; stream-codification: https://whatworksintrading.substack.com/p/kristjan-qullamaggie-and-stockbee
- What it teaches: Three setups only, taught through an enormous public archive of annotated winner charts and livestream walk-throughs: breakouts from tight bases in leading stocks, episodic pivots, and parabolic shorts. The chart craft is pattern recognition trained by volume of examples ("studying charts for hours, going back through the biggest winners in market history").
- Annotation style: Daily chart, the prior advance, the tight base boxed or bracketed, the breakout level, and the 10 / 20 day moving averages the position will be trailed on. Minimal indicators. X itself is now mostly ideas and commentary; the deep annotation lives in the site guides, streams and the chart database tradition he preaches.
- Identification rules (from his site and stream transcripts as codified by others; flagged as codifications, not a single primary text read in this pass):
  - Breakout: stock already up roughly 30 to 100 percent or more in the prior 1 to 3 months, then an orderly 2 to 8 week consolidation "surfing" the rising 10 or 20 day average, tight and shallow, then a break to new highs. Stop below the breakout day's low / consolidation low, and never wider than about one ADR (average daily range). Sell a third to a half after 3 to 5 days of advance, trail the rest on the 10 or 20 day average.
  - Episodic pivot: Bonde's setup (entry 4) with his execution: gap up about 10 percent or more on a genuine surprise, flat base for months before, buy the opening-range high on the gap day, stop at the day's low.
  - Parabolic short: a stock up 50 to 100 percent (large caps) or several hundred percent (small caps) in days to weeks with 3 to 5 or more straight up days, extended far from its averages; short the first lower high / break of the prior day's low, stop above the high.
  - Risk frame repeated everywhere: roughly 0.25 to 1 percent account risk per trade, 10 to 20 percent position size, never more than about 30 percent in one name overnight; adds only when already green.
- Recurring heuristic (as summarised from his public material): "Buy strong stocks breaking out of tight ranges, risk a little, cut losers fast, and let the winners run." And the behavioural corollary he repeats: most of the time the right move is to do nothing and sit in cash until an A+ setup appears.
- Cadence: X posting is episodic in recent years compared with his streaming peak; the durable teaching corpus is the website and archived streams, which is where a student should go. Impostor handles are a documented problem; the Gecko study explicitly warns to verify the handle.
- Limits: Return figures are self-reported and should be taken as his account, as the secondary source itself cautions. Rules above are reconstructed from transcripts and third-party cheat sheets, which occasionally disagree on exact percentages; the primary site is the arbiter. He was in the seed file's people index as a studied trader, not on the TRADER-FEED roster.

## 8. Mark Minervini — @markminervini

- URL: https://x.com/markminervini ; books: Trade Like a Stock Market Wizard; Think and Trade Like a Champion. Codifications read via search: https://makecade.com/guides-minervini-vcp.html and TradingView Trend Template / VCP implementations.
- What it teaches: SEPA (Specific Entry Point Analysis): a strict Trend Template gate that a chart must pass before pattern reading is even allowed, then the Volatility Contraction Pattern (VCP) as the entry structure.
- Annotation style: The VCP chart is the signature image of this school: successive pullbacks labelled by depth (T1, T2, T3, T4), volume drying up underneath, and a single horizontal pivot line at the final contraction's high. X posts mix trade examples with heavy workshop / Master Trader Program promotion (the reason the seed file cut him from the monitoring roster while keeping the method).
- What is marked: 50, 150 and 200 day SMAs and their stacking order, 52-week high and low, contraction depths, the pivot, the stop below the final contraction's low, the RS line / RS rating.
- Identification rules (Trend Template, as codified consistently across sources):
  1. Price above the 150 and 200 day averages.
  2. 150 day above the 200 day.
  3. 200 day rising for at least a month, preferably several.
  4. 50 day above both the 150 and 200 day.
  5. Price above the 50 day.
  6. Price at least 30 percent above its 52-week low.
  7. Price within 25 percent of its 52-week high.
  8. Relative strength rating strong (70 minimum in most codifications; he prefers 80-90 plus).
  - VCP anatomy: pullbacks halve as supply is absorbed, for example minus 24 percent, then minus 12, then minus 6, then minus 2.5, over 2 to 4 contractions (can run 2 to 6) spanning roughly 2 to 60 weeks; volume falls to multi-week lows into the final contraction (volume dry-up); buy the close through the pivot on expanding volume (codifications use at least about 1.4 times the 50-day average) and do not chase extended entries; maximum stop 7 to 8 percent, with quality VCPs allowing 3 to 5 percent.
- Cadence: Frequent X posts, but in October 2026 (seed-file check) dominated by workshop promotion with infrequent trade disclosure.
- Limits: Learn the method from the books and the many faithful codifications, not from the current X feed. Exact thresholds vary slightly between codifiers (25 vs 30 percent off the low, RS floor); the books govern. Paid training is expensive and heavily marketed.

## 9. Peter Brandt — @PeterLBrandt

- URL: https://x.com/PeterLBrandt ; Factor LLC / Factor Report: https://www.peterlbrandt.com/factor-report-march-13th/ ; CFA Magazine interview (PDF via his site): https://www.peterlbrandt.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/10/PETER-BRANDT-CFA-MAGAZINE-interview-October-2018.pdf
- What it teaches: Classical charting in the Schabacker and Edwards and Magee tradition, applied mostly to futures, FX and crypto: a pattern is a record of accumulation or distribution, and the trade only exists when a horizontal boundary breaks.
- Annotation style: The cleanest charts in this census. Daily or weekly price, one pattern boundary pair, a measured-move target, and almost nothing else. No indicator stack. X posts are single-pattern calls with the boundary price stated.
- What is marked: Rectangle boundaries, right-angle (ascending / descending) triangle horizontal boundaries, head-and-shoulders necklines, flags and pennants, the breakout close, the throwback / retest.
- Identification rules (from his own Factor material and CFA interview, via search excerpts):
  - Preferred patterns: head and shoulders, rectangles, and right-angle triangles; he explicitly minimises symmetrical triangles, wedges and trendlines as unreliable and prone to morphing into larger patterns.
  - Duration matters: chart configurations of at least about 12 weeks for the major patterns.
  - In his later stated rule (as quoted in republications of his teaching): trade only patterns where the breakout is through the horizontal boundary; you find out much more quickly whether you are right or wrong.
  - Entry on a decisive close beyond the boundary, or on the breakout plus throwback; no anticipation. Stop just beyond the structural failure point (opposite boundary / neckline / defining swing).
  - Risk routine as quoted in republications: risk about 0.5 percent of equity per trade, get the stop to breakeven or better within 2 to 3 days, take half off when profit equals about 1 percent of equity, and tighten protection as price nears the measured target.
  - His self-test for trade quality: "When I place a trade, I think: a year from now when I look back at the chart, will I be able to see in the chart the day and the price at which I took a position? If the answer is yes, then it is a good trade."
- Recurring motto: "Strong opinions, weakly held."

## Cross-cutting synthesis for the mastery program

- Three drawing grammars dominate X: (1) the base-and-pivot grammar (Minervini, Qullamaggie, Bonde, Zanger, Moglen, PrimeTrading, Soreide, Caruso), where the drawn objects are a base boundary, a trend line over the pullback, and one pivot price, and volume dry-up inside the base is the quality test; (2) the average-price grammar (Shannon, Wujastyk, McMillan), where the drawn object is an AVWAP from a named event and the question is who is in control since that anchor; (3) the cycle / stage grammar (Weinstein via @stageanalysis, Kell), where nothing is drawn until the chart is classified, and the classification dictates the action.
- Echo Analysis stands alone in this census with a fully written drawing standard (tool choice, wick-to-wick anchoring, timeframe order, log scale, zone-not-line, close-based invalidation, two-level confluence minimum). It is the only source here whose Fibonacci rules could be followed identically by two students on the same chart.
- The shared heuristic across otherwise rival schools: do not buy the level, buy the reaction / confirmation at the level. Echo says it verbatim; Zanger's rule 9, Brandt's decisive-close rule, Bonde's opening-range trigger, and PrimeTrading's DTL-or-pivot-or-H1 trigger ladder are the same idea in different vocabularies.
- Stops are always structural in this census, never arbitrary percentages alone: low of the breakout day, low of the final contraction, the opposite pattern boundary, the neckline, LOD, or the prior week's low. The percentage caps (Minervini 7 to 8 percent, Qullamaggie one ADR) act as vetoes on structurally wide stops.
- Cadence is itself a craft signal: the teachers with a fixed routine (Meisler's hand-posting, Keller's ordered morning ChartLists, Stage Analysis's weekly watchlist, PrimeTrading's daily dated portfolio update, Bonde's daily breadth-first replies) teach process by repetition. The X feed is the routine made visible; the playbooks and books are where the routine is explained.
- Biggest access gap for a student: the annotated chart books are paywalled almost everywhere (Factor Report, All Star Charts Premium, Alphatrends, TraderLion / Deepvue, Real Money), while the rules are mostly free. A mastery program can therefore learn the rules from public sources, as this census does, but cannot fully audit a teacher's live annotation record without subscribing.
