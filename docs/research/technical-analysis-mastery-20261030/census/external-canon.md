### 1.1 The ChartSchool reading protocol (applies to every pattern)

1. **Prior trend is mandatory for a reversal.** "Without a prior uptrend to reverse, there cannot be a Head and Shoulders reversal pattern (or any reversal pattern)." Trend is established by peak/trough sequence (rising peaks+troughs = uptrend; falling = downtrend; horizontal = trading range), trendlines, or moving averages. Trends classified major (>6 months), intermediate (1-6 months), minor (<1 month).
2. **Name the anatomy in order**, point by point, before calling the pattern.
3. **Volume is part of the pattern**, not an afterthought (see per-pattern volume scripts below).
4. **The pattern does not exist until the break.** Confirmation = a *convincing* break of the defining line (neckline, triangle line, resistance/support), ideally with volume expansion. Intraday pokes do not count as the finished article's standard.
5. **Polarity:** broken support becomes resistance and vice versa; the retest ("return move" / throwback) is the second-chance entry and a validation test.
6. **Measured target:** pattern height projected from the breakout point, treated explicitly as "a rough guide" to be subordinated to prior support/resistance, Fibonacci, and long-term moving averages.

### 1.2 Head and Shoulders Top — READ IN FULL

- Prior uptrend required. Three successive peaks: middle (head) highest, shoulders lower and roughly equal; symmetry preferred, not absolute (widths/heights may differ).
- Neckline: connect reaction low 1 (end of left shoulder / start of head) and low 2 (end of head / start of right shoulder). Slope matters: down-sloping neckline is more bearish than up-sloping.
- Right shoulder is a lower high vs the head, usually in line with the left shoulder.
- Volume script: highest ideally on left-shoulder advance; diminished on head advance (new high on less volume = warning 1); pick-up on decline from head (warning 2); decrease on right-shoulder advance; expansion on the neckline break (final confirmation).
- Completion: neckline support broken convincingly, with volume expansion. After the break, neckline often flips to resistance on a retest.
- Target: vertical distance head-to-neckline, subtracted from the neckline break point.

### 1.3 Head and Shoulders Bottom — READ VIA SEARCH EXTRACT

Mirror of the top: prior downtrend, three troughs, middle deepest, shoulders shallower and ideally equal in height/width, neckline through the two intervening reaction highs. Key difference stressed by ChartSchool: **volume expansion on the neckline breakout is "absolutely required" for a bottom**, whereas for a top it is welcomed but the pattern can complete on the price break. Target: head-to-neckline distance added above the neckline.

### 1.4 Support and Resistance — READ IN FULL

- Definitions: support = level where demand is thought strong enough to prevent further decline; resistance = level where supply is thought strong enough to prevent further rise. Both are supply/demand junctures, not magic lines.
- Where established: prior reaction lows (support) and prior reaction highs (resistance). Strength grows with repeated tests and with higher timeframe.
- **Zones, not just lines:** because TA "is not an exact science," use zones when the range is wide/long; exact levels when the range is tight (<~2 months, narrow). A close 1/8 beyond a level does not automatically count as a break.
- Polarity principle (taught with worked HAL, $NDX, PeopleSoft examples): broken support becomes resistance because trapped buyers sell at breakeven ("escape" supply overhang); broken resistance becomes support.
- Trading range logic: range = balance; break above = bulls won, break below = bears won. False breakout warning: WorldCom example — brief poke above 62, gap down nullified it, then support at 55 broke.
- Drawing nuance from the Lucent example: if lows align better on a sloped neckline than a horizontal support line, draw the sloped line — it gave the break one point earlier.

### 1.5 Trendlines — READ VIA SEARCH EXTRACT (TA 101 Part 8/9 + Trend Lines article references)

- Uptrend line: positive slope, connects **two or more low points**, extended forward; first two points establish, additional points validate. Acts as support; price above = uptrend intact; break below = demand weakened, trend change imminent.
- Downtrend line: negative slope, connects two or more high points; acts as resistance; break above = supply decreasing.
- A trend itself is read as "a tilted support/resistance zone." Channel = trendline plus a parallel return line (see Bulkowski channels for the stricter touch rules).

### 1.6 Triangles, Wedges, Flags, Pennants, Cup — ChartSchool rules as extracted

- **Falling wedge (bullish):** prior downtrend at least 3 months old as a reversal (typically forms 3-6 months); upper resistance line through at least 2, ideally 3, successively lower reaction highs; lower support line through at least 2 successively lower reaction lows; lines must **converge**, support sloping down less steeply than resistance; parallel lines = descending channel, not a wedge. Confirmation: convincing break above the upper line, ideally with volume expansion. Intraday break without close/volume = not confirmed.
- **Rising wedge (bearish mirror):** begins wide, contracts as prices rise; both lines slope up, support steeper than resistance; volume ideally falls inside the wedge; confirmed only by a convincing break below support. "Regardless of type (reversal or continuation), rising wedges are bearish."
- **Triangles (canon summary across ChartSchool-derived sources):** ascending = flat resistance + rising support (bullish bias, confirm on close above flat resistance); descending = flat support + falling resistance (bearish bias); symmetrical = converging lines of similar slope, direction resolved by the break, usually in the direction of the prior trend. Volume contracts inside, expands on breakout.
- **Flags / pennants:** short-term continuation after a sharp move (flagpole). Flag = small parallelogram / rectangle sloping against the prior trend; pennant = small symmetrical triangle. Pause on light volume, resumption on a burst of activity; classically near the midpoint of the move; a pause that drags for dozens of candles stops being a flag.
- **Cup with handle:** rounded U-shaped cup (not V), rims near the same price, then a short handle consolidation (flag/pennant-like, drifting down) on the right; confirmation on breakout above handle/cup-rim resistance on volume.

### 1.7 Gaps — READ VIA SEARCH EXTRACT (Gaps and Gap Analysis + TA 101 Part 15)

- Strict definition: up gap = today's low > yesterday's high; down gap = today's high < yesterday's low. No traded prices in between.
- **Common gap:** inside a trading range or just after a sharp move; no change in psychology; tends to fill.
- **Breakaway gap:** gaps out of a base / top / consolidation at a pattern completion, on above-average volume; signals a change in market psychology; should stay unfilled to remain valid; gap edge becomes strong support/resistance.
- **Runaway / continuation / measuring gap:** near the middle of a short/intermediate trend, in trend direction; can project the second half of the move (measuring use); may come in a series.
- **Exhaustion gap:** in trend direction at the end of an extended trend, on exceptionally high volume / near-panic or euphoria; valid only if price reverses soon after and fills the gap. Easily mistaken for a runaway gap — volume climax + quick fill is the discriminator.
- Canon warning quoted in class material citing ChartSchool: "all gaps get filled" does **not** hold for breakaway and runaway gaps.

### 1.8 Moving Averages — READ VIA SEARCH EXTRACT

- Lagging / trend-following family (with MACD). Longer MA = slower, fewer signals; shorter = faster, more whipsaws. Price vs MA and MA slope define trend state; MAs act as dynamic support/resistance (ChartSchool candlestick article uses "trading below the 20-day EMA" as one operational downtrend test). Crossovers of short over long MA are the elementary trend-change signal. Specific MA article not opened in full in this pass — marked as extract-level only.

### 1.9 RSI — READ IN FULL

- Wilder (1978, *New Concepts in Technical Trading Systems*), 14 periods default, 0-100 bounded. RSI = 100 - 100/(1+RS), RS = average gain / average loss, with Wilder smoothing [(prev avg x 13) + current] / 14. Needs long history to stabilise (SharpCharts uses 250 prior points).
- Overbought >70, oversold <30 (Wilder). Shorter lookback (e.g. 10, or 2 for very short-term with 80/20) increases sensitivity.
- **Overbought/oversold works best in ranges.** In a strong trend RSI can stay extreme through multiple readings; the McDonald's worked example shows three overbought readings that only produced consolidations before the fourth marked the peak.
- **Divergence (Wilder):** bullish = price lower low, RSI higher low (RSI does not confirm = strengthening momentum); bearish = price higher high, RSI lower high. More robust after an overbought/oversold reading. Explicit warning: "divergences are misleading in a strong trend" — SPY example shows three bearish divergences inside a continuing uptrend.
- **Failure swings (price-independent):** bullish = RSI dips below 30, bounces above 30, pullback holds above 30, then breaks its prior RSI high. Bearish = mirror above/below 70.
- **Brown / Cardwell ranges (the expert layer):** bull-market RSI range roughly 40-90 with 40-50 as support; bear-market range roughly 10-60 with 50-60 as resistance. Cardwell positive reversal = RSI lower low but price higher low (usually RSI low 30-50); negative reversal = RSI higher high but price lower high (RSI high 50-70). Lesson: price action first, indicator second.
- Use rule: never alone — combine with trend, support/resistance, other confirmation.

### 1.10 MACD and MACD-Histogram — READ IN FULL (MACD) + EXTRACT (Histogram)

- Gerald Appel, late 1970s. MACD line = 12-day EMA - 26-day EMA (closes); signal line = 9-day EMA of MACD; histogram = MACD - signal. Standard settings 12,26,9. Unbounded — **not** an overbought/oversold tool.
- Three signal families, in frequency order: (1) **Signal-line crossovers** — MACD turns up through signal = bullish, down through = bearish; treat crossovers at positive/negative extremes with caution; volatility inflates crossover count. (2) **Centerline crossovers** — MACD above zero = 12-EMA above 26-EMA = upside momentum regime; durability depends entirely on whether a real trend follows (Pulte Homes: 4 crosses worked in trends; Cummins: 7 crosses whipsawed without trends; 3M: one cross lasted 10 months). (3) **Divergences** — bullish: price lower low + MACD higher low, identified on closes with clear reaction troughs in both series, confirmed by signal-line cross + resistance break (GOOG 2008 example); bearish mirror (GameStop example, followed by support break and support-to-resistance throwback).
- Divergence caution identical to RSI: early uptrends/downtrends routinely print divergences as momentum normalises from its initial surge while MACD stays on the trend side of zero.
- Values are price-scale dependent (a $20 stock's MACD range is not comparable to a $100 stock's); use PPO for cross-security comparison.
- Histogram (Thomas Aspray, 1986): distance between MACD and signal; peak-trough and slant divergences in the histogram anticipate the signal-line cross because MACD itself lags.

### 1.11 Volume and volume indicators — EXTRACT LEVEL

ChartSchool's volume doctrine is distributed, not single-article, in this pass: volume must expand in the direction of the break (breakouts, neckline breaks, Wyckoff SOS/SOW), contract inside consolidations (triangles, wedges, flags, Phase B ranges), and climax at climaxes (selling/buying climax, exhaustion gaps). Named volume tools in the canon: On-Balance Volume (cumulative, can trend unlike bounded oscillators), Accumulation/Distribution Line and Chaikin Oscillator (3-day vs 10-day EMA of ADL; zero-line crosses and divergences; Money Flow Multiplier positive when close is in upper half of range), Money Flow Index (volume-weighted RSI, 14 periods, 80/20, with 90/10 as "truly" extreme), VWAP (intraday only, single-day, price above/below VWAP = intraday trend read). Full OBV/ADL articles not opened in this pass.

### 1.12 Candlesticks (ChartSchool / Greg Morris tradition) — EXTRACT LEVEL

Reversal patterns require a prior trend to reverse (downtrend for bullish reversals, testable via MA, peak/trough, or trendline). Robustness protocol: look for the reversal **at support/resistance**, with momentum confirmation (positive divergence in MACD/PPO/Stochastic/RSI etc.), rather than trading the candle shape alone. Hammer / inverted hammer, engulfing, and the Morris catalogue are the named set; full candlestick dictionary not opened in this pass.

### 2.1 Bulkowski's general identification method (the transferable standard)

1. **Price trend** is a table row for every pattern: state the direction leading in (e.g. HST: upward; double bottom: downward; descending triangle: any).
2. **Touches:** a trendline is not real on one touch. Typical minima: triangles — at least 3 touches on one line and 2 on the other, as distinct peaks/valleys; broadening — at least 5 touches total (3 on one side, 2+ on the other); channels — at least 2 touches per line; wedges (via ChartSchool-comparison extracts) — 5 touches total is his good-pattern standard.
3. **Crossing / white space:** price must cross the pattern from trendline to trendline, "nearly filling the available space." Patterns with abundant white space are rejected — this is his most distinctive visual test.
4. **Duration floors:** e.g. wedges need at least ~3 weeks; shorter converging pauses are pennants by definition.
5. **Confirmation is a CLOSE outside the boundary** — "The pattern confirms as a valid one when price closes outside one of the trendlines." For down-sloping HST necklines, confirm on a close below the right armpit (horizontal line) instead, because price may never close below a steep neckline.
6. **Measure rule:** height of pattern x (% meeting price target for that pattern) projected from the breakout price. Targets are probabilistic, pattern-specific, not the naive 100% height ChartSchool introduces with.
7. **Failure language:** throwback (up breakouts) / pullback (down breakouts) rates are published per pattern and "hurt performance"; **busted patterns** (break one way <=10%, reverse, close beyond the opposite side / pattern extreme) are treated as a new, powerful signal in the new direction.
8. **Context filters that recur:** position in yearly range (best/worst thirds differ by pattern), inbound trend length and velocity, volume trend direction, breakout distance to apex for triangles (~61-65% of the way to apex is median).

### 2.2 Head-and-Shoulders Top — READ IN FULL

- Price trend: upward leading in. Shape: three peaks, middle highest, "proportional, and not lopsided," visually like a head on two shoulders.
- Symmetry rule (stricter than ChartSchool): shoulders should peak near the same price, be nearly the same distance from the head, and look similar (both wide or both narrow).
- Volume: highest on left shoulder, then head; trends downward 61% of the time.
- Neckline joins the two armpits. Confirmation: close below an up-sloping neckline, or below the right armpit if the neckline slopes down.
- Stats (bull market, >2,800 perfect trades, stats updated 8/26/2020): performance rank 9/36, break-even failure 19%, average decline 16%, pullback rate 68%, 51% meet the (adjusted) price target.
- Complex variant: multiple heads/shoulders allowed but usually many of one, not both; volume typically higher on left shoulders; for steep necklines use the horizontal line through the lowest right-side armpit as the confirmation line.

### 2.3 Descending Triangle — READ IN FULL

- Price trend: any. Shape: bottom trendline horizontal, top trendline sloping down.
- Touches: >=3 on one line, >=2 on the other. Crossing/white-space rule stated as "important."
- Volume recedes 78% of the time, "quite low just before the breakout."
- Breakout: any direction; upward 53% overall, but 63% upward when price rises into the pattern (continuation context). Confirmation: close outside a trendline.
- Stats (>1,300 trades): rank 33/39 up breakouts, 15/36 down; failure 22%/23%; average rise 38% / decline 15%; throwback/pullback ~60%/58%; target met 64%/50%.
- Apex timing: median breakout 61-65% toward the apex; price tends to turn at the apex time; triangles far up a rising trend "flame out quicker."

### 2.4 Double Bottoms and the Adam/Eve taxonomy — READ IN FULL (Eve & Adam) + EXTRACT (taxonomy, Eve & Eve, Ch.5)

- Four varieties by valley shape: Adam & Adam, Adam & Eve, Eve & Adam, Eve & Eve (mirrored for double tops by peak shape).
- **Adam** = narrow, tall, V-shaped, often a single bar / one long spike, slender through its height. **Eve** = wide, rounded, conglomeration of bars near the same price, widens as it rises; if spiky, spikes are more numerous and shorter. Identification shortcut he teaches: ask whether the two valleys "look the same or different."
- Eve & Adam double bottom rules: prior downtrend; rise between bottoms at least 10% (allow variations); bottoms within 0-4% of each other in price; separation several weeks, mostly 2-7 weeks (median 23 days); volume higher on the left bottom; **confirmation = close above the peak between the valleys** — without waiting, ~48% continue lower unconfirmed in his warning.
- Eve & Adam stats (759 perfect trades): rank 20/39, failure 12%, average rise 42%, throwback 67%, target met 72%. Measure rule: (peak-to-lowest-valley height x target %) added to the breakout peak.
- Tactics worth carrying: "Big W" (tall, steep, clean left side) tends to retrace toward the downtrend origin; a post-confirmation "handle" (sideways waffle) that breaks up often starts a strong trend; flat-base pothole bottoms on the weekly scale precede large rises; avoid confirmed double bottoms that merely retrace inside a primary downtrend (his Lesson Chart 2 failure case).

### 2.5 Channels — READ IN FULL

- Price fits between two parallel (or nearly) trendlines, both tilted up or both down; horizontal = rectangle, not a channel.
- Touches >=2 per line as distinct peaks/valleys; crossing rule applies; breakout = close outside either boundary, any direction.
- Trading protocol: trade **with** the channel slope — buy rebounds at the bottom line of an up-channel, sell at the top, never short an up-channel / never go long a down-channel; a close outside against the position is the exit and a possible new-trend signal. Partial rise inside a pattern predicts a downward breakout; partial decline predicts an upward breakout (partial-move doctrine, also used in broadening formations).
- No performance statistics published for channels (he states this plainly).

### 2.6 Other Bulkowski patterns — READ VIA SEARCH EXTRACT

- **Broadening Bottom:** prior downtrend; megaphone shape (higher peaks, lower valleys); top line slopes up, bottom slopes down; >=5 touches total; price must fill the space side-to-side; volume up-trends 65-67% of the time; a **partial decline** (price rises toward the top line, turns down short of it, then breaks up) is the prized predictor of an upward breakout. Failure rank 25/39 — mid-to-poor base pattern, redeemed by the partial decline.
- **Diamond Bottom:** prior downtrend; first half broadening (higher peaks/lower valleys), second half symmetrical triangle (lower peaks/higher valleys), forming a (often tilted) diamond; 1-2 touches per trendline tolerated, lines may cross outliers; volume trends down 67%; upward breakout 74% (close outside a boundary).
- **Inverted Cup with Handle:** smooth rounded inverted cup (allow exceptions), cup rims bottom near the same price, handle to the right, handle must not rise above the cup top and typically retraces 30-60% of cup height; confirms on close below the right cup lip; target = handle height subtracted from the right rim low. (Normal cup with handle is the bullish mirror; its full page was not opened in this pass.)
- **One-Day Reversal Top (event pattern):** judged on a 3-bar window; occurs in a short-term uptrend; the reversal bar's open and close both within 25% of its intraday low; adjacent bars' highs below the reversal bar's midpoint (the bar stands "like a tree atop a peak"); bar at least as tall as the 1-month average bar; high volume expected (waived in his test because the pattern is rare).
- **Bullish 2-Step (5-bar event pattern):** Bar 2 lower low + lower close than Bar 1; Bar 3 lower low than Bar 2 but close above Bar 1; Bar 4 closes below Bar 3; Bar 5 lower low than Bar 4 but closes above Bars 3 and 4. Breaks up 79% in stocks. His test protocol given verbatim: buy stop 1 cent above pattern high, stop 1 cent below pattern low, target = 2x pattern height above the high.
- **3L-R (4-bar):** three consecutively lower lows (only lows matter), final bar's high above the first bar's high.

## 3. Wyckoff Method — Public Materials

**Names / URLs:** StockCharts ChartSchool, *The Wyckoff Method* (market analysis + stock selection articles) https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/~gitbook/pdf?page=eujiQqa7fmPo42FiWfjI&only=yes&limit=100 — READ IN FULL; Wyckoff Analytics tutorial as reproduced for ChartSchool (FlipHTML5 mirror, pages 2 and 9 via search extract): https://fliphtml5.com/yfvuy/tjut/Wyckoff-Method_Wyckoff-Analytics_english/2/ ; Public schematic explainers READ VIA SEARCH EXTRACT: Chart Whisperer https://chartwhisperer.ca/wyckoff-method ; BrokerSpecs https://brokerspecs.com/education/wyckoff-method
**Status:** ChartSchool tutorial free and open. Wyckoff Analytics' own site / courses are commercial; the canonical tutorial text circulates free via ChartSchool permission. Original Wyckoff course (1931) is historical, not freely hosted in full in anything found here.
**What it teaches:** A complete market-reading routine, not a pattern: judge the broad market first, then relative strength, then the trading range's phase, then price/volume bar-by-bar (effort vs result), with Point & Figure cause counting for targets. Origin: Richard D. Wyckoff, *Studies in Tape Reading* (1910), *How I Trade and Invest in Stocks and Bonds* (1924), 1931 correspondence course.
**Limits:** The schematic is explicitly a model with variants — ChartSchool/Wyckoff Analytics present multiple accumulation/distribution schematics, with and without springs/UTADs; phases can be hard to label in real time (Phase C without a spring is called out as the hard case). Secondary explainers (blogs, broker sites) compress and sometimes mislabel phases; the ChartSchool/Wyckoff Analytics text is the reliable public standard in this census.

### 3.1 The two meta-rules and the five-step routine (ChartSchool, READ IN FULL)

- Rule One: "Don't expect the market to behave exactly the same way twice. The market is an artist, not a computer." Guidelines, not rigid rules; every formation is a variation.
- Rule Two: today's behaviour is significant only compared with yesterday / last week / last year. No predetermined never-fail levels.
- Five steps: (1) Determine the broad market's present position and probable trend (bar + P&F charts of indices; trend = rising/falling peaks and troughs, or range until a break). (2) Select stocks in harmony with that trend, showing relative strength (in uptrends: bigger % rises on rallies, smaller % falls on reactions than the index; judged by direct chart comparison). (3) Select stocks with a sufficient "cause" (P&F count) to meet the objective. (4) Determine readiness to move (phase/event analysis of the range, volume tests). (5) Time the commitment with the broad market (e.g. position within trend: prefer longs when uptrend is oversold/pulled back, shorts when downtrend is overbought/bounced; avoid new longs overbought, new shorts oversold).

### 3.2 The three laws

1. **Supply and Demand:** price rises when demand exceeds supply, falls when supply exceeds demand; read bar-by-bar from price + volume.
2. **Cause and Effect:** the horizontal extent of accumulation/distribution (the cause, measured by P&F horizontal count) determines the size of the markup/markdown (the effect). ChartSchool worked examples: 2007 S&P top, 34 columns x 3-box reversal x 10 pts = ~1,020 pt projection; 2009 bottom, nested 20-column (target ~1,270) and 42-column (target ~1,930) counts from the same 670 low. Wyckoff himself "cautioned against taking these projections too seriously."
3. **Effort vs Result:** volume = effort; price spread + close location = result. Harmony (big volume, wide spread, progress) = move is genuine. Divergence (heavy volume, narrow spread / little progress) = absorption by the other side, earliest warning of reversal. Public explainers add the modern gloss: cumulative volume delta as a high-resolution form of this law (Chart Whisperer extract — secondary source, flagged as such).

### 3.3 Accumulation schematic — phases and events (ChartSchool / Wyckoff Analytics text)

Trading range (TR) bounded by support (SC low area) and resistance (AR high).

- **Phase A — Stops the downtrend.** PS (Preliminary Support): first substantial buying, widening spreads on increased volume inside the downtrend. SC (Selling Climax): panic selling, wide spread, heavy volume, absorbed by professionals; often a failed support break that closes back well off the low (ChartSchool's Oct-2011 S&P spring/climax example: intraday break below 1,100/1,080, close back above 1,120, on high volume). AR (Automatic Rally): sharp bounce as selling exhausts; its high + SC low set the TR boundaries. ST (Secondary Test): revisit of SC area on less selling, narrower spread, decreased volume, holding at/above SC low.
- **Phase B — Building the cause.** Extended ranging between SC and AR; institutions accumulate; multiple STs; volume generally diminishes; upthrust-type probes at the top of the range may occur. Function: build a cause large enough for the markup.
- **Phase C — The decisive test.** **Spring / shakeout:** price moves below TR support (established in A/B) and quickly reverses back inside the TR. A low-volume spring or low-volume test of the shakeout = supply exhausted = high-probability long initiation point (at least partial). An SOS soon after the spring validates it. Variant: Phase C can occur as a test higher inside the TR with no spring — identification is then challenging (their words).
- **Phase D — Demand dominates.** SOS (Sign of Strength): advance on widening spread and increasing volume, at least to the top of the TR / through resistance ("Jump Across the Creek" in the older vocabulary). LPS (Last Point of Support): reaction after SOS on smaller spread and diminished volume, holding at/above former resistance (now support). LPSs are the textbook add/initiate points. Sequence: advances (SOSs) strong, reactions (LPSs) weak.
- **Phase E — Markup.** Price leaves the TR; demand in full control; reactions short-lived; re-accumulation ranges ("stepping stones") may form on the way up.
- Buy points in the full cycle (ChartSchool): (1) spring / selling climax (aggressive, highest reward, above-average failure risk), (2) resistance breakout on expanding volume, (3) throwback to broken resistance turned support, (4) re-accumulation breakout in markup, (5) pullback support at trendline / prior consolidation / 50% retracement of the last up-leg.

### 3.4 Distribution schematic — mirror

- **Phase A:** PSY (Preliminary Supply): first substantial selling in the uptrend. BC (Buying Climax): sharp advance on heavy volume met by professional selling. AR (Automatic Reaction): decline that, with BC, sets TR boundaries. ST: retest of BC area.
- **Phase B:** ranging at the top; supply/demand tests; volume character deteriorates (down-day volume exceeding up-day volume is ChartSchool's 2007 Dow tell).
- **Phase C:** **Upthrust (UT)** = brief move above TR resistance that fails back inside. **UTAD (Upthrust After Distribution)** = the late, larger version above the BC high — the classic bull trap. Distribution can complete without a UTAD (schematic variant noted explicitly).
- **Phase D:** SOW (Sign of Weakness): decline on widening spread / increased volume toward or through support. LPSY (Last Point of Supply): weak, low-volume rally to former support (now resistance) after SOW — textbook short point.
- **Phase E:** Markdown. Sell/short points mirror the buy points: lower peak inside distribution, support breakdown on expanding volume, throwback to broken support turned resistance, re-distribution breakout down, 50% bounce resistance.
- Tops vs bottoms asymmetry (Wyckoff via ChartSchool): tops are long, drawn-out distributions; bottoms are relatively short and violent (climax/spring).

### 4.1 The three hard rules (never to be broken — EWI wording across all sources)

1. **Wave 2 never retraces more than 100% of Wave 1** (it cannot go beyond Wave 1's origin).
2. **Wave 3 is never the shortest** of the actionary waves 1, 3, 5 (and always travels beyond the end of Wave 1).
3. **Wave 4 never enters the price territory of Wave 1** in a standard impulse (overlap exceptions: diagonal triangles, and — in some modern statements — highly leveraged markets).
Breaking any rule invalidates the count instantly and forces a recount. "A rule should never be disregarded... analysts who routinely break any of the rules are practicing some form of analysis other than that guided by the Wave Principle."

### 4.2 Structure taxonomy with internal subdivisions

- **Motive (5):** subwaves 1-3-5 actionary, 2-4 reactionary. Impulse subdivides 5-3-5-3-5. One complete cycle = motive five (numbers) + corrective three (letters a-b-c) = 8 waves, fractal at every degree.
- **Extension:** most impulses contain an extension (exaggerated subdivisions) in **one and only one** of waves 1, 3, or 5; wave 3 is the most commonly extended, wave 1 the least.
- **Truncation:** wave 5 fails to go beyond the end of wave 3.
- **Diagonals (3-3-3-3-3 or 5-3-5-3-5, converging lines, wave 4 overlaps wave 1 by design):** ending diagonal only as wave 5 of an impulse or wave C of a zigzag/flat; leading diagonal only as wave 1 or wave A. Subwaves are typically zigzags; waves 2 and 4 usually retrace 62-80% of the preceding wave.
- **Zigzag (5-3-5):** A = impulse/leading diagonal, B = any corrective (never beyond A's start, usually retraces 38-50% of A... B retracement guidance varies by text; C = impulse/ending diagonal, usually equals A and ends beyond A's end.
- **Flat (3-3-5):** B retraces at least ~70% of A, typically 100-138% (expanded flat: B beyond A's start); C is an impulse/ending diagonal, usually 100-162% of A.
- **Triangle (3-3-3-3-3):** five corrective subwaves A-B-C-D-E, each a three; occurs as wave 4, wave B, or the final wave of a combination — never as wave 2 in the strict canon; wave E often falls short of the trendline.
- **Combinations:** double/triple threes (W-X-Y, W-X-Y-X-Z) joining two/three corrective patterns.

### 4.3 Guidelines (strong tendencies, used to rank counts)

- **Alternation:** if wave 2 is a sharp correction, expect wave 4 to be sideways, and vice versa; alternation also applies inside corrective patterns (in form, depth, complexity).
- **Equality:** two of the actionary waves (often 1 and 5 when 3 is extended) tend toward equality in length/time.
- **Channeling:** draw a line through wave 1 and wave 3 endpoints, parallel through wave 2 (or the 2-4 line with a parallel through 1/3) — wave 5 typically ends at or throws over the channel line; channels also contain corrective waves.
- **Depth of corrections:** corrections tend to end within the price territory of the prior wave 4 of one lesser degree; after a fifth-wave extension, the correction is sharp and retraces to the area of wave 2 of the extension.
- **Wave personality:** wave 1 = tentative, broadly disbelieved; wave 2 = sharp, sentiment reverts to prior pessimism/optimism but never beyond origin; wave 3 = strongest, broadest, unmistakable, gaps and expanding breadth; wave 4 = sideways, building exhaustion; wave 5 = optimistic/pessimistic climax on narrower breadth and momentum divergence; A-B-C personalities mirror this in corrections. Personality is a genuine identification aid in the canon, taught as Lesson 14 / Tutorial Lesson 7.
- **Fibonacci / ratio analysis:** corrective retracements cluster at Fibonacci proportions of the prior motive wave (wave 2 deep, wave 4 shallow per alternation); motive multiples: extended wave often = 1.618x the non-extended reference; wave C often equals wave A (1.0) or 1.618x; ratio analysis is applied as multiple simultaneous relationships — the count that satisfies the most relationships is preferred.
- **Volume (Elliott guideline):** volume tends to expand in wave 3, contract in corrections, and diminish in wave 5 vs wave 3 in equity indices.
- **Working method:** maintain preferred + alternate counts ranked by how many guidelines each satisfies; every count carries a stated invalidation (the rule-break level). EWI's own metaphor (syndicated article, READ VIA EXTRACT): the Wave Principle is the road map; specific rules give "make-or-break" levels for each interpretation.

## 6. Cross-canon reading protocol (what the four families agree on)

1. Context before pattern: prior trend / market phase first (ChartSchool prior-trend rule; Bulkowski price-trend row; Wyckoff Step 1; Elliott degree and larger count).
2. Lines need repeated touches to exist (2 minimum to draw, 3+ / 5 total to trust, by family).
3. No break, no pattern: confirmation is a close beyond the defining line, ideally with volume expansion in the break direction.
4. Volume has a script inside the pattern (contract in consolidation, expand on resolution, climax at climaxes) and a divergence (effort vs result / oscillator non-confirmation) is the standard early warning in all four families.
5. Broken levels flip polarity and the retest is a distinct, named event (throwback/pullback, LPS/LPSY, support-turned-resistance).
6. Targets are measured (pattern height, P&F cause count, Fibonacci multiples) and explicitly provisional, subordinated to the next real level.
7. Every read carries an invalidation: neckline reclaim, close back inside the range, spring failure below the SC low on volume, Elliott rule-break level.
