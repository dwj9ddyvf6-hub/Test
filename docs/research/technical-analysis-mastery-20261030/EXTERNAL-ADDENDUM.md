# External Addendum Proposal: Binding Rules From the External Canon

Status: PROPOSAL ONLY, filed 2026-10-30. This file proposes additions to Midna chart read skill (~/workspace/custom-skills/local-stock-analysis/SKILL.md). It does not edit that skill. The parent applies it after the internal volume is verified.

## Candlestick recognition rules (TA-Lib canon)

1. Calibrate before classifying: judge every body and range against the average body and average range of the prior 10 candles.
2. Call a body doji like only when it is under 10 percent of the prior 10 candle average range.
3. Call a shadow long only when it exceeds the real body, and very long only when it exceeds twice the body.
4. Judge engulfing and harami on real bodies only: engulfing fully contains the prior body, harami sits fully inside it.
5. Apply the midpoint test to Piercing and Dark Cloud reads: no close past the midpoint of the first body, no pattern.
6. Require star patterns to penetrate: the third body must close at least to the midpoint of the first body, and deeper penetration earns more weight.
7. Never name a hammer family candle on shape alone: state the prior trend first, because the trend assigns the name.
8. Write the confirming close and the invalidating close for every candle pattern before the next candle prints.
9. Treat a doji as indecision only: never complete a turn call on a doji without a confirming close beyond its range.
10. When several candle labels fit one bar, rank them and keep one primary label: level, trend, and volume decide the ranking.

## Bulkowski identification checks (ThePatternSite)

11. Run the identification rows in writing before naming any pattern: prior trend, touches, crossing, duration, confirmation line.
12. Reject any candidate with abundant white space: price must cross the pattern from line to line, nearly filling the space.
13. Enforce touch minimums: triangles 3 touches on one line and 2 on the other, wedges 5 total, broadening formations 5 total, channels and rectangles at least 2 per line.
14. Enforce duration floors: under about 3 weeks a converging pause is a pennant, not a wedge or triangle; a flag ends by about 4 weeks.
15. Confirm only on a close outside the defining line; for a steep down sloping Head and Shoulders neckline, confirm on a close below the right armpit instead.
16. Discount every measured target by the pattern published percentage meeting target, then subordinate it to the next real level.
17. Name Adam versus Eve on every double top and double bottom, left to right, and enforce the tolerances: extremes within about 0 to 4 percent, intervening swing at least 10 percent, separation of several weeks.
18. Log throwback and pullback as expected events at their published rates, and treat a busted pattern as a new signal in the new direction.
19. Demand more proof from low ranked patterns: the lower the Bulkowski rank, the more touches, volume, and close quality required before the pattern earns a call.
20. Classify triangles and wedges by fitted line slopes, never by silhouette: flat side means triangle, parallel means channel, same sign converging slopes near a 1.0 ratio means wedge.

## Wyckoff phase read (ChartSchool Wyckoff Method)

21. Run the five steps in order: market position first, relative strength second, cause third, readiness fourth, timing fifth. Never start at the range.
22. Draw the trading range boundaries from the climax and the automatic move, then freeze them for the life of the range.
23. Label every range event with its volume character beside it: PS, SC, AR, ST, Spring, SOS, LPS, or the distribution mirrors PSY, BC, AR, ST, UT, UTAD, SOW, LPSY.
24. Judge effort versus result at both boundaries on every test: heavy volume with little progress is absorption, and location decides its direction.
25. Compare each test of a level with the last test of the same level: shrinking spread and volume per test is the absorption ledger.
26. Require a spring to recover promptly inside the range, then demand the low volume test before trusting it; a high volume spring earns less trust than its test.
27. Confirm demand with the Phase D sequence: advances strong, reactions weak, SOS then LPS holding at the flipped boundary.
28. Count the cause on Point and Figure at one fixed standard setting per instrument, and reject any base whose count cannot reach the objective.
29. State the invalidation with the read: a spring fails on a close and build below the spring low on expanding volume, and an SOS fails on a heavy volume fall back through the Creek.
30. When two phase labels fit, write the preferred read, the alternate, and the bar that decides between them.

## Classical boundary discipline (Brandt, Weinstein, ChartSchool)

31. Draw every boundary through the congestion, not through a single stray extreme; exclude out of line movements from the line.
32. Prefer horizontal boundaries: rectangles, right angle triangles, and near horizontal necklines outrank two sloping line patterns.
33. Treat the close as the verdict: the daily close decides the day, the Friday close decides the week, and intraday pokes are tests, not completions.
34. Classify the weekly stage before any daily read: price against the 30 week average and its slope gives Stage 1, 2, 3, or 4, and only Stage 2 breakouts are buys.
35. Keep broken levels on the chart as ice lines: the retest of a flipped level is a named event, and a close back beyond it is the failure signal.
36. Draw support and resistance as zones spanning the touch cluster, and require at least two independent reactions before a level exists.
37. Apply the squint test and the 3 touch rule to trendlines: one touch is a point, two are a hypothesis, three or more are a validated line.
38. Compute the geometry ratio before acting: distance to invalidation versus distance to objective, from the drawn lines, and pass when the geometry does not pay.
39. Answer the one year test before entry: the entry day and price must be visible on the chart a year from now.
40. Classify every gap by location and volume (common, breakaway, runaway, exhaustion) and mark it as a zone; a quick fill after climax volume is what separates exhaustion from runaway.

## Practice and review routines (practitioner canon)

41. Run the first look in fixed order every time: market, stage, prior trend, swing sequence, location, volume script, candidate pattern, invalidation sentence.
42. Print ENTRY, SL, and TARGET on the chart itself with the invalidation in words and scenario B beside them; no read is complete without all three.
43. Journal on the chart at the time of the read: save a clean screenshot before later bars exist, with the expected volume script written as a prediction.
44. Never move a line before grading: extend lines forward in time only, and grade the saved screenshot, not the memory of the read.
45. Grade every logged read at 2 weeks and at 4 weeks on the seven dimension rubric, and keep a running tally by pattern and by flaw.
46. Run Bar Replay drills with hidden future bars: decide and log before revealing, and count passed reads that would have worked as drill data.
47. Keep one pattern immersion at a time: definition, 20 historical examples, 10 near misses that fail exactly one row, then 20 replay repetitions.
48. Redraw the day top down at night with actual entries and exits plotted on it, and close with written improvement questions.
49. Sketch the weekly shape of the five most watched charts from memory every Sunday, then check and journal the differences.
50. Keep the working chart minimal: zones, one boundary pair, one validated trendline, and at most one indicator window; delete any line with no stated rule.
