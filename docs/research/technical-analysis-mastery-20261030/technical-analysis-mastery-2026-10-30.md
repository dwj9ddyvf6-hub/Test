# Technical Analysis and Visual Chart Reading: A Mastery Study

## Purpose

This playbook teaches the craft of reading charts. Its posture is learn to do it: how a master looks at a chart, in what order, what she draws, what she refuses to draw, and how she turns a picture into a falsifiable plan with a buy zone, an invalidation, targets, and a review date. It is not an invalidation study and it is not a debunking exercise. Where the desk has measured a method, the measurement is quoted as a prior to read beside, never as a reason to stop learning the method. The sources are the desk corpora and the open canon inventoried in the companion census: the Echo and Max playbook reverse engineered from 134 X charts, the Echo Algorithm from six newsletter issues and a 240 chart corpus, the five poster X chart studies, StockCharts ChartSchool, Thomas Bulkowski and ThePatternSite, the Wyckoff method materials, the desk chart reader in code, the local stock analysis skill with its Pass A geometry and Pass B perceptual read, and the desk genome pattern calibration on 1,838 names.

One sentence carries the whole book, and it is Echo's: a level is not a trade. Price arriving at a level is an area to watch. The trade is the reaction at the level.

## Section 1: The Eye First

A master does not start with an indicator, a pattern name, or a trade idea. She starts with her eyes, in a fixed order, and she earns each next step from what the chart actually shows. Order is the skill. When the order slips, the eye finds the pattern it wanted to find and stops looking.

### Rule zero: gestalt before measurement

Open the chart at the highest resolution available and look at the whole thing before any part of it. The chart reading method in the composite confidence corpus puts perception first for a practical reason: in the chart vision research it cites, most chart failures were early perception errors, a misread axis, a confused series, an invented label, and an early error cascades through everything built on it. Do five things before interpreting anything.

1. Inventory the frame. Name the symbol, the timeframe, the date range, linear or log scale, and whether the last bar is complete or still forming. On a multi hundred percent move, Echo's rule calls for log scale.
2. Register every series. Say what each colour and line is: price, volume, the 20, 50 and 200 period averages, RSI, MACD, VWAP, or a drawn trendline. Never read a value off a series you have not registered.
3. Transcribe before you interpret. Write down the recent swing high, the recent swing low, where price sits relative to the key averages, and the largest recent volume bars. Numbers first, story second.
4. State the gestalt in one sentence, shape only, no pattern name and no direction call. A long base breaking upward. A steep advance now going sideways at the highs. A fall that has stopped falling and is carving a floor.
5. Run a counter check. Ask what could mislead you: a cropped window hiding a prior top, a timeframe chosen to flatter a view, smoothed candles that hide wicks, missing volume, a line drawn after the fact. Smoothed candle styles such as Heikin Ashi discard wick information that stops depend on. The desk reads true OHLC for that reason. Name the smoothing if it is present.

Only after those five steps do you measure. Pass A in the local stock analysis skill is the desk version of the separation: a deterministic geometry pass computes pivots, averages, ATR, clusters and divergences, and Pass B is the visual read of the rendered weekly, daily, 1 hour and 10 minute charts. If the image and the geometry disagree on a material fact, stop and reconcile the bar, the adjustment basis or the scale. Never narrate through a disagreement.

### The first pass, in order

Work seven questions in this exact sequence. Do not move on while the current answer is vague.

First, trend. Price against its averages as geography: in the Echo and Max playbook the 50 period average is the actionable pivot and the 200 period average, or the 200 week average, is the regime line and final backstop. Above the 200, buyers hold the regime. Below it, sellers do. Then the swing sequence: higher highs and higher lows is an uptrend in structure whatever an oscillator says, lower highs and lower lows is a downtrend, and a sequence that stops making progress is transition. Then slope and spacing: a rising average with price holding above it on pullbacks is a trend with a floor, while a flat average crossed every week is balance. Output one word for regime, bullish, bearish or transitioning, plus the structural reason in one line.

Second, structure. Name the arrangement of swings, not a single candle. Use confirmed pivots only: a pivot is a 5 bar fractal high or low and it is known only at confirmation, never at the extreme bar itself, so every pivot carries an extreme time and an availability time. Name bases, ranges, continuation shapes in context, and failure shapes, with boundaries in price and age in time. A triangle is lines converging on a decision point. A flag is a pause after a sharp leg. The shape tells you where the decision will happen. The close through the boundary, with volume, tells you the decision was made. No pattern is a call before its confirmation close.

Third, location. Place price in three ranges at once: inside the current structure (top, middle or bottom; mid structure is where the playbook says patience, in its words corrective, not buy time), inside the larger move (how far from the base, how much room to the next shelf), and against its key shelves listed as zones, not thin lines. A zone is a band built from confluence, scored in Section 2. Output a location sentence with every level named by its anchor and its distance in ATR.

Fourth, volatility state. Read recent range against ATR(14): quiet, normal or expanding, and where that state sits in the structure. Compression stores energy and the next expansion travels. Expansion at highs with long upper wicks is a different state from expansion at lows after a climax bar. The volatility read feeds risk directly: stops placed inside normal noise, below about 1.5 ATR, get eaten before a thesis can work.

Fifth, life stage. This is the Echo question, and it comes before any indicator: where are we in the cycle, and what single level decides it? Impulse leg, correction leg, base and reversal attempt, or terminal pattern. Section 4 gives the wave rules. A stage call with no deciding level is an opinion. A stage call with a deciding level is a read. Keep the two closing questions separate, always: is it still going up, and is it safe to buy here. A late stage chart can answer yes to the first and no to the second at the same time.

Sixth, participation. Volume is the tell, read after structure and location, and it confirms or caps what you have seen. It never creates a read on its own. Check volume on the climax or the low, volume on the break, volume on pullbacks (drying volume on a pullback is the best evidenced pullback filter in the corpus), and wide range red bars on expanding volume on the 1 hour frame as the first distribution tell. If volume is not shown, say so and cap conviction.

Seventh, the deciding level. End the first pass with the one level that decides, written in conditional grammar: losing X confirms the move to the zone at Y to Z. Reclaiming A on a close, with volume, opens B. Patience while price sits between X and A. Draw both scenarios, primary and alternate, each with a terminus, on the same chart. A zone becomes a setup only on a reaction at the zone, never on arrival. A wick through a zone does not kill it. A close below the bottom of the zone does.

### The cascade and the staging

No timeframe reads alone. The weekly count governs, the daily times it, the intraday refines it, and the higher timeframe wins conflicts. The weekly answers regime and life stage and may be drawn simply, because its job is posture, not timing. The daily answers structure, location, volatility and the deciding level, and it is the primary working frame for a swing read. The 1 hour answers rhythm: staircase of higher lows or overlapping chop, distribution bars present or absent. The 10 minute frame refines an entry or exit around a level the daily already chose; it never invents a level and never overturns the weekly regime. Agreement across timeframes is one witness, not three, because the frames share the same underlying transactions. Name what the lower frame adds, or do not cite it as separate support. When frames disagree, downgrade conviction and name the disagreeing frame.

Expert charts are staged in two halves, a habit the X chart study found in trader after trader and confirmed at scale for Echo across 240 charts. The left half is history staged as evidence: swings labelled at their pivots, at most two trendlines anchored on major swing extremes spanning the whole pattern, levels drawn as zones and boxes, and every measured level carrying its derivation with its price, ratio and price together on the same short line. The right half is empty future reserved for the plan: the projected path drawn into the void, future pivots waiting at their prices, the objective marked with the size of the move beside it. The desk standard is stricter and is the standard to learn: history on the left carries the named pattern itself plus volume, RSI and MACD panes with divergences drawn only where the record carries them; the plan on the right carries a shaded buy zone, a labelled invalidation line, targets labelled by role, a dashed expected path through reserved future space, and R and the expiry clock printed on the chart itself. The expected path stays dashed and visually subordinate to real price. Drawing the future in the same ink as history is the hype trick of the genre. Drawing it dashed, labelled as stages rather than dates, is the craft version. The 5 second test checks the finished staging: a well staged chart gives a reader, in about five seconds, where we are in the structure, the two prices that matter now, and the destination.

A first pass ends in one of four states. Watch: a confluent zone is identified, price has not reached it or has reached it without a reaction, so the trigger is set in advance and the reader waits. Setup: price has reacted at a confluent zone, volume supports the reaction, and the deciding level and invalidation are named with room of at least about 1.5 ATR for the stop. In motion: the confirmation close has printed through the boundary on expanding volume, so the next shelf and the trailing invalidation are located, and the trend question is still separated from the initiation question. No read: frames conflict without resolution, the structure needs more lines than the discipline allows, volume is missing where the call depends on it, or the data is stale or partial. Abstain as an evidence status, state exactly what is missing, and name what would reopen the read. No view is not a bearish call.

## Section 2: Structure and Levels

Structure is the skeleton of a chart. Levels are the places where that skeleton has already borne weight. Everything else in this playbook hangs off these two things. A reader who cannot state the trend, name the swings that define it, and draw the two or three levels that actually decide the chart has nothing to attach a pattern to.

### The reading order for structure

Run this order on every chart before naming any pattern or indicator. Frame first, with timeframe, span and scale named, and a log scale advisory on moves above roughly 300 percent. Weekly regime first, daily structure second, intraday timing last. Trend in words with the swing sequence that proves it. Mark the swings, confirmed major swings only. Build the level map: horizontal zones first, then at most two trendlines, then the 50 and 200 averages, then anchored VWAP where an anchor event exists, then gaps left open. Rank the levels; most charts have two or three that matter. State the deciding level in one sentence: this chart is decided at this zone, because these independent constructions meet there. If you cannot finish that sentence, the chart is mid structure and the correct read is patience. State both scenarios with prices before leaving the section.

### Swings, pivots and tests

A pivot in the desk chart reader, `chart_read.py` in the Best Setups engine, and in Pass A is a confirmed 5 bar fractal: a high higher than the two bars on each side, or a low lower than the two bars on each side, known only when the confirming bars have printed. Use pivots with both their extreme time and their availability time. Unconfirmed wiggles anchor nothing.

Level tests are kind aware. A bar below a prior high can never be counted as a rejected low test, and each test kind carries its own meaning. Count touches by independent episode, not by repeated bars inside one episode: three bars leaning on the same shelf in the same week are one episode of evidence, not three. Level clusters in Pass A are built as 2.5 ATR scaled pivot zones, so a zone has a width set by the instrument's own volatility, not by the reader's optimism.

Support and resistance obey polarity: a shelf that held as resistance and is then broken on a close tends to act as support on the retest, and the reverse below. ChartSchool states the rule and the desk reader uses it: the level's history travels with it after the flip, which is why a broken shelf is redrawn as a zone with its new role, not deleted.

### Trendlines: two, anchored, and dead means dead

Draw at most two trendlines on a chart. Anchor them on confirmed major pivots spanning the whole pattern, months not wiggles. Two points make a hypothesis. Three or more touches validate it. The rigorous variant in the TA Deep Dive brief uses convex hull trendlines and defines a break precisely: a close at least 0.25 ATR beyond the line. A line price has already closed through decisively is dead. Report it as dead and do not redraw it to fit the new story. If a pattern needs five lines, there is no pattern.

### Zones and confluence: the definition

A level drawn as a hairline is a claim of precision the market does not honour. The desk draws levels as zones and boxes, and a zone is earned by confluence. Confluence has a definition in this playbook, and it is a count, not a feeling: independent constructions landing inside the same ATR scaled band. Score every candidate zone for each of these that lands inside it:

* a Fibonacci retracement of a named parent swing,
* a Fibonacci extension of a sibling leg,
* a moving average cluster inside the band, with the 50 as the actionable pivot and the 200, or the 200 week, as the regime line and final backstop,
* a trendline arriving at the band,
* a prior pivot shelf that acted as support or resistance,
* a round number.

A lone Fibonacci level is a watch level, not a zone. The Echo Algorithm requires at least two Fibonacci constructions in the same zone before calling it high probability, and the playbook requires an average cluster, a trendline arrival or a prior shelf to promote a Fibonacci band to a buy zone. The playbook's worked example is CAVA: a 0.5 to 0.618 retracement band sitting exactly on the 200 day average. One construction is a line to watch. Two or more independent constructions in one band is a zone that can carry a plan.

### Wyckoff structure, used as vocabulary

The Wyckoff materials in the census, ChartSchool's tutorial and the Wyckoff Analytics reproduction, give the base its working vocabulary, and the desk reader uses the terms only when the events are present: selling climax, automatic rally, secondary test, spring only when its test follows, sign of strength, then last point of support. The discipline is in the conditional terms. A spring is not a low that dipped below the range; it is a dip below the range that is tested and holds. Naming the parts in order, and refusing a term whose confirming event has not printed, is what separates a Wyckoff read from a Wyckoff story. The Point and Figure horizontal count from the same tradition is one projection method among several, and like every target in Section 6 it is labelled by its method when it is used.

### Gaps, anchored VWAP and round numbers

Gaps left open are levels: an unfilled gap is unfinished business with a price attached, and ChartSchool's gap articles treat breakaway, runaway and exhaustion gaps as different events with different locations in a move, so the gap's stage matters as much as its price. Anchored VWAP belongs on the map only where a real anchor event exists, an earnings release, a climax day, a breakout day, because an anchored average with an arbitrary anchor is decoration. Round numbers earn their place in the confluence score because orders cluster there in fact, not because they look clean on the page.

## Section 3: Patterns

Patterns are interpretations of structure, location and volume, named after those three are read. A pattern name that arrives before context is a failure mode, and Section 6 lists its cure. This section gives the identification discipline the canon agrees on, drawn from ChartSchool, Bulkowski's ThePatternSite, and the desk pattern engine and calibration.

### The rules every pattern obeys

Prior trend is mandatory for a reversal. ChartSchool states it without qualification: without a prior uptrend to reverse, there cannot be a head and shoulders reversal, or any reversal pattern. Name the anatomy in order, point by point, before calling the pattern. Volume is part of the pattern, not a footnote: the classical scripts expect contraction inside the formation and expansion on the break, and the desk pattern engine uses expansion on the break with 1.4 times as its breakout volume confirmation reference. Confirmation is a close through the boundary or neckline, not a wick through it. No pattern is a call before its confirmation close. Targets are scaled, not taken at full height by default: the CRA work on Bulkowski verified patterns adds the scaling discipline, targets scaled by the target percent for the pattern family, with a confirmation close required before the target is live.

### The families, and how to earn each name

Head and shoulders top and bottom: a prior trend, a left shoulder, a head above it, a right shoulder below the head, and a neckline drawn through the intervening lows or highs, confirmed on a close through the neckline. Bulkowski's pages add the measurement habit the desk keeps: record the pattern height, the breakout price, and the family's measured failure behaviour beside the call, so the name carries its own track record with it.

Double tops and bottoms, including the Adam and Eve taxonomy from Bulkowski: two peaks or troughs at comparable prices, where Adam shapes are sharp and pointed and Eve shapes are rounded and wide, and the four combinations read differently. The pattern is not confirmed at the second peak. It is confirmed on the close through the intervening valley or peak.

Triangles, ascending, descending and symmetrical: converging lines, a flat side against a sloping side for the directional variants, and touches that satisfy the family's touch and crossing tests before the name is kept. In the desk reader, triangles and pennants are demoted to context until they confirm: they locate the decision, they do not make it.

Wedges, rising and falling: converging lines sloping together, read as exhaustion when they arrive after a long move in the direction of the slope. The desk gates are strict: repeated touches over weeks, five or more touches and three or more weeks in the chart reader's record, or the wedge word is not used. ChartSchool's minimums are looser, and the cross checks in the census show exactly that disagreement; the desk runs the stricter gate and says so.

Flags and pennants: a sharp leg, then a pause that is smaller and quieter than the leg. Run the trap screen the Echo Algorithm names before promoting any apparent continuation: too shallow, volume wrong, no three waves in the pullback, too quick, with pullback duration below 25 percent of the impulse duration as the quickness test. Two or more signs reclassify the expected path to a rejection watched at the 0.5 to 0.786 retracement of the pullback, with a further leg to follow.

Bases and continuation classics: cup with handle, rounding bottom, double bottom and top, head and shoulders, and the selling climax base are the elite detectors in the desk chart reader, run first because their identification rules are the tightest. Broadening and diamond formations from Bulkowski complete the classical shelf: expanding or contracting volatility inside a drawn boundary, confirmed the same way as everything else, on a close through the boundary with volume.

### Calibration beside the name

When you name a pattern in a desk read, look up its measured family state in the genome pattern calibration: 2,390,032 pattern instances measured on the 1,838 name roster from 2020 to 2026, outcomes in R and ATR units, family by family. Quote the measured prior when the family is measured. State plainly when it is thin, unmeasured or unavailable; the product names several unmeasured families, including wyckoff_spring, wyckoff_sos, bull_flag and bear_flag. Never invent a rate from impression. Families with no lawful objective carry no invented rate, and thin cells with measured n below 30 are excluded from calibration use.

## Section 4: Waves and Fibonacci

This section carries Echo's method, the desk's Elliott implementation in Silicon `femto/core/elliott_wave.py`, and the Fibonacci discipline that binds them. Echo labels counts on daily and weekly charts, the weekly governs, and cycle position is named before any Fibonacci is drawn. That order is invariant: impulse or correction first, measurement second.

### The three inviolable rules

Every impulse count in this playbook is held to three hard rules, checked in code by the desk counter and stated by the Echo and Max playbook in the same form:

1. Wave 2 never retraces all of wave 1.
2. Wave 3 is never the shortest of waves 1, 3 and 5.
3. Wave 4 does not overlap wave 1 in a standard impulse.

A count that breaks a rule is not a creative count. It is a dead count. The counter outputs INVALIDATED with its invalidation price rather than quietly relabelling, and it classifies every attempt honestly as ACTIVE_COUNT, AMBIGUOUS, NO_VALID_COUNT or INVALIDATED. Ambiguity is a first class answer. The anti repaint audit is the standard: a count that changes its past when new bars arrive was never a count. Around the three rules sit the working expectations: an impulse has five legs not three, wave 3 is normally the strongest leg and carries the momentum peak, pullbacks are smaller and quieter than the legs they correct, and each wave lands in its Fibonacci zone.

Corrections are three legs, A, B and C, or a complex W, X and Y when the first correction does not finish the job. Echo classifies corrections by the shape of A, the depth of B, and whether C completes, fails or runs. Corrections after a completed impulse commonly end in the deep retracement bands, which is where the Fibonacci craft begins.

### Fibonacci: two tools, never substituted

The single most expensive Fibonacci error, in Echo's words the mistake that costs people money, is substituting one tool for the other. The retracement tool and the trend based extension tool produce different 1.618 prices from the same swing, because they answer different questions.

Use the retracement tool for pullback depth: waves 2 and 4, and post cycle corrections. Draw it on a named swing, wick to wick, because swings are defined by their extremes, and name the swing by its dates and prices in the read. The bands that matter: 0.5 to 0.618 for a shallow correction end, and 0.786 to 0.887 for the deep ends Echo calls his signature territory, including the 0.786 and 0.887 generational bottoms in the playbook. In the coded Echo lens the wave 2 zone is 0.5 to 0.786 with the sweet spot at 0.618 to 0.786, and a retrace beyond 0.886 downgrades conviction rather than extending the zone indefinitely. A retracement band becomes a buy zone only with confluence, per the definition in Section 2: at least two Fibonacci constructions in the same band in the Echo Algorithm, plus an average cluster, a trendline arrival or a prior shelf in the playbook rule.

Use the three point trend based extension tool for targets: waves 3, 5 and C. The Echo target ladder, in role order, is T1 at the wave 3 1.272 stall watch, the objective at the 1.618 extension of wave 1, and the extended case at 2.0 to 2.618 flagged only when momentum carries through 1.618. Every extension price is labelled projected wherever it appears, because no recorded level stands behind it, and it is never swapped for a retracement price that happens to look similar.

Decisions on Fibonacci swings are close based even though anchors are wick based. Keep the two jobs in separate sentences: anchors from extremes, triggers and invalidations from closes. A wick into a band is arrival. A reaction at the band, a bullish engulfing or hammer at the zone or a close back above the fast averages after touching it, on bounce volume above the pullback mean, is the trigger. Invalidation for an Echo impulse entry is the start of wave 1, the 1.0 retracement, as the absolute; for a nested 1 to 2, 1 to 2 count it is below the start of the larger wave 1.

### Elliott Wave International and the canon beside Echo

The Elliott Wave Principle tutorial materials in the external canon teach the same grammar from the other side of the desk: motive and corrective waves, degree, and the alternation and equality guidelines that sit under the three rules as expectations rather than laws. Read them as guidelines with that rank. The rules invalidate. The guidelines incline. A read that treats a guideline as an invalidation, or a rule as a suggestion, has the hierarchy backwards, and the desk counter's classification exists to keep that hierarchy mechanical.

## Section 5: Indicators as Servants of the Eye

Indicators serve the eye. They are read after cycle position, structure, location and volume, in that order, and none of them creates a read on its own. This section fixes the order among the servants themselves: volume first, then RSI, then MACD, then the relative strength line, then breadth as market context.

### Volume first

Volume is the tell of the whole method, and Section 1 already gave its four checks: climax volume at the lows, expansion on the break, drying volume on pullbacks, and expanding red volume on the 1 hour as the first distribution tell. Two desk rules bind its use. Any volume weighted signal on hourly or finer bars uses Alpaca SIP only; Yahoo hourly volume is disqualified desk wide after the desk's paired fetch reproduction showed impossible hourly volumes against the daily tape. And a breakout on contracting volume caps conviction and is stated as a cap in the read, never smoothed over. The healthy sequence, in the chart reader's falsifier language, is contraction inside the pattern and expansion on the break.

### RSI: momentum context first, divergence craft second

Read RSI first as momentum context: which regime of the move the oscillator is living in, whether it holds its midline on pullbacks in an uptrend or loses it, and whether extremes arrive where the structure says extremes belong. Read it second for divergence, under the Echo rule the whole desk copies: count RSI or MACD divergence only at pattern extremes, a bearish divergence at a pattern top or wedge terminal, a bullish divergence at a correction low. Divergence arms a level. It never pulls the trigger. The trigger is named in advance with its price: the 50 average reclaim or loss, or the reaction at the zone itself. A divergence drawn mid range, or drawn where Pass A carries no divergence pair, is decoration and is removed. The desk calibration is quoted beside the craft, honestly: the genome measurement gives divergence families their own outcome slices, and the reader quotes the family state rather than assuming the signal's worth.

### MACD: trend and momentum confirmation

Use MACD in the order a master uses it. First the weekly regime: line position and direction as trend context. Then the daily line for confirmation or contradiction of the structure read. Then the histogram for acceleration: a histogram that shrinks while price still rises is deceleration, stated as such, and a histogram divergence at a pattern extreme joins the divergence craft above, under the same arming rule. Crossovers are never used alone mid range; a crossover in the middle of a range is the oscillator describing chop, not calling a turn. ChartSchool's MACD and histogram articles in the canon carry the formula and interpretation in full, and the desk record notes double MACD variants as experimental material, not doctrine.

### The relative strength line and breadth

The relative strength line, the name plotted against its index, answers a question price alone cannot: is this name leading, keeping pace, or lagging while the index does the work. The teacher corpus in the census, Minervini and TraderLion among them, treats a rising RS line into a breakout as part of the setup and a falling RS line as a cap, and the desk copies that rank: RS strengthens or weakens a stance, it never creates one. Breadth is market context of the same rank: percent of names above the 50 day and 200 day averages, advancers against decliners, and sector rotation over 5 and 21 sessions, dated and stated in one line. Breadth confirms or contradicts an index trend and caps conviction when it narrows. It does not rescue a broken single name structure.

### Putting the servants together

A worked pass runs in one breath: cycle position named, structure and location stated, volume verdict given as the tell, RSI read for context and then for divergence at the extreme only, MACD read for regime, confirmation and acceleration, RS line checked against the index, breadth dated and its cap applied, and the verdict written conditionally with trigger and invalidation priced and both scenarios carried. Any disagreement between the eye, the computed pass and the panes is reconciled before the read is delivered, never narrated through.

## Section 6: The Working Read

A working read is a sequence that runs in the same order every time, so a later reader can see where a judgment came from and where it can be checked. The operative standard is the local stock analysis skill, which defines two modes, both paper only: chart read mode answers what a named ticker is doing, and full analysis mode adds strategy lenses, a structural screen, market context and synthesis to the same visual work. A read is not an order and never implies one.

### The eight steps

1. Geometry first, Pass A, computed fresh and blind. Read its data status before anything else: on NO_VIEW, stop with no analysis at all; on STALE, carry the caveat through the whole output; on PARTIAL, name what is missing. Save the JSON to the dated reads folder. After any edit to the geometry code, rerun its parity test and require all 18 checks green before trusting the output.
2. The perceptual read, Pass B, with the eye on the actual renders: weekly, daily, 1 hour and 10 minute charts viewed as files, never narrated from numbers alone. Whole before parts, axis and frame inventory, legend and series registry, transcription before interpretation, context before pattern names, a counter deception sweep, and counts marked and enumerated where the read depends on them.
3. Strategy lenses, in full analysis mode only, run after the visual read is written so they cannot contaminate the eye. Each lens is reported from its own ledger and universe, labelled by edge class, CORE, VALIDATED, WATCH, CONTESTED or EXPERIMENTAL. A contested lens is reported with both verdicts and leaned on with nothing. A lens that does not apply says so once and goes silent. A missing lens is never presented as neutral bullish. The structural screen verdict, INTACT, WATCH, BROKEN or UNKNOWN, caps the final stance at neutral negative on BROKEN no matter what any lens says.
4. Market context, as a conditioner and a cap. A quick Pass A on SPY, QQQ where relevant, sector membership and relative strength, and the context conditioners where present: attention tags, the sentiment composite reported by its components rather than its headline, and theme state. Conditioners strengthen or weaken a stance. None creates one. State the cap in words when it binds.
5. Synthesis, which is accounting, not averaging. Lens table first. Consilience stated where validated and core lenses agree, with agreement among correlated lenses counted once and said so. Every conflict stated baldly in its own section. The gap named as the finding when it is the finding. The final stance only as strong as the weakest required evidence, with the owning layer named: signal, structure or context. Abstention is a valid final stance and is never converted into a call.
6. Construction of the plan, below.
7. Drawing to the annotation standard, below.
8. Logging, then the 2 to 4 week review, below.

Every full read carries a three branch scenario tree. For each branch with a visible trigger, state the trigger with its price named in advance, the confirmation, the target with its method, the invalidation, and a confidence of LOW or MED, never HIGH on a single read. Leave a branch empty when it has no visible trigger; an empty branch is information. Close the tree with one conditional verdict line in the Echo and Max grammar, level named and patience explicit: losing a named level confirms the move to the named buy zone.

### Constructing the plan

The buy zone is a box earned by confluence, built in Section 2's order: name the swing wick to wick with its dates and prices, draw the retracement, score the candidate band for independent shelves, apply the threshold (a lone Fibonacci level is a watch level), then wait for the reaction. Until a reaction prints, the state is WATCH. A starter at the zone and full size on the average flip is the sizing grammar in the playbook.

Invalidation is born with the read, not added after a loss. A verdict that cannot name its invalidation is not emitted. State it as a close based condition at a named price, and write the wick versus close rule in the invalidation sentence itself: a wick through the zone does not kill it, a close below the zone bottom does. Respect the stop distance floor the desk autopsies earned: stops tighter than 1.5 ATR were consumed by noise before the thesis could work, so 1.5 ATR is the floor and 1.5 to 2.5 ATR is the working band for swing reads, with the ATR at read time stated so the distance can be checked later.

Targets carry two labels, role and method, or they are decoration. Roles in ladder order: T1, the first stall or partial zone; the objective, the primary target the plan is judged against; extended, flagged only when momentum carries through the objective. Methods, kept separate because the calibration record shows they are not interchangeable: recorded level (a prior high, a round number, a measured base top, a wall; in the genome cross check, recorded level objectives reached first most often among the three kinds), pattern measured move (the height of the named pattern projected from the trigger line, scaled by the family's target percent), and projected (a Fibonacci extension or wave projection with no recorded level behind it, labelled projected wherever it appears).

R is printed, computed, and judged to the final objective. Risk per unit is entry reference minus invalidation. Reward is target minus entry reference. Show R to T1 and R to the objective. Logical R to the final objective governs, R to T1 is shown, and a setup with R below 1 to its objective never boards. Record R in the log at read time. Expectancy, built from the base rate and the R actually on offer, is the decision variable; a beautiful R attached to an unmeasured pattern is still unmeasured.

### The annotation standard

One spec, two surfaces: a single chart spec object feeds both the card renderer and the plan chart renderer, so the card and the chart draw and label identically, and any disagreement between surfaces makes the chart defective, not artistic. Every finished plan chart draws, without exception: the buy zone as a shaded labelled box at the forefront in layman clear language; invalidation as an explicit labelled line with its price; targets labelled by role, each with its price; the named pattern itself drawn on the price pane (a pattern named in text but not drawn is not yet a call); at most two trendlines labelled as Resistance line or Support line and anchored on the cited pivots; every moving average the call cites and no uncited average clutter; the dashed expected path into reserved future space, primary and alternate both drawn, each with its terminus visible; volume, RSI and MACD panes with divergences drawn only where the record carries them; the expiry clock; and R printed on the chart, to T1 and to the objective. What never gets traded away under deadline pressure: the buy zone, the invalidation, the labelled targets, the named pattern drawn, the dashed expected path, the three panes with honest divergences, the expiry clock, and R on the chart. Before a chart is finished, cross check by eye what the spec enforces in code: drawn averages match the geometry, trendline labels match the lines, the zone on the chart matches the zone in the text to the cent, the invalidation matches the scenario tree, and the printed R recomputes from the three printed prices. Any no stops the publish.

### Logging and the review loop

A read that is not logged cannot be graded, and a reader who cannot be graded cannot improve on purpose. Log at read time: the Pass A JSON, the renders, and the written read with its reference class, cycle position, structure, volume tell, lens table in full mode, conflicts, scenario tree, plan construction with both Rs, pre-mortem, self critique, and an evidence tag on each load bearing claim (computed, measured, lit derived, derived inference, validated model or judgment; drop any claim with no computed or measured anchor). Every read also closes with a pre-mortem on how the read is wrong in two weeks and a self critique stating the strongest case against the delivered lean, and it answers the two questions separately: still going up, and safe to buy here.

Review every read at two weeks and at four weeks, from the original log, renders and geometry, not from a fresh chart alone. Score the branch first against the read's own geometry: target hit, invalidated, expired triggered or expired untriggered, with the same session rule the genome calibration uses (on daily bars, when objective and invalidation are both touched in one session, count invalidation first). Grade process separately from outcome: a correct process that lost is a keep, a sloppy process that won is a defect to cure. Check the volume tell, the trigger honesty, and the plan arithmetic. Write the autopsy in plain sentences with the one change the next read will make because of it. Aggregate monthly by setup family and by confidence label; a label that wins less often than the label below it is uncalibrated and is renamed or retrained before it is used again.

### Master failure modes of the eye, and their cures

Recency: the last bars shout, the pattern whispers. Cure: enforce the order, weekly regime written before the daily is described, all four timeframes rendered before the reference class line, full pattern anchors transcribed before the last bar. When the gestalt of the whole chart and the summary of the last week disagree, name the disagreement and let the higher timeframe govern.

Anchoring: the first level named becomes the level defended. Cure: compute blind and transcribe first, score every candidate zone by its confluence stack including the anchor candidate, draw the alternate scenario with its own terminus on every chart, and recompute fresh on a re read rather than extending an earlier verdict. A level that cannot cite its anchor swing, its average or its shelf leaves the chart.

Forcing patterns: seeing the beloved shape in ordinary noise. Cure: confirmation law and checklists run out loud: no pattern is a call before its confirmation close, elite detectors first with triangles and pennants demoted to context, wedge gates enforced, the impulse checklist run (five legs, three rules held, wave 3 strongest, pullbacks smaller and quieter, each wave in its zone), and the trap screen run on every claimed continuation. When a rule fails, the claim ends and the read falls back to structure language: range, base, extension, breakdown recovery. ABSTAIN and NO TRADE are valid outputs.

Wick versus close confusion: treating an intraday probe as a decision. Cure: predefine the rule per level before looking at the outcome and write it in the trigger and invalidation sentences. Anchors from extremes, decisions from closes. Zones are judged on closes; a wick through does not kill a zone and a close below its bottom does.

Supporting cures run with them: context inventoried before any pattern call cures pattern name first; episode based counting cures one episode masquerading as many touches; counting correlated evidence once cures stacking; quoting only the published table or the reader's own scored log cures invented base rates; the LOW or MED cap cures confidence inflation; and the explicit volume cap cures missing volume treated as neutral.

## Cheat Sheet: Midna's Reading Protocol

Run in order, on any chart, every time. Write each line. When a line cannot be written from what the chart shows, that blank is the finding.

1. Frame: symbol, timeframe, date range, linear or log scale, last bar complete or forming, every series registered.
2. Data status: fresh geometry computed this run; stale or partial data named and carried through the read; no view means no analysis.
3. Gestalt: one sentence, shape only, no pattern name, no direction call.
4. Weekly regime: one word, bullish, bearish or transitioning, plus one structural reason; price against the 200 period regime line stated.
5. Cycle position: impulse, correction, base, reversal attempt or terminal, named before any indicator, with its deciding level.
6. Daily structure: named shape, boundaries in price, age, confirmed 5 bar pivots only, extreme time and availability time kept apart.
7. Location: top, middle or bottom of the structure; nearest shelves above and below as zones; every level anchored and distanced in ATR.
8. Confluence score: Fibonacci retracement, Fibonacci extension, average cluster, trendline arrival, prior pivot shelf, round number; a lone Fibonacci level is a watch level, two or more independent constructions make a zone.
9. Trendlines: at most two, anchored on confirmed major pivots, three or more touches to validate, a line closed through is dead and reported dead.
10. Volatility: quiet, normal or expanding; current ATR(14) written down; stop distance checked against the 1.5 ATR floor.
11. Volume verdict: confirms, neutral or contradicts, with the bars that earn it; climax, breakout, pullback and 1 hour distribution checked; missing volume caps conviction and is stated.
12. Indicators as servants: RSI for momentum context, divergence only at pattern extremes and only arming a level; MACD for weekly regime, daily confirmation and histogram acceleration; crossovers never used alone mid range; relative strength line against the index; breadth dated in one line.
13. Wave check, when a count is claimed: wave 2 never retraces all of wave 1, wave 3 never the shortest, wave 4 never overlaps wave 1; retracement tool for depth, extension tool for targets, never substituted.
14. Pattern check, when a pattern is claimed: prior trend for reversals, anatomy named in order, confirmation close required, family base rate quoted from the published table or stated as not established.
15. Two questions answered separately: still going up, and safe to buy here.
16. Scenario tree: three branches, each with trigger price, confirmation, target with method, invalidation and LOW or MED confidence; empty branches left empty; one conditional verdict line written.
17. Plan: buy zone as a box with its confluence stack, reaction state named WATCH or SETUP; invalidation as a close based price born with the read; targets labelled by role and method; R to T1 and R to objective computed, shown and printed, with R below 1 to the objective excluded from boarding.
18. Chart drawn to standard: pattern drawn, buy zone shaded, invalidation labelled, targets labelled, dashed primary and alternate paths into reserved future space, volume RSI and MACD panes, divergences only where the record carries them, expiry clock and R on the chart.
19. State declared: watch, setup, in motion or no read, with the next action that state requires; patience stated plainly when patience is the call.
20. Log and review: geometry, renders and written read saved with dated triggers; pre-mortem and self critique included; review dates set at two weeks and four weeks.
