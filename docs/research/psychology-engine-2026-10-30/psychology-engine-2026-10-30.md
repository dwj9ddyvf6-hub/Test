## What the engine is

The Psychology Engine is an information system. It builds one daily Psychological State from nine named gauges, each gauge shown with its current percentile in its own backfilled history. It issues 2 to 4 falsifiable forecasts through registered hooks, graded on the estate forecast ledger discipline. Its consumers are Midna, in her own simulated book (her pre open read and her trade cards), and one terminal reader page, working name Market Mood.

It changes no desk sleeve, no sizing rule and not the Tensor engine. It orders nothing. Its claimed skill is capped in advance by proposition P4 from the companion study: no composite this engine builds is expected to beat a 2 percent monthly out of sample R squared for broad index returns over any five year live window, and any wording anywhere that implies more is a defect to be corrected on sight.

Four standing rules govern everything:

1. **History is backfilled, honestly.** Historical input data goes as deep as the real published record honestly goes, from the original sources, with provenance per series. Percentile bands, extreme zones and regime history come from the backfilled distribution, never from a cold start.
2. **Reconstructions are labelled.** A historical gauge value computed from historical inputs with a formula written today is a RECONSTRUCTION wherever it appears: in the store, in exhibits, on the page, in the consumer file. It is never presented as a forecast the engine made at the time.
3. **The ledger is never backfilled.** A prediction exists only if it was written before its horizon. Backfilled inputs calibrate gauges. They do not create a track record.
4. **Independence accounting.** Channels that share inputs cast one vote, not two. The CNN Fear and Greed index is a context tile only, never a gauge of record, because it is built from inputs this engine already counts. Modelled estate lanes are labelled modelled wherever they feed a gauge.

Uniform conventions, stated once:

* **Orientation.** Every mood gauge is oriented so high means risk seeking, confident, greed side behaviour, and low means fearful, risk averse, capitulation side behaviour. Inputs that naturally run the other way (put and call ratios, VIX, credit spreads, bearish share) are inverted at ingestion, and the inversion is written in the formula. Two gauges keep their own orientation on their face: G4, where high means fragile, and G8, whose values are event probabilities, not mood scores. Neither is ever averaged into mood.
* **The one percentile rule.** The percentile of a gauge on a date is 100 times the share of its historical observations on or before that date with value less than or equal to the current value. No observation dated after the reading is ever in the set. A gauge shows NO BAND, with the words "insufficient history", until its stored series holds at least 250 observations at its native cadence, or at least 120 monthly observations for a monthly gauge. The value still displays. The percentile does not. Percentiles use the full stored history, not a trailing window. One exception: the options gauge also reports a trailing 3 year percentile, because the put and call series has documented structure breaks in 2020 and 2022, and hook F2 is written against the trailing 3 year figure. Both display, labelled. They are never averaged together.
* **The one zone table.** Applied to the full history percentile of an oriented mood gauge: 0 to 2 Extreme fear; 2 to 5 Severe fear; 5 to 20 Fear; 20 to 40 Cautious; 40 to 60 Neutral; 60 to 80 Confident; 80 to 95 Greed; 95 to 98 Severe greed; 98 to 100 Extreme greed. For G4 the same bands read: 95 to 100 Extreme fragility; 80 to 95 High fragility; 40 to 80 Ordinary leverage; 5 to 40 Low leverage; 0 to 5 Minimal leverage.
* **Clock rule.** Every input enters the daily state with its true as of date, never its download date. A Tuesday COT position published Friday enters as Tuesday position. If an input is older than two of its own cadence periods at state time, its tile flags stale and prints the as of date. Stale is a factual state, not an apology, and it never blocks the rest of the state.
* **Absence rule.** A missing input is stored and displayed as absent, named, never as zero. A gauge with a missing core input computes from its remaining inputs only where its formula says so, and flags partial with the missing input named.

## Chapter 1: The gauges, and what each one reads

Six gauges are core mood gauges and vote in the composite: G1, G2, G3, G5, G6, G7. G4 is the fragility axis. G8 is the belief anchor. G9 is the overnight transfer panel. G8 and G9 display from day one and vote in nothing until their own ledger grades argue otherwise. Social and belief channels are collected from day one because uncollected history is unrecoverable, and ranked out of the v1 composite because their estate history starts at collection.

### G1. What People Say (stated sentiment)

What it measures: what investors say they expect, in surveys, before any money moves. Stated belief, with NAAIM shading toward revealed positioning because respondents report exposure they claim to hold.

Inputs, all free: the AAII Sentiment Survey weekly spreadsheet (bullish, bearish and neutral shares; archive July 1987, VERIFIED) and the NAAIM Exposure Index weekly file (mean, median, quartiles and respondent dispersion; published Thursday for Wednesday exposure; archive July 2006, VERIFIED). Investors Intelligence is not an input. It is paid, and is named here as the gap.

Computation: `aaii_spread` equals bullish percent minus bearish percent, weekly. NAAIM mean as published, weekly, never thresholded raw because the sample is structurally bullish (historical median near 72 percent long); only its percentile is read. The gauge value is the mean of the two full history sub score percentiles. If one survey is missing for a week, the gauge computes from the other and flags partial. Stored alongside, never inside the gauge value: NAAIM dispersion and AAII neutral share, displayed as the disagreement line. Rising dispersion at a flat mean is a headline fact on the tile.

Cadence: weekly, carried into every daily state with its as of Wednesday date.

Day one state: **absent.** Both publisher routes failed from this network at build: the AAII spreadsheet routes returned HTTP 404 (publisher bot wall) and the NAAIM page carries no data file link. No substitute source was used. The collector code for both is built and retries on the daily lane. That restraint is correct and the gap is still a gap: the survey triad was ranked third in the measurement shortlist, and on day one the engine has no stated belief vote from living surveys at all. G2 carries the slow stated side alone.

### G2. Slow Climate (the regime spine)

What it measures: the slow mood of households and the slow speculation climate of the market itself, at monthly frequency. This gauge sets the regime context inside which every faster gauge is read. It is the deepest history in the engine.

Inputs, all free: the University of Michigan Index of Consumer Sentiment via FRED (UMCSENT, with UMCSENT1 for the early quarterly portion; archive November 1952, VERIFIED; quarterly to 1977, monthly from January 1978), plus preliminary prints from the Surveys of Consumers site stored as a separate vintage; and the Baker and Wurgler investor sentiment index, level and orthogonalised versions, from the Wurgler NYU Stern faculty spreadsheet (monthly from July 1965, VERIFIED as published span; every pull stores its download date and file hash because the index recomputes when its components revise).

Computation: each series becomes its own full history percentile, using only observations up to that month. Michigan quarterly era stays quarterly and is labelled as such; it is never interpolated to monthly. The gauge value is the mean of the two percentiles where both exist. Before July 1965 the gauge is Michigan alone and flags single source era. The gauge is a regime label input, not a timing input, and no surface may attach directional return language to G2 alone: the Baker and Wurgler index explains about 0.3 percent of monthly market return variance in the Zhou (2018) accounting carried in the study.

Cadence: monthly. Preliminary Michigan enters marked preliminary and is replaced by the final print as a new vintage, never by overwriting.

Day one state: **built, but on Michigan alone, which is a named deviation.** The Wurgler spreadsheet route is refused from this network (the Stern data page returns 403 and the current file names return 404). The single source state is the design own pre 1965 form, extended past 1965 by circumstance and named as a deviation in the audit. Stored observations: 677 monthly. Value 0.3, percentile 0.6, zone Extreme fear, as of 2026-09-01, in the extreme zone since 2026-08-01. Read that percentile for what it is: a Michigan only reconstruction percentile, not the two source gauge the design specifies.

### G3. Where Money Sits (positioning)

What it measures: revealed positioning. Where leveraged and institutional money actually sits in index futures, where fund money is flowing, and how much off exchange volume is being sold short. A crowding and forced flow map first, a return signal second.

Inputs, all free unless marked: CFTC Commitments of Traders Legacy report, futures only, for the S and P 500, Nasdaq 100 and Dow index contracts plus VIX futures (futures only from 1986, VERIFIED, with the pre September 1992 caveat stored in the series header); CFTC Traders in Financial Futures (TFF), dealer, asset manager and leveraged fund categories (published from 2006; complete annual files listed from September 2009, VERIFIED as stated; 2006 to 2009 coverage to be checked file by file); ICI weekly estimated flows and monthly long term flows, stored as separate vintages because weekly estimates revise (archive depths UNVERIFIED in the measurement study, recorded as downloaded); and the estate Morgue DIX reading, reused not rebuilt, with the per name dark values the Morgue computes and discards stored by this program FINRA collector rather than recomputed.

Computation: for each index contract family, TFF leveraged fund net position and Legacy noncommercial net position, each scaled by total open interest. The positioning sub score is the mean full history percentile of that scaled net across the S and P 500 and Nasdaq 100 families, TFF form where it exists and Legacy form before it, with the junction marked as a series break. The flow sub score is ICI equity plus hybrid net flow over the trailing four weeks, scaled where ICI publishes category assets, in its own percentile. The short pressure sub score is the DIX index reading inverted, displayed as context from day one and kept out of the gauge value until the DIX record reaches 250 sessions. The gauge value is the mean of the positioning and flow sub scores. COT roll weeks are flagged in the store, and a gauge move in a flagged roll week carries the flag onto the tile, because contract rolls create false positioning jumps.

Cadence: weekly for COT (positions as of Tuesday, published Friday 15:30 ET) and ICI estimates; daily for the DIX context line.

Day one state: **partial, TFF positioning leg only.** Stored observations: 617 weekly, covering the TFF pull from 2015 forward (12 annual files). The Legacy deep archive to 1986 was not pulled in this build, and the ICI flow tables were not pulled either. Both are named gaps in the store and the gauge flags partial. Value 27.07, percentile 35.7, zone Cautious, as of 2026-10-27. So the deepest true positioning archive in the catalog is, in the built engine, an eleven year series, not a forty year one. The design target and the built state differ, and this book reports the built state.

### G4. Leverage and Fragility (the forced flow axis)

What it measures: how much selling a move would force. Not mood. Leverage stock in brokerage accounts, fast leverage in crypto perpetuals, and the dealer hedging configuration that amplifies or damps a move. This is the v1 form of the positioning convexity idea: a qualitative panel of named inputs in their own zones. The quantified forced flow index with published elasticities is a separate future commission, because an elasticity this program has not estimated is theatre if printed as a number.

Inputs: FINRA margin statistics, monthly Excel (debit balances, free credit in cash accounts, free credit in margin accounts; from January 1997, VERIFIED; the February 2010 collection break marked in the store); crypto perpetual funding rates for BTC and ETH via Coinalyze aggregated (desk key already held; the auth header must be named exactly `api_key` and sent via curl or `http.client`, never `urllib`) with direct exchange APIs (Binance, Bybit, OKX public endpoints, free) as cross check and fallback; and the estate Morgue Map net gamma exposure sign and size for SPY, QQQ and IWM, reused not rebuilt, modelled and convention signed from overnight open interest, with the tile saying modelled every time.

Computation: the margin leverage ratio is debit balances divided by debit balances plus free credit cash plus free credit margin, monthly, plus its 12 month change, both percentile ranked on the full FINRA history from 1997. Level without scaling is never displayed as a signal. Funding pressure is the seven day mean of BTC and ETH funding rates, annualised by the venue convention stated in the store, percentile ranked on stored funding history, with the sign printed in words (crowded long or crowded short), because fragility lives on both sides. The amplifier state is the Morgue Map net GEX sign per index ETF, printed as damping (positive) or amplifying (negative) with net GEX in dollars per 1 percent move, with no percentile until the thin estate Map record matures. The gauge value is the mean of the margin percentile and the absolute funding pressure percentile. The signed funding direction and the amplifier state are separate printed fields, never folded into that mean.

Cadence: monthly for margin (published in the third week of the following month, carried with its true month as of date), funding aggregated daily, amplifier daily pre open.

Day one state: **no band, correctly.** The margin history is full from 1997. The funding leg holds 96 daily observations (OKX funding history depth; Binance refused from this network), which is under the 250 observation minimum, so the gauge shows NO BAND by its own rule. ## Chapter 2: History, provenance, percentile bands and calibration

### The store

Root: `~/workspace/terminal-lineup/psychology-engine/`, also reachable at `~/workspace/psychology-engine/` through a symlink, which satisfies the design store root path.

* `history/series/`: one file per input series. A series the design lists that a source fails to deliver is created with its header and an absence note, so the store enumerates its own gaps.
* `history/gauges/`: one file per gauge, G1 to G9.
* `history/provenance/PULL-LOG.md`: every pull, live or backfill, with date, series, route, rows added, rows revised, downloaded span and any failure, in the collector own words at pull time.
* `history/backtests/`: the backtest record, a different folder and a different word from the ledger, always.
* `history/per_name/`: per name stores, including the FINRA per name short volume store described in Chapter 5.
* `live/raw/`: raw snapshots, one folder per collector, dated, byte for byte before any parsing. Parsing reads the snapshot, never the network.
* `live/state/`: daily state JSON files, one per session.
* `exhibits/`: `CALIBRATION-EXHIBITS.md` and `exhibits.json`.
* `output/`: `state-latest.json`, the one page `STATE.md`, a dated state archive, and the terminal page feed.

Every series file begins with a provenance header carrying, in order: series name; source (publisher and exact product or file); source route used, copied from the pull code; retrieved date; last pull date; revision policy; what the date column means; archive claim with its VERIFIED or UNVERIFIED label and by whom; downloaded span actually in the file; dated methodology or collection breaks; any scaling, inversion or unit change applied; and the hash of the source file as downloaded, where a single source file exists.

Vintage rule: series that revise (FRED, ICI, FINRA margin, Baker and Wurgler, Michigan preliminary versus final) store first published and latest values in separate columns (`value_first`, `value_latest`) wherever the source makes the first published value obtainable. Where it does not, the header says so, and every backtest on that series carries the note "revised values, live vintage unavailable". A value is never overwritten in place.

Gauge files carry their own header naming the formula version (the design, by gauge number), the input series files, and a `kind` column whose only values are `reconstruction` and `live`. There is no third value. Every row dated before the engine live start, 2026-10-30, is a reconstruction.

Point in time discipline inside the reconstructions: a gauge value for a date is computed only from input observations whose as of date is on or before that date and whose publication date is on or before that date where the source publishes on a lag (COT, margin, ICI finals, Michigan finals). Where a historical publication lag cannot be reconstructed honestly, the header states the assumed lag and the affected rows carry the flag "lag assumed". Exhibits print two percentiles per reading, never mixed in one column: the expanding percentile (rank against the record up to that date) and the full history percentile (rank in the whole stored record to today). They answer different questions.

Formula changes after live start create a new formula version. The historical series is recomputed under the new version as a new file version, the old file is kept, and live grading under the old version is closed out, never rewritten.

### Percentile bands

Bands come from the percentile rule and zone table stated at the front of this book, computed on the backfilled distribution of each gauge own stored series. Two consequences deserve plain statement.

First, a band is only as honest as its series. G2 band on day one is a Michigan only band. G5 band is a SKEW only band. G6 breadth band is a 4 index band. G3 band is a 2015 forward TFF band, which contains no 2008 and no 2000, so a G3 percentile of 35.7 means 35.7 against 2015 to 2026 positioning, not against the full positioning record the design intended. Each tile flag names its partial state so a reader can apply exactly that discount.

Second, four gauges have no band at all on day one, by the minimum history rule working as designed: G1 (no data), G4 (96 observations), G7 (71 observations) and G8 (no percentile zones by design until a year of stored history exists, and in fact only 2 stored observations). A gauge with no band still displays its value where it has one. It does not display a percentile it has not earned.

## Chapter 3: The composite state rule

Every session, after the close and before 04:00 ET the next morning, the compiler publishes one Psychological State containing: a named regime; the gauge table (all nine gauges, each with value, full history percentile, zone in words, as of date and status flags); the fragility line (G4) and the belief anchor summary (G8) as their own lines, never inside the regime score; a disagreement statement in words, always present even when it reads gauges agree; an extremes list (every gauge currently in a 0 to 5 or 95 to 100 zone, named, with the date it entered the zone); and the forecast hooks due, fired or graded that day, each with its ledger reference.

The combination rule, plainly:

1. Take the six core mood gauges (G1, G2, G3, G5, G6, G7) and use each available gauge current oriented percentile.
2. The composite score is the **median** of the available core percentiles. The median, not the mean, because one gauge at a broken or spiking extreme must not drag the state with it, and because a reader can recompute a median from the printed table by hand.
3. If fewer than four core gauges are available (not stale beyond two cadence periods, not absent), no composite score is published. The regime is named Insufficient data and the missing gauges are named. A state built on three gauges wearing the clothing of six is a provenance violation.
4. Regime names from the median: 0 to 5 Capitulation zone; 5 to 20 Deep fear; 20 to 40 Fearful; 40 to 60 Neutral mix; 60 to 80 Risk seeking; 80 to 95 Greed; 95 to 100 Euphoric excess.
5. G2 role is printed, not hidden in the median: every state carries the line "Slow climate: (G2 zone)", because the same fast fear reading means one thing inside a slow greed climate and another inside a slow fear climate.
6. G4 overlays, applied after the regime name, in order. If the G4 fragility percentile is 90 or above and the regime is Neutral mix or higher, the regime becomes Complacent fragility when the G6 volatility block is in its lowest tercile, and otherwise gains the suffix ", fragile". Complacency is the Minsky build phase, and it gets its own name so it cannot hide inside a benign median. If G4 is 90 or above and the regime is Deep fear or Capitulation zone, the regime gains the suffix ", forced flow active", the state names the forcing legs in their extreme zones, and the state says in those words that while forcing dominates, contrary readings carry no force. **On day one neither overlay can fire**, because G4 has no percentile. That is the rule working honestly, and it is also a real limitation of the day one state: a fragility suffix the design promises cannot appear until the funding history accrues past 250 observations or a design amendment changes the banding basis for the margin leg.

Disagreement, handled by naming it, never by averaging it away:

* The state computes and prints the interquartile range and the full range of the core percentiles.
* Contested rule: if the full range is 60 percentile points or more, or if at least one core gauge sits in a 0 to 20 zone while another sits in an 80 to 100 zone, the regime name gains the prefix "Contested:" and the state must name the poles in words: which gauge is fearful, which is greedy, and each one one line reason from its own inputs. The median still prints. It prints second, after the poles.
* Family fracture note: when the stated family (G1, G2) and the revealed family (G3, G5, G6) disagree by the contested rule between families specifically, the state prints the sentence "People say one thing and hold another", with both sides percentiles. This program supplies that daily fracture reading to the estate Family Fracture engine territory as a store. It does not duplicate its forecast.
* A contested state is a complete state. No surface in this program writes "on balance" over a contested reading, and the consumer file carries the contest into the pre open read unresolved, because Midna judgment, not an average, is the consumer of a disagreement.

What the composite is not: not a return forecast (it enters no sizing, no sleeve and no order path; its only forecast content is the four hooks of Chapter 4, each separately ledgered); not a single fear and greed dial of the CNN kind (that index may appear on the terminal page only as a context tile labelled as an outside composite with no audited archive, never in the gauge table); and not exempt from the P4 cap stated at the front of this book.

The mechanism itself is graded GREEN in the build audit, on this evidence: median of available core gauges (four of six on day one), Insufficient data below four proven by synthetic test, the contested rule proven by synthetic test naming both poles, the G4 overlay code present, extremes with entry dates published, and the stated versus revealed fracture sentence implemented. Green for the mechanism means the rule computes as specified on the inputs it is given. It does not mean the inputs are complete. They are not, and Chapter 1 names exactly how.

## Chapter 4: The forecast hooks, and how they grade

Four hooks, each drawn from a proposition in the companion study, each stating its horizon, its grading rule and its ledger write, and each written into the ledger before its horizon starts. Belief forecasts and return forecasts are graded on separate boards, and fragility forecasts on the accuracy of the conditional map. Every hook carries the kill condition of its parent proposition. A hook that meets its kill condition is retired in public, on the page and in the state document, with its full record shown.

All four hooks write through the estate forecast machinery, not a second ledger: the append only hash chained ledger (`forecast/ledger.py` and `forecast/ledger.jsonl`), the registry (`forecast/registry.json`, with this program entries also held in the engine own `registry-psychology.json`), and the grading module (`forecast/grading.py`), with program specific resolvers for outcome kinds the estate module does not yet resolve (survey prints, realised volatility windows, basket spreads from the French library), registered like any other so the Record and the terminal audit layer can recompute them. The interface is append only from this program side. The program code contains no code path that opens the ledger file for writing directly. Day one ledger scan: zero psychology rows exist in the estate ledger and zero backdated rows; the hash chain verifies. The 65 rows appended to the estate ledger during the build window were the estate own daily refresh, verified by inspection, not this program forecasts.

### F1. Next survey print (belief board, from P1)

Cadence weekly, issued Thursday after the AAII print is stored and before the next survey week voting closes. Forecast: the direction and a probability distribution over three buckets for next week AAII bull minus bear spread (spread widens bullish by more than plus 2 points; spread within plus or minus 2 points; spread narrows or falls by more than minus 2 points). The model is the trailing return revision model from the study: the spread forecast is a stated function of trailing 3 month and trailing 1 month index returns, coefficients fitted only on data before the forecast date and refit on a stated annual schedule, each refit starting a new model version in the registry. Horizon one week, to the next AAII print. Grading: Brier score on the three buckets, against two named baselines graded alongside every week, the base rate forecaster (historical bucket frequencies) and the persistence forecaster (next week equals this week). Belief board only: a season of beating persistence on beliefs says nothing about returns, and the grade display says so. Kill: if the trailing return model loses to a releases only comparison model on pre registered error over 36 consecutive live months at the monthly Michigan leg of P1, or loses to persistence on Brier over the first 52 weekly AAII forecasts, F1 is retired and survey forecasting reverts to measurement only.

Day one status: **registered, not fired, and it could not fire.** No AAII print exists in the store to forecast from (G1 absent). Named, not substituted.

### F2. Stress predicts variance, not direction (variance board, from P5)

Cadence event triggered, checked daily after the close. Trigger: the G5 equity put and call ratio enters above its trailing 3 year 95th percentile, with the 2020 and 2022 regime break flags attached to the trigger record. Forecast: a probability, stated as a number between 1 and 99 percent, that SPX realised volatility over the next 20 trading days exceeds its unconditional median realised volatility for 20 day windows, computed from the history store. The forecast explicitly makes no directional claim, and the ledger entry direction field is the registered value "none, variance only". Horizon 20 trading days from the session after the trigger. Grading: Brier score against the base rate (the historical frequency of above median realised volatility windows, about 50 percent by construction, adjusted for the overlapping window convention stated in the registry entry). Separately and for information only, the grader records the 20 day index return and its trailing 5 day return control, so directional content, if it ever appears, is visible in the record rather than asserted. That record is not a forecast and is never scored as one. Kill: if directional content survives the trailing return control with a stable sign across both the pre 2020 and post 2022 regimes in the backtest record, or across 30 live triggers, F2 is replaced by the directional version that earned it, through a design amendment, never by editing F2 in place.

Day one status: **armed, trigger cannot evaluate.** The put and call leg is absent at build (G5 partial), so the trigger has nothing to read. Named, not faked.

### F3. Coincidence at extremes (cross section board, from P22 and P3)

Cadence monthly, issued on the first session after the Baker and Wurgler monthly value used is published and stored. Forecast: conditional, and silent in most months. When at least two of four registered families are simultaneously in their high sentiment extreme (surveys: G1 or G2 in a 95 plus zone; flows and leverage: G3 flow sub score or G4 margin leg at or above the 95th percentile; issuance: the Baker and Wurgler issuance components in their top quartile, read from the stored index components where the published file carries them, otherwise from the G2 level as the registered fallback, named as a fallback in the entry; options stress: G5 at or below the 5th percentile, the fear side, an asymmetry that is registered, not improvised), the hook forecasts that over the next 6 months a high sentiment beta basket underperforms a low sentiment beta basket. Baskets are the characteristic sorts of P3 from the Ken French data library (free): small, young, high volatility, unprofitable, non dividend paying against the opposite characteristics, as the library published sorts allow, with the exact sort files named in the registry entry. The mirror forecast (bottom extremes, high beta outperforms) is the same hook with the sign field flipped. Horizon 6 months, non overlapping episodes counted separately from overlapping observations, with the independent episode count printed at every grading. Any display resting on fewer than ten independent episodes is labelled preliminary. Grading: the conditional spread against zero, and against the best single family gauge alone, graded alongside as the named competitor, because P22 claim is that coincidence beats its best component, not merely that coincidence works. The index own return over the window is recorded and is not the grade. Kill: if the coincidence rule fails to beat its best component on the pre registered metric in the backtest record, F3 never enters the live ledger at all, and the engine ships its family gauges with no composite forecast claim, with that outcome recorded in the build log. Live, the hook is retired if the sign reverses over the first six independent live episodes.

Blocker, recorded in `history/backtests/BACKTEST-RECORD.md`: the Ken French characteristic sort files and the Baker and Wurgler issuance components were not pulled. ### F4. Capitulation is a zone, not a date (return board, from P19)

Cadence event triggered, checked daily after the close. Trigger, the joint capitulation signature of P19: index volume in its top decile against its own history, VIX in its top decile, and down day breadth in its bottom decile, all on the G6 store, on the same session or within three sessions of each other, registered as one signature with its first date named. Forecast: a probability, 1 to 99 percent, that the index trades at least 5 percent below the first signature close at some point within the following 60 sessions. The base rate from the backtest record is printed inside the forecast itself. The forecast plain language, fixed in the registry, is: "capitulation signature seen. History says the low is a zone measured in weeks and percent, not this date." Horizon 60 sessions. Grading: Brier score against the printed base rate, plus the path record (days to the eventual low, depth below the signature close) published at grading. Kill, deliberately the hardest in the design to meet: if first signatures mark final lows within 5 percent in more than 70 percent of graded episodes across backtest and live boards combined, the engine may, with the record shown, upgrade capitulation to a timing input, through a design amendment. Until then, any wording anywhere in this program outputs that treats a capitulation signature as a bottom call is a defect.

### Live collectors

Twelve collectors, one per source family, no shared fate: `collect_surveys` (AAII and NAAIM, Thursday after publication plus a Friday catch up); `collect_michigan_bw` (Surveys prints, FRED UMCSENT, Wurgler spreadsheet, daily check acting only when a new vintage appears); `collect_cot` (CFTC Legacy and TFF, Friday after the 15:30 ET release plus a Saturday catch up); `collect_flows_margin` (ICI tables weekly, FINRA margin monthly watch); `collect_finra` (FINRA daily short volume, short interest, and the FNQC file the Morgue DIX consumes, daily after FINRA publication for short volume and on FINRA publication clock for the rest); `collect_cboe` (put and call, SKEW, VIX family, VIX futures settlements, daily after the close); `collect_prices` (panel daily bars and FRED OAS, daily after the close); `collect_attention` (Trends pinned pulls, GDELT, Wikipedia pageviews, daily after the GDELT day file closes); `collect_social` (StockTwits sample, Reddit mentions, daily aggregation of intraday samples); `collect_belief` (Kalshi, Polymarket and Metaculus daily snapshots, daily at a fixed time after the United States close; this is the collector named in the verification failure); `collect_crypto` (Alternative.me index, Coinalyze and exchange funding, BTC and ETH prices, daily plus the Monday 04:00 ET weekend pair run); and `collect_estate` (Morgue Map card, Morgue Tape session summary, modelled IV ledger summary, daily after the Morgue own cycle, reading its published outputs only).

One dead source never silences the state, and a silent collector is itself a defect the next state document names. Re runs are idempotent: the natural key of each series (date, plus contract or series identifiers where applicable) is unique in the store, enforced at write. That enforcement sentence is exactly where the G8 failure lives: the belief series as written has no key column beyond its first column, so enforcement by first column collapsed distinct markets into one row. Credential discipline: Coinalyze calls use curl or `http.client` with the header named exactly `api_key`, never `urllib`. In the built engine the working route is curl, because the `http.client` route fails TLS in this environment by bypassing the egress proxy; curl is one of the two routes desk law allows, and its day one snapshot landed. No new key was requested for v1.

The FINRA collector is also a cure, not only a feed: it writes the per name daily short volume store the estate Short Volume Ledger tool had never accrued, and the per name dark values the Morgue DIX computes and discards, into per name stores in the shapes existing tools and engines already expect. ### Backfill

One backfill script per archive, on one shared harness. Checkpointed after every chunk (archive, last completed chunk, rows written, source hashes seen), so a killed run resumes from its checkpoint. The build itself was killed and resumed three times across transport fixes, with the store intact, which is the harness working under real conditions rather than in a test narrative. Idempotent: re running a completed backfill adds zero rows and reports already complete with its span. Provenance stamped: the first act of every backfill is writing the series header from what the download actually contains, including the downloaded span, which may be shorter than the archive claim; a shorter span is recorded and the claim corrected to the span with the original claim kept as a note. The store describes the download, not the brochure. Backfills that join eras (Legacy to TFF, VXO to VIX, quarterly to monthly Michigan, pre 1992 to weekly COT) write junction rows with a break marker and a short junction note, and never smooth across the seam. Long pulls run detached and are monitored by their effects (checkpoint growth, rows in store), never by a process name pattern.

### The compiler

`compiler/build_state.py`, run daily after the last scheduled collector window, and in historical mode for the gauge history and the exhibits. It reads the series store (never the network, never raw snapshots directly except in a declared reparse), computes each gauge at its native cadence up to today, appends new live gauge rows with kind live, computes today percentiles by the uniform rule, applies the composite rule, the overlays and the disagreement rules, checks the hook triggers and collects grades that fall due, then writes the state JSON, the dated copy, the one page STATE.md and its archive copy, the consumer file and the terminal page feed, and prints a one line run receipt (gauges computed, gauges stale, hooks fired, grades landed, failures named) that the scheduling lane files with the run. A run that computes the state from a store it also silently repaired is forbidden: the compiler never writes to `history/series/`. Repairs happen in collectors, in the open. If the compiler cannot reach four core gauges, it still writes the state, named Insufficient data, with the gaps named. It never writes yesterday state with today date.
