# Psychology and Sentiment in Markets: The Study

For the owner. Compiled 2026-10-30 from the two source studies in `research_notes/psychology-engine-2026-10-30/` (STUDY-THEORY.md and STUDY-MEASUREMENT.md).

Plain words throughout. No em dashes, by instruction.

## What this study is, and what it is not

This is a study of what market psychology actually does, what can honestly be measured about it, and what would count as predicting what the crowd will think next.

It is not a trading system. It does not size anything, it does not change any desk sleeve or the Tensor engine, and it does not claim that any effect named here is currently tradable after costs.

One asymmetry runs through everything below. Psychology explains a large part of why prices can depart from a narrow fundamental value. It explains far less, on its own, about timing. A reader who keeps that one sentence in mind will read the rest correctly.

## How the grades work

Every major claim carries a grade and a citation.

* **Robust:** found in multiple independent samples, countries or data types, with a mechanism that predicts where the effect should be stronger or weaker, and that pattern actually observed.
* **Moderate:** replicated, but size, sign or horizon depends materially on sample, construction or regime. Usable as a conditional input. Dangerous as a standalone rule.
* **Fragile:** published and plausible, but sensitive to specification, period or multiple testing corrections. Treat as a hypothesis, not a fact.
* **Debunked or failed in its strong form:** the headline claim did not survive direct replication, out of sample tests or live deployment. A weaker cousin may still be true and is named where it exists.
* **Unverifiable as stated:** framed so loosely that no observation could refute it. Excluded from the testable propositions.

Three background facts discipline every grade. First, multiple testing is endemic: Harvey, Liu and Zhu (2016), *Review of Financial Studies* 29(1), catalogue more than 300 published factors and argue a new factor needs a t statistic above roughly 3 to be credible. Second, broad replication is sobering: Hou, Xue and Zhang (2020), *Review of Financial Studies* 33(5), find a large majority of 452 published anomalies fail under uniform stricter methods, and McLean and Pontiff (2016), *Journal of Finance* 71(1), find published predictors decay by roughly a quarter to a half after publication. Third, almost every sentiment proxy is correlated with past returns, so a variable that merely repackages momentum or reversal will look predictive until those are controlled, and then often collapse. Apply a standing haircut to any published effect size. Assume the live effect, if real, is materially smaller.

## Part 1: The mechanisms that matter

A bias label is not an explanation. Each mechanism below is stated the same way: the mechanism, the footprint it should leave in positioning, the market consequence that follows only if enough capital shares it at the same time, and the boundary where you should stop invoking it.

### 1. Prospect theory and loss aversion

Source: Kahneman and Tversky (1979), *Econometrica* 47(2); formalised for asset prices by Barberis, Huang and Santos (2001), *Quarterly Journal of Economics* 116(1). The often quoted loss aversion number near 2.25 comes from Tversky and Kahneman (1992), *Journal of Risk and Uncertainty* 5. It is a laboratory median, not a constant of human nature.

Four parts do the work. Reference dependence: outcomes are coded as gains and losses against a reference point such as purchase price, a recent high or a round number. Diminishing sensitivity: the first 5 percent matters more than the move from 40 to 45 percent, and in the loss domain the value function turns convex, so a holder deep in a loss prefers a gamble that might restore breakeven. Loss aversion proper: realising a loss closes a mental account at a painful value (Thaler 1985, *Marketing Science* 4(3)). Probability weighting: small probabilities are overweighted, which makes lottery-like payoffs and insurance-like payoffs attractive in the same person at the same time.

Footprint: reluctance to sell below the reference price even when the thesis is broken, averaging down and moving to higher volatility instruments after losses, realising gains early, and demand for skewed payoffs such as deep out of the money options out of proportion to expected return. Aggregated, myopic loss aversion can generate the equity premium puzzle (Benartzi and Thaler 1995, *Quarterly Journal of Economics* 110(1)), and probability weighting alone can price positively skewed stocks low in expected return terms (Barberis and Huang 2008, *American Economic Review* 98(5)).

Boundary: prospect theory predicts conditional behaviour given a reference point and a framing. It does not predict that a market must rise or fall on a given day. An engine that claims loss aversion is high today without saying whose reference point, formed when, has said nothing testable. Grade: robust as a description of individual choice, moderate as a direct market timing input. Market experience reduces some anomalies in field settings (List 2003, *Quarterly Journal of Economics* 118(1)), so do not assume laboratory sizes survive among professionals unchanged.

### 2. Overconfidence and trading frequency

Sources: Barber and Odean (2000), *Journal of Finance* 55(2); Barber and Odean (2001), *Quarterly Journal of Economics* 116(1); Odean (1999), *American Economic Review* 89(5); theory in Daniel, Hirshleifer and Subrahmanyam (1998), *Journal of Finance* 53(6).

Keep three forms separate. Overestimation: believing your expected return or skill is higher than it is. Overplacement: believing you rank higher than you do. Overprecision: believing your information is more precise than it is, shown as confidence intervals that are too narrow. Overprecision has the cleanest trading implication: trade larger, trade more often, diversify less, update less on contrary evidence. Self attribution then protects the error, because wins are credited to skill and losses to bad luck.

Evidence and size: in Barber and Odean (2000), 66,465 households from 1991 to 1996, the most actively trading quintile earned about 11.4 percent per year net against a market return of about 17.9 percent, a gap of roughly 6.5 percentage points per year, done largely by trading costs and poor trade timing. Odean (1999) found the stocks individual investors bought underperformed the stocks they sold over the following year by on the order of 3 percentage points before costs. Men traded about 45 percent more than women in the same sample and reduced net returns by about 0.94 percentage points per year more (Barber and Odean 2001). Taiwanese day trading records show the same direction at large scale (Barber, Lee, Liu and Odean 2009, *Review of Financial Studies* 22(2)).

Footprint: rising turnover without a matching rise in information events, larger position size and higher leverage after winning periods, and options activity concentrated in short dated contracts.

Boundary: turnover is not a pure confidence meter. It also rises with genuine news, lower transaction costs, app design and volatility itself. The zero commission era after 2019 mechanically raised retail turnover. Read turnover only after normalising for cost regime, volatility and news intensity. Grade: robust for the cost of excessive trading, moderate for turnover as a real time sentiment gauge.

### 3. The disposition effect

Sources: Shefrin and Statman (1985), *Journal of Finance* 40(3), who coined the term; Odean (1998), *Journal of Finance* 53(5); Grinblatt and Keloharju (2001), *Journal of Finance* 56(2); Kaustia (2010), *Journal of Financial and Quantitative Analysis* 45(3).

Mechanism: selling a winner closes a mental account in the pleasant gain domain. Holding a loser keeps the account open and preserves the gamble to breakeven, while selling would force the painful loss to be experienced as realised. Regret and pride add force on both sides.

Evidence: Odean (1998), 10,000 accounts from 1987 to 1993, investors realised gains at a rate roughly 50 percent higher than losses, about 0.148 for gains against 0.098 for losses. The effect weakened in December, when tax loss selling gives an offsetting rational motive, which is itself a good check that the pattern is not a mechanical artefact. The winners sold went on to outperform the losers retained by about 3.4 percentage points over the following year. The effect appears in many countries and in housing (Genesove and Mayer 2001, *Quarterly Journal of Economics* 116(4)).

Market consequence: Grinblatt and Han (2005), *Journal of Financial Economics* 78(2), model how aggregate unrealised capital gains predict future returns. When holders sit on losses, supply is withheld and bad news is incorporated slowly, which contributes to momentum. When holders sit on large gains, eager realisation caps advances. The darker implication is for crashes: the same population that rides moderate losses can flip to capitulation once a pain or margin threshold is crossed, producing abnormally low selling on the way down and abnormally high selling at the bottom.

Grade: robust at the aggregate level as behaviour, weak as a day to day index timing tool, because the distribution of unrealised gains across holders is slow moving and hard to observe without holdings data.

### 4. Herding and information cascades

Sources: Bikhchandani, Hirshleifer and Welch (1992), *Journal of Political Economy* 100(5); Banerjee (1992), *Quarterly Journal of Economics* 107(3); Scharfstein and Stein (1990), *American Economic Review* 80(3); Lakonishok, Shleifer and Vishny (1992), *Journal of Financial Economics* 32(1); Chiang and Zheng (2010), *Journal of Banking and Finance* 34(8).

Two mechanisms share one word and must not be confused. In an informational cascade, agents decide in sequence and see predecessors actions but not their private signals. After a few early actions agree, a later agent may rationally follow the crowd against his own signal. Once a cascade starts, private information stops entering the public pool, so the cascade rests on a thin base and is fragile: a small piece of public news can reverse it. No irrationality is required. In reputational herding, a manager who fails conventionally risks less career damage than one who fails unconventionally, which clusters professional holdings even when private views differ. A third pattern looks similar but is not psychology at all: systematic trend following produces correlated buying of what has risen because the rule says so.

Evidence is method dependent. Lakonishok, Shleifer and Vishny (1992) found only modest herding among pension managers by their trade based measure. Return dispersion methods find dispersion compressing in extreme markets in many countries, consistent with herding in stress, but dispersion can also compress when one macro factor dominates returns, which is exactly what happens in crises. That identification problem has never been fully solved and must be stated whenever a dispersion number is shown. Explicit coordination, as in forum organised buying, is not a cascade in the strict sense: participants communicate directly, formation is faster, a narrative is visible, and platform moderation and broker restrictions act as circuit breakers.

Grade: robust as a mechanism, fragile as a single number. Measure the footprints separately: dispersion, flow correlation and positioning correlation, and report disagreement among them.

### 5. Attention-driven buying

Sources: Barber and Odean (2008), *Review of Financial Studies* 21(2); Da, Engelberg and Gao (2011), *Journal of Finance* 66(5); Peng and Xiong (2006), *Journal of Financial Economics* 80(3); Barber, Huang, Odean and Schwarz (2022), *Journal of Finance* 77(6).

Mechanism: buying and selling are asymmetric tasks. A seller can only sell what he holds. A buyer choosing from thousands of stocks faces a search problem, and attention solves it by shrinking the choice set to whatever caught his eye: extreme moves, high volume, headlines, trending lists. Net buying by individuals therefore concentrates in attention grabbing stocks not because they were evaluated as best, but because they entered the consideration set at all. Institutions show the pattern far less.

Evidence: abnormal Google search volume for a ticker predicted higher prices over the following two weeks and partial reversal over the following year in the 2004 to 2008 sample of Da, Engelberg and Gao (2011), stronger for smaller retail held names. On Robinhood data, stocks with extreme herding by platform users earned negative average returns over the following month, on the order of 5 percent in the most extreme events as reported (Barber, Huang, Odean and Schwarz 2022).

The signature sequence, where the theory is working, is: attention spike, retail order imbalance to buy, short horizon price pressure, elevated volume, then decay and partial reversal over days to weeks.

Boundary, and it matters: attention is not sentiment. Attention measures salience, not direction. A stock can dominate attention because holders fear collapse. Direction needs a second variable: tone, order imbalance or options skew. Much failed sentiment engineering comes from treating mention volume as if it were bullishness. Grade: robust for attention predicting retail buying and short term pressure. Not supported for attention alone predicting direction beyond a few weeks.

### 6. Anchoring

Sources: Tversky and Kahneman (1974), *Science* 185(4157); George and Hwang (2004), *Journal of Finance* 59(5); Hirshleifer (2001), *Journal of Finance* 56(4).

Judgements start from a salient number and adjust too little. Markets supply salient numbers for free: purchase price, the 52 week high and low, round numbers, the all time high, an offering price, an analyst target. Nearness to the 52 week high predicts future returns in the direction of continuation in United States data, and in the original sample outperformed standard price momentum measures (George and Hwang 2004). Grade the fact and the story separately: fact moderate to robust in equity cross sections, story contested, because two opposite stories are both told about the same observation.

Anchors are among the most measurable psychological objects in a market, because the candidate anchors are public prices. Distance to the 52 week high, to the all time high, to a volume weighted cost proxy and to round levels can be computed from free price data with no inference about inner states. That measurability is a reason to prefer anchor based variables over mood based variables in an honest engine.

### 7. Recency, extrapolation and the narrative fallacy

Sources: Greenwood and Shleifer (2014), *Review of Financial Studies* 27(3); De Bondt and Thaler (1985), *Journal of Finance* 40(3); Jegadeesh and Titman (1993), *Journal of Finance* 48(1); Bordalo, Gennaioli and Shleifer (2018), *Journal of Finance* 73(1); Taleb (2007), *The Black Swan*, for the popular term.

Survey expectations extrapolate. Greenwood and Shleifer (2014) compare six survey measures of expected returns against the expectations implied by standard models and find survey expectations are strongly positively correlated with recent past returns and price levels, and negatively predict subsequent realised returns at the aggregate level. The crowd expects most after prices are already high, and subsequently earns least. That inverts the naive reading of bullish surveys: at roughly annual horizons, high stated expected returns are a contrary indicator in that dataset, because they encode extrapolation rather than information.

The narrative fallacy is the storytelling layer. Humans compress a price path into a causal story with heroes, villains and a moral, then trade the story. Stories require a protagonist, prefer intention over accident, resist base rates, and once formed they filter new evidence. A narrative also solves a coordination problem: it gives strangers the same reason to buy the same thing at the same time, in portable form. Narratives are transmission infrastructure, not decoration.

Horizon structure is essential. Prices tend to continue at horizons of months (momentum), while extreme past performance tends to reverse at multi year horizons. Survey expectations extrapolate at both horizons, which is why they can negatively predict at the annual horizon while price momentum positively predicts at the monthly horizon. Grade: extrapolation in surveys is robust. The leap to fading every bullish reading tomorrow is not supported. The negative predictability is at aggregate, roughly annual horizons, is modest in explanatory power, and comes with no timing precision.

### 8. FOMO and envy

The academic treatment is thinner here than the popular treatment, and this study says so plainly. Adjacent rigorous work: Hong, Kubik and Stein (2004), *Journal of Finance* 59(1), on social interaction and participation; Kaustia and Knupfer (2012), *Journal of Financial Economics* 104(2), on peer returns raising an individual entry probability in Finnish registry data; Frank (1985), *Choosing the Right Pond*, on relative wealth.

Decompose FOMO instead of treating it as a primitive emotion. First, regret over counterfactual wealth: the pain is not poverty, it is watching a salient peer group become rich through an action that was available to you. It scales with peer salience and similarity, not absolute returns. Second, social proof under uncertainty: when valuation is genuinely hard, observed buying by others is fallibly treated as information. Third, anticipatory regret asymmetry: missing a continued rise is imagined as more blameworthy than joining and losing alongside everyone. Losing with the crowd is narratable as bad luck. Missing out alone is narratable as a personal failure.

That decomposition predicts where FOMO bites hardest: assets with no anchor valuation, visible scoreboards, low friction entry, and a story that makes early participants look clever rather than lucky. It predicts weak FOMO for dull, hard to discuss, institutionally held assets regardless of returns. FOMO buying is late cycle by construction, because the peer gains that trigger it must first accumulate and become visible, and it is reflexive: buying raises prices, enlarges peer gains, and recruits the next cohort. It ends when the supply of new entrants is exhausted or when a sharp drawdown flips envy into relief at not participating, and the same social machinery that built the position can liquidate it quickly.

Grade: peer effects on participation are robust in registry data. The full FOMO loop in speculative episodes is moderate, resting substantially on case evidence. Measure its footprints: new holder growth, small trade share, search growth, cohort entry after peer gains. Do not claim to measure the feeling.

### 9. Panic and capitulation

Sources: Brunnermeier and Pedersen (2009), *Review of Financial Studies* 22(6); Coval and Stafford (2007), *Journal of Financial Economics* 86(2); Khandani and Lo (2011), *Journal of Financial Markets* 14(1); Shleifer and Vishny (2011), *Journal of Economic Perspectives* 25(1); Kindleberger (1978), *Manias, Panics, and Crashes*.

Panic is not simply a large amount of fear. It is a change in the structure of selling, from discretionary to forced and from price sensitive to price insensitive. An initial decline tightens constraints: margin calls, value at risk limits, de-leveraging rules, redemptions, collateral haircuts. Constrained holders must sell regardless of their view of value, and they sell what is liquid, not only what fell. Their selling lowers prices and raises measured volatility, which tightens the same constraints again. Brunnermeier and Pedersen name the two loops: the loss spiral and the margin spiral. Liquidity withdraws at the same moment, so price impact per unit of selling rises precisely when selling volume rises. That convexity is the mechanical heart of a crash, and it requires no irrationality at the moment of the crash itself. The irrationality, if any, was installed earlier, in the leverage and crowding that made the system fragile.

Capitulation is the terminal phase in flow data: the last discretionary holders cross a pain threshold and sell into the forced flow, producing climactic volume. The folk claim that climactic volume marks the bottom contains a kernel of mechanism: once forced and capitulating sellers are exhausted, the marginal seller disappears. It is overstated as a timing rule. Volume climaxes also occur mid decline, forced selling can persist for weeks when redemptions are staggered, and in 2008 new forced sellers were manufactured by the decline itself.

Footprints that help tell forced from discretionary selling: selling concentrated in liquid widely held names rather than the names with the worst news (the fire sale signature), commonality in selling across funds with shared holdings, spreads and depth deteriorating faster than volume alone would explain, correlations spiking toward one, and margin contraction where observable.

Grade: robust as an account of amplification. Not a bottom prediction device. Use panic variables to describe fragility and widen uncertainty bands. Never convert a capitulation reading into a confident buy call.

### 10. How individual psychology becomes a system

**Keynes and higher order beliefs.** Keynes (1936), *The General Theory*, Chapter 12. Contestants in his beauty contest must pick not the faces they find prettiest, nor the faces they think others will find prettiest, but the faces others will think others will think, and so on. Professional investment under career pressure operates at the third degree and beyond: anticipating what average opinion expects average opinion to be. Formal work: Morris and Shin (2002), *American Economic Review* 92(5); Allen, Morris and Shin (2006), *Review of Financial Studies* 19(3). Three consequences follow. Public signals are overweighted, because a public signal moves your belief about everyone else as well as your own belief, so a widely broadcast sentiment reading can move prices partly because it is widely broadcast. Prices can be sticky in the wrong place and jumpy at thresholds. And the forecast target changes: a crowd that is bullish is one object; a crowd that is bullish, believes the crowd will become more bullish, and holds leveraged positions that force buying on strength is a different object entirely.

**Reflexivity.** Soros (1987), *The Alchemy of Finance*; Soros (2008), *The New Paradigm for Financial Markets*. In its philosophical form reflexivity is unverifiable as stated, because any outcome can be redescribed as reflexive after the fact. In its mechanical form it reduces to specific feedback channels, each measurable with a sign and a lag: price to collateral to credit to demand to price; price to flows to mandated buying to price; price to volatility to risk model limits to forced selling to price. Those channels are real and are formalised better by others, including Geanakoplos (2010), *NBER Macroeconomics Annual* 24, on the leverage cycle. Do not build a reflexivity index. Build channel monitors, and label the regime reflexive only where several channels point the same way.

**Minsky.** Minsky (1986), *Stabilizing an Unstable Economy*; Minsky (1992), Levy Economics Institute Working Paper No. 74. Stability is destabilising. Borrowers migrate from hedge finance (cash flow covers interest and principal), to speculative finance (covers interest, must roll principal), to Ponzi finance (covers neither, depends on appreciation or new borrowing). Stability validates risk taking, compresses premia and lending standards, and shifts the population toward the fragile end, not because anyone decides to destabilise the system but because each step is individually rewarded. Kindleberger five stages (displacement, boom, euphoria, profit taking, panic) are the narrative calendar laid over that balance sheet migration. The strongest support is long run credit data: Schularick and Taylor (2012), *American Economic Review* 102(2), using 140 years for 14 advanced economies, find credit growth is a powerful predictor of financial crises, and Jorda, Schularick and Taylor (2015), *Journal of Monetary Economics* 76, find bubbles funded by credit are associated with deeper recessions and slower recoveries than equity bubbles without leverage. The damage variable is leverage attached to the bubble, not overpricing alone. The dot com collapse destroyed large paper wealth with a mild recession. The housing collapse produced a systemic crisis because the overpricing sat on leveraged balance sheets. Minsky role in an engine: a fragility prior at the slowest frequency, not a timing input.

**Shiller and narratives.** Shiller (2017), *American Economic Review* 107(4); Shiller (2019), *Narrative Economics*; Shiller (1984), *Brookings Papers on Economic Activity* 1984(2). Narratives are contagious stories that change what actions feel appropriate. A narrative has a contagion rate and a recovery rate. When contagion exceeds recovery it goes epidemic, and its economic effects scale with prevalence, not truth. Narratives usually travel as constellations: clusters of mutually reinforcing stories that share a moral, which lower the social cost of acting and raise the social cost of abstaining. Timing runs both ways: stories cause price moves, and price moves cause stories, because a large move demands a story and one is supplied within hours. Any regression of price on narrative tone that ignores that simultaneity overstates the predictive content of tone. Keep this separate from the socionomics program associated with Prechter, which infers its central variable from the phenomenon it is meant to explain and is graded unverifiable as stated to fragile. Shiller weaker claim is defensible: specific identifiable stories spread measurably and affect specific decisions. Measure named narratives, not mood in the abstract.

**Baker and Wurgler and sentiment as a priced factor.** Baker and Wurgler (2006), *Journal of Finance* 61(4); Baker and Wurgler (2007), *Journal of Economic Perspectives* 21(2); Baker, Wurgler and Yuan (2012), *Journal of Financial Economics* 104(2). Sentiment here is a propensity to speculate that expresses itself most where valuation is most subjective and arbitrage is most limited. The index is the first principal component of six proxies, each first orthogonalised to macroeconomic conditions: the closed end fund discount, NYSE turnover, the number of IPOs, average first day IPO returns, the equity share in new issues, and the dividend premium. The central result is cross sectional and conditional, and is routinely misquoted as a market timing result. When sentiment is low, subsequent returns are relatively high for small, young, high volatility, unprofitable, non dividend paying, extreme growth and distressed stocks. When sentiment is high, those same categories earn relatively low subsequent returns. Large, old, profitable, dividend paying stocks are comparatively insulated. A replication and extension by Leong, Li, Li, Peng and Xu (2025), *Economic Inquiry*, confirms the pattern in the original 1963 to 2002 sample and in a new 2002 to 2023 United States sample, finds instability in some specifications, and finds failure in China. Grade: moderate to robust in the United States, conditional internationally. Huang, Jiang, Tu and Zhou (2015), *Review of Financial Studies* 28(3), build an aligned index from the same family and report monthly in sample and out of sample R squared around 1.7 percent and 1.2 percent, against near zero for the original index at the aggregate level. Even the improved aggregate predictor explains roughly one percent of monthly return variance. Economically interesting and practically humbling, and both halves must survive into any display.

**Limits to arbitrage and sentiment beta.** Shleifer and Vishny (1997), *Journal of Finance* 52(1); Barberis and Thaler (2003), in *Handbook of the Economics of Finance*; Pontiff (2006), *Journal of Accounting and Economics* 42(1-2). Arbitrage has three frictions, each sufficient alone: fundamental risk, noise trader risk, and agency and funding risk. Most arbitrage capital is other people money, managed under evaluation windows shorter than the life of a mispricing. When mispricing widens, arbitrageurs lose money in the short run, investors withdraw, and liquidation is forced at the worst moment. Performance based arbitrage is therefore least supplied exactly when expected returns to arbitrage are highest. Stocks differ in how much sentiment can move them because they differ in arbitrage cost: idiosyncratic volatility, short sale constraints and borrow cost, low float, low institutional ownership, absence of earnings anchors, and story intensity. The same market level sentiment extreme implies a large expected effect in a small unprofitable high volatility name and a small effect in a mega cap payer. Applying one sentiment dial to every name equally is a theory error, not a simplification.

**Noise trader risk.** De Long, Shleifer, Summers and Waldmann (1990a), *Journal of Political Economy* 98(4); (1990b), *Journal of Finance* 45(2); Black (1986), *Journal of Finance* 41(3). Noise traders trade on beliefs not warranted by fundamental information, and their sentiment is unpredictable. Unpredictability converts their presence from a profit opportunity into a source of risk: an arbitrageur betting against them bears the risk that sentiment becomes more extreme before it corrects, so he limits his position and prices can deviate from fundamentals in equilibrium. The companion paper adds the destabilising rational speculator: if feedback traders buy after rises, a rational speculator who receives good news buys more than fundamental value, anticipating feedback buying tomorrow and intending to sell into it. Riding can be return maximising for a speculator whose horizon is shorter than the bubble life, and the collective result is a larger bubble and a worse break. An extreme therefore does not forecast an immediate reversal. It forecasts elevated variance of the future path, fatter tails in both directions in the near term, and elevated reversal risk at longer horizons. Grading an engine only on directional hit rate would discard the part of the theory most likely to be true.

**Does fear move fast and greed move slow?** The proverb is conditional, not universal. Robust part: large down moves are on average sharper and more volatility generating than large up moves of similar size (Black 1976; Christie 1982; Bekaert and Wu 2000, *Review of Financial Studies* 13(1)). Also true in the other direction: speculative excess in issuance, first day IPO returns and turnover typically builds over quarters, post crisis distrust can persist for years, and in modern episodes greed has been the fast variable, as coordinated retail episodes in January 2021 produced vertical moves in days. Verdict: fear is fast when leverage and forced selling are present, because transmission is mechanical. Fear is slow when it takes the form of distrust and abstention, because abstention has no forcing mechanism. Greed is slow when it must be funded by credit formation and issuance pipelines. Greed is fast when transmitted by social coordination into liquid low friction instruments. Measure the transmission channel present in the episode and let the speed follow from the channel. Do not hard code the proverb.

## Part 2: The empirical record, honestly graded

### Surveys

AAII has polled members weekly since 1987. Investors Intelligence has classified newsletter writers since 1963. Michigan measures household economic sentiment with roots in the 1950s. Yale under Shiller publishes investor confidence and crash confidence series from the late 1980s and 1990s depending on the series.

Core finding: stated beliefs extrapolate the recent past and negatively predict aggregate returns at horizons around a year (Greenwood and Shleifer 2014, above). Explanatory power is a few percent of the variance of subsequent annual returns at most. Brown and Cliff (2004), *Journal of Empirical Finance* 11(1), and (2005), *Journal of Business* 78(2), find sentiment levels strongly related to contemporaneous and past returns, some contrary predictive content at long horizons, and weak near term power once past returns are accounted for. Fisher and Statman (2000), *Financial Analysts Journal* 56(2), find AAII and newsletter aggregates negatively related to subsequent S and P 500 returns in their samples, with the newsletter aggregate stronger. The standing problem: the number of independent extreme episodes in any survey history is in the low dozens at most, so confidence intervals around any extreme zone rule are wide, and rules fitted to famous extremes (October 2008, March 2009, December 2018) risk describing those episodes rather than stating a law.

Grades: survey extrapolation robust. Survey contrary prediction at annual horizons moderate. Weekly AAII extremes as a standalone short term timing signal fragile. Zhou (2018), reviewing to December 2016, reports the Baker and Wurgler index explains about 0.3 percent of monthly market return variance, its equally weighted version 0.006 in R squared terms, and a partial least squares combination 0.019. Real, small, and routinely oversold.

### Put and call ratios and options volume

The strongest options result is not about the aggregate ratio. Pan and Poteshman (2006), *Review of Financial Studies* 19(3), use signed buyer initiated option volume at the individual stock level and find stocks with high put to call ratios from buyer initiated volume underperform over the following week, with a long short spread on the order of 40 basis points per week in their headline specification, concentrated in volume classified as information motivated. Note the sign and the level: at single name level, heavy put buying predicts further declines in that name in the short run, an information pattern, not a contrary bounce. The aggregate contrary story and the single name information story point in opposite directions at short horizons. An engine that merges them into one dial will be wrong in one of its two uses at all times.

For the aggregate index ratio the evidence is practitioner research, exchange studies and newsletter backtests rather than settled peer reviewed consensus. Reported shape: extreme high equity put and call readings cluster around short term lows and are followed by above average returns over the next one to four weeks on average, with enormous dispersion, frequent mid decline extremes that fail, and performance that degrades when thresholds fitted on earlier decades are applied unchanged after the 2020 break (retail call buying permanently raised call volume) and after the daily expiry expansion. See also Simon and Wiggins (2001) and Bandopadhyaya and Jones (2008), both finding some predictive content with regime sensitivity and small magnitudes.

Confounds: put volume mixes speculation, hedging of long portfolios, and structured product and dealer flows whose sentiment sign is ambiguous or reversed. Index put buying by an institution hedging a large book is insurance purchased precisely because the buyer intends to stay long.

Grades: signed single name option volume in the information direction moderate. Aggregate put and call extremes as a contrary indicator over one to four weeks fragile to moderate, usable only as a percentile extreme against its own recent regime. Any fixed rule of the form ratio above 1.0 means buy is debunked as a universal rule by the structural change in the series itself.

### Fund flows and performance chasing

Return chasing is among the most replicated facts in empirical finance. Sirri and Tufano (1998), *Journal of Finance* 53(5), document the convex flow to performance relation: top performing funds receive disproportionate inflows, poor performers suffer muted outflows. Friesen and Sapp (2007), *Journal of Banking and Finance* 31(9), estimate timing decisions cost equity fund investors on the order of 1.5 percent per year relative to buy and hold in their sample. Industry DALBAR analyses report larger gaps in the same direction, with contested methods. The direction is not in serious dispute. The exact annual cost is sample and method dependent and should never be quoted as a constant.

The predictive version is Frazzini and Lamont (2008), *Journal of Financial Economics* 88(2): stocks heavily held by funds experiencing inflows subsequently underperform, with a headline three year value weighted spread between extreme flow quintiles of about minus 0.85 percent per month in the original 1980 to 2003 paper. An independent automated replication matches the in sample spread closely (about minus 0.67 percent per month) and then finds the effect essentially vanishes out of sample in 2004 to 2010 (about minus 0.04 percent per month). A result can be genuinely present in its discovery sample, genuinely replicable by code, and still fail to persist. Grades: performance chasing as behaviour robust. The dumb money cross sectional effect moderate in sample, fragile as a forward expectation at its published size.

What survives the haircut: flow extremes at category level remain a clean measure of where extrapolative demand is concentrated, and flow driven price pressure in underlying holdings is real and mechanical while it lasts (Coval and Stafford 2007; Lou 2012). Ask flows three questions: where is demand concentrated, how much forced trading does it imply, and has the concentration reached historical percentile extremes. Asking only whether prices will reverse gets a fragile answer.

### Issuance and IPOs: the supply side

Firms are better sentiment traders than households. Ritter (1991), *Journal of Finance* 46(1), finds United States IPOs from 1975 to 1984 underperform matched firms by economically large margins over three years, on the order of 29 percentage points cumulative in the headline comparison, concentrated in the heaviest issuance years. Loughran and Ritter (1995), *Journal of Finance* 50(1), extend the result to seasoned offerings. Measurement debates shrink the size, weakest in large firms and value weighted portfolios, but the direction has survived four decades and international samples. Grade: robust in direction, moderate in size.

Baker and Wurgler (2000), *Journal of Finance* 55(5), show the equity share in total new issues negatively predicts aggregate market returns at annual horizons in data back to 1928. First day IPO returns averaged roughly 7 percent in the 1980s, roughly 15 percent in the early 1990s in commonly reported Ritter tabulations, and roughly 65 percent in 1999 to 2000, before falling back. Grade for equity share and IPO heat as slow contrary state variables at annual horizons: moderate. As timing tools for quarters: fragile. Pipelines take months, and hot windows can stay hot far longer than a contrary trader can stay solvent.

### Consumer sentiment

Carroll, Fuhrer and Wilcox (1994), *American Economic Review* 84(5), find sentiment helps forecast consumption growth beyond standard variables. Howrey (2001) finds the Michigan index helps predict recession probability and growth. Barsky and Sims (2012), *American Economic Review* 102(4), decompose confidence innovations and conclude a large share reflects news about future fundamentals rather than pure animal spirits. Much of what consumer sentiment knows is real information about jobs, incomes and inflation arriving through households before official statistics. Lemmon and Portniaguina (2006), *Review of Financial Studies* 19(4), find both the macroeconomic component and a residual sentiment component negatively predict subsequent returns for small stocks and stocks with low institutional ownership, the hard to arbitrage segment. Qiu and Welch (2006) find changes in the Michigan index related to small stock excess returns in their specifications.

Grades: consumer sentiment as macro information moderate to robust. As a cross sectional proxy for small and retail held stocks moderate. As an aggregate stock timing signal fragile, because a high level can mean strong household fundamentals and extrapolative optimism at once, and the two channels fight.

### Media tone

Tetlock (2007), *Journal of Finance* 62(3), builds a pessimism factor from the Wall Street Journal Abreast of the Market column from 1984 to 1999 and finds high pessimism predicts downward pressure on the Dow the following day, followed by reversion over the following days, and extreme pessimism in either direction predicts elevated volume. Magnitudes are small, on the order of a few basis points per day for a one standard deviation move. Tetlock, Saar Tsechansky and Macskassy (2008), *Journal of Finance* 63(3), find the fraction of negative words in firm specific news predicts low earnings and low returns. Loughran and McDonald (2011), *Journal of Finance* 66(1), show general purpose negative word lists badly misclassify financial text and supply the finance specific dictionary that became the field standard. Garcia (2013), *Journal of Finance* 68(3), extends the newspaper approach from 1905 to 2005 and finds return predictability of tone concentrated in recessions, with a one standard deviation pessimism shock associated with a move on the order of 12 basis points in the next day Dow return in recession periods, and much weaker content in expansions.

Grades: moderate to robust for the existence and shape of the effect, fragile for any claim of economically large daily timing profits after costs, contested on whether tone causes sentiment or merely records it. The honest answer is both, in proportions that vary by episode. Tone should be expected to matter most exactly when trading on it is most dangerous.

### Social media mood: the famous study, the fund, and the aftermath

Bollen, Mao and Zeng (2011), *Journal of Computational Science* 2(1), analyse about 9.8 million tweets from 2008 and report that a Calm mood dimension Granger causes Dow changes at lags of several days, with a neural network including mood predicting daily Dow direction with 87.6 percent accuracy in their test window. A London fund, Derwent Capital Markets, launched on the strength of the approach in 2011, raised reported tens of millions, and closed after about a month of trading in 2012. No audited multi year live record demonstrating anything like 87.6 percent directional accuracy ever appeared.

Lachanski and Pav (2017), *Econ Journal Watch* 14(3), reconstruct alternative Calm series, including for November and December 2008 data the original authors collected but did not use, and find the reconstructed measures do not Granger cause the Dow in that held out window. They document specification fragility across lexicon choices, aggregation and lag selection, in a very short 2008 sample containing the crisis, an election and Thanksgiving. Grade: aggregate social mood predicting the index at multi day horizons with high accuracy is debunked in its strong form.

What survives is firm level and smaller. Bartov, Faurel and Mohanram (2018), *The Accounting Review* 93(3), find aggregate opinion in firm specific tweets before earnings predicts earnings and announcement returns. Sprenger, Tumasjan, Sandner and Welpe (2014), *European Financial Management* 20(5), find message volume, sentiment and agreement related to returns, volume and volatility at stock level with small magnitudes. Cookson and Niessner (2020) document that disagreement on social platforms is associated with trading activity and has asset pricing content at firm level. Platform access is a provenance fact: social data access has tightened repeatedly since 2023 and series break without notice, so any gauge built on a platform feed must carry a break flag and a death plan.

### A short honour and dishonour roll

Sunshine and weather: Hirshleifer and Shumway (2003), *Journal of Finance* 58(3), report morning sunshine in the city of a main exchange associated with higher daily returns across 26 exchanges from 1982 to 1997. Later scrutiny raises multiple testing, data handling and out of sample decay concerns. If present, a few basis points at most and untradable after costs. Grade: fragile. A teaching example of how a plausible mechanism plus many cities plus many weather variables can produce a beautiful false positive.

Sports sentiment: Edmans, Garcia and Norli (2007), *Journal of Finance* 62(4), find national team losses in major tournaments followed by negative next day returns in the losing country market on the order of minus 38 basis points in their headline estimate, with no symmetric gain after wins. Grade: moderate to fragile. Real as evidence that mood shocks can move prices, not usable as an engine input for a United States equity book.

The Super Bowl indicator and calendar folklore: data mined coincidences with no mechanism. Grade: debunked as prediction.

Closed end fund discounts: Lee, Shleifer and Thaler (1991), *Journal of Finance* 46(1), argue discounts move with small investor sentiment. Later work complicates the pure sentiment reading with fees, liquidity and tax explanations for parts of the average discount, while comovement and issuance timing remain hard to explain without a sentiment like common factor. Grade: moderate as a barometer, contested as a complete theory of the discount level.

VIX: a price of insurance, not a sentiment survey. It embeds a variance risk premium, so its level mixes expected volatility with the price of insurance (Bollerslev, Tauchen and Zhou 2009). Whaley (2000) popularised the fear gauge label. High VIX levels are followed on average by above average equity returns over subsequent months in United States history, with the small number of independent episodes problem: deep spikes are roughly 1998, 2008 to 2009, 2011, 2015, 2018, 2020 and 2022, a sample of about seven, not seventy. Grade: robust as a measure of the price of insurance and of stress, fragile as a precise bottom timing rule.

Margin debt: rises into the 2000, 2007 and 2021 peaks and contracts sharply in the breaks, a Minsky consistent footprint. As a level it trends with market capitalisation and must be normalised before any percentile language is honest. Grade: moderate as fragility, fragile as timing.

### Sentiment at the great extremes

1929. The Dow peaked on 3 September 1929 at 381.17 and fell 12.8 percent on 28 October and 11.7 percent on 29 October. The honest record is indirect: brokers loans had grown to roughly 8.5 billion dollars by October 1929, investment trusts traded at large premiums to net asset value, and newspaper archives show the new era narrative constellation in full epidemic form. White (1990), *Journal of Economic Perspectives* 4(2), remains the balanced account. Mechanisms visible: Minsky migration funded by broker loans, extrapolation, narrative epidemic, then forced liquidation. Lesson: the leverage and premium series are the transportable gauges. There is no AAII survey for 1929 (it begins in 1987), no published VIX (the index begins in 1990 in an earlier construction), and no modern put and call volume. Pretending otherwise would be fabrication with footnotes.

1987. On 19 October 1987 the Dow fell 22.6 percent in one session, still its largest one day percentage decline. The Brady Commission report (1988) assigns a central amplifying role to portfolio insurance, a rules based strategy that sold index futures mechanically as prices fell, interacting with illiquidity and futures and cash dislocation. Shiller (1987) survey evidence collected immediately after found investors cited the decline itself and anxiety about further decline, rather than any specific news, as their reason for selling. Lesson: the most dangerous seller is not the frightened human but the rule that must sell.

2000. The Nasdaq Composite peaked on 10 March 2000 at 5048.62. The Shiller cyclically adjusted price to earnings ratio peaked at about 44 in December 1999, the highest in its history (Shiller online data, revised as earnings revise). Average first day IPO returns reached roughly 65 percent in 1999 to 2000. Equity fund inflows set records in early 2000, peaking almost exactly with prices. Margin debt peaked in March 2000. The Nasdaq then fell about 78 percent peak to trough by October 2002, while the economy had only a mild recession. Lesson: when valuation, issuance, flows and leverage extremes coincide, the joint extreme carries information no single series extreme carries. Coincidence counting across independent families is a design principle, not a decoration.

2008. Two phases a single dial would merge disastrously. In the build from 2003 to 2007 the operative sentiment was complacency: spreads compressed, covenant quality deteriorated, housing narrative prevalence was epidemic, measured volatility was low while fragility accumulated. In the break, mechanics were forced: the VIX closed at 80.86 on 20 November 2008, AAII bearishness reached the mid 50 percent range at its crisis extremes, and fire sale comovement dominated name specific news. The market bottomed on 9 March 2009, months after the worst readings. The extreme zone was entered in October 2008 and the market fell for roughly five more months, losing roughly a further quarter of its value after its first VIX extreme. Lesson: extremes mark regimes, not dates.

2020. From its 19 February 2020 closing high the S and P 500 fell about 34 percent to its 23 March 2020 close, the fastest decline of that depth from a peak in its history. The VIX closed at 82.69 on 16 March 2020, exceeding the 2008 closing record under the same modern methodology. Baker, Bloom, Davis and Terry (2020) document that no previous infectious disease outbreak, including 1918 influenza, produced a remotely comparable United States market reaction. Lesson: cycle speed is regime dependent. Calibration windows must include 2020 or every speed parameter will be biased slow, and bands computed without 2020 will call ordinary stress unprecedented.

2021. GameStop, with short interest above 100 percent of float in reported figures of the era, rose from under 20 dollars in early January 2021 to an intraday high of 483 dollars on 28 January 2021, pre split prices, before broker restrictions on opening purchases, imposed under clearing collateral pressure, broke the move. The SEC staff report on the episode (2021) documents social media attention, retail order flow, short covering and options market maker hedging, a gamma feedback loop in which call buying forced dealer buying of the underlying. The broader 2021 complex showed record retail call buying, SPAC issuance at multiples of all prior history, elevated IPO first day returns and nominal margin records at once. The deflation of 2022 then showed speculative high sentiment beta segments falling far more than the broad index, the Baker and Wurgler cross sectional prediction operating live. Lesson: the fastest modern transmission runs through options positioning and social coordination, both capable of completing a full cycle inside a month. An engine sampled monthly would have missed the entire episode. Sampling frequency is a theory choice.

Summary across six episodes spanning 92 years: the constants are leverage or mechanical feedback on the way down, issuance and flow extremes near tops, narrative epidemics as the transmission story, and the failure of any single gauge extreme to date the turn. The variables are speed and the lead series. That is why a calibrated engine is a panel of family gauges with coincidence and fragility logic on top, not a single fear and greed dial.

### Grade table, in one place

* Performance chasing in flows: robust. Flow based return prediction at published sizes: fragile forward, moderate in sample.
* Prospect theory mechanisms and the disposition effect: robust as behaviour, weak as standalone index timing.
* Attention predicting retail buying and short term pressure: robust. Attention alone predicting direction: not supported.
* Survey extrapolation: robust. Survey contrary prediction at annual horizons: moderate. Weekly survey extreme timing: fragile.
* Baker and Wurgler cross section: moderate to robust, United States, conditional. Best aggregate sentiment timing: about 1 percent of monthly return variance. Treat larger claims as unproven.
* Issuance and IPO long run underperformance: robust in direction. Equity share as slow contrary state: moderate.
* Consumer sentiment for macro information: moderate to robust. For the small stock cross section: moderate. For aggregate timing: fragile.
* Media tone pressure and reversal at daily horizons: moderate to robust in shape, small in size, recession amplified. Causal direction: contested, both ways.
* Firm specific social media information: moderate. Aggregate Twitter mood prediction in its strong form: debunked.
* Weather: fragile. Sports loss effect: moderate to fragile. Super Bowl indicator: debunked.
* VIX as stress price: robust. VIX thresholds as bottom dates: fragile.
* Noise trader risk, limits to arbitrage, Minsky credit fragility and panic amplification: robust as mechanisms. None of them is a timing device.

## Part 3: The map of everything measurable

A tour by family. Every channel is tagged by what it actually measures, because confusing the three types is the most common error in sentiment work.

* **Stated belief (SB):** what people say they think. Surveys, posts that declare a view.
* **Revealed positioning (RP):** what people have actually done with money. Flows, positioning, options prints, margin, shorts.
* **Attention (AT):** what people are looking at, regardless of direction. Search, news volume, social volume.

Attention is not sentiment. Positioning is not belief. A channel excellent at one is often silent on the others. Archive depths below are marked VERIFIED where the measurement study checked them against the source or its documentation in the 2026-10-30 research pass, and UNVERIFIED where they rest on publisher claims or secondary sources with no download checked. An unverified depth is a lead, not a fact. Cost classes: FREE, KEYED FREE (a free key or a key the desk already holds: Alpaca, TastyTrade read only, Coinalyze), PAID (named so the gap is explicit, never silently filled with a substitute that measures something else).

### Family (a): Surveys and stated sentiment

* **AAII Sentiment Survey.** Weekly, published Thursday morning for the Wednesday close of voting. FREE spreadsheet. Archive July 1987, VERIFIED. SB. Self selected AAII members stating a six month outlook. Small samples: AAII itself reported an average of about 315 respondents per week in early 2013. Long run published means are near 38.8 percent bullish, 30.5 percent neutral, 30.6 percent bearish, so a 50 percent bullish print is extreme even though it sounds moderate.
* **AAII Asset Allocation Survey.** Monthly. FREE summary, fuller data member gated. Archive November 1987, reported, UNVERIFIED by download. Self reported stocks, bonds and cash shares.
* **Investors Intelligence Advisors Sentiment.** Weekly survey of newsletter writers. PAID. Archive 1963, publisher reported, UNVERIFIED by download because it is paywalled. SB. A professional opinion sample with a very long run, locked behind a subscription. Named as a gap in any free engine.
* **NAAIM Exposure Index.** Weekly file published Thursday for Wednesday exposure. FREE. Archive July 2006, VERIFIED. Responses range from minus 200 percent (leveraged short) to plus 200 percent (leveraged long). Mean, median and dispersion published. The closest survey to positioning, because respondents report exposure they claim to hold. Structurally bullish sample, historical median near 72 percent long, so levels need percentile context, never raw thresholds.
* **University of Michigan Surveys of Consumers.** Monthly, preliminary mid month and final end of month, FREE via FRED (series UMCSENT, with UMCSENT1 for the early quarterly portion). Note FRED carries Michigan with a one month delay at the source request; the Surveys site publishes sooner. Archive November 1952, VERIFIED via FRED. Quarterly until 1977, monthly from January 1978. Index base 1966 Q1 equals 100. SB. Household mood about finances and business conditions, not about stocks directly. The longest honest mood series in the catalog. Revised after first release, so preliminary and final are separate vintages.
* **Conference Board Consumer Confidence.** Monthly press release FREE, full history and components licensed. Archive 1967, publisher reported, UNVERIFIED by download. A methodology and sample frame revision in 2024 was announced by the publisher and must be treated as a series break until documented otherwise. Its jobs plentiful versus hard to get component is its most market relevant part.
* **Bank of America Global Fund Manager Survey.** Monthly, PAID, with FREE press fragments. Archive 2001, publisher reported, UNVERIFIED, no public archive. Cash levels and the most crowded trade question are its famous contrarian exhibits. Verdict: monitor press fragments with provenance. Never build a stored series from fragments and call it the survey.
* **Baker and Wurgler investor sentiment index.** Monthly spreadsheet on Jeffrey Wurgler NYU Stern faculty page. FREE. Archive monthly from July 1965, VERIFIED as published span. A composite of issuance and trading behaviour, not a survey. Vintage trap: the index is recomputed when its components revise, so a value downloaded today is not necessarily the value published at the time. Store the download date and file hash with every pull.
* **CNN Fear and Greed Index.** Daily display composite of seven inputs (momentum, strength, breadth, put and call, junk bond demand, volatility, safe haven demand). FREE to view, no official historical download. Archive UNVERIFIED; third party scraped histories exist with unverifiable provenance. Verdict: context only, never a stored gauge of record. It is built from inputs a serious engine already counts separately, so counting it again would be double voting.
* **Also named:** State Street Investor Confidence Index (monthly, from institutional custodial transaction data, headline FREE and depth PAID, 1998 reported), Duke CFO Survey (quarterly), and Yale Stock Market Confidence Indices (monthly, some series from 1989 reported), including Yale crash confidence, the subjective probability of a crash, which is conceptually unique and almost never used in desk composites. All UNVERIFIED by download in the measurement study.

Evidence for the family is in Part 2. One line version: surveys are calibration, regime and extreme zone instruments. The published record does not support them as standalone timing signals. Traps: self selection, method breaks (AAII mail to online in 2000; Michigan mode and sampling changes), revision and vintage, small samples presented as national mood, and structural bias that makes raw thresholds meaningless.

### Family (b): Positioning and flows

* **CFTC Commitments of Traders, Legacy report.** FREE compressed annual files. Weekly: positions as of Tuesday, published Friday 15:30 ET. Futures only from 1986, VERIFIED. Before 30 September 1992 only mid month and month end observations exist, and the CFTC warns the pre 1992 mid month data may contain uncorrectable errors. RP. Large trader positioning in index futures (S and P 500, Nasdaq 100, Dow) plus gold, oil, rates and VIX futures. The deepest true positioning archive in the catalog.
* **CFTC Disaggregated and Traders in Financial Futures (TFF).** FREE, same clock. Published from 2006; complete annual files listed from September 2009, VERIFIED as stated on the CFTC page, so 2006 to 2009 coverage must be checked file by file before use. RP. TFF splits financial futures positioning into dealer, asset manager, leveraged fund and other: the cleanest public view of leveraged fund crowding in index futures.
* **Mutual fund and ETF flows.** Investment Company Institute weekly estimates and monthly long term flows, FREE aggregates; EPFR and Lipper detail are PAID. Weekly estimates about one week lag, monthly finals weeks lag. ICI weekly series from 2007 reported, monthly history much longer in fact books, both UNVERIFIED by download. RP. Weekly estimates are revised in monthly finals, so store both vintages.
* **FINRA margin statistics.** FREE monthly Excel, published in the third week of the following month. Archive January 1997, VERIFIED. Before February 2010 NYSE and FINRA collected separately; from February 2010 one collection under FINRA Rule 4521, a marked break. RP leverage. Level is meaningless without scaling by market capitalisation or by free credit. The scaled series and its rate of change are the gauge.
* **FINRA short interest.** FREE, API keyed free. Twice monthly, settlement dates mid month and month end, published on the seventh business day after settlement, so a mid month position read at month end is two weeks old. Online grid and API cover five rolling years, VERIFIED via FINRA documentation; longer downloadable files exist but their start was not verified. RP position stock, not a flow. Days to cover and short interest as a share of float are the usable forms.
* **FINRA daily short sale volume.** FREE daily files, published after the close for that day. Daily files are published back to 2009 in widely cited FINRA documentation, start UNVERIFIED by download and must be confirmed on the file index before any backfill claim. RP flow, gross. It includes market maker and hedging shorts, so a high share is pressure, not bearish conviction. Confusing short interest (a stock) with short volume (a flow) is the single most common error in retail sentiment content.
* **FINRA ATS weekly transparency.** FREE, weekly with a multi week lag. ATS reporting began in 2014, reported, UNVERIFIED by download. RP and AT. Where volume actually executed off exchange.
* **Insider transactions, SEC Form 4.** FREE via EDGAR. Filings due within two business days of the trade. Electronic Form 4 mandatory from June 2003; earlier filings exist incompletely, depth before 2003 UNVERIFIED for completeness. RP. Buys carry more signal than sells, because sales are routine for liquidity and diversification. Only open market codes P and S count for the classic signal; derivative exercises misread as conviction are a standard trap.
* **Form 13F institutional holdings.** FREE via EDGAR, quarterly, filed up to 45 days after quarter end. Practically usable from the late 1990s, completeness UNVERIFIED. RP, lagged and long only. Useful for crowding overlap, useless for timing. A June 30 holding is not an October fact.
* **Named gap:** prime brokerage positioning summaries and hedge fund exposure estimates from bank research are PAID, with no public archive. Free substitutes (TFF leveraged funds, 13F overlap) measure neighbouring things and must be labelled substitutes, not equivalents.

Evidence: COT categories predict returns in some commodity markets and weakly in financial futures, and COT measures are themselves predicted by past returns, which caps their independent content (Wang 2001; Zhou 2018). Treat COT as a crowding and forced flow map first and a return signal second. Flows chase returns contemporaneously (Warther 1995; Edelen and Warner 2001). Heavily shorted stocks underperform, concentrated in small low institutional ownership names, with squeeze episodes cutting the other way (Asquith, Pathak and Ritter 2005). Insider purchase intensity predicts positive abnormal returns, strongest in small firms (Lakonishok and Lee 2001; Jeng, Metrick and Zeckhauser 2003).

### Family (c): Options implied psychology

* **Put and call ratios.** Cboe daily statistics and historical files FREE; also computable from Alpaca OPRA prints for index ETFs, KEYED FREE. Daily after close. Cboe historical files commonly cited from 2003 (total) and 2006 (revised equity series), start UNVERIFIED by download. RP. Index put and call is dominated by hedging; equity put and call is closer to directional speculation. Volume based and open interest based ratios measure different things and must never be averaged together.
* **Implied volatility level and term structure.** VIX, VIX9D, VIX3M, VVIX from Cboe history files FREE; estate routes KEYED FREE. VIX daily closes from 1990, VERIFIED (close only from 1990, OHLC from 1992; methodology changed 22 September 2003 from the S and P 100 based old VIX, continued as VXO with history from 1986 until its 2021 discontinuation; VIX futures from 2004). RP priced: the price of insurance. It conflates expected volatility, risk premium and hedging demand, and honest use says so.
* **Cboe SKEW index.** FREE daily history file. From 1990 as reported by Cboe, UNVERIFIED by download. RP priced. The price of far out of the money SPX puts relative to a lognormal benchmark: tail insurance demand. Evidence for incremental return prediction is weak; its honest role is pricing tail demand.
* **Dealer gamma exposure, walls and flip.** Modelled, from Alpaca open interest and greeks in the estate Morgue Map, KEYED FREE; vendor equivalents are PAID. Daily before the open from overnight open interest. Estate record is short and starts at estate collection. Any long gamma history computed later is a RECONSTRUCTION and must be labelled one, because free expired contract archives are incomplete. RP modelled. An amplifier map: it says how moves may be damped or amplified, not which way the crowd leans. Academic base is thin (Garleanu, Pedersen and Poteshman 2009); practitioner claims of wall magnetism exceed the published evidence.
* **Open interest change and concentration.** Alpaca carries open interest, KEYED FREE, daily, from estate collection start. Note the estate holds two chain routes with different fields: TastyTrade nested chain snapshots carry no open interest, no volume and no implied volatility, and the two routes must never be conflated.
* **Unusual options flow.** Estate Morgue Tape from Alpaca OPRA for SPY, QQQ and IWM, KEYED FREE, intraday, from estate collection, thin and capped. Vendor depth is PAID. A large share of unusual prints are hedges, spreads or closes. A veto discipline is part of the measure, not an optional filter.
* **Volatility risk premium.** Computed from Cboe and price history, FREE inputs, daily, as deep as VIX (1990) in index level form as a RECONSTRUCTION. Whether insurance buyers overpay on average, and by how much now versus history: the fear tax rate.

### Family (d): Price derived mood

* **Breadth.** Percent above 50 and 200 day averages, advance decline line and ratios, McClellan oscillator and summation, computed from daily bars, KEYED FREE via Alpaca. Daily after close. Depth is panel depth: the estate shipped panel held 1,833 names and 399 sessions to 2026-10-23 at census. Any breadth history before a survivorship aware panel is a RECONSTRUCTION, and a breadth series computed on today members flatters history. Published exchange advance decline data reaches the 1930s in vendor form, PAID depth. RP: participation, whether a move is broad or narrow.
* **Breadth thrusts.** Zweig form: the 10 day advance share moving from below 40 percent to above 61.5 percent. Same panel dependence. Published event lists reach back decades as RECONSTRUCTIONS. Roughly two dozen Zweig thrusts since the 1940s in most counts, varying by data source. The published forward record is striking and the sample is tiny, so every thrust statistic carries a small sample warning by construction. A rare event flag, never a probability.
* **New highs minus new lows, percent at 52 week highs.** Computed from daily bars, KEYED FREE, panel dependent. Leadership expansion or contraction at the extremes of the cross section.
* **Volatility complex beyond VIX.** VIX futures curve (front versus third month), VVIX, realised volatility. Cboe futures settlement files FREE, computed measures KEYED FREE. VIX futures from 2004, VERIFIED. VVIX history from 2007 reported, UNVERIFIED. Curve inversion duration is a cleaner regime clock than any single day spike.
* **Credit spreads.** ICE Bank of America high yield and investment grade OAS and the Moody Baa minus Aaa spread, via FRED, FREE (keyed free for the API). Daily, one to two days lag, revised, so store vintage dates. High yield OAS on FRED from December 1996 as published on the series page, treated as VERIFIED for that start; the long Moody spread reaches 1919 in FRED long series, reported. RP priced: the bond market price of fear, and the fastest honest cross asset check on equities. Credit usually confirms or denies an equity fear day within the same week. For equity timing, spreads are coincident to slightly leading and most valuable as a veto.
* **Safe haven and risk ratios.** Gold versus equities, Treasury versus equities, yen and franc strength, copper versus gold, small versus large, discretionary versus staples, computed from free daily prices. ETF based ratios reach only to each fund inception (GLD 2004, TLT 2002, for example); index and futures versions reach deeper as RECONSTRUCTIONS. State the instrument with every ratio. Evidence is regime folklore grade individually, useful as a panel.
* **Display composites** such as CNN style breadth and strength composites and the estate Regime Dial are a display layer, not a source. An engine should feed the dial, never cite it as evidence, and never count a composite and its own inputs as two votes.

### Family (e): Attention and narrative

* **Google Trends.** FREE, rate limited and sampled. Daily values with about one to two days lag; weekly for long pulls. Data begins January 2004 as published by Google. The FEARS published sample runs 2004 to 2011 (Da, Engelberg and Gao 2015). AT, with a fear subset that behaves like stated belief. Central trap: Trends values are relative indices from 0 to 100 within the pull, rescaled per request, so two pulls are not automatically comparable. Collection revisions in 2011, 2016 and 2022 are breaks in any stitched series.
* **Wikipedia pageviews.** FREE API and dumps. Daily, about one day lag. API from July 2015, VERIFIED as the API stated start; earlier dump based counts from December 2007 exist with different methodology. AT. Curiosity and alarm directed at a name or concept. Bot and vandalism spikes are contaminants.
* **GDELT.** FREE files, DOC 2.0 API for a rolling recent window, and BigQuery free tier. Updates every 15 minutes in GDELT 2.0. Events coded archive from 1 January 1979, VERIFIED via GDELT documentation. GKG 1.0 themes from 2013; GKG 2.1 tone rich fields from February 2015, as documented by the project. Pre 2013 tone does not exist in GKG form and must never be implied. AT plus machine tone. Tone is a word count product, not reading comprehension, and the 1979 archive and the modern firehose are not the same newspaper universe, so tone comparisons across decades compare coverage systems as much as mood.
* **Headline and article language.** Loughran and McDonald dictionaries FREE from the authors. Newswire archives PAID (Factiva, RavenPack). Tetlock (2007) sample is 1995 to 1999 Wall Street Journal text: the effect is published, a free long archive of equivalent text is not available. Estate history starts at its own collection start. AT, and stated belief of journalists. Media pessimism is distinct from household mood and often leads it by days.
* **Earnings call language.** Transcripts partial and delayed on free tiers; full archives PAID. Company filings carry prepared remarks and some Q and A only irregularly. No honest free long archive; any claimed depth UNVERIFIED. Stated belief of executives. Access, not method, is the constraint.
* **Analyst revision tone and breadth.** I/B/E/S and Zacks are PAID; free portal fragments are partial and present biased. No free archive of record. Named gap. Do not simulate it from headline counts.
* **Economic Policy Uncertainty index and World Uncertainty Index.** FREE published spreadsheets from policyuncertainty.com. Monthly, about one month lag; daily variants for some countries. United States monthly EPU from 1985 as published, with a newspaper based historical component reaching 1900 in the authors construction, treated as reported. AT: how much of the news hole is occupied by policy uncertainty talk. A narrative occupancy gauge, not a fear gauge.
* **Fed communications language.** FREE from the Federal Reserve. Statements archive from 1994; communications before the statement era are different objects. Event driven. The usable unit is tone shift between consecutive statements.

Evidence for the family is in Part 2 (Tetlock, Garcia, Loughran and McDonald, Da, Engelberg and Gao on both search attention and FEARS). One update worth carrying: a 2026 thesis revisiting FEARS on 2020 to 2024 data finds the reversal pattern unstable across subperiods while the sentiment to volatility link persists. The honest reading is that search fear is a live mood measure whose return mapping is regime dependent, which is an argument for grading it on a forward ledger rather than importing 2015 coefficients.

### Family (f): Social pulse

* **X (formerly Twitter).** PAID for any serious read volume since 2023; academic access discontinued in 2023. No free archive of record. Any dataset offered free with claimed depth to 2006 is UNVERIFIED provenance. The fastest narrative surface and the most manipulated one. Poster population is not investor population.
* **Reddit, including WallStreetBets.** Official API KEYED FREE live, with rate limits. Historical Pushshift dumps are restricted since 2023 and availability is unstable. Honest depth is live collection from start date only; Pushshift based research datasets reaching 2005 are contested on redistribution and completeness, so any pre collection history is UNVERIFIED. SB and AT, with brigading, irony and coordinated posting as first class contaminants.
* **StockTwits.** KEYED FREE developer access, real time. Stream from collection start; limited historical pulls, and the labelled dataset used in research typically starts around 2010 in vendor form, free archive depth UNVERIFIED. The only social channel where posters label their own direction, which makes it the cleanest social stated belief source and still a self selected one.
* **YouTube finance.** Data API v3, KEYED FREE quota. Hours lag. Search and statistics from collection start; platform data reaches 2005 in principle, API returned history for older videos is partial. AT primarily: a slow attention gauge for speculative appetite, with thin published evidence.
* **TikTok and short video finance.** Restricted FREE for approved researchers, otherwise PAID. No public archive. The youngest retail cohort first touch. Verdict: named, monitored qualitatively, excluded from stored gauges on access grounds.
* **Discord and Telegram public groups.** Much of it is not lawfully or stably collectable under platform terms. No archive of record. Verdict: do not build on access that violates terms or evaporates on enforcement. Note the habitat exists, and that its absence biases any social composite toward public, older platforms.
* **Seeking Alpha and blog commentary.** FREE tier fragments. No archive of record for free use. Chen, De, Hu and Hwang (2014) find Seeking Alpha opinion tone predicts returns and earnings surprises, the strongest published social text result, on a platform whose free access has narrowed since.

Durable social findings: message volume predicts volatility and disagreement predicts volume, while the directional return effect of bullishness is statistically present and economically tiny (Antweiler and Frank 2004, on 1.5 million message board posts). Disagreement is a measurable persistent object distinct from analyst disagreement (Cookson and Niessner 2020). Traps: bot contamination that spikes exactly during manipulation events, survivorship in cleaned archives, demographic drift (the 2018 poster population is not the 2026 one, so bands calibrated on one misread the other), irony defeating dictionary tone, and platform API regime changes destroying continuity.

### Family (g): Belief markets

* **Polymarket.** Public Gamma and CLOB APIs, FREE, no key for reads. Real time. Platform launched 2020; per market history from market creation, VERIFIED in structure. Money at stake. Liquidity ranges from deep (elections) to trivial (many macro markets), and a price in a thin market is a quote, not a consensus.
* **Kalshi.** API, KEYED FREE account key. Real time. Exchange launched publicly in 2021; series history from each series start. The cleanest United States macro belief prices, with published contract specifications. Position limits and participant mix differ from offshore venues, which is information, not a defect.
* **PredictIt.** Legacy political markets, wound down after regulatory action. Historical data survives in academic mirrors, 2014 to its wind down era, completeness UNVERIFIED. Included as a cautionary archive: belief market history survives platform death only if someone stored it. Store prices daily from day one for any belief market watched.
* **Metaculus and Manifold.** Public APIs and exports, FREE. Metaculus from 2015 and Manifold from 2022, as platform reported. Scored forecaster aggregates (Manifold is play money). The only belief channel that grades its own participants. Question selection is the bias: they forecast what someone chose to ask.
* **Fed funds futures as the benchmark.** CME delayed FREE summaries; full data PAID. Exchange records from 1988, free depth UNVERIFIED. Any Kalshi or Polymarket Fed price should be displayed against the futures implied probability, never alone.

Evidence: prediction market prices are hard to beat with polls in liquid markets (Wolfers and Zitzewitz 2004, *Journal of Economic Perspectives* 18(2); Berg, Nelson and Rietz 2008 on the Iowa Electronic Markets), with documented failure modes in thin markets, in long dated contracts (favourite longshot bias), and where hedging or expressive betting dominates. For macro series the Kalshi research literature is young. The honest claim is calibration potential, not proven edge. The value to an engine is a daily money weighted probability for the exact events that drive narrative regime changes, in a form a forecast ledger can grade natively.

These are prices of events, not probabilities of events. Prices carry hedging and expressive demand and a premium. Displaying them as pure probabilities without that caveat misinforms the reader.

### Family (h): Crypto psychology

* **Crypto Fear and Greed Index (Alternative.me).** FREE public API returning full history as JSON. Daily from 1 February 2018, VERIFIED. The published recipe is volatility 25 percent, momentum and volume 25 percent, social media 15 percent, surveys 15 percent with that component reported paused in later years, Bitcoin dominance 10 percent, Google Trends 10 percent. A single number retail gauge whose value is partly that the crowd watches it: a reflexive gauge. It has no peer reviewed predictive record comparable to the equity survey literature, and practitioner work finds it largely coincident with price.

Coinalyze aggregated depth UNVERIFIED by download. Desk law, recorded because it has bitten before: the Coinalyze auth header must be named exactly `api_key` and sent via curl or `http.client`, never `urllib`, which capitalises header names and gets a valid key rejected. * **Crypto open interest and liquidation prints.** Exchange APIs and aggregator free tiers, real time to hourly. Liquidation history is the shallowest and most revision prone series in this family, and exchange reported liquidations undercount by design on some venues. Treat prints as floors.
* **Stablecoin supply and flows.** Issuer attestations, on chain data via free public nodes and explorers; cleaned vendor panels PAID. * **Exchange balances and wallet flows.** On chain computation; Glassnode and CryptoQuant are PAID panels with FREE headline metrics. On chain from genesis in principle (2009 for Bitcoin). * **CME crypto futures positioning.** CFTC COT, FREE, weekly. Bitcoin futures COT from the December 2017 contract launch. RP. The regulated institutional slice, directly comparable in form to the equity index COT series.

The academic base is young. Published work on funding rates documents strong mean reversion and a role as priced carry with crash risk. The honest roles for crypto psychology in an equity centred engine are two: crypto exposures exist in the wider book, and crypto trades through United States nights and weekends, making its funding and fear gauges the only live mood instruments when equity markets are closed. That second role needs no predictive claim to justify collection.

### Cross family traps, applied to the whole catalog

1. **Vintage versus revised values.** FRED series, ICI flows, margin statistics and the Baker and Wurgler index all revise. Keep first published and latest values separately, with retrieval dates, or every backtest quietly uses information nobody had.
2. **As of clock discipline.** A Tuesday COT position published Friday, a Michigan final print about a month already ended, a 13F holding from last quarter: each enters a daily state with its true as of date, never its download date.
3. **Percentile honesty.** A band is only as honest as its window. Bands computed on a backfilled reconstruction must say so, and bands must never be computed on a window that includes the future relative to the reading being banded.
4. **Independence accounting.** Channels that share inputs cast one vote, not two.
5. **The paid wall is a fact, not an embarrassment.** Investors Intelligence depth, the Bank of America Fund Manager Survey, EPFR, Lipper detail, RavenPack tone, full transcript archives and the X firehose are PAID. Name them as gaps and build only on what can be stored and proven.

## Part 4: Predicting what the crowd will think next

Three kinds of statement must never be blurred, because most sentiment products blur exactly these lines.

* **Measurement** is a statement about now: the current percentile of a gauge, the current positioning, the current narrative prevalence. It is not a forecast and must never be scored as one or displayed in forecast language.
* **Extrapolation** is a statement about soon that assumes persistence or mechanical continuation: flows continue because mandates and habits continue them, a narrative keeps spreading because its contagion rate currently exceeds its recovery rate, forced selling continues while constraints keep binding. It is a real forecast, conditional and usually short horizon, and its skill comes from mechanics.
* **Genuine prediction** is a statement about later that requires forecasting a change in belief itself: the crowd will become fearful, the narrative will saturate and turn, positioning will unwind. This is the claim class with the thinnest evidence in the entire literature. It may be issued only into a live ledger, only with base rates attached, and only under proper scoring.

### The best ideas from the brainstorm

Each idea below states its mechanism, the data it needs, whether that data is obtainable free today, and its grade from the measurement study. Grades: v1 means buildable for a first working engine from free data obtainable today. LATER means data is obtainable free today but build, validation or accrual time puts it after v1. SPECULATIVE means a real mechanism whose data or method is not yet obtainable to audit standard for free. It is named so it is not forgotten, and never smuggled into v1 under a friendlier label.

**1. Positioning convexity: forecast the forced, not the feelings. Grade: LATER as a computed index, v1 as a qualitative panel.**
The single most forecastable element in crowd dynamics is not what people will think. It is what some holders will be forced to do at specified prices, because forcing functions are written down in margin agreements, risk rules, option contracts and redemption terms. Given a positioning map (COT leveraged fund nets, margin debt scaled, funding rates, gamma sign and size, short interest and days to cover, wall geometry), estimate the forced flow released by a 1, 2 and 5 percent adverse move: margin calls, value at risk cuts, dealer hedging in negative gamma, short covering, crypto liquidation cascades. Two identical sentiment levels are different risks at different convexity. Nobody publishes this as a single daily number for free, and the estate unusually owns most of the inputs. The elasticities must be published with any such index or the number is theatre. Related formal pieces: dealer gamma hedging demand (Ni, Pan and Poteshman 2008; Barbon and Buraschi 2021 on dealer gamma and intraday patterns), volatility targeting and leveraged fund rebalancing arithmetic (Brunnermeier and Pedersen 2009; Cheng and Madhavan 2009), and short interest as two forecasts in a trench coat, informed bearish positioning (Desai, Ramesh, Thiagarajan and Balachandran 2002) versus squeeze convexity, with borrow cost, recall risk, float and catalyst proximity deciding which dominates. Report both channels with their conditioning variables, never a net score.

**2. Narrative velocity and saturation. Grade: LATER.**
If narratives spread epidemically, their dynamics have a forecastable shape. In susceptible, infected, recovered logic, prevalence growth is proportional to current prevalence times the remaining susceptible share. Early growth is exponential and easy to extrapolate. Late growth decelerates as the susceptible pool is exhausted. Buying is done by new adopters, not by the already convinced, so the price relevant variable is the flow of new adopters. Central claim, stated so it can die: price pressure from a narrative peaks near the point of maximum growth in prevalence, which precedes peak prevalence, which precedes peak price whenever valuation overshoot requires continued new buying. The dot com and housing constellations fit this shape in archives; meme episodes fit it at very high frequency, with mention growth peaking days before price in the best documented cases, though published lead and lag evidence is thinner and more episode specific than the theory. Grade for the claim itself: plausible mechanism, moderate direct evidence, carried into the propositions rather than asserted. Saturation signatures beyond raw counts: marginal new content becomes derivative, audience composition shifts from specialists to generalists, search phrasing shifts from discovery to confirmation and price checking, and counter narratives achieve comparable velocity. Pre register the narrative definitions, the corpora and the checklist, freeze them before grading, and treat any later amendment as a new separately graded series. Data: GDELT themes and tone, Trends, Reddit and StockTwits mentions per topic, all obtainable free today with social history from collection start.

**3. Attention to flow lead times. Grade: LATER, with the collection in v1.**
Attention precedes flow by a measurable lag that varies by channel: search attention leads retail flow by days (Da, Engelberg and Gao 2011 show pressure over the following two weeks), social spikes lead options prints by hours to a day in targeted names, and fund flow follows news tone by weeks. Estimate the lag structure per channel on your own accrued data, then an engine can say not only that attention spiked but when its flow echo is due, and can check whether the echo arrived. An attention spike with no flow echo is distribution into interest, a different fingerprint. This is extrapolation grade forecasting: the interest is already measured, and the forecast is that measured interest converts to flow at a historically estimable rate. Among the best supported short horizon forecasts available, with the standing caveat that attention needs tone or imbalance to supply direction.

**4. Dealer exposure as a crowd amplifier. Grade: v1 as a displayed factor panel.**
The crowd state is only half the system. The other half is transmission. The same panic produces a slide in positive gamma (dealers buy dips to hedge) and a cascade in negative gamma (dealers sell into declines). Publish mood percentile, gamma regime and VIX curve state as three separate factors, and one labelled composite only as their product, with no new forecast claim until a ledger grades the interaction.

**5. Language shift metrics. Grade: SPECULATIVE for earnings calls, LATER for Fed and social text.**
Before positions change, grammar changes. Rising certainty language marks conviction hardening. Rising first person plural marks herding identity. A shift from conditional to future declarative tense marks the moment a scenario becomes an assumption. Rising second person challenge language marks polarisation. Computable with dictionaries and parsers on free text: FOMC statements, filed earnings remarks, social samples, GDELT quoted text. The Fed variant is the dark horse: about 30 years of statements, a tiny corpus, exact timestamps, and a narrator whose language moves the whole curve. Call text on free tiers biases the sample toward firms that file remarks, and that bias must be printed with any such gauge.

**6. Capitulation and euphoria fingerprints. Grade: v1 as a fingerprint panel with reconstruction history.**
Extremes are multivariate events no single gauge timestamps well, but the joint fingerprint is distinctive. Capitulation fingerprint, as an example set: breadth at washout levels, VIX curve inverted for multiple sessions, equity put and call extreme with index put and call merely elevated, short share falling as shorts cover, AAII bears above 55 percent or bulls below 20 percent, a FEARS basket spike, credit spreads wide but no longer widening for five sessions. Euphoria fingerprint: NAAIM above 100, scaled margin at highs with cash at lows, hot issuance climate, single name call buying extremes, extreme crypto funding, crowded trade language saturating. Score a fingerprint matcher against backfilled history (how many days in the last 40 years matched at least k of n conditions, and what followed) as a RECONSTRUCTION table, until live matches accrue. Any forward claim enters through a forecast ledger only.

**7. Calendar, payday and tax season layer. Grade: v1 as an annotation layer, LATER as a quantitative adjustment.**
Retail flow has a pay cheque rhythm: 401(k) contributions on payroll cycles, tax refunds concentrated February to April (IRS weekly filing statistics are free), tax loss selling in December, bonus season January to March, quarter end window dressing and expiry weeks. These are scheduled liquidity wearing moods clothes. An engine that does not model them will rediscover them as sentiment every year. Cheap to build, and it prevents a known class of false discoveries.

**8. Weather and daylight. Grade: SPECULATIVE, curiosity rank only.**
Covered in the dishonour roll above (Saunders 1993; Hirshleifer and Shumway 2003; Kamstra, Kramer and Levi 2003, contested). Tiny effects, replication scarred literature. Acceptable only as a clearly labelled curiosity tile if the build is near free, and it must never share a composite with serious gauges.

**9. Sports and news cycle displacement of attention. Grade: LATER.**
Attention is a fixed budget. Major sporting events, elections, wars and celebrity news displace market attention measurably, and displacement predicts lower volatility and delayed reaction to news released into the shadow (DellaVigna and Pollet 2009 on Friday announcements; Edmans, Garcia and Norli 2007 above). A displacement gauge (total finance attention today versus its seasonal norm, plus a maintained event calendar) tells an engine when silence means apathy and when it means distraction. The gauge is easy. The event calendar maintenance is the cost, and the payoff is interpretive rather than directional.

**10. Survey dispersion and disagreement as the gauge. Grade: v1.**
Every survey publishes a mean and hides the story in the spread. NAAIM publishes respondent dispersion; AAII neutral share is a crude confusion proxy; Michigan publishes cross sectional disagreement in research files. Rising dispersion at a constant mean predicts volume and volatility more reliably than returns (Antweiler and Frank 2004; Cookson and Niessner 2020). Store dispersion wherever a source publishes it and headline disagreement next to direction on every survey tile. This is a storage and display decision more than a research project, which is exactly why it belongs in v1.

**11. The quiet gauge: abstention and absence as signal. Grade: LATER.**
Forecasters, posters and traders going quiet when inputs degrade is itself information. Social posting volume from normally active accounts falling, options prints thinning in a name before news, insiders filing nothing in a window where the firm historically files: each needs a baseline per source, which a daily state store provides. The estate already owns a version of this idea in its abstention engine territory. Reuse that machinery rather than forking it.

**12. Crypto to equity mood transfer at the open. Grade: v1 as a stored panel, forecast use waiting for grades.**
Crypto trades the hours equities cannot. Weekend funding extremes, weekend Fear and Greed swings and Sunday evening Bitcoin moves are the only live crowd readings available before a Monday pre open read. Archive the weekend crypto state next to the Monday equity state and grade the association on a forward ledger instead of assuming it. History from 2018 for Fear and Greed and from about 2019 for liquid funding, all free.

**13. Revision breadth: what forecasters change, not what they say. Grade: LATER.**
Levels of opinion are stale compromises. Revisions are decisions. Three revision objects are free: Michigan preliminary to final revisions, ICI weekly estimate to monthly final revisions, and Metaculus community prediction updates on standing questions, a timestamped revision time series. A revision breadth gauge (net direction of revisions across all watched series this week) is a meta sentiment measure no retail dashboard publishes. It is a byproduct of correct vintage storage: build the storage first, read the gauge later.

**14. Crowding overlap from 13F plus short interest plus options. Grade: LATER.**
A name held by many 13F filers, heavily shorted and carrying rich call premium is a crowded stage set: any spark produces a two sided stampede. A crowding score per name, refreshed on the slowest leg (quarterly 13F), is a risk map for position sizing rather than a timing tool. Coverage has to widen beyond a partial board universe before it earns its keep.

### Options and prediction markets as belief aggregates

An options chain is a priced distribution. Implied skew, term structure and the risk neutral densities recoverable from option prices (Breeden and Litzenberger 1978) state what the market will pay for each future state, filtered through risk aversion and hedging demand. Two disciplined uses follow. First, the gap between risk neutral and physical probabilities is itself information: when downside states are priced far above historical base rates, either fear or hedging demand is extreme, and variance and skew risk premia carry return information at monthly horizons (Bollerslev, Tauchen and Zhou 2009; Carr and Wu 2009). Second, changes in the aggregate distribution forecast changes in positioning better than changes in price. The standing error is reading implied probabilities as forecasts of events. They are prices of events, systematically biased by the premium.

Where a liquid prediction market exists on a relevant event, its price is the best calibrated belief aggregate in this study (Wolfers and Zitzewitz 2004; Berg, Nelson and Rietz 2008). It should anchor the belief state, and any engine view departing from it should be required to name its edge. Where no liquid market exists, which is most sentiment questions, the engine is on its own and must say so. Programmatic history varies by platform and must be captured contemporaneously if it is ever to be graded. A series that is not captured does not exist later. PredictIt is the proof.

A final route follows from Keynes: forecast revisions of beliefs, not beliefs. Survey expectations are revised toward recent returns, analyst expectations toward recent earnings and price action with documented sluggishness, and positioning toward recent performance through flows. A model of the revision process, estimated on long backfilled histories, can forecast next month survey level or next quarter flow concentration better than it can forecast returns. Grade beliefs and returns on separate boards. Belief forecasting is the home turf, is more winnable, and is independently useful to a reader deciding how crowded the room she is entering has become. Conflating the two scoreboards is how sentiment programs delude themselves.

### Contrary indicator or momentum input: the decision rule

The literature resolves the apparent contradiction into four conditioning variables. Classify a reading by these before attaching any directional language.

1. **Horizon.** At days to a few months, measured sentiment and the positioning it creates tend to continue: flows persist, feedback buying operates, underreaction dominates. At one to three years, extreme sentiment tends to reverse. The crossover zone is regime dependent and is where most practical errors occur. Any output that does not print its horizon next to its direction is incomplete by design.
2. **Crowding and capacity.** Sentiment is a momentum input while believers still have capacity to act: cash to deploy, borrow available, mandates unfilled, susceptible audience unconverted. It becomes a contrary input as capacity exhausts: leverage at percentile extremes, cash at lows, narrative prevalence saturated, issuance flooding demand. Capacity variables are not confirming decorations. They are the switch that determines the sign, which is why the extremes record insists on coincidence across families: the contrary case is made by exhaustion evidence, not enthusiasm evidence alone.
3. **Forcing.** While forced flows dominate, sentiment direction continues regardless of valuation or survey levels, and contrary positioning is run over. Contrary readings acquire force when forcing is spent: forced sellers exhausted, volatility falling from extremes so risk rules stop demanding sales, hedging configuration flipped to stabilising. The historical sequence at bottoms is forcing, exhaustion, then reversal, and the middle term is the only one detectable in real time, through flow and volatility signatures rather than price hope.
4. **Who holds it, and how hard it is to arbitrage.** The same aggregate level implies continuation in the broad index and reversal risk concentrated in the high sentiment beta tail. Directional language must always carry a scope: index, segment or named tail. A bearish call on speculative segments can be right in a rising index year, and an engine graded only on the index will mislearn its own best skill.

One rule for the reader page: sentiment continues until capacity or forcing says otherwise, and reverses when exhaustion across at least two independent families coincides with the forcing flow abating, at a horizon of months, in the high sentiment beta segment first. Every clause is a place where live data can prove it wrong, which is the point.

### Scoring discipline

Pre registration: every forecast states at issue the variable, the instrument or segment, the horizon, the threshold or distribution claimed, and the base rate from backfilled history, timestamped into a live ledger before the horizon begins. The ledger is never backfilled. Amendments create new forecasts; they do not edit old ones.

Proper scoring: directional claims graded with Brier scores against a base rate forecaster who always predicts the historical frequency. Distributional claims with continuous ranked probability scores or log scores. A forecaster who cannot beat the base rate on a proper score over a pre stated sample does not have skill on that question, whatever its hit rate on selected episodes. Report calibration alongside discrimination.

Episode honesty: financial time series contain far fewer independent observations than their day counts suggest. Report the number of independent episodes (non overlapping horizons, distinct regimes) alongside the number of observations, and label any claim resting on fewer than ten independent episodes as preliminary.

Separation of scoreboards: belief forecasts, return forecasts and fragility forecasts are graded separately. A fragility forecast is graded on the accuracy of the conditional map when moves occur, not on whether moves occurred.

Kill conditions in advance: every proposition below carries the observation that would retire it. A proposition that is never allowed to die is marketing.

## Part 5: The testable propositions

Each proposition is falsifiable as written, with its horizon, its data (restricted to free and public sources or keys already held), its grading board, and its kill condition. Boards: live ledger means graded only forward, from when the forecast was actually written. History backtest means graded on backfilled inputs and labelled a backtest and a RECONSTRUCTION wherever reported. Passing a backtest earns a proposition the right to enter the live ledger. It earns nothing else.

**P1. Survey extrapolation, belief board.** Next month AAII bullish share and Michigan based expected return proxies will be better predicted by trailing three month market returns than by any fundamental release in the same window, in the sense that a trailing return only model achieves lower out of sample error than a releases only model. Horizon monthly, rolling. Data: AAII archive (free, from 1987), Michigan (free), index prices. Board: history backtest, then live ledger. Kill: the trailing return model loses to the releases only model on pre registered error over 36 consecutive live months.

**P2. Survey contrary content, return board.** When the AAII bull minus bear spread enters its top or bottom decile against full history from 1987, subsequent 12 month broad index returns will be lower after top decile readings than after bottom decile readings, by a margin exceeding zero. Horizon 12 months, overlapping observations episode adjusted. Board: history backtest (independent episodes expected under ten, labelled preliminary), then live ledger. Kill: the sign reverses over the first five independent live episodes, or the backtest spread is fully explained by trailing returns alone in a pre registered control.

**P3. Baker and Wurgler cross section.** In months following top tercile readings of the Baker and Wurgler index, a high sentiment beta basket (small, young, high volatility, unprofitable, non dividend paying) will underperform a low sentiment beta basket over the next six months, and the reverse after bottom tercile readings. Horizon six months. Data: Wurgler published index (free), Ken French data library characteristic baskets (free). Board: history backtest from 1965 (early decades are RECONSTRUCTIONS), then live ledger. Kill: the conditional spread is zero or wrong signed over any rolling ten year backtest window after 1990, or over the first six independent live episodes.

**P4. Aggregate timing humility.** No sentiment composite built under this program will achieve a monthly out of sample R squared for broad index returns above 2 percent sustained over any five year live window. A proposition about the engine itself, entered deliberately as a guardrail. Horizon five year windows. Board: live ledger only. Kill: a pre registered composite sustains above 2 percent monthly out of sample R squared over a full five year live window with no construction changes inside the window, in which case the guardrail is retired and the composite is promoted with its full record displayed.

**P5. Put and call extremes predict variance, not direction.** Equity put and call readings above their trailing three year 95th percentile will be followed by 20 trading day realised volatility above its unconditional median in at least 60 percent of episodes, while their directional implication for 20 day index returns will be within noise of zero once trailing five day returns are controlled. Horizon 20 trading days. Data: Cboe daily statistics (free). Board: history backtest with regime flags at the 2020 and 2022 structure breaks, then live ledger. Kill: directional content survives the trailing return control with a stable sign across both the pre 2020 and post 2022 regimes, in which case the proposition is replaced by the directional version that earned it.

**P6. Signed option volume, single names.** Among liquid optionable names, buyer initiated put heavy volume (top quintile put to call by signed volume where classifiable through held broker data) predicts negative abnormal returns over the following five sessions, in the information direction of Pan and Poteshman (2006), not the contrary direction. Horizon five sessions. Data: options volume through Alpaca and TastyTrade read only routes already held. Board: live ledger primarily; historical signed volume archives are not freely available at the required quality, and any partial history test must be labelled with its coverage. Kill: the live spread is zero or contrary signed over the first 200 qualifying name weeks.

**P7. Attention converts to flow.** Abnormal search and mention volume for a ticker (top decile against its own trailing year) predicts positive retail order imbalance over the following five sessions, and, where retail imbalance cannot be observed directly, predicts volume and volatility elevation over the same window. Horizon five sessions. Data: search proxies and platform mention counts from free sources, prices and volume from Alpaca. Board: history backtest where search history honestly reaches (mid 2000s onward), then live ledger, with platform break flags. Kill: attention spikes show no forward flow, volume or volatility content beyond the spike day itself over 100 live episodes.

**P8. Attention pressure reverses.** Names in P7 that also show extreme herding signatures (many small trades, leaderboard or trending list appearance where observable) will show negative average returns over the following 20 sessions, in the direction of Barber, Huang, Odean and Schwarz (2022). Horizon 20 sessions. Board: live ledger only; the original platform dataset is no longer published, so no honest full history backtest exists and none will be simulated. Kill: the 20 session average is zero or positive over the first 50 qualifying live episodes.

**P9. Dumb money, modern form.** Category level flow extremes (top decile net inflows as a share of category assets, from ICI summaries and free ETF flow aggregates) predict underperformance of the category relative to the broad index over the following 12 months, at a size smaller than the Frazzini and Lamont (2008) in sample estimates. Horizon 12 months. Board: history backtest from the 1990s, then live ledger. Kill: flow extreme categories match or outperform over the backtest post 2010 window and the first four independent live episodes combined, in which case flows revert to pure measurement status.

**P10. Issuance response.** Hot issuance windows (IPO count and average first day return, and equity share in new issues, each in top quartile against backfilled history) will be followed by below median subsequent 12 month returns for the newly issued cohort against matched seasoned firms, and by rising issuance volume for at least two further quarters as supply chases demand. Horizon 12 months for returns, two quarters for supply persistence. Data: Ritter IPO data (free), Federal Reserve and SIFMA issuance summaries where free. Board: history backtest to 1980 for IPOs and to 1928 for equity share (early values are RECONSTRUCTIONS from published aggregates), then live ledger. Kill: the issued cohort matches its benchmarks over two consecutive hot windows in backtest or live record.

**P11. Media tone pressure and reversal.** A finance dictionary pessimism score (Loughran and McDonald word lists, free) on a frozen corpus of market columns and front page financial coverage will show the Tetlock (2007) shape: extreme pessimism days predict negative next day index returns and positive cumulative returns over days two to five, with the effect at least twice as large in NBER recession months as in expansion months, per Garcia (2013). Horizon one and five days. Board: history backtest on the frozen corpus where archives honestly reach, then live ledger. Kill: the recession interaction is absent or reversed in both backtest and the first live recession, whenever one occurs. Until a live recession exists, that clause stays ungraded and must display that status rather than borrow the backtest grade.

**P12. Narrative growth peaks before price.** For named pre registered narratives tracked by frozen phrase sets in search and news corpora, the growth rate of narrative prevalence will peak before prevalence peaks, and in episodes where the narrative is attached to a tradable asset or segment, the associated price peak will occur after the prevalence growth peak in at least 60 percent of episodes. Horizon episode based, days to quarters by narrative class, pre registered per narrative. Board: history backtest on archived episodes (housing 2005 to 2007, dot com 1998 to 2000 in available corpora, labelled RECONSTRUCTIONS of prevalence), then live ledger. Kill: growth peaks coincide with or follow price peaks in the majority of the first ten graded episodes across boards.

**P13. Saturation checklist.** Episodes in which at least three of the pre registered saturation signatures are present (derivative marginal content, generalist audience migration, confirmation phrasing shift in search, counter narrative velocity parity) will be followed by declining narrative prevalence within one narrative generation (pre registered per class) in at least 65 percent of episodes. Horizon one narrative generation. Board: live ledger only for the checklist in its frozen form; historical application is a RECONSTRUCTION and is reported as exploratory. Kill: hit rate below 50 percent over the first 12 live episodes.

**P14. Positioning convexity map.** The fragility map (estimated forced flow per incremental 5 percent index decline, built from leveraged fund assets, margin debt normalised by market value, volatility targeting exposure proxies and dealer gamma configuration where computable) will rank realised episodes correctly: declines that occurred with a high fragility reading will show larger volume, larger correlation compression toward one, and larger volatility response per point of decline than declines with a low fragility reading. Horizon episode based. Data: FINRA margin (free, monthly), issuer fund asset data, options open interest via held routes, prices. Board: history backtest in coarse monthly form, then live ledger at full frequency. Kill: fragility readings show no association with decline mechanics across the backtest episodes of 2000 to 2002, 2008, 2020 and 2022 and the first three live decline episodes.

**P15. Dealer gamma configuration.** On days when estimated aggregate dealer gamma for the index is in its most negative quintile, intraday price moves will show greater continuation (positive autocorrelation of 30 minute returns) than on most positive quintile days. Horizon intraday, graded monthly in aggregate. Data: options open interest and prices via held routes, captured contemporaneously. Board: live ledger only; no free historical archive supports an honest backtest, so none is claimed. Kill: the continuation difference is absent or reversed over the first 60 graded days in each configuration class.

**P16. Skew is a price, not a probability.** Displayed downside implied probabilities from the index options chain will exceed realised event frequencies over matched horizons, by a margin that widens in top quintile VIX regimes. Horizon 30 days rolling. Board: live ledger; historical versions run only on implied volatility summary series (VIX and SKEW, Cboe, free; SKEW from 1990 as published, with methodology notes) and are labelled partial. Kill: implied downside frequencies match realised frequencies within sampling error over the first 24 live months, in which case the premium caveat is removed from displays as empirically unjustified for this market in this era.

**P17. Prediction market anchor.** On questions where a liquid prediction market and the engine both hold views, the market price will be better calibrated (lower Brier score) than the engine view over rolling samples, and engine departures from the market price will not add accuracy. Horizon per event. Data: published prediction market prices captured contemporaneously. Board: live ledger only; uncaptured historical prices cannot be graded honestly after the fact and will not be reconstructed for grading. Kill: the engine view beats the market on Brier score over the first 40 jointly graded events, in which case the anchor rule is revisited with the record displayed.

**P18. Channel dependent asymmetry.** Index declines accompanied by rising margin contraction and volatility rule selling signatures will reach a given drawdown depth faster than rises of the same depth, while social coordination episodes will rise faster than they fall in their first phase. Horizon episode based. Data: prices, FINRA margin (monthly, flagged for frequency), narrative corpora. Board: history backtest on index episodes from 1928 (prices are the one series that honestly reaches; Dow daily history is published and free in standard research form), then live ledger. Kill: no speed difference by channel across the backtest episode set at conventional significance with episode counts reported, or the live record reverses the backtest.

**P19. Capitulation is not a date.** Volume and volatility capitulation signatures (joint top decile readings of index volume against its own history, VIX, and down day breadth) will identify zones, not bottoms: in at least half of historical and live episodes, the index will trade at least 5 percent below the first capitulation signature close within the following 60 sessions, as it did after October 2008. Horizon 60 sessions. Data: prices, volume, Cboe VIX (free). Board: history backtest to 1990 on modern VIX and to 1928 on volume and breadth alone with the reduced gauge labelled, then live ledger. Kill: first signatures mark final lows within 5 percent in more than 70 percent of graded episodes, in which case the engine may, with the record shown, upgrade capitulation to a timing input. This kill condition is deliberately the hardest in the list to meet. The proposition exists to protect the reader from the engine.

**P20. FOMO cross section.** Speculative episodes will concentrate as the FOMO decomposition predicts: assets combining no cash flow anchor, visible scoreboard salience and low friction retail access will show larger attention, flow and new holder responses to a given peer gain shock than anchored institutionally held assets, by an ordering measurable across episodes. Horizon episode based. Board: history backtest on the 2020 to 2021 episode complex and earlier manias where proxies honestly exist, then live ledger. Kill: the predicted ordering is absent in the majority of graded episodes, in which case the FOMO decomposition is retired to narrative status and removed from engine logic.

**P21. Disposition overhang.** A capital gains overhang proxy (share weighted distance of the holder base from estimated reference prices, built from price and volume history in the Grinblatt and Han (2005) form) will add predictive content for cross sectional momentum beyond price momentum alone in large backtests, and will add nothing reliable at index level. Horizon one to six months cross sectional; index clause graded annually. Board: history backtest; a pure history proposition until it passes, entering the live ledger only on passing. Kill: no incremental content beyond price momentum in two independent backtest decades, or incremental content appears at index level in backtest. Either falsifies half the claim as written, and the proposition is graded as failed, not edited into a survivor.

**P22. Coincidence beats single gauges.** A pre registered coincidence rule (extremes in at least two of four families: surveys, flows and leverage, issuance, options stress) will identify subsequent 12 month periods of below median returns for the high sentiment beta segment more reliably than the best single family gauge alone, in backtest and live record combined grading with boards reported separately. Horizon 12 months. Board: history backtest to the start of the shortest family in the rule as configured (the rule must print that start date), then live ledger. Kill: the coincidence rule fails to beat its best component on the pre registered metric in backtest, in which case the engine ships single family gauges with no composite, and the composite idea is recorded as killed by its own test.

## Closing note

If this study leaves a reader with one habit, it should be suspicion of the single number. The crowd is real, its psychology is measurable, and some of its future is forecastable: the forced part mechanically, the attentive part by conversion, the narrative part by epidemic shape, and the belief part by the revision process. The return part is forecastable least, latest, and with the smallest sizes, and every famous case of the opposite claim, examined closely, turned out to rest on a short sample, a revised series, a mined specification, or a fund that quietly closed. Build the panel. Backfill the inputs and label the reconstructions. Keep the ledger sacred. Grade beliefs and returns on separate boards. Let propositions die in public. An engine that does those things will know a few true things about what the crowd will think next, and will know that it knows them, which in this field is the rarer achievement.
