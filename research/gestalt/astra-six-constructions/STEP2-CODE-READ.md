## What original/two_clocks.py actually does

1. Policies: 12 named policies. Eight start hedged and release the hedge on a trigger (first positive BTC bar, buying returns, two positive bars, asset repair, after one bar, market switching); four are the inverse "add" policies that start unhedged and add the hedge on the corresponding trigger, computed as 1 minus the release fraction path. Plus unhedged and fixed_beta baselines inside the same family list. Horizons: 6 and 18 bars (24h and 72h).
2. Event input: the code does NOT select events. It reads a preserved parent event list (CL-03226-detail.json) from a hardcoded path on the origin machine and applies one filter of its own: the BTC signal bar (one bar before the event timestamp) must be negative, else the event is skipped. All P&L is therefore conditional on the inherited list.
3. Beta: rolling 180-bar covariance of asset close/open returns with BTC close/open returns, divided by rolling 180-bar BTC variance, shifted one bar. Evaluated at the signal bar. Matches the report and the spec.
4. Hedge trigger for the highlighted policy (add_two_market_up): the hedge fraction is 0 until the first bar k (k at least 2) whose two preceding completed bars both have positive BTC close/open returns; from bar k onward the fraction is 1. Signals come from completed bars and orders act at the next bar open. Matches the report's "next open after two consecutive completed positive BTC bars."
5. Hedge sizing: short BTC perpetual units equal lagged beta divided by the perpetual price at the first event bar, times the active fraction. Fixed at entry terms; never rebalanced. Matches the report.
6. Endpoints and terminal prices: the asset return is the last held bar's close over the first bar's open, minus 1. The hedge exits at the last held bar's perpetual close. The spec states this convention explicitly ("the source uses the last held bar's close for terminal prices").
7. Costs: fee 0.001 (10bp) times every absolute change in hedge units times its execution price, including the opening change from zero and the closing change to zero. Asset cost is 0.001 times (1 plus the gross asset price ratio), i.e. 10bp on the $1 entry and 10bp on the exit value. Matches the report's "10 basis points per one-way turnover at marked execution notional on both legs."
8. Funding: archived perpetual funding on an 8-hour clock. An event/horizon row is excluded and listed if the funding timestamps do not exactly match the complete expected 8-hour clock with interval 8, or if any required bar is missing from asset, market, or perpetual series. At each funding timestamp the cash flow is the minimum of the before-quantity and after-quantity alternatives, with zero included as the alternate at entry and exit. Positive funding pays the short. Matches the report, including the "less favorable cash flow" boundary rule.
9. Reporting: per policy, horizon, and period (period boundaries come from CUTS in edge_attribution), the code writes mean net, an increment versus fixed_beta, and mean hedge fraction, with summaries from a 7-calendar-day block bootstrap of 500 draws. Outputs: design.json, event_paths.json, results.json written into the script's own directory. The design record carries the amendment disclosure: four inverse policies added after inspecting the initial release results; selection described as previously explored events with no untouched holdout; limits list includes survivor universe, post-search selection, next-open fills, funding mark proxy, margin not modeled.
10. External behavior: NONE found. No network calls, no credential access, no order capability, no writes outside its own output files. It reads local archives and the parent event JSON from hardcoded origin-machine paths under /workspace/edge-audit/. Because of those hardcoded paths the original file cannot run on this estate as-is; the package's run_historical.py is the sanctioned portable route (same calculations, paths rewired to vendor/ and the bundled data, writes confined to runs/historical inside the bundle). Step 3 and 4 run the portable route and diff it against the original to confirm the calculations are intact.

## What original/conditional_contracts.py actually does

Pure standard library. feasible_states enumerates past states (each bool or unknown) crossed with the three monotone future pairs (False,False), (True,False), (True,True); the adverse future pair (False,True) is excluded by construction of the future list, and a past pair with the higher target already hit but the lower not hit is excluded only when identical_past_windows is set. conditional_floor computes payoffs low + 1 - high over feasible states and returns the minimum minus the two supplied prices, labeled CONDITIONAL_MODEL_ONLY with assumptions and omissions listed (fees, funding, timing, oracle/collateral, executable quotes). The main block self-checks the report's example: unknown history gives a negative minimum; verified higher-target-not-previously-hit gives 1 - 0.40 - 0.55 = +0.05; the (0,1) state is present with unknown history and absent with the verified fact. No network, no reads, one write of its own examples JSON when run as main. Matches the report's supplied-state model and its 0.95 cost / 1.00 floor example.
