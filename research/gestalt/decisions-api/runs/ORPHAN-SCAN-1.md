## The register

### O-01. Small-cap post-earnings drift (PEAD), restricted form
- Mechanism: underreaction to earnings surprise; inattentive
  holders reprice over days (Ball and Brown 1968; Bernard and
  Thomas 1989: top minus bottom SUE decile about 4.2 percent over
  60 days, small-firm 60-day CAR +5.3 percent vs large +2.8).
- Capacity: tens of thousands of dollars per name; a book under
  1M dollars is already near the useful limit diversified across
  a quarter's announcements (Deep Dive Two section 5.2).
- Why big capital cannot take it: the surviving effect lives in
  microcaps with wide spreads and thin analyst coverage; position
  sizes that matter to a fund move the price and pay the spread
  that is the effect's moat.
- Dive verdict, applied: DEAD in large caps since 2006
  (Martineau 2022); the microcap coefficient faded to
  insignificant by 2016 to 2019. Test ONLY microcap, horizon at
  most 5 days, net of actual spread, or not at all. The textbook
  60-day large-cap version is REFUSED BY NAME.
- Forward grading: sealed SUE-ranked microcap entries, 5-day
  horizon, fill-based net return vs QQQ and SPY at B5.

### O-02. Fire-sale and forced-flow reversal
- Mechanism: distressed funds sell pro-rata regardless of price;
  liquidity providers who buy the pressure earn the reversal
  (Coval and Stafford 2007; lowest-heterogeneity quintile long
  fire-sales and short fire-purchases earned 6.95 percent
  risk-adjusted over the next year).
- Capacity: small by construction; the dislocation is the
  capacity limit. Dive estimate: 10 to 50K dollars per name,
  plausible, not measured.
- Why big capital cannot take it: quarterly cadence, tiny dollar
  capacity per name, and returns that arrive over quarters, below
  any institutional deployment minimum.
- Dive verdict, applied: PLAUSIBLE ORPHAN. Full treatment in
  designs/OPPONENT-MODEL-EVIDENCE-2026-11-15.md.
- Forward grading: FIT-measure flagged names, entered after the
  pressure quarter, graded quarterly vs SPY.

### O-03. Index rebalance liquidity provision (patient side only)
- Mechanism: passive funds must trade at the effective-date
  close on a public schedule; a patient provider sells into
  addition demand and buys deletion supply at that close.
- Capacity: small and episodic (June and December Russell
  events, small names).
- Why big capital cannot take the patient side at our scale:
  the front-running side is an institutional speed arms race;
  the provision side in small names is too small to staff.
- Dive verdict, applied: liquidity provider at the effective
  close ONLY, never front-runner.
- Forward grading: per-event fill-based returns, graded per
  rebalance window vs SPY.

### O-04. Spin-offs (forced-selling orphan equities)
- Mechanism: index funds and mandate-bound holders receive
  SpinCo shares they cannot hold and sell price-insensitively;
  the SpinCo opens with no coverage and no natural buyer.
  Credit Suisse 2012 to 2022 dataset: spin-offs outperformed the
  S and P 500 by 13.4 percent in the first 12 months; JPMorgan
  since 1999: roughly 10 percent first-year outperformance;
  Cusatis, Miles and Woolridge 1993 is the peer-reviewed anchor.
- Capacity: spin-off floats are frequently small-cap; the
  forced-selling window absorbs only modest size before the
  discount closes. Stated as a range with cost model at conveyor
  registration, per the design gate.
- Why big capital cannot take it: a large fund's position would
  be a rounding error or a float-dominating stake; coverage and
  mandate machinery cannot process a nameless small SpinCo.
- Dive verdict: not separately verdicted in Deep Dive Two
  section 5; graded here under the section 5.1 gate (low
  turnover, event-driven, cost model on picked names).
- Forward grading: entries after the forced-selling overhang
  window (the documented pattern concentrates in years 1 to 3,
  not day 1), graded at 6 and 12 months vs SPY and QQQ.

### O-05. Post-bankruptcy emergence equities (selective only)
- Mechanism: creditors who never wanted equity receive and dump
  emergence shares; the equity opens orphaned. Eberhart, Altman
  and Aggarwal 1999 found large excess returns; Jiang, Wang and
  Yang 2021 find the median post-reorganization stock earned
  roughly zero abnormal return and the effect faded in the 2010s;
  Verdad (BankruptcyData, 2008 to 2019 emergences above 100M
  market cap): mean 1-year return +19.8 percent vs Russell 2000
  +11.2, but median minus 15 percent and about 22 percent lost
  over 80 percent within two years. Selection is the whole game:
  EBIT yield above 20 percent names averaged +61 percent over
  two years in the Verdad cut; negative EBIT yield averaged
  minus 21.
- Capacity: emergence floats are small and OTC-heavy; the
  outperformance in the Duke summary concentrates in OTC names.
- Why big capital cannot take it: mandate, custody and headline
  constraints plus tiny floats.
- Dive verdict: not in Deep Dive Two section 5; graded under the
  gate, with the scan's own warning on the face: the naive
  version (own every emergence) is REFUSED on the 2021 evidence;
  only a pre-registered selection rule (positive EBIT yield at
  emergence) may be tested.
- Forward grading: emergence cohort entries under the selection
  rule only, graded at 12 and 24 months vs Russell 2000 proxy
  and SPY, median reported beside mean (the mean lies here).

### O-06. Closed-end fund discount narrowing
- Mechanism: a CEF's price and NAV are untethered (no creation
  and redemption); discounts average 10 to 15 percent
  historically (Lee, Shleifer and Thaler 1991), widen in stress
  past 20 percent, and narrow on catalysts (activism,
  liquidation, conversion, distribution policy). The discount
  change is a return component independent of NAV performance.
- Capacity: CEFs are often small relative to institutional
  portfolio sizes, which the sources name as exactly why the
  trade is immaterial to large managers; per-fund capacity is
  bounded by float and daily volume, costed at registration.
- Why big capital cannot take it: position sizes are immaterial
  at fund scale and convergence timing is uncertain over years.
- Dive verdict: not in Deep Dive Two section 5; graded under the
  gate (turnover is minimal, which passes Novy-Marx and Velikov
  easily; the risk is duration, not friction).
- Forward grading: Z-score entries (discount wide vs the fund's
  own history), graded on price return minus NAV return vs SPY
  at 6 and 12 months.

### O-07. Microcap illiquidity premium (neglected names)
- Mechanism: thin coverage, wide spreads and no institutional
  sponsorship price microcaps cheap on every metric (Acuitas,
  The Case for Microcap, Sep 2025: the Fama and French small-cap
  premium is in practice a microcap premium, 92.5 percent of the
  Russell Microcap Index sits in the bottom three Fama-French
  size deciles). Amihud illiquidity pricing is the academic
  frame.
- Capacity: the binding constraint itself; Acuitas names it
  directly: investors can only hold small amounts before owning
  an uncomfortable share of the company, and 5 percent ownership
  triggers SEC filing duties.
- Why big capital cannot take it: capacity and regulatory
  thresholds, by the source's own statement.
- Dive verdict: consistent with Deep Dive Two section 5.1 (the
  predictability versus dollar-profitability gap lives in
  illiquid names); any test carries the signal-weighted spread
  model or it does not run.
- Forward grading: low-turnover neglected-name basket, graded
  annually vs Russell Microcap proxy and SPY, net of measured
  spreads on the picked names.

### O-08. Odd-lot and retail order-flow imbalance (signal only)
- Mechanism: marketable retail imbalance predicts next-day
  returns (Boehmer, Jones, Zhang and Zhang 2021; interquartile
  imbalance worth +3.81bp pre-pandemic, +5.91bp pandemic, +6.69bp
  post, per Deep Dive Two section 5.2), split roughly half
  liquidity provision, half informed.
- Capacity: not the constraint; cost and data are. Gross edge
  4 to 7bp against spread, fees and feed cost.
- Dive verdict, applied: SIGNAL OR FILTER ONLY, never a
  standalone daily book. Daily turnover sits in the Novy-Marx
  death zone unless execution is near-free.
- Forward grading: as a timing filter on existing sleeves only;
  graded by the host strategy's delta with and without the
  filter, never as its own P and L line.

### O-09. Wide-spread defined-risk options structures (maker-only, or nothing)
- Mechanism as marketed: apparent mispricing inside wide quotes
  on long-dated, deep OTM or single-name illiquid options.
- Dive verdict, applied: NO source in Deep Dive Two documents
  net-of-spread profitability; UNTESTABLE AS MARKETED. Apparent
  mid-price edges are usually stale-mid artefacts, and the
  spread is a cost the buyer pays. The only admissible form:
  limit orders INSIDE the spread as a maker, with fill-based
  (not mid-based) accounting. If fill versus mid cannot be
  measured, there is no edge to register.
- Capacity: quote size is tiny because adverse selection is
  severe; that is the finding, not an inconvenience.
- Forward grading: maker fills only, fill-based P and L vs the
  same structure at mid, graded monthly; any month graded on
  mids voids the candidate.

### O-10. Tiny-float effects (registered as a hazard, not an edge)
- Mechanism claimed: floats under 10 to 20M shares move violently
  on small demand. The scan's sources are cautionary, not
  supportive: tiny floats are documented vectors for coordinated
  manipulation (the CHOW case: 2.6M share float, an 84.3 percent
  single-day collapse, litigation alleging a pump-and-dump), and
  practitioner retrospectives argue float matters far less in
  2026 than ATR and volume.
- Verdict: NO edge documented in any source read. Registered so
  the family has a named refusal on file: tiny float is a risk
  flag for exclusion screens, not a candidate. It counts in the
  scan's 10 rows as a REFUSAL, not a candidate.
- Forward grading: none. Revisit only on a peer-reviewed source
  documenting a net, fill-based effect.

## Explicit non-orphan (named so it is not smuggled in)

Overnight (close to open) SPY and QQQ: NOT an orphan
(unlimited capacity in the most liquid instruments on earth).
Dive verdict: allocation timing only, never a standalone daily
round-trip strategy.
