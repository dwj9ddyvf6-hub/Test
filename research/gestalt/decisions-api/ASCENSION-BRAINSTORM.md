## LEVEL 4: THE FLEET IS AN ECONOMY

Stop managing sleeves like a portfolio and start governing them like an economy. The fleet has a currency (risk budget), a central bank (the allocator), business cycles (regimes), inflation (correlation creep, where every sleeve secretly becomes the same trade), and unemployment (armed sleeves producing nothing). Economic policy tools map directly:

- **Interest rates on capital.** In hostile regimes the price of risk rises: sleeves pay more (in required expectancy) to hold capital. In benign regimes capital is cheap and exploration is subsidized.
- **Quantitative easing for exploration.** Young sleeves and new candidates get subsidized paper capital during their learning phase, explicitly as monetary policy, with the subsidy on the books and a sunset date. The conveyor stops starving its own children.
- **A correlation tax.** Sleeves whose fires correlate with the fleet's existing book pay a tax in reduced allocation; genuinely diversifying sleeves earn a subsidy. The fleet's hidden sameness becomes a priced externality instead of a surprise.
- **GDP accounting.** The fleet's product is graded expectancy per unit of drawdown, published nightly like national statistics, with the components named: consumption (proven sleeves compounding), investment (candidates in forward test), waste (defect and unfed drag, now measured in dollars of idle capital).

Falsifier: an economy-governed allocator fails to beat the plain equal-vol allocator on forward R over two quarters. If policy adds nothing, we go back to being a portfolio.
