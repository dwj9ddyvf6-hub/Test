# KG-8: LITERATURE CHECK ON THE THREE MECHANISMS BEHIND TODAY'S FINDS

Scope: internet research only, primary sources (journal papers, a Federal Reserve research post, working papers). No trades, no code changed. Our numbers are the ones measured today, Sun 2026-11-15, in this program's own lanes: the overnight Focus-names edge (Lane 9 widening: +16.46bp over 2,229 nights, t 4.97, vs SPY +3.53bp and QQQ +4.85bp on the same nights; alive at 5bp of daily friction, dead at 15bp); LS1 (overnight leg conditioned on the weekend Bitcoin move: BTC-up weekends +42.06bp, t 4.36, n=250; BTC-down weekends -20.24bp, n=212; spread 62.3bp; unconditioned weekend legs +13.47bp, t 1.64); LS2 (overnight leg conditioned on the same day's intraday sign: +26.93bp, t 6.15, n=1,118 after up intraday days vs +5.82bp after down intraday days; and +22.43bp, t 4.10 after down overnights); KG-4 (post-entry phase curve: fleet earns night 1 at +43.4bp excess, n=5,648, then bleeds, N2-3 -152.3bp, N4-5 -260.5bp, N4+ -839.1bp; Tensor decays monotonically from +29.0bp to -846.2bp; the Fires family runs the opposite shape, N1 -429.8bp, N2-3 +1,378.2bp at win rate 0.898, N4+ +1,027.2bp).

## LEADS FIRST: WHAT THE LITERATURE DOES TO OUR LIVE LEADS

**THREAT TO LS1, stated plainly.** The one published paper on exactly LS1's mechanism finds the effect is one-sided and young. Mourey, Shahrour and Soiman (Finance Research Letters, 2025) find that negative weekend cryptocurrency returns significantly predict Monday equity declines, while positive weekend returns have no effect, and that the transmission strengthens markedly only after the May 2022 LUNA collapse. Our LS1 spread is built from two legs of comparable size (+42.06bp up, -20.24bp down). Under the published result, the up leg, the larger half of our spread, should not exist in broad indices, and neither leg should be strong before mid-2022. Our full-span 2018-2026 estimate has never been split at that break. Either the Focus basket's Friday-close-to-Monday-open leg is a genuinely different object from Mourey's Monday index returns, or our 62.3bp is carried by the post-2022 subsample plus the unconditional overnight premium. This is the single most decision-relevant fact in this file, and the split that resolves it is named as Mechanism 2's variation below. Source: Mourey, Shahrour, Soiman (2025), "A crypto-stock weekend effect: Predicting Monday stock returns using weekend cryptocurrency returns," Finance Research Letters 86, 108661, abstract at https://econpapers.repec.org/article/eeefinlet/v_3a86_3ay_3a2025_3ai_3ape_3as1544612325019154.htm

**SECOND THREAT TO LS1's DATING, AND SUPPORT FOR ITS CHANNEL.** A 2026 arXiv study of weekend crypto volatility finds weekend cryptocurrency variance predicts Monday variance in the S&P 500, gold and a Treasury ETF only after 2024 (one standard deviation of the weekend factor associated with 27 to 54 percent higher fitted Monday variance; out-of-sample QLIKE losses reduced 11 to 22 percent), with an endogenous break in late 2023 around anticipated US spot-Bitcoin ETF approval, and no predictive content before 2024. The transmission channel LS1 relies on demonstrably exists in the recent regime, and two independent papers date its birth or strengthening to 2022-2024, not 2018. Source: "Weekend Crypto Volatility and Monday Market Risk," arXiv:2610.35702, https://arxiv.org/html/2610.35702v1

**THREAT TO THE OVERNIGHT COMPLEX'S PERMANENCE, WITH A NAMED CAUSE.** Boyarchenko, Larsen and Whelan (Review of Financial Studies, 2023) documented a large overnight drift in E-mini futures concentrated in the 2:00-3:00 a.m. ET European-open window, roughly 3.7 percent per annum over 1998-2020 and more than 60 percent of the contract's close-to-close return. In their July 2026 update, that window has averaged close to zero since 2021, in ES, NQ and YM alike. Their framework prices the expected overnight return as closing order imbalance times return variance divided by liquidity-provider risk-bearing capacity, and they attribute the fade to one input: the dispersion of end-of-day relative signed volume fell from a standard deviation of 6.5 percent to 2.9 percent, while VIX (20.4 to 19.4 mean) and overnight liquidity barely moved. Two NightShares ETFs built on the effect (NSPY, NIWM, launched June 2022) closed fourteen months later. This is a different object from ours (an intranight futures window vs our close-to-open equity basket leg), and our own Lane 9 halves on 2018-2026 still print +14.88bp and +18.03bp, so the Focus overnight premium has not visibly died the same death. But the published record now contains one overnight phenomenon, documented by its own discoverers, going to zero post-publication with the mechanism measured. Source: Boyarchenko, Larsen, Whelan, "The Disappearing Overnight Drift," Liberty Street Economics, Federal Reserve Bank of New York, July 1, 2026, https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/2026/07/the-disappearing-overnight-drift/ ; original paper: Review of Financial Studies 36(9), 3502-3547 (2023).

**STRENGTHENING FOR THE OVERNIGHT CORE.** The split itself is among the best-documented objects in empirical asset pricing. Lou, Polk and Skouras (Journal of Financial Economics, 2019) document strong firm-level overnight and intraday return continuation with an offsetting cross-period reversal, lasting for years, and find that profits on all 14 strategies they decompose are earned entirely overnight or entirely intraday, typically with opposite signs across the two components; 100 percent of momentum's abnormal returns occur overnight. Cliff, Cooper and Gulen (2008) found the US equity premium over their sample was solely due to overnight returns. Our measured magnitudes sit sensibly inside this literature: index overnight returns run about +3.5 to +5bp per night in it, matching our same-nights SPY +3.53bp and QQQ +4.85bp, and our Focus basket's +16.46bp is a selected-basket multiple of the index figure, not a different species of number. Sources: Lou, Polk, Skouras (2019), "A tug of war: Overnight versus intraday expected returns," Journal of Financial Economics 134(1), 192-213, https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v134y2019i1p192-213.html ; Cliff, Cooper, Gulen (2008), "Return differences between trading and non-trading hours: Like night and day," SSRN Working Paper 1004081, as cited in https://www.tradiens.com/essays/night-shift

**LS2 DISAGREES WITH TWO NAMED THEORIES AND AGREES WITH A THIRD.** Lou, Polk and Skouras's cross-period reversal says a strong intraday component forecasts a weak overnight component (at their monthly horizon). The inventory-management model behind the Fed's overnight drift makes the same signed prediction at the daily horizon: a down intraday day leaves liquidity providers long inventory that is compensated overnight, so down days should precede strong nights; the 2025 news-based decomposition paper states this prediction explicitly ("Inventory management predicts reversals from intraday returns to overnight returns"). LS2 measures the opposite sign at the exact day-to-night boundary: nights after up days pay +26.93bp, nights after down days pay +5.82bp. The published result LS2's sign agrees with is Barardehi, Bogousslavsky and Muravyev: portfolios formed on past intraday returns display short-term momentum (and reversal), consistent with investors underreacting to the information conveyed by other investors' trading, while portfolios formed on past overnight returns display no short-term momentum, only long-term reversal. Their horizons are weeks to months, so LS2 extends the continuation sign to a boundary, the single day-to-night crossing, where two other frameworks predicted reversal. Sources: Barardehi, Bogousslavsky, Muravyev, "What Drives Momentum and Reversal? Evidence from Day and Night Signals," SSRN 4069509 (published version, Review of Financial Studies), https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4069509 ; "Does Overnight News Explain Overnight Returns?" (2025), arXiv:2507.04481, https://borowiecki.dev/pdf/2507.04481

**KG-4 AGREES WITH THE DECAY LITERATURE IN SHAPE, EXTENDS IT IN SIGN, AND ITS FAMILY SPLIT IS WHAT THE THEORY PREDICTS.** Di Mascio, Lines and Prucyk's "Alpha Decay" (Inalytics sample: over 700 institutional portfolios, 1.15 million orders, 2001-2013) documents that alpha on newly purchased stocks starts at about +36bp in the first month and decays to zero by month twelve, following a power or exponential shape with a half-life of about four months, with no subsequent reversal, while managers' average holding period is 2.2 years. Our fleet curve has the same front-loaded shape compressed to nights, but it does not decay to zero, it goes through zero and bleeds (-839.1bp at N4+), which pure information decay does not produce; the bleed needs a second mechanism (carry, costs, or adverse selection in who stays in). The Fires family's opposite shape is not an anomaly against this literature: Garleanu and Pedersen (Journal of Finance, 2013) prove that predictors have different mean-reversion (alpha-decay) speeds and that optimal trading weights each signal by its own decay speed, and the canonical slow signal in the literature, post-earnings-announcement drift, keeps drifting for roughly 60 trading days (Bernard and Thomas, 1989: extreme-decile hedge about +4.2 percent over the 60 days after the announcement, largest in the first two weeks, with a quarter to a third of it concentrated in the three-day windows around subsequent earnings announcements). A family whose entries are earnings-event driven should earn in later phases; a family of fast technical signals should not. KG-4's split by family is the decay literature's central claim, measured at nightly resolution. Sources: Di Mascio, Lines, Prucyk, "Alpha Decay," SSRN working paper, https://top1000funds.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/SSRN-id2580551.pdf ; Garleanu, Pedersen (2013), "Dynamic Trading with Predictable Returns and Transaction Costs," Journal of Finance 68(6), 2309-2340, NBER WP 15205 at https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w15205/revisions/w15205.rev1.pdf ; Bernard, Thomas (1989), "Post-Earnings-Announcement Drift: Delayed Price Response or Risk Premium?", hedge magnitude and 60-day window as reported at https://alpha-suite.org/blog/earnings-surprise-stocks and https://www.eventstudytools.com/overview-practical-applications

## MECHANISM 1: THE OVERNIGHT / INTRADAY SPLIT

What the literature says. The decomposition is exact and the two components behave like different assets. Lou, Polk and Skouras (2019, sample 1993-2013 plus nine non-US markets) attribute this to investor heterogeneity: different clienteles dominate the open and the rest of the session, producing own-period continuation (past overnight returns forecast future overnight returns, past intraday forecast future intraday, measurably for years) and cross-period reversal between them. Bogousslavsky (Journal of Financial Economics, 2021; 30 years of US intraday data) shows anomalies accrue in radically different intraday patterns: size and illiquidity premia are realized in the last thirty minutes, profitability and idiosyncratic volatility accrue gradually through the day but earn large negative returns overnight, consistent with mispricing at the open and with overnight margin and lending-fee frictions. Berkman, Koch, Tuttle and Zhang (Journal of Financial and Quantitative Analysis, 2012; 3,000 largest US stocks, 1996-2008) document the attention channel: positive overnight returns followed by intraday reversals, concentrated in stocks that recently attracted retail attention, with average overnight returns of about +0.1 percent per night reversing by about -0.07 percent intraday across all stocks, and 0.2 to 0.6 percent overnight bumps in high-attention, hard-to-arbitrage subsamples. Barardehi, Bogousslavsky and Muravyev add the information split: news mostly drives overnight returns, trading mostly drives intraday returns. The 2025 news-decomposition paper, using 2.4 million news articles, finds news-topic prevalence and response differences explain a large part of the overnight-intraday gap. On decay: the tug-of-war patterns persist for years in Lou, Polk and Skouras, but the generic post-publication record is hostile (McLean and Pontiff, Journal of Finance, 2016: 97 published predictors average 26 percent lower returns out of sample and 58 percent lower after publication), and the Fed's futures drift (above) is the one overnight-specific case of complete post-2021 disappearance with a measured cause. Sources: Bogousslavsky (2021), https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v141y2021i1p172-194.html ; Berkman, Koch, Tuttle, Zhang (2012), abstract at https://econpapers.repec.org/article/cupjfinqa/v_3a47_3ay_3a2012_3ai_3a04_3ap_3a715-741_5f00.htm ; McLean, Pontiff (2016), "Does Academic Research Destroy Stock Return Predictability?", Journal of Finance 71(1), 5-32, doi:10.1111/jofi.12365.

Our numbers against it. The core split agrees: our intraday leg was worthless (Sharpe 0.01 in Lane 9) while the overnight leg carried everything, the same one-sided accrual Lou, Polk and Skouras find for momentum-class strategies, and our index controls match the literature's index magnitudes. LS2 disagrees in sign with the cross-period reversal at the daily boundary (see leads). One quiet agreement worth noting: our secondary LS2 read, +22.43bp (t 4.10) for the overnight leg after down OVERNIGHTS, is a same-component reversal at one day, which none of the cited papers' headline results predict either (Lou, Polk and Skouras find overnight continuation at monthly lags); the day-scale overnight component may mean-revert even while the month-scale component continues. That tension is untested at both scales on our basket.

**The one variation the literature suggests that no lane ran:** condition the Focus overnight premium on the day's closing order imbalance, the Fed framework's own pricing variable. In Boyarchenko, Larsen and Whelan's 2007-2020 data, days sorted by end-of-day signed volume show a wide overnight spread: large negative closing imbalances are followed by positive overnight returns, large positive imbalances by flat or negative ones. Our 1Min cache can build a final-hour signed-volume proxy for the basket, and the test crosses it with LS2: the inventory theory predicts the premium concentrates after heavy sell closes, which is a different conditioner from the full-day intraday sign LS2 used, and the two can be run as a two-by-two (final-hour imbalance sign x full-day intraday sign) to see which one is carrying the other. No lane today conditioned on closing imbalance at any horizon.

## MECHANISM 2: WEEKEND BITCOIN AS A CONDITIONER FOR MONDAY EQUITY OVERNIGHT RETURNS

What the literature says. Mourey, Shahrour and Soiman (2025), using the 20 largest cryptocurrencies (about 85 percent of market capitalization) in a Bayesian/Kalman framework, find weekend crypto performance predicts Monday stock returns across the Nasdaq, Russell 2000, S&P sector indices and the S&P Crypto Index, with the strong asymmetry and the post-LUNA strengthening described in the leads. The arXiv weekend-volatility study (2017-2026, Bitcoin, Ether, XRP against the S&P 500, gold and a Treasury ETF) finds the volatility version of the same channel, significant only post-2024, driven by a common weekend crypto factor rather than any single coin, and explicitly not reproduced by Friday crypto volatility, which rules out a generic "crypto was volatile recently" reading. The older calendar literature is context, not support: the classic equity weekend effect (Monday returns lower than Friday, documented since Cross 1973 and French 1980, and weaker in recent decades per a 2024 meta-analysis of 1980-2022 studies) is an unconditional day-of-week pattern, while both 2025-2026 papers make the Monday outcome conditional on what crypto did while equities were closed, which is the timing-asymmetry mechanism LS1 also exploits: information arrives in a market that never closes and is impounded into equities only at the reopen. Sources: Mourey et al. (2025), as above; "Weekend Crypto Volatility and Monday Market Risk," arXiv:2610.35702, as above; day-of-week meta-analysis as summarized at https://www.disciplineaiapp.com/post/day-of-week-effect (secondary summary, used for context only).

Our numbers against it. Sign agreement on the leg the literature certifies: after BTC-down weekends our Monday overnight leg is -20.24bp against an unconditioned weekend leg of +13.47bp, a -33.7bp displacement in Mourey's predicted direction, on the exact window (Friday close to Monday open) where the timing asymmetry should bite first. Disagreement on the other leg: we measure +42.06bp after BTC-up weekends, a +28.6bp displacement above the unconditioned mean, where Mourey finds positive weekend returns have no effect on Monday equities. Extension in one respect: both papers measure Monday equity returns on broad indices; LS1 measures the overnight leg only, on a 29-name high-beta basket, and finds the effect on both basket subsets (carry5 +47.37bp, rest +41.03bp on BTC-up weekends), so if the up leg is real it is a basket-and-leg-specific object the index-level literature would dilute. Timing disagreement, unresolved: our estimate pools 2018-2026, while Mourey dates the strengthening to after May 2022 and the volatility paper dates its channel to after 2024; a pooled t of 4.36 is consistent with a strong late subsample carrying a null early one.

**The one variation the literature suggests that no lane ran:** the asymmetric, break-split rerun of LS1. Re-estimate each leg separately against the unconditional weekend overnight mean (not against each other), in three subsamples split at the literature's own dates: before May 2022 (LUNA), May 2022 to December 2023, and January 2024 onward (spot-ETF era). Mourey predicts the down leg is significant only from the second subsample on and the up leg is null in all three; our current pooled numbers predict both legs in all three. Whichever way it prints, LS1's registered form changes: either it survives as an eight-year object, or it is re-registered honestly as a post-2022 (or post-2024) object with a younger falsifier clock. No lane today split LS1 by sign against the unconditional mean or by era.

## MECHANISM 3: POST-ENTRY RETURN DECAY BY HOLD PHASE

What the literature says. Three clocks are documented, and they are not the same clock. The information clock: Di Mascio, Lines and Prucyk measure institutional alpha decaying smoothly from +36bp in month one to zero at month twelve, half-life about four months, no reversal after exhaustion; Garleanu and Pedersen show the optimal response is to weight signals by their decay speed and adjust partially toward a forward-looking aim portfolio, trading aggressively only on signals whose benefit accrues long enough to amortize costs. The event clock: Bernard and Thomas's PEAD drifts for about 60 trading days, front-loaded into the first two weeks with disproportionate weight on the windows around the next earnings announcements, and modern replications find it shrunk (post-2000 samples nearer 1.5 to 3.2 percent per 60 days on extreme deciles, near zero in liquid large caps per Martineau 2022, as reported by EventStudyTools). The publication clock: McLean and Pontiff's 26 percent out-of-sample and 58 percent post-publication shrinkage is decay across calendar years of a strategy's life, not across days of a position's life, and should not be conflated with KG-4's phase curve. On optimal exit timing specifically, the published guidance is the Garleanu-Pedersen principle rather than a fixed horizon: exit speed should match the signal's own decay rate, which is a per-signal quantity to be estimated, exactly the object KG-4's family curves measure at nightly resolution. Sources: as cited in the leads; PEAD modern magnitudes as reported at https://www.eventstudytools.com/overview-practical-applications

Our numbers against it. Agreement in shape for the fast families: the fleet, Tensor and Lazarus curves are front-loaded and monotone, the nightly analogue of Di Mascio's power-law decay, and Unblock-2's independent Lazarus result (the family's registered 15 percent stop and 40-session horizon are too tight for its own fire stream; widening stops or extending horizons lifts mean outcomes by +500 to +2,700bp per trade across eight sleeves) is the same horizon-mismatch finding Di Mascio document for institutions (alpha gone in twelve months, positions held 2.2 years), read from the other side: our exits are misaligned with our signals' decay curves in both directions depending on family. Extension in sign: our curves cross zero and keep going (fleet N4+ -839.1bp; Lazarus N4+ -1,545.5bp on few dates), which neither pure decay nor PEAD predicts; a position that merely held exhausted alpha should flatten, not bleed, so the late phases are measuring something else, most plausibly who remains in the trade and why. Agreement by construction for Fires: its N2-3 earnings of +1,378.2bp at a 0.898 win rate are the shape a slow-incorporation, event-driven signal class should have under Bernard and Thomas, and KG-4's family split is therefore evidence for the decay literature's core claim (decay speed is a property of the signal, not of the market) rather than an exception to it. Clustering caveat from KG-4 stands: family late-phase cells rest on 1 to 3 distinct entry dates, so these are leads with named forward checks, not verdicts.

**The one variation the literature suggests that no lane ran:** decay-weighted exits. Fit each family's KG-4 phase curve to an exponential decay, extract a per-family half-life, and in the Unblock-2 replay harness replace the fixed exits with a Garleanu-Pedersen partial-adjustment exit: scale out each night in proportion to the fitted remaining alpha, so fast families (Lazarus, fitted half-life near one night) exit almost immediately and slow families (Fires) barely scale out before their late-phase earnings arrive. Compare against the two exit rules already tested (the registered base and the crude DROP_stop / DROP_horizon variants). The literature predicts the decay-weighted exit beats both, with the gain concentrated in Lazarus and near zero in Fires; today's lanes varied stops and horizons as constants but never made the exit rule a function of the family's own measured decay curve.
