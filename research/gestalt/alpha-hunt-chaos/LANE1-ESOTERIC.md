## Cluster I: Market plumbing and microstructure oddities

### 1. Leveraged and inverse ETF daily-reset rebalancing flow

**Mechanism:** A fund targeting leverage L on daily return r must trade (L^2 minus L) times AUM times r at the close to restore its multiple, roughly 6% of NAV per 1% move for a 3x fund and 12% for a minus 3x fund. Because L^2 minus L is positive for both long (L=2,3) and inverse (L=-1,-2,-3) products, long and inverse funds trade in the SAME direction as the day's move and reinforce rather than cancel. Swap counterparties hedge the reset in the underlying and futures in the last minutes, concentrated in the closing auction. The flow is computable in advance from public AUM, L, and the intraday move by about 15:30 ET.

**Why consensus misses it:** Mainstream coverage treats leveraged ETFs as a buy-and-hold decay story for retail suitability, not as a predictable, signed, end-of-day flow in the underlying. Index-level work nets long vs inverse AUM as if they offset; the arithmetic says they add. Single-stock leveraged ETFs, the fastest-growing part of the complex, are newer than most textbook treatment.

**Cheapest first test:** Compute implied rebalance dollars as the sum of (L^2 minus L) x AUM x return-to-15:30 for TQQQ/SQQQ, SOXL/SOXS, NVDL/NVDS from issuer daily holdings files, join to Alpaca 1-minute bars 15:30 to 16:00 and the next morning's open, and test whether predicted flow explains late-day continuation and overnight reversal scaled by predicted flow over ADV. Data assets: issuer holdings files, Alpaca bars.

**Source:** https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228231757_The_Dynamics_of_Leveraged_and_Inverse_Exchange-Traded_Funds (Cheng and Madhavan 2009); mechanics from daily holdings at https://developer.stockfit.io/blog/leveraged-etf-rebalancing

### 2. SEC fails-to-deliver spikes as a forward signal

**Mechanism:** An FTD is a sale not delivered by settlement at NSCC/CNS. Boni (2006) shows fails were consistent with strategic failing when borrow is expensive: the seller chooses the fail over paying a special borrow fee. Stratmann and Welborn (2016) find stocks with significant FTDs experience negative abnormal returns proportional to FTD level, and shorts in high-FTD names earn positive estimated profits: FTDs reveal informed shorting undeterred by the constraint, not operational noise. ETFs are roughly 10% of market cap but over 78% of FTDs (operational shorting from creation/redemption market making), so equity and ETF FTDs must be separated.

**Why consensus misses it:** Retail folklore reads FTD and threshold-list appearance as bullish ("naked shorts must cover, squeeze imminent"). The academic record points the other way for single-name equities (continuation down), and the folklore ignores the two-week publication lag, the ETF operational component, and that a fail is a revealed willingness to short despite cost, not a forced-buy timer.

**Cheapest first test:** Download the free SEC FOIA Fails-to-Deliver files plus FINRA short interest files, normalize fails by shares outstanding, form spike events (fails above 0.5% of outstanding, or top-decile jumps), and measure forward 5/10/20-day returns vs matched controls in Alpaca daily bars, ETFs split out, trading only on dates the file was knowable. Data assets: SEC FTD files, FINRA short interest, Alpaca bars.

**Source:** https://www.scribd.com/document/353081613/2016-Informed-Short-Selling-Fails-To-Deliver-And-Abnormal-Returns (Stratmann and Welborn, Journal of Empirical Finance 2016)

### 3. Securities lending fee and utilization spikes

**Mechanism:** Shorting requires borrowing stock at a continuously repriced loan fee; utilization is on-loan over lendable supply. Most names are general collateral (under 0.5% per year); a thin specials tail runs 10 to 100%+. Engelberg, Reed and Ringgenberg show fee level and variance (short-selling risk) predict low future returns and price delay; the Loan Fee Anomaly work (Engelberg et al., Management Science 2024) finds loan fees predict the cross-section better than 102 other anomalies. Daniel, Klos and Rottke (2025) update it: a portfolio formed on 1-day-lagged borrow cost earns large negative alpha in the high-fee leg, close to one-for-one with the fee. The squeeze reading is the same plumbing reversed: at 95-100% utilization there is no borrow left, recalls force covers, and any catalyst produces convex buying. Direction depends on conditioning: fee-as-information vs fee plus saturation plus catalyst.

**Why consensus misses it:** Short interest is free, twice-monthly and lagged, so consensus uses it as THE shorting variable and treats borrow fee as a trading cost, not a signal. The lending market is OTC and vendor-gated (Markit, S3, FIS), invisible on retail platforms, so the highest-Sharpe anomaly in the comparison set is literally not in most datasets.

**Cheapest first test:** No Markit licence needed for v1: infer synthetic borrow from TastyTrade option chains via put-call parity deviations on hard names, plus FINRA short interest and SEC FTD as free proxies. Test whether a fee spike (doubling in 5 days) predicts next-10-day returns differently in the 90-99th percentile vs the saturated above-99th tail. Data assets: TastyTrade chains, FINRA short interest, SEC FTD files.

**Source:** http://wp.lancs.ac.uk/fofi2026/files/2026/03/FoFI-2026-140-Simon-Rottke.pdf (Daniel, Klos and Rottke 2025); author page for Short-Selling Risk (JF 2018) and The Loan Fee Anomaly (2024): https://www.joeyengelberg.com/

### 4. Closing auction imbalance and closing-auction volume share extremes

**Mechanism:** The closing auction is now about 7.5% of daily volume (up from 3.1% in 2010), driven by passive and ETF benchmarking to the close. Auction price deviations from the pre-close midpoint are small on average (about 8 bp) but occasionally exceed 63 bp (top 1%), grow with auction volume and imbalance, and revert about 85% overnight, consistent with uninformed price pressure, not information. The older MOC literature (Cushing and Madhavan 2000) finds the same from imbalance publications directly: returns following sell imbalances are positive overnight and next day, following buy imbalances negative.

**Why consensus misses it:** Consensus treats the closing print as THE efficient price of the day (it is the NAV, benchmark and performance price) and closing volume growth as a liquidity-improvement story. The deviation-and-reversal is dismissed as spread noise because on a typical day it is; the tradeable mass sits in the extreme tail of imbalance and volume share, which index-level averages wash out.

**Cheapest first test:** Alpaca bars suffice for v1: proxy auction share as closing-print volume over total day volume, deviation as close vs the 15:55 midpoint or VWAP. Sort single names on extreme deviation times extreme auction share, measure close-to-next-open and open-to-close reversal, excluding index-rebalance and earnings days first, then testing them separately. Data assets: Alpaca bars; exchange NOII imbalance feeds as the v2 upgrade.

**Source:** https://static1.squarespace.com/static/6310c0b9bb63a25599f4418c/t/634ffc92f81e226b2c30654f/1666186387645/who-trades-at-the-close_June2021.pdf (Bogousslavsky and Muravyev, Who Trades at the Close?, Journal of Financial Markets 2023)

### 5. Odd-lot order imbalance, decomposed by who is actually trading

**Mechanism:** Odd lots (under 100 shares) were historically read as uninformed retail. O'Hara, Yao and Ye (2014, Journal of Finance) overturn the premise: odd lots were 24% of trades at the median (60%+ in some names), were excluded from the consolidated tape until December 2013, and contribute about 35% of price discovery, consistent with informed and HFT traders splitting orders into odd lots to avoid detection and pinging for hidden liquidity with 1-share trades. Johnson's odd-lot imbalance study finds odd-lot order imbalance correlates with lagged and contemporaneous returns, with marketable odd lots carrying more explanatory power than round-lot-derived odd lots. The open question is the modern mix: retail fractional flow vs algo slicing, distinguishable by size bucket, sub-penny and timing signatures.

**Why consensus misses it:** Two opposite consensuses both miss it: old technical analysis ("odd-lot theory," fade dumb retail) assumes odd lots are retail; modern quant datasets long deleted odd lots as noise or, post-2013, pool them without asking who generated them. In high-priced names odd lots are now the majority of prints, so any order-imbalance measure that truncates them is systematically biased.

**Cheapest first test:** Alpaca SIP trades include odd lots: build daily odd-lot buy/sell imbalance signed by the quote rule, split by trade size (1 share, 2-9, 10-99) and by price level (above $500 names vs below $50), and test next-day and next-5-day predictive content vs round-lot imbalance on the same names. The 1-share ping bucket vs the 10-99 retail-like bucket is the discriminator. Data asset: Alpaca SIP trade tape.

**Source:** https://serpwatch.aabri.com/manuscripts/193132.pdf (Johnson, Odd-Lot Order Imbalance and Returns); foundation: O'Hara, Yao and Ye, What's Not There: Odd Lots and Market Data, JF 2014, https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v69y2014i5p2199-2236.html

### 6. Off-exchange share extremes and dark short-volume decomposition

**Mechanism:** Roughly 40-50% of US equity volume now executes off-exchange: ATS dark pools plus wholesaler internalisation of retail flow. Two distinct signatures hide in one aggregate. (a) FINRA TRF short-volume ratio: SqueezeMetrics (2018) shows from free FINRA files that off-exchange short volume is largely market makers shorting TO FILL BUY ORDERS, so a high dark short ratio proxies buying pressure and correlates positively with intraday returns ("short is long"). (b) ATS share extremes and large late-reported block prints: institutions splitting parent orders into dark venues leave a rising dark-share footprint before lit price moves, while a single outsized block at a round premium or discount is a negotiated transfer, not directional flow. FINRA publishes ATS volume by venue and ticker weekly (about 2-week lag for Tier 1) and daily TRF short-volume files the same evening; the lag structure dictates what horizon is even testable.

**Why consensus misses it:** Retail flow tools label every off-exchange print "dark pool" with a direction the data do not contain (FINRA weekly ATS data have no side, price, or per-trade size), and consensus academic work focuses on lit order books. Wholesalers internalise over 90% of retail marketable orders, institutions use ATSs, market-maker shorts are inventory: the aggregate off-exchange share is uninterpretable until decomposed by venue type and by short vs long TRF volume.

**Cheapest first test:** Free data only: FINRA daily short-sale volume files plus FINRA OTC Transparency weekly ATS files plus Alpaca bars. Test 1 (daily): dark short ratio extremes vs next-day return, controlling for total off-exchange share. Test 2 (weekly, lag-aware): ATS share jumps above 2 sigma vs its own 12-week mean vs forward 2-4 week returns, respecting actual publication dates. Data assets: FINRA TRF and ATS files, Alpaca bars.

**Source:** https://www.sqzme.co/monitor/download/pdf/short_is_long.pdf (SqueezeMetrics, Short is Long, 2018); FINRA ATS structure and lags: https://tapeboard.com/dark-pool/T

### 7. Fractional-share flow as a retail sentiment gauge (and a volume contaminant)

**Mechanism:** Bartlett, McCrary and O'Hara (2024, Journal of Financial Economics), "Tiny Trades, Big Questions": brokers accept dollar-based orders ($10 of a $3,000 stock), hold the fractional remainder on their own book, and report executions with distinctive latency and reporting signatures that let the authors identify fractional trades inside TAQ. Fractional intensity concentrates in high-priced stocks, meme stocks, IPOs and SPACs; it inflates reported share volume (a $5 order counts as a trade), distorts standard volume and liquidity measures, and in the replication study fractional intensity significantly predicts future stock-level volatility. Aggregated, timed fractional flow is therefore a high-frequency retail-sentiment gauge that standard volume is silently contaminated by. Note: brokers' actual batching timestamps are not published; the testable version of this concept is the tape signature, not the batching window.

**Why consensus misses it:** Consensus treats share volume and trade count as raw facts. If a rising share of trades are fractional aggregations of many tiny dollar orders, batched, principal-facilitated and reported differently, then volume spikes in expensive and meme names are partly a denominator artefact plus a retail-attention signal, and screens built on volume, trades-per-share or Amihud illiquidity inherit the distortion without knowing it.

**Cheapest first test:** CRSP-style proxy without TAQ: trades-per-share and average trade size from Alpaca SIP trades by day; flag names and days where trade count surges while dollar volume does not (the fractional signature, strongest in above-$300 names). Test whether the proxy predicts next-week realised volatility and retail-attention reversals vs a pure dollar-volume control. Data asset: Alpaca SIP trade tape.

**Source:** https://agentic-sciences.github.io/assets/papers/tiny_trades_analysis.pdf (replication of Bartlett, McCrary and O'Hara 2024, JFE)

### 8. Opening auction dislocation vs the overnight futures path

**Mechanism:** Lou, Polk and Skouras (2019, JFE), "A Tug of War": close-to-close returns decompose into overnight (close to open) and intraday (open to close) components driven by different clienteles, retail-heavy flow near the open vs institutions during the day. The components continue in their own period and reverse across periods, for years: past overnight returns positively forecast future overnight returns and negatively forecast future intraday returns, and vice versa. At the single-morning level this is the opening-auction dislocation: overnight futures (ES/NQ, trading about 23 hours) impound global news continuously, while the cash opening auction impounds it in one print plus accumulated retail market-on-open orders and market-maker overnight inventory charges. When the cash open overshoots the futures-implied open, the intraday leg tends to give it back; when it undershoots, the opposite, conditional on the gap not being single-name fundamental news.

**Why consensus misses it:** Consensus models use close-to-close returns as one object, so the two offsetting components cancel and look like noise. Futures traders watch the gap as a level ("gap fill") without the clientele conditioning; equity quants watch overnight returns as a premium to collect (this estate already does, for Focus names) rather than the cross-period reversal of THIS MORNING's dislocation as a 9:30-10:30 single-name signal.

**Cheapest first test:** Alpaca bars only: for liquid single names plus SPY/QQQ, compute the overnight futures return, the cash opening gap vs the futures-implied gap, and opening-auction volume share. Fade or follow the residual dislocation over 9:30-10:30 and open-to-close, split by earnings vs non-earnings days. Data assets: Alpaca bars (equities and premarket), ES/NQ futures path.

**Source:** https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/87481/ (Lou, Polk and Skouras, A Tug of War, JFE 134, 2019)

### 9. T+1 settlement-cycle effects: fail concentration and close-out calendar shift

**Mechanism:** The US moved T+2 to T+1 on 28 May 2024, compressing affirmation to trade-date evening and halving the time to source borrow, FX and cash. The Ontario Securities Commission's CDS study (Jan 2021 to Jun 2025) is the cleanest public post-mortem: no significant change in the AVERAGE fail rate after T+1, but post-T+1 daily fail spikes, when they occurred, were concentrated in a few securities with high fail values. A second channel (Pastorek and Albrecht 2026, on crypto spot ETFs): FTDs act as an intertemporal liquidity buffer, so volatility does not rise on trade date; it materialises around the regulatory settlement and close-out date and spills into the next session, with FTDs arising systematically when lending constraints bind. Settlement-date clustering is the tradable translation: pressure is deferred to a known future date, not diffused. Under T+1 a fail also ages into Reg SHO close-out one day sooner, shifting the forced-buy calendar relative to options expiry and index events in ways pre-2024 FTD studies cannot describe.

**Why consensus misses it:** The policy debate graded T+1 at the average ("fails didn't spike, transition succeeded") and stopped. The average hides the investable structure: concentration (few names, known dates), ex-date and FX-funding mismatches for cross-border holders, and the shifted close-out calendar.

**Cheapest first test:** SEC FTD files (T+1 vintage) plus Alpaca bars: rebuild the FTD event study on post-May-2024 data only, measuring returns and volatility specifically on settlement and close-out window days (T+1, threshold-list day 5, Rule 204 close-out) vs trade date, and test whether fail concentration (top-5 names' share of daily fail dollars) predicts cross-sectional dispersion. Data assets: SEC FTD files, Alpaca bars, DTCC/OSC published fail-rate series as the free aggregate control.

**Source:** https://www.osc.ca/en/news-events/reports-and-publications/impact-t1-settlement-failed-trades (OSC, The Impact of T+1 Settlement on Failed Trades); settlement-date volatility channel: http://ftp.mendelu.cz/RePEc/men/wpaper/109_2026.pdf

### 10. VIX settlement (VRO/SOQ) as a cross-market basis

**Mechanism:** VIX futures and options do not settle to spot VIX. They settle cash, Wednesday morning, to a Special Opening Quotation (SOQ, ticker VRO) computed from the OPENING TRADE PRICES of a single strip of out-of-the-money SPX options about 30 days out: AM settlement, opening auction only, midpoint used only if a series does not trade. Griffin and Shams (2017, RFS 2020) document that at VIX settlement, volume spikes only in the OTM SPX options included in the VIX calculation, more in options with discontinuously high weight in the formula, with no comparable spike in OEX/SPY options or in the European contract with a different settlement design, a pattern they argue is inconsistent with hedging and consistent with settlement pressure, with transient settlement deviations around 31 bp on VIX in their conservative estimate. Cboe disputes the interpretation; the plumbing fact is undisputed: a large, liquid VIX derivatives position settles off thin opening prints in a different, less liquid market, in a finite, known window. The generalisable oddity is the AM-vs-PM basis: AM-settled index products settle off opening prints of hundreds of series, PM-settled weeklies off the close, and the two bases diverge most on high-overnight-news mornings.

**Why consensus misses it:** Consensus treats VIX settlement as a calendar note for VIX traders, and strike pinning (a different mechanism, excluded here) as the only expiry plumbing. The SOQ is a CROSS-market settlement, SPX option opening auctions determining VIX payouts, so neither the VIX desk nor the SPX desk owns the basis, and VRO minus Tuesday-close VIX is rarely tracked as a time series in its own right.

**Cheapest first test:** Cboe publishes VRO settlement values and VIX futures settlements; TastyTrade chains give Tuesday-close VIX marks. Build the VRO basis series (VRO minus prior close spot VIX; VRO minus the expiring future's Tuesday settlement) on monthly and weekly VIX expiries, condition on the overnight SPX move and SPX option opening volume, and test whether extreme basis mornings predict SPX/VIX mean reversion into the Wednesday close. No OPRA tape needed for v1. Data assets: Cboe settlement files, TastyTrade chains.

**Source:** https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2972979 (Griffin and Shams, Manipulation in the VIX?, RFS 2020); settlement mechanics: https://www.cboe.com/tradable-products/vix/vix-futures/

### 11. Dealer vanna and charm hedging: the vol-and-clock flows

**Mechanism:** Dealer delta does not only move with price (gamma). It moves when implied vol moves (vanna) and when time passes (charm), and both force mechanical re-hedging in the underlying. Customers are structurally net buyers of index options, especially OTM puts, so dealers are net short vol (Garleanu, Pedersen, Poteshman 2009, proven with holdings data). When VIX falls sharply after an event resolves, OTM call deltas rise and OTM put deltas shrink; dealers short those options must buy underlying to stay flat: the "vol-crush rally," directional flow caused entirely by falling volatility with no bullish news. Charm runs on the clock: overnight, OTM put deltas decay toward zero, dealers long the underlying as a hedge against short puts need less of it, and systematic selling concentrates at the open, growing heavier into weekly and monthly expiry. The honest audit (butterflow) notes the Greek math is solid and dealer net-short-vol positioning is confirmed, but the causal price-impact link is unproven, and Cboe's own study sizes dealer net gamma at only 0.04-0.17% of daily S&P futures liquidity; the flow claim is exactly what a first test should measure rather than assume. This is distinct from the estate's GEX regime gates (a vol-state map): vanna/charm is a signed FLOW forecast from the vol path and the calendar, not a regime label.

**Why consensus misses it:** The industry's standard framework stops at gamma. Vanna and charm are treated as pricing trivia for options desks, not as a forecastable, calendar-anchored flow in the underlying; and because the flows are largest exactly when "nothing is happening" (quiet afternoons, post-event vol crushes), they get attributed to random drift or unnamed "buying."

**Cheapest first test:** From the estate's stored SPX chain snapshots (terminal chain snapshot collector), compute aggregate dealer vanna and charm exposure under the standard customer-positioning sign convention, then test two registered predictions on Alpaca SPY bars: (a) large down-VIX days with high measured vanna show excess positive drift in the following hours vs matched down-VIX days with low vanna; (b) high-charm mornings (Thu/Fri of expiry weeks) show excess negative first-30-minute drift vs matched mornings. Data assets: terminal SPX chain snapshots, Alpaca bars.

**Source:** https://www.datafirsttrading.com/guides/vanna-vs-charm ; evidence audit: https://butt2rflow.github.io/en/posts/vanna-charm/ ; practitioner exposure explainer: https://www.gexmetrix.com/blog/vanna-charm

## Cluster IV: Liquidity plumbing, information flow, and flow-induced pressure

### 12. Fed net-liquidity plumbing: TGA rebuilds, tax-date drains, and reserve scarcity

**Mechanism:** The Treasury General Account is the federal government's checking account at the Fed. Every dollar that accumulates in the TGA (quarterly tax settlements, heavy net bill and coupon issuance, post-debt-ceiling rebuilds) is a dollar extinguished from commercial bank reserves unless offset by Fed asset growth or ON RRP balances. For years the ON RRP facility absorbed the drain; with the RRP effectively exhausted (about $0.18B in late October 2026 per H.4.1 watchers), TGA swings now hit reserves dollar-for-dollar, and funding stress shows up first as SOFR trading at a premium to IORB (plus 6-8 bp in late October 2026, vs the minus 2 bp norm earlier in 2026). The practitioner "net liquidity" series (WALCL minus TGA minus ON RRP, all free on FRED) is tracked as a coincident risk-appetite gauge by macro desks, and Goldman has warned publicly that the 2027 debt-ceiling TGA rebuild colliding with QT aftermath and large auctions is a triple-pressure liquidity event. The unexamined version for this estate: not the weekly level, but the CALENDAR of known drains (tax dates Sep 15 / Dec 15 / Jan 15 / Apr 15, refunding settlement days, debt-ceiling rebuild ramps) as a pre-registered headwind/tailwind overlay on index expressions, and the SOFR-IORB spread as a funding-stress state variable beside VIX.

**Why consensus misses it:** Equity consensus treats liquidity plumbing as a rates-desk topic and a chart for macro newsletters; the series are weekly, accounting-identity-driven and unglamorous, so they never enter equity signal stacks. The drains are also announced in advance (Treasury refunding statements publish the settlement calendar), which makes them feel "priced in" without anyone having tested whether the reserve transmission still moves equity vol at the margin now that the RRP buffer is gone.

**Cheapest first test:** All data free on FRED (WALCL, WTREGEN/WDTGAL, RRPONTSYD, WRESBAL, SOFR, IORB) plus Alpaca SPY/QQQ daily bars: event-study the known drain windows (tax settlement weeks, refunding settlement days, the 2023 and any 2027 TGA rebuild ramps) for SPY realised vol and close-to-close returns vs matched non-drain weeks, and test SOFR-IORB spread spikes as a next-week vol state. Data assets: FRED, Alpaca bars.

**Source:** https://martyau79.substack.com/p/30oct2026-traditional-markets-and (Au79 Macro Research, H.4.1 net-liquidity accounting, Oct 2026); forensic drain audit: https://www.gemral.com/blog/post/treasury-general-account-volatility-bank-reserve-absorption-september-2026

### 13. Earnings announcement premium, sorted by options-implied jump tail risk

**Mechanism:** Stocks earn a large average premium for simply being held through earnings announcements (the earnings announcement premium, about 7-10% annualised in the US, traceable to Ball and Brown 1968 and formalised by Frazzini and Lamont, and Savor and Wilson's risk-based account: announcement days carry disproportionate systematic risk). The unexamined refinement: Liu, Chan and Faff (2022, Journal of Banking and Finance) build a jump-implied variance contribution index (JIVX) from pre-announcement option prices, the share of implied variance attributable to a jump on the announcement itself, and show it prospectively sorts the premium: a portfolio buying announcers with high pre-news JIVX and selling low-JIVX announcers earns a net risk-adjusted 82 bp in the three days after the announcement. The options market is pricing WHICH announcements carry real jump risk; the equity premium concentrates where the jump share is high. This is not the estate's earnings IV work (which trades the implied-vs-realised MOVE size, long or short vol by quintile): this is the equity announcement premium itself, cross-sectionally sorted by an options-implied jump decomposition.

**Why consensus misses it:** The announcement premium is treated as a uniform calendar fact (hold everything through earnings, or avoid everything through earnings), and the options literature reads pre-earnings option prices for direction or for move size, not for the jump-vs-diffusion split of implied variance as a sorter for the equity premium.

**Cheapest first test:** TastyTrade chains on the Focus 30 plus the terminal's earnings archive (priced_moves.json and realised_moves.json, 206 rows): for each upcoming print, compute a JIVX-style jump share from the term structure of implied variance around the announcement date (front expiry straddling the print vs the next expiry), bucket announcers by jump share, and measure the 3-day post-announcement equity drift by bucket on Alpaca bars, first on the 206-row archive, then forward. Data assets: TastyTrade chains, terminal earnings archive, Alpaca bars.

**Source:** https://pure.bond.edu.au/ws/files/190520485/AM_What_can_we_learn_from_firm_level_jump_induced_tail_risk_around_earnings.pdf (Liu, Chan and Faff, JBF 138, 2022)

### 14. Index implied-volatility spread: the smile slope as a market timing series

**Mechanism:** Atilgan, Bali and Demirtas (2015, Journal of Business and Economic Statistics) define the implied volatility spread on S&P 500 options (OTM put IV minus ATM call IV, the slope of the smile) and find a significantly NEGATIVE link to future market returns at daily and weekly frequencies: a 1% wider spread reliably indicates roughly 0.02-0.10% lower next-week returns. Their interpretation is information flow: informed traders express negative views in OTM puts first, and the smile slope aggregates that positioning before it reaches the index. The relation strengthens exactly when information is moving: during windows when many S&P 500 constituents announce earnings, when cash-flow and discount-rate news is large, and when consumer sentiment is at extremes, and it survives controls for conditional volatility, the variance risk premium and macro variables. The estate prices single-name and index vol constantly but has never carried the SPX smile SLOPE as a standalone timing series for index expressions.

**Why consensus misses it:** Vol desks watch the smile for pricing and hedging, quants watch VIX level and term structure for timing; the slope of the smile at matched tenor sits between the two crafts and is owned by neither. It is also counterintuitive as a signal: a steeper put skew "feels" like hedging demand (noise) rather than informed flow (signal), and the paper's sample ends in 2008, so the post-0DTE surface has never been re-measured for it.

**Cheapest first test:** From the estate's stored SPX chain snapshots, compute the daily OTM-put-minus-ATM-call IV spread at the 30-45 day tenor, and regress next-day and next-week SPY returns on it over the snapshot history we hold, split by earnings-density weeks (count of Focus 30 / S&P prints that week) vs quiet weeks. Data assets: terminal SPX chain snapshots, Alpaca SPY bars.

**Source:** https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1993289 (Atilgan, Bali and Demirtas, JBES 33, 2015)

### 15. Mutual fund fire sales and flow-induced price pressure (front-run and reversal)

**Mechanism:** Coval and Stafford (2007, NBER WP 11357; Journal of Financial Economics) show that mutual funds suffering extreme outflows must sell existing positions regardless of price, and funds with extreme inflows must buy, creating uninformed price pressure concentrated in the stocks those funds hold in common. Fire-sale stocks are beaten down and forced-purchase stocks bid up; the pressure then REVERSES over the following quarters, and because future flow-driven trades are predictable from current flows and holdings, an investor providing liquidity against distressed funds, or front-running anticipated forced trades, earns highly significant returns. The 2026 update sharpens the vehicle split: mutual fund flows generate sizable price impact and fire-sale dynamics (a 1% flow-induced purchase of shares outstanding produces about a 1.64% contemporaneous run-up followed by reversal; the most fire-sale-exposed stocks fall about 2.27% through the quarter), while separate-account flows through transition managers generate essentially none, so the signal lives specifically in the mutual fund wrapper and its quarterly holdings disclosure.

**Why consensus misses it:** The mechanism is old and famous as a story but operationally hard: it requires joining fund flows to holdings to stocks, the holdings are quarterly and lagged, and modern attention moved to ETF flows (which arbitrage away faster). The estate has never built the holdings-times-flows pressure score for its own universe, and the post-2020 explosion of single-stock and thematic mutual funds changed which names carry concentrated common ownership.

**Cheapest first test:** Public data only: mutual fund holdings from SEC N-PORT filings (EDGAR, monthly for many funds, machine-readable) times fund flow estimates from reported TNA changes; build a flow-induced trading pressure score for Focus 30 and desk-universe names, then test on Alpaca daily bars whether extreme-pressure names show the documented contemporaneous move and subsequent 1-2 quarter reversal. A cheaper v0 uses only quarterly 13F/N-PORT snapshots around known distressed-fund quarters. Data assets: SEC EDGAR N-PORT/13F, Alpaca bars.

**Source:** https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w11357/w11357.pdf (Coval and Stafford 2005/2007); vehicle split update: https://hkujcesgri.hku.hk/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Same-Dollar-Different-Impact.pdf (2026)

### 16. Attention itself: search-volume price pressure and its reversal

**Mechanism:** Da, Engelberg and Gao (2011, Journal of Finance), "In Search of Attention": Google Search Volume Index for a ticker is a direct measure of retail attention, only weakly explained (about 5%) by size, news, turnover or analyst coverage, so attention is its own state variable. An increase in SVI predicts higher prices over the next two weeks (small Russell 3000 names with large SVI increases beat large-decrease names by about 0.08% per week in the original sample) and then a reversal within the year: classic Barber-Odean price pressure, because attention creates net BUYING (the set of potential buyers is far larger than the set of existing holders who can sell). SVI spikes also contribute to large IPO first-day returns and subsequent long-run underperformance. The modern, unexamined version for this estate: attention spikes measured on tickers, company names and product names in Google Trends (and Wikipedia pageviews as a free daily cross-check) as a crowding gauge on the Focus 30's retail-heavy names (PLTR, RKLB, CRWV, EOSE class), where the estate currently reads price and flow but never attention itself.

**Why consensus misses it:** Professionals dismiss search data as a retail curiosity and a 2011 result; the signal is small per name per week, needs a cross-section to harvest, and sits in marketing-analytics tooling rather than trading datasets. The reversal half of the finding (attention gains unwind) is even less used than the pressure half.

**Cheapest first test:** Google Trends weekly SVI (free) plus Wikipedia pageview API (free, daily) for the Focus 30 tickers and company names over 2-3 years, joined to Alpaca daily bars: replicate the two-week pressure and longer reversal split on our names only, and test whether SVI spikes add anything on top of the volume and return spikes we already log (the paper's claim is that attention is NOT just a transform of those). Data assets: Google Trends, Wikipedia pageviews API, Alpaca bars.

**Source:** https://www3.nd.edu/~zda/Google.pdf (Da, Engelberg and Gao, Journal of Finance 66, 2011)

### 17. Nocturnal trading: the 8pm-4am session as a price-discovery and sentiment layer

**Mechanism:** Eaton, Shkilko and Werner ("Nocturnal Trading," 2025/2026 working paper) study the overnight session (8:00 PM to 4:00 AM ET) that barely existed before 2023: it is predominantly retail, roughly 80% of flow originates from the Asia-Pacific region, about 50% of dollar volume concentrates in just 10 stocks, liquidity comes from only a few market makers on essentially one dominant venue (Blue Ocean ATS), and yet the session contributes economically significant PRICE DISCOVERY, particularly for non-US firms and macro-oriented ETFs, with trading costs comparable to extended hours. Nocturnal traders appear to trade on price-relevant information, but transaction costs leave average net profitability near zero unless positions are held longer. Alpaca itself now offers overnight trading on Blue Ocean with a dedicated data feed. For this estate the unexamined object is the nocturnal session as a SIGNAL layer for the regular session: overnight ATS prints in our names are the first US-venue reaction to Asian-session news and Asian retail positioning, hours before the premarket consensus forms. This is distinct from the estate's close-to-open overnight edge (a close-to-open holding phenomenon): this is the microstructure INSIDE the overnight window, who trades, in what names, and whether its direction and intensity forecast the 9:30 open and the regular session.

**Why consensus misses it:** US desks sleep through it and US datasets mostly start at 4:00 AM; the session sits outside the SIP's operating window with no consolidated NBBO, so standard feeds literally do not contain it. It is also dismissed as uninformed retail flow, which the paper's price-discovery result directly contradicts for the names where it concentrates.

**Cheapest first test:** Alpaca's overnight (Blue Ocean) feed for the Focus 30 plus the top nocturnal names: measure overnight session return, volume share and trade-count concentration per name, and test whether nocturnal direction predicts the regular session's open-to-close move and whether nocturnal volume spikes flag next-day realised vol, on the sample since the feed became available. Data assets: Alpaca overnight feed (feed=boats), Alpaca regular-session bars.

**Source:** https://www.conftool.pro/mfa2026/index.php/Eaton-251-Nocturnal_Trading.pdf?page=downloadPaper&filename=Eaton-251-Nocturnal_Trading.pdf&form_id=251&form_version=final (Eaton, Shkilko and Werner, Nocturnal Trading); venue and session structure: https://sapinover.com/overnight-101

## Cluster II: Behavioral, calendar, and exotic sentiment anomalies

### 18. Sunshine and cloud cover over the exchange city

**Mechanism:** Morning sunshine in the city of a country's leading stock exchange lifts mood; investors misattribute that good mood to good prospects, evaluate risk more optimistically, and bid prices up that day. Cloud cover does the reverse. Saunders (1993) first showed NYSE returns were lower on cloudy New York days; Hirshleifer and Shumway (2003) extended it to 26 exchanges, 1982-1997, finding sunshine strongly significantly correlated with daily returns (New York annualised return about 24.8% on perfectly sunny days vs about 8.7% on perfectly cloudy days), while rain and snow were unrelated after controlling for sunshine.

**Why consensus misses it:** The efficient-markets view says one day's cloud cover in one city cannot rationally change the value of a national index, so the result is filed as a curiosity. The authors themselves note frequent trading on it is killed by modest transaction costs, which lets desks dismiss it as untradeable even if statistically present. Later individual-investor work (Goetzmann and Zhu) found cloud cover correlated with index returns but not with individual investors' own trading, weakening the simple mood-trading story, and that tension is part of the record.

**Cheapest first test:** Alpaca daily bars for SPY/QQQ and Focus names vs free NOAA or Open-Meteo daily cloud-cover percentage for New York City (morning hours). Compare mean return on top-quintile sunny vs bottom-quintile cloudy days, deseasonalised against expected cloudiness for that day of year, exactly as Hirshleifer and Shumway do. Data assets: Alpaca bars, NOAA/Open-Meteo weather.

**Source:** http://www.tylergshumway.org/Hirshleifer-GoodDaySunshine-2003.pdf (Hirshleifer and Shumway, Good Day Sunshine, Journal of Finance 58, 2003)

### 19. Seasonal Affective Disorder: the daylight cycle in returns

**Mechanism:** Shortening days in fall and winter trigger seasonal depression in a clinically documented share of the population, and depression raises risk aversion. As nights lengthen through fall, investors shun risk and prices are depressed; as days lengthen after the winter solstice, risk aversion recedes and returns recover, producing a daylight-linked seasonal cycle in returns rather than a calendar-month effect per se. Kamstra, Kramer and Levi (2003) find returns significantly related to hours of daylight through fall and winter across multiple exchanges, stronger at higher latitudes, and six months out of phase in the Southern Hemisphere.

**Why consensus misses it:** It competes with better-known seasonals (January, tax-loss, Halloween) that are correlated with daylight, so mainstream work attributes the pattern to those. Critics argue SAD, temperature and generic seasonality measure a similar seasonal factor, so the specific SAD channel is treated as not separately identified.

**Cheapest first test:** Alpaca daily bars for SPY/QQQ/IWM vs a deterministic daylight-hours series for New York latitude (computable free from date and latitude). Regress fall and winter returns on the Kamstra-Kramer-Levi SAD variable (hours of night in excess of 12, fall/winter only), controlling for January and month dummies. A Southern Hemisphere ETF adds the latitude contrast cheaply. Data assets: Alpaca bars, computed solar series.

**Source:** https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=208622 (Kamstra, Kramer and Levi, Winter Blues: A SAD Stock Market Cycle, American Economic Review 93, 2003)

### 20. Daylight Saving Time change weekends

**Mechanism:** The spring and fall clock changes desynchronise sleep for the whole market on the same weekend. Sleep loss raises anxiety, impairs problem-solving and judgment, and pushes investors toward safety on the first session after the change. Kamstra, Kramer and Levi (2000) reported mean returns on DST weekends in the US, Canada, UK and Germany 200-500% larger in negative magnitude than on regular weekends, estimating a roughly $31bn one-day US market-cap loss for the spring change in their sample.

**Why consensus misses it, including the debunking record:** This is the most contested item in this cluster in the literature itself. Pinegar (2002) argued the result was driven by two international crises (Oct 1987, Oct 1997) and overstated significance. Gregory-Allen, Jacobsen, Marquering et al. (2009), testing 22 markets and about 1,150 DST events vs KKL's roughly 200, find no evidence of a DST effect once robust errors and weekend and month effects are handled. That debunking is recorded here as the literature's state, not as a verdict on a fresh test.

**Cheapest first test:** Alpaca daily bars for SPY/QQQ: Friday-close to Monday-close returns on the two US DST Mondays per year (second Sunday in March, first Sunday in November, post-2007 rule) vs all other Mondays, with crisis Mondays flagged separately. The tiny event count per year is the known power problem, so pool as many years as Alpaca history allows and pre-specify spring vs fall separately. Data asset: Alpaca bars.

**Source:** https://silo.tips/download/the-daylight-saving-time-anomaly-in-stock-returns-fact-or-fiction (Gregory-Allen, Jacobsen and Marquering 2009, re-testing Kamstra, Kramer and Levi, AER 90, 2000)

### 21. Lunar cycle: new moon vs full moon returns

**Mechanism:** Popular and some psychological literature links the full moon to disturbed sleep, depressed mood and erratic behaviour, the root of "lunacy." If mood moves marginal (especially individual) investors, returns should be lower around full moons and higher around new moons, with no fundamental, volume or volatility channel. Dichev and Janes (2003), on about 100 years of US data, found returns in the 15 days around new moons roughly double those around full moons. Yuan, Zheng and Zhu (2006), on 48 countries, found a 3-5% per annum new-minus-full difference, not explained by volatility, volume, macro announcements or other calendar anomalies, and stronger in small-cap, NASDAQ and emerging markets.

**Why consensus misses it:** No plausible rational channel, and a mechanism mainstream finance treats as astrology-adjacent, so it is rarely taught except as a data-snooping cautionary tale. Yuan et al. themselves flag data-snooping; later work (Keef and Khaled on 62 indices; Reschenhofer and Lingler 2013) finds a new-moon effect without a full-moon effect, or no significant lunar relationship at all. Both halves are the record.

**Cheapest first test:** Alpaca daily bars for SPY/QQQ/IWM vs a free lunar-phase calendar (computable from the 29.53-day synodic cycle, cross-checked against US Naval Observatory phases). Pre-specify exactly the Yuan et al. windows: 15-day and 7-day windows centred on new vs full moon, no window-shopping. Data assets: Alpaca bars, computed lunar calendar.

**Source:** https://personal.lse.ac.uk/YUAN/papers/lunar.pdf (Yuan, Zheng and Zhu, Are Investors Moonstruck?, Journal of Empirical Finance 13, 2006)

### 22. Geomagnetic storm activity and stock returns

**Mechanism:** Geomagnetic storms, disturbances of Earth's magnetic field from solar activity, are linked in psychological and medical literature to depression, anxiety and altered mood. Investors misattribute storm-induced bad mood to bad economic prospects, demand shifts toward riskless assets, and risky prices fall or rise less. Krivelyova and Robotti (Atlanta Fed, 2003) find unusually high geomagnetic activity in the previous week has a negative, statistically and economically significant effect on the following week's returns for all US indices and most international indices, with higher returns in quiet geomagnetic periods and a stronger effect in small caps (more individually held).

**Why consensus misses it:** It sits furthest from any cash-flow story, requires space-weather data no trading desk normally loads, and the proposed channel is mood misattribution rather than a direct fundamental hit, so it is categorised with exotic curiosities rather than risk factors, despite coming from a Federal Reserve Bank working paper.

**Cheapest first test:** Alpaca daily and weekly bars for SPY/QQQ/IWM vs NOAA Space Weather Prediction Center free geomagnetic indices (daily Ap/Kp and storm-day counts). Replicate the paper's timing: prior-week unusually high Ap (top decile or storm threshold) vs next-week returns, controlling for Monday, January and daylight variables as the paper does. Data assets: Alpaca bars, NOAA SWPC indices.

**Source:** https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/title/working-papers-federal-reserve-bank-atlanta-8586/playing-field-656780/fulltext (Krivelyova and Robotti, Playing the Field: Geomagnetic Storms and the Stock Market, Atlanta Fed WP 2003-5b)

### 23. National sports sentiment: tournament elimination losses

**Mechanism:** International sports results are a sudden, exogenous, nationally correlated mood shock affecting a large share of a country's population at once. Losses hurt more than wins help (loss aversion, plus elimination asymmetry: a win only advances, a loss ends the run). Edmans, Garcia and Norli (2007), on 1,162 international soccer matches across 39 countries, find a significant next-day market decline after losses, about minus 49 bp after a World Cup elimination loss in the published version, stronger in small stocks and more important games, with a loss effect also after international cricket, rugby and basketball, and no corresponding win effect.

**Why consensus misses it:** A soccer result has no cash-flow implication for listed firms, and the authors explicitly reject a productivity or revenue explanation, so a fundamentals desk has no slot for it. It is also an event study with mood as an unobserved intermediate, which mainstream work treats as suggestive behavioural evidence rather than a tradeable factor, and US-centric desks underweight it because the strongest sport-market pairing is non-US soccer.

**Cheapest first test:** Alpaca daily bars for SPY/QQQ/IWM on the session after US national-team World Cup or major tournament elimination losses, and for breadth, country ETFs (EWU, EWG, EWJ) after their national teams' elimination losses, using a free match-results list (FIFA or Wikipedia tournament pages). Pre-specify elimination games only, next trading day only. Data assets: Alpaca bars, public match records.

**Source:** http://www.efmaefm.org/0EFMSYMPOSIUM/2006/papers/76-EFM06 -Sports sentiment.pdf (Edmans, Garcia and Norli, Sports Sentiment and Stock Returns, Journal of Finance 62, 2007, working-paper version retrieved)

### 24. Music sentiment: a Spotify-based national mood index

**Mechanism:** Instead of pre-specifying a mood event, measure mood directly and continuously from revealed behaviour: the positivity (valence, from Spotify's audio-features algorithm) of the songs a country actually streams, stream-weighted over its Top 200. Edmans, Fernandez-Perez, Garel and Indriawan (2022), across 40 countries, 2017-2020, validate the measure against sunnier and lengthening days and loosening COVID restrictions, then find a 1-SD rise in music sentiment associated with plus 8.1 bp same-week national market return (4.3% annualised), followed by minus 7.0 bp the next week, a pattern they interpret as sentiment-induced temporary mispricing correcting, plus higher volatility on absolute sentiment, equity mutual fund inflows, and lower government bond returns.

**Why consensus misses it, including the replication record:** The proxy is endogenous (people may listen to happy music BECAUSE markets rose) and platform-dependent, so it does not fit event-study clean-identification norms. A 2026 replication (Weiberg and Heldmann, Finance Research Letters) replicates all main findings for 2017-2020 but finds that for 2021-2024 the one-week reversal disappears entirely, the sentiment-volatility link vanishes and the flight-to-safety relation inverts, which the replicators interpret as a structural break toward a more macro-dominated regime. Recorded as the literature's current state.

**Cheapest first test:** Free Spotify Charts or Spotify API Top 200 US daily valence (stream-weighted) vs Alpaca weekly (Friday-to-Friday, as in the replication) SPY/QQQ returns: same-week correlation and next-week reversal, run separately for pre-2021 and post-2021 splits to match the published design instead of pooling. Data assets: Spotify Charts/API, Alpaca bars.

**Source:** https://EconPapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:145:y:2022:i:2:p:234-254 (Edmans et al., Music Sentiment and Stock Returns Around the World, JFE 145, 2022); replication: https://epub.uni-bayreuth.de/9517/1/1-s2.0-S1544612326006008-main.pdf (Weiberg and Heldmann, FRL 102, 2026)

### 25. Prediction-market vs futures/options-market divergence on macro events

**Mechanism:** Kalshi (and Polymarket) trade contracts paying $1 if a specific CPI print, payroll outcome or FOMC decision occurs, so their prices ARE a full risk-neutral probability distribution, readable directly with minimal model assumptions, unlike fed funds futures (a single expected rate, cannot express a 50 bp outcome cleanly), surveys (stale point forecasts, no uncertainty), or options-implied densities (indirect, with risk-premia distortions, and for many macro variables no options exist at all). Diercks, Katz and Wright (Fed Board / NBER, Jan 2026) find Kalshi distributions are well-behaved and broadly consistent with established instruments, but in several episodes allocate probability mass in ways that may better reflect plausible outcomes; Kalshi's median/mode had a perfect forecast record the day before FOMC meetings in their sample (a statistically significant improvement over fed funds futures), and a statistically significant improvement over Bloomberg consensus for headline CPI. Where the prediction-market distribution persistently disagrees with the futures-implied distribution, one of the two participant pools (prediction markets skew retail; futures and options skew institutional and hedging) is pricing the tail differently, and that gap is the candidate equity-vol signal: for example the prediction market pricing materially more 50 bp-cut or hot-CPI tail than futures imply, ahead of an SPY/QQQ repricing.

**Why consensus misses it:** Until Kalshi's CFTC-regulated macro markets (launched 2021), prediction markets were thin, unregulated or election-focused, so macro finance in them remains underexplored in the authors' own words. Desks already have CME FedWatch, so a second odds source looks redundant; the paper's point is the instruments are not measuring the same object (full meeting-by-meeting distribution vs an expected-rate path).

**Cheapest first test:** Free public data only, no trading: Kalshi public API (CPI and FOMC markets) and Polymarket public odds, logged daily alongside CME FedWatch and fed funds futures-implied probabilities. Flag days where Kalshi vs futures disagree by a pre-set threshold (over 10 pp on the modal outcome, or materially different tail mass) in the week before a CPI or FOMC event, then check Alpaca SPY/QQQ returns over the event window against non-divergence events. Kalshi-vs-Polymarket disagreement on the same event is itself a data-quality flag in this design. Data assets: Kalshi API, Polymarket, CME FedWatch, Alpaca bars.

**Source:** https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w34702/w34702.pdf (Diercks, Katz and Wright, Kalshi and the Rise of Macro Markets, NBER WP 34702, Jan 2026; also FEDS 2026-010)

### 26. Pre-holiday effect and the January size seasonality, modern record

**Mechanism (two related calendar claims):** (a) Pre-holiday: returns on the trading day before a market holiday are unusually high. Ariel (1990) found pre-holiday returns 9-14x normal days, accounting for about a third of annual return in 1963-1982; Lakonishok and Smidt (1988), on 90 years of DJIA, found pre-holiday returns about 23x the normal daily rate, usually attributed to holiday mood and optimism and short-covering ahead of a closure. (b) January/size: tax-loss selling and window-dressing depress (especially small, individually held) losers in December; buying resumes in January, so small caps historically outperformed large caps disproportionately in January (Wachtel 1942; Rozeff and Kinney 1976; Keim 1983).

**Why consensus misses it, modern post-2015 record:** These are the textbook examples of publication decay. McLean and Pontiff (2016, Journal of Finance) show published predictors' returns fall 26% out-of-sample and 58% post-publication on average. For January specifically, Enow (2024), a post-2015 test on Nikkei 225, JSE, CAC 40, DAX and NASDAQ over Feb 2019 to Feb 2024, finds no significant January difference and concludes the effect is no longer evident in that sample, while tabulating the split post-2016 literature (no effect in Patel 2016, Perez 2018 across 86 countries finding an INVERTED January effect, Sahoo 2021 India; effect present in Hendrawaty and Huzaimah 2019 Indonesia, Nisar et al. 2021 UK). Both the original claim and its modern non-replication are part of the record. Note the estate's filed tax-loss convergence season plan trades December-January flows in beaten-down names; this concept is the index and size-spread calendar claim, a different object.

**Cheapest first test:** Alpaca daily bars: (i) SPY/QQQ/IWM returns on the roughly 8-10 US pre-holiday sessions per year vs all other sessions; (ii) IWM-minus-SPY return in January vs other months, and in the last week of December vs first week of January separately, to catch front-running shifting the window. Data asset: Alpaca bars.

**Source:** https://www.researchgate.net/publication/381372027_Revisiting_the_January_effect_anomaly_evidence_from_international_stock_markets (Enow 2024); decay framework: McLean and Pontiff, JF 71, 2016, https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jofi.12365

### 27. Temperature anomalies and air pollution in the exchange city

**Mechanism (two variants, same local-mood channel):** (a) Temperature: psychology links low temperature to aggression (more risk-taking) and high temperature to both aggression and apathy, with apathy dominating at high temperatures. Cao and Wei (2005), across many world markets, find a statistically significant negative temperature-return correlation across the whole temperature range, robust to Monday, tax-loss, cloud-cover and SAD controls. A 2019 PLOS ONE study adds a physical channel check: the negative hot-temperature effect on Dow returns in 1885-1914 largely weakened after the NYSE installed air conditioning in 1903. (b) Air pollution: fine particulate matter (PM2.5) impairs mood and cognition and lowers risk tolerance. Heyes, Neidell and Saberian (NBER 2016) link Manhattan PM2.5 to S&P 500 returns, 2000-2014: a 1-SD increase in PM2.5 reduces same-day returns by 11.9% (relative, in their preferred specification), raises VIX, rebounds within about a day, and critically only NEW YORK pollution matters, not pollution elsewhere, consistent with a strictly local effect on the concentrated pool of market influencers.

**Why consensus misses it:** Both variables are hyper-local to the exchange city while the index is national and global, which reads as implausible to a fundamentals desk; temperature is also entangled with seasonality, SAD and cloud cover, so it is usually absorbed into a generic seasonal dummy rather than tested as a daily anomaly. Air pollution at least carries a falsification structure (other cities' PM2.5 should not matter, and in the paper it does not), but the data (EPA monitors) sit outside every standard market dataset.

**Cheapest first test:** Alpaca daily bars for SPY/QQQ vs (i) free NOAA or Open-Meteo daily temperature for New York City (deviation from seasonal normal, not level, to separate it from SAD), and (ii) free EPA AirNow or OpenAQ daily PM2.5 for Manhattan. Test same-day return and next-day reversal separately, and run the paper's placebo for free: Chicago and Los Angeles PM2.5 on the same days should show no effect if the local channel is what is being measured. Data assets: Alpaca bars, NOAA/Open-Meteo, EPA AirNow/OpenAQ.

**Source:** http://www.yorku.ca/mcao/cao_wei_JBF.pdf (Cao and Wei, Stock Market Returns: A Note on Temperature Anomaly, JBF 29, 2005); https://EconPapers.repec.org/paper/nbrnberwo/22753.htm (Heyes, Neidell and Saberian, The Effect of Air Pollution on Investor Behavior, NBER WP 22753, 2016)

## Cluster III: Corporate-action and institutional plumbing

### 28. Corporate spinoffs: post-distribution drift and forced selling

**Mechanism:** When a parent distributes SpinCo pro-rata, every parent holder automatically receives SpinCo whether they want it or not. S&P 500 index funds, large-cap, sector and ESG funds whose mandate does not include the (usually smaller, often non-index) SpinCo must sell regardless of price, as do active holders for whom a 1-5% stub position is too small to research. That price-indifferent selling concentrates in the first 30-90 days into a thin, newly listed float with little analyst coverage and only carve-out financials in the Form 10-12B. Cusatis, Miles and Woolridge (1993) found about 13.4% first-year cumulative excess return for spinoffs in 1965-1988; Veld and Veld-Merkoulova's (2009) meta-analysis puts the announcement return at plus 3.02%. Later samples find a smaller, outlier-driven effect; the forced-selling dip in the first days after distribution is the most consistently documented leg.

**Why consensus misses it:** Sell-side coverage starts late, index and mandate selling is treated as "no information" and ignored rather than modelled as a supply schedule, and the investable window is defined by distribution mechanics (size of SpinCo relative to parent, index eligibility, when-issued trading) not by fundamentals. Screens that require clean quarterly financials systematically arrive after the dislocation.

**Cheapest first test:** Build the event list from SEC EDGAR full-text search for Form 10-12B and 8-K spin-off completion notices, record parent vs SpinCo market cap at distribution, then measure SpinCo abnormal return vs SPY on Alpaca daily bars from distribution date at +5/+21/+63/+126/+252 days, split by SpinCo at 5-25% of parent vs larger. Data assets: SEC EDGAR, Alpaca bars.

**Source:** https://alpha-suite.org/blog/spinoff-arbitrage-strategy

### 29. IPO lockup expiration: the known supply cliff

**Mechanism:** Insiders, employees and pre-IPO holders are contractually locked up, typically 180 days from pricing, disclosed in the S-1/424B4. While locked, float is artificially small. At expiry a large, known overhang becomes saleable at once. Field and Hanka (2001), across 1,948 lockups, document a statistically prominent minus 1.5% three-day abnormal return around expiry and a permanent plus 40% increase in average trading volume, both much larger for venture-capital-backed firms, where VCs distribute and sell more aggressively than executives. A 2007-2025 replication cited in the sources finds minus 1.97% over a (-10,+1) window, i.e. much of the drift occurs BEFORE day 0 as the market anticipates supply.

**Why consensus misses it:** The date is public months in advance, so it is assumed fully priced, yet the literature's puzzle is precisely that it is not fully priced. Mainstream IPO coverage focuses on pricing day and first earnings, not on float-supply calendars, staggered and early-release provisions, or the VC-vs-PE-vs-founder composition of the unlocking shares, which is where the cross-sectional variation lives.

**Cheapest first test:** Extract IPO pricing date and lockup terms from SEC EDGAR S-1/424B4 filings, approximate expiry as pricing plus 180 days mapped to the next trading day where the filing is silent, pull Alpaca bars and volume for (-20,+10) days around expiry vs SPY, and condition on unlock size as a percentage of float and on VC-backed status from the prospectus. Data assets: SEC EDGAR, Alpaca bars.

**Source:** https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v56y2001i2p471-500.html (Field and Hanka, Journal of Finance 56, 2001)

### 30. S&P 500 and Nasdaq-100 additions and deletions: the modern inclusion effect

**Mechanism:** Announcement (typically 5-7 business days before the effective date) forces every tracker to buy the addition and sell the deletion at the close, regardless of price. In the classic price-pressure story that inelastic demand produced plus 3.4% abnormal return in the 1980s and plus 7.4% in the 1990s, with deletions at minus 16.1%. Greenwood and Sammon (Journal of Finance, 2025, "The Disappearing Index Effect") find additions at about 1.0% in 2010-2020 and deletions at minus 0.6%, both statistically indistinguishable from zero in their sample. Two structural offsets explain the change in their account: over 70% of modern additions are migrations from the S&P MidCap 400 (MidCap trackers sell what S&P 500 trackers buy), and non-index institutions and liquidity providers now supply shares ahead of the effective date. Direct additions from outside the S&P family, with no offsetting sale, are reported separately at plus 5.4% in the 2010s in one cut cited in the sources: the migration-vs-direct distinction is the key conditional variable. Post-effective drift in a 450-event 2000-2025 practitioner study is negative at T+5 (minus 0.80%) and T+21 (minus 1.43%), consistent with a pre-effective run-up reversing. Russell reconstitution is a different index family and is already covered in this estate; it is excluded here.

**Why consensus misses it:** Folklore still trades "inclusion equals plus 7%" as a timeless law, while academic summaries that say "the effect is gone" average together migrations and direct adds, announcement window and post-effective window, and large liquid names with small illiquid ones, exactly the splits where any remaining effect would hide. Nasdaq-100 changes, with different selection rules and a heavy options and QQQ flow overlay, are usually lumped in with S&P 500 evidence despite different plumbing.

**Cheapest first test:** Collect S&P Dow Jones Indices press releases (announcement date, effective date, predecessor index) for 2020-2026 additions and deletions, classify migration vs direct, then use Alpaca bars to measure abnormal return vs SPY in three separate windows: announcement to effective, effective close, and +5/+21 days post-effective, never collapsing them into one number. Data assets: index provider announcements, Alpaca bars.

**Source:** https://wealthengine.blog/2026/10/07/sp-500-inclusion-twilio/ (practitioner synthesis citing Greenwood and Sammon, JF 2025)

### 31. Ticker and name changes: thematic mania and ticker confusion

**Mechanism:** A name is a salient, costless-to-change signal that screens, news algos, thematic ETFs and retail search all key off. Cooper, Dimitrov and Rau (2001) found about 74% cumulative abnormal return around ".com" renames in 1998-99 regardless of actual internet involvement. The pattern repeated for blockchain renames in 2017-19 (Long Island Iced Tea to Long Blockchain is the canonical case) and now for AI: Demir, Er and Hadad (2026 draft), across 38 AI renames in 10 markets, 2022-2025, find plus 10.3% abnormal return in early days and plus 22.8% over [0,10] under the market model, dissipating by about 30 days, concentrated in smaller and younger firms and strongest for firms with NO prior substantive AI activity and no subsequent AI activity. In parallel, plain ticker effects operate without any rename: Rashes (2001) documented excess comovement and mistaken trading between similar tickers (MCI vs MCIC), and ticker-fluency work links pronounceable, fluent names and tickers to broader investor recognition.

**Why consensus misses it:** Fundamental models treat a name as zero-information packaging, compliance treats "AI washing" (SEC warnings, 2024) as a disclosure problem rather than a flow event, and most event studies pool genuine pivots with pure relabels. The heterogeneity (prior activity, subsequent follow-through, firm size, hot-theme intensity at announcement) is the signal, and it requires hand-classifying the rename, not just flagging it.

**Cheapest first test:** Harvest name and ticker changes from SEC EDGAR 8-Ks, company press releases and exchange corporate-action notices, hand-tag theme (AI, blockchain, green, none) and prior-activity status from the filing text, then measure Alpaca-bar abnormal returns [-3,+3], [0,+10], [0,+30] vs SPY, split by theme vs non-theme renames matched on size. Add a confusion leg: list near-identical ticker pairs from the exchange symbol directory and check volume comovement on the larger pair member's news days. Data assets: SEC EDGAR, exchange notices, Alpaca bars.

**Source:** https://stolgaer.com/papers/ai_returns.pdf (Demir, Er and Hadad 2026 draft); fluency/confusion literature: https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/quaeco/v61y2016icp89-96.html

### 32. Forward stock splits in the fractional era, and reverse splits as the mirror

**Mechanism:** A split changes no cash flows; the classic stories are signalling (management splits only if it expects the high price to persist: Ikenberry, Rankine and Stice 1996 find about plus 7.9% in year 1 and plus 12.1% over 3 years for 2-for-1 splits), attention (headline splits draw gradual retail buying, and fractional-share and options-contract accessibility improves at lower nominal prices), and liquidity and clientele change. The modern mega-cap wave (AAPL 4-for-1 and TSLA 5-for-1 in 2020, AMZN and GOOGL 20-for-1 in 2022, NVDA 10-for-1 in 2024) revived the folklore. A 1,968-event 2000-2025 study finds plus 1.31% in the 5 days BEFORE the effective date (t=3.81), no significant post-split drift in aggregate (plus 0.58% at 1 year, t=0.72), with 2-for-1 splits the exception at plus 2.58% at 1 year (t=2.45). Announcement-to-effective, not post-effective, is where most movement sits. Reverse splits are the mirror plumbing: usually distress and listing-compliance driven, often preceded by decline, and data tables frequently mis-encode them as forward splits, a universe-cleaning problem the source documents in detail.

**Why consensus misses it:** Retail and media coverage conflate announcement return, pre-effective run-up and post-split drift into one "splits are bullish" claim, pool operating companies with ETF and fund share splits, and ignore ratio and market-cap conditioning. Academic drift estimates are pre-2020 and pre-fractional-trading; the nominal-price clientele channel has changed since zero commissions and fractional shares.

**Cheapest first test:** Build a clean split calendar from SEC EDGAR 8-Ks and exchange notices (announcement date AND ex-date, ratio, operating company vs fund, with extreme-ratio records verified against the filing), then use Alpaca bars to measure abnormal return vs SPY separately for announcement [+1,+5], announcement-to-ex, and post-ex [+21,+63,+252], stratified by 2-for-1 vs 3-4-for-1 vs 5-for-1-plus and by market cap; run reverse splits as a separate, oppositely signed sample. Data assets: SEC EDGAR, Alpaca bars.

**Source:** https://blog.cetaresearch.com/stock-split-performance-event-study/

### 33. Buyback blackout windows and actual repurchase pace from 10-Qs

**Mechanism:** Most issuers self-impose a blackout on open-market repurchases in the roughly 2-4 weeks before earnings, when they possess material non-public information, operating instead only under pre-set Rule 10b5-1 plans adopted in an open window; Rule 10b-18 provides the volume, price and timing safe harbour (at most 25% of ADV, no opening or closing prints) when they do buy. The result is a recurring, calendar-predictable withdrawal and return of what is, in aggregate, the largest single buyer in US equities (about $1T per year for the S&P 500): softness into earnings from the missing bid is the practitioner hypothesis, re-engagement after the print is the tailwind leg. Separately, authorisations are cheap talk; the EXECUTED pace disclosed quarterly in the 10-Q/10-K issuer-repurchase table (shares bought, average price, remaining authorisation) is hard data. Acceleration into a drawdown, buying below prior repurchase prices, or quietly letting an authorisation lapse each convey different information, and announcements cluster 5-10 business days after earnings precisely because the blackout has lifted.

**Why consensus misses it:** Research and news count authorisations, not execution; blackout calendars are firm-specific and undisclosed as a formal schedule, so aggregate flow models smooth over a lumpy, earnings-calendar-driven bid; and 10b5-1 adoption, which mutes the blackout effect for that name, is buried in 10-Q language and rarely coded as a variable.

**Cheapest first test:** For a Focus-universe panel, collect earnings dates, scrape the repurchase table and 10b5-1 language from SEC EDGAR 10-Q/10-K filings quarter by quarter to compute executed dollars over authorisation and implied daily pace, then use Alpaca bars to compare returns and volume in the [-20,-1] pre-earnings window vs the [+1,+20] post-earnings window for heavy executors vs authorisation-only names, conditioning on disclosed 10b5-1 status. Data assets: SEC EDGAR, Alpaca bars.

**Source:** https://buybackstocks.com/learn/when-do-companies-buy-back-stock/

### 34. Congressional trading disclosures (STOCK Act), tested at the disclosure date

**Mechanism:** Under the STOCK Act (2012), members of Congress must file Periodic Transaction Reports within 45 days of a trade over $1,000, in dollar brackets, including spouse and dependent trades. The copycat hypothesis is that committee, briefing and legislative knowledge leaks into trades before it is public. The literature arc is sharply period-dependent: Ziobrowski et al. found about 10-12% per year abnormal return for Senate portfolios in 1993-98 and about 6% for the House in 1985-2001, measured from TRANSACTION date, pre-STOCK Act. Chen and Sacerdote (NBER, 2026), hand-collecting all PTRs 2012-2023 (334 legislators, 3,403 firms, about 71,000 trades), find the average trade lags the market by about 26 bp over a year, party leaders underperform by about 4.4 pp and committee-matched trades by about 2.8 pp at 1 year, with trade direction instead following prior analyst revisions and StockTwits attention. Recent conditioned work re-opens one narrow leg: returns, where found, concentrate BEFORE disclosure, and in committee-jurisdiction SALES preceding declines, which matters because a copier only gets the disclosure, a median 27 days late in a 2014-2026 House sample, 13% of it after the 45-day deadline.

**Why consensus misses it:** Headlines and copycat products (the NANC and KRUZ ETFs, about $650M in mimicking vehicles by March 2026 per the source) market the 1990s transaction-date result as if it were a disclosure-date, post-STOCK Act result. The investable question, what is left after a 27-45 day lag, in brackets, with no exact size or price, is a different test from "did the member beat the market," and conditioning by person, committee and buy-vs-sell is usually skipped in favour of copying all of Congress at once. This estate has no Congress wire at all; the gap was named in the November records.

**Cheapest first test:** Download House and Senate PTRs from the official congressional disclosure datasets, stamp each trade with DISCLOSURE date (not transaction date), and measure Alpaca-bar abnormal returns from disclosure date at +5/+21/+63 days, split by buy vs sell, committee-jurisdiction match (committee rosters by quarter), and member activity level, with the all-Congress pooled portfolio as the null comparison. Data assets: congressional disclosure datasets, Alpaca bars.

**Source:** https://www.scienceofmoney.org/do-members-of-congress-trade-on-inside-information-a-new-study-says-they-re-trad-553/ (synthesis of Chen and Sacerdote, NBER 2026)

### 35. 13F plumbing: disclosure lag, herding, and quarter-end window dressing

**Mechanism:** Managers over $100M in 13(f) securities disclose long US equity positions quarterly, 45 days after quarter-end: a stale, long-only snapshot with no shorts, no cash, no non-US or bond exposure, options without strike or expiry economics, and confidential treatment that can hide a building position for several quarters (Berkshire's Chubb position, built from July 2023 and revealed May 2024, is the documented example). Two predictable distortions sit on top of the lag. (1) Herding: Lakonishok, Shleifer and Vishny (1992) career-risk herding, managers hold what peers hold, strongest in small caps and thin-information names, creates crowded "hedge fund hotels" whose exits are correlated. (2) Window dressing: Agarwal, Gay and Ling (Review of Financial Studies, 2014), across 2,623 funds, 1998-2008, show poorly performing, less-skilled managers disproportionately buy the quarter's winners and sell its losers into the snapshot, churn excessively, and perform worse afterward; Carhart et al. (2002) document the related last-day price-marking pattern ("leaning for the tape"). The filing-date information dividend itself is short: one study cited in the sources finds a small positive excess return only in about 2 days after disclosure.

**Why consensus misses it:** 13F coverage is consumed as a real-time "smart money bought X" feed, ignoring the 45-plus-90-day staleness, the missing short and hedge leg, passive arithmetic (index funds' near-identical percentage ownership across mega-caps is tracking, not conviction), and the fact that the snapshot date is the one date holdings are actively managed for optics. Crowding is reported as validation rather than as correlated-exit risk.

**Cheapest first test:** Pull 13F information tables from SEC EDGAR (free bulk filings), build quarter-over-quarter new, exit and crowded-name flags (count of filers initiating the same name), join to Alpaca bars to test (a) filing-date [+1,+2] drift for new positions vs adds, and (b) turn-of-quarter behaviour: last-3-days vs first-3-days returns and volume for the quarter's winners heavily held by mutual funds vs matched losers (the window-dressing fingerprint), plus next-quarter reversal of dressed names inferred from the following 13F. Data assets: SEC EDGAR 13F bulk data, Alpaca bars.

**Source:** https://corpgov.law.harvard.edu/2014/09/17/window-dressing-in-mutual-funds/ (Agarwal, Gay and Ling, RFS 2014); filing-lag summary: https://longbridge.com/topics/100000000858858?channel=n287511213

### 36. Odd-lot tender offers at a premium

**Mechanism:** In an issuer or third-party tender, SEC Rule 14d-10(f) and Rule 13e-4 permit an odd-lot provision: holders of fewer than 100 shares who tender ALL their shares get priority acceptance, exempt from proration, at the same best price as everyone else. Fixed-price tenders average about 10.7% premiums and Dutch-auction tenders about 4.8% in the literature cited in the sources; Lakonishok and Vermaelen (1990) and Larcker and Lys (1987) document odd-lot holders capturing the full premium while large holders are prorated. Because the position is capped at 99 shares, maximum dollar profit per account is a few hundred dollars, economically meaningless to an institution and material to a small account, so the spread is not competed away. The sibling plumbing: special dividends and return-of-capital distributions run on similar mechanics, where the ex-date price adjustment is mechanical but retail holders and some data and options systems mis-handle ROC characterisation and special-dividend contract adjustments, creating ex-date pricing noise distinct from ordinary dividends.

**Why consensus misses it:** It is structurally unscalable, so no institutional research covers it, no fund can run it, and it lives only in the text of SC TO-I and SC TO-T filings; there is no screen for "odd-lot provision: yes" in standard terminals. Frequency is low (a handful of actionable offers per year), so it never appears in factor or event-study datasets built from price data alone.

**Cheapest first test:** Scan SEC EDGAR full-text search daily for SC TO-I and SC TO-T filings containing "odd-lot" and "priority," extract tender price, deadline and conditions, compare to the current Alpaca quote to compute the premium on a 99-share lot, and paper-track acceptance and completion vs withdrawn offers. For the special-dividend leg: pull distribution notices from EDGAR 8-Ks, flag special and ROC distributions over 5% of price, and measure the ex-date drop vs the distribution amount on Alpaca bars. Data assets: SEC EDGAR, Alpaca quotes and bars.

**Source:** https://github.com/korroai/oddly/blob/HEAD/PAPER.md

### 37. ETF creation/redemption quirks: premium/discount, AP behaviour, and illiquidity transmission

**Mechanism:** Authorised Participants keep ETF price near NAV by creating shares when the ETF trades at a premium (deliver the basket, receive ETF shares) and redeeming at a discount (reverse). The arbitrage only works if the basket can actually be traded at NAV prices. When the underlying is illiquid, halted, closed (foreign hours) or stressed, the band widens, persistently for niche ETFs holding illiquid assets and acutely in stress. March 2020 is the canonical episode: US investment-grade corporate bond ETFs averaged a 3.4% discount to NAV in mid-March, some over 8%, for about 20 trading days. The practitioner and issuer literature (Invesco; BlackRock evidence to the SEC) argues the NAV was the stale number, marked off non-executable dealer quotes, and the ETF price was real-time price discovery for the underlying, a reading supported by discounts collapsing after the Fed's SMCCF (which could buy corporate bond ETFs) was announced, flipping to premiums in early April. The same sources report APs did NOT step away in that episode in Invesco's experience and ICMA's survey (active AP count rose), so "AP step-away" is a hypothesis to test per episode, not a given. The transmission leg runs both ways: heavy ETF redemption in an illiquid underlying forces basket selling, while a large persistent discount is itself a tradeable signal about underlying marks.

**Why consensus misses it:** Premium/discount is reported as a single daily percentage vs a NAV whose construction (evaluated pricing, fair-value marks, cash vs in-kind baskets, custom redemption baskets) is not examined; equity-ETF intuition (tight bands, transparent baskets) is imported into bond, loan, muni, commodity and single-country ETFs where it does not hold; and in stress, commentators label the discount an ETF failure rather than asking which price, ETF or NAV, subsequently proves right when the underlying trades again.

**Cheapest first test:** Use issuer-published daily premium/discount histories (iShares, State Street, Invesco fund pages) for a panel of niche bond, loan and muni ETFs plus LQD and HYG as liquid controls, join to Alpaca bars for the ETF and to a proxy for the underlying, and test: (a) persistence, how many days a discount over 1% survives outside stress; (b) in stress episodes, whether the underlying index or NAV subsequently moves toward the ETF price (the price-discovery direction); (c) creation and redemption flow data from issuer files around discount spikes as the AP step-away or step-in measure. Data assets: issuer premium/discount and flow files, Alpaca bars.

**Source:** https://www.invesco.com/content/dam/invesco/apac/en/pdf/insights/2026/october/invesco-how-the-etf-market-structure-holds-up-during-extreme-volatility-part3-october-2026.pdf
