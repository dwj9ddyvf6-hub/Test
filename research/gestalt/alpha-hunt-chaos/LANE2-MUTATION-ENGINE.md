# ALPHA HUNT CHAOS LAB, LANE 2: THE MUTATION ENGINE

Owner order Sun 2026-11-15: generate candidates by mutating the existing fleet, at scale, under sealed selection. This lane delivers the design and a working prototype. No trading, no desk code changes; the desk tree was read only.

SCOPE: Surface = this design, the operator catalog, the generator spec, the sealed funnel spec, the novelty archive spec, and a prototype that emits real mutated candidate records. Constraints = read-only on `~/workspace/muse-desk-v3`; candidates are records, not code; nothing here enters any book except through the funnel in Section 4 and the estate's existing falsifier and promotion machinery. Outcome = a generator that can produce thousands of structured, countable candidate trials from the real fleet, feeding a selection process whose arithmetic is honest about multiple comparisons. Proof = prototype output on disk (Section 6), deterministic rerun verified by hash. Edges = the evaluator (replay/shadow resolution) is other lanes' machinery (the expression shadow-ledger resolver, Lane 8, stays build priority one); this engine generates and registers, it does not grade its own children.

## 1. Why mutation, in one paragraph

The generate-and-test arithmetic (Section 5) says raw random ideas carry a true-edge rate pi of roughly 0.1 to 2 percent per effectively independent candidate, so hundreds to thousands of candidates are needed before even one real edge is likely. Uniform random generation wastes that budget on candidates with no mechanism at all. Mutation of the existing fleet starts every candidate one step from something that already parses, already has data, already has an exit discipline and a cost model. The parent's machinery is inherited; only the mutated joint is new. That raises pi per draw and, just as important, makes each candidate cheap to evaluate because the parent's harness, universe plumbing, and journaling already exist. The chaos is deliberate but it is chaos applied to working machines, not noise typed into a void.

## 2. Mutation operator catalog (16 operators)

Each operator lists its mechanism, the reason it could plausibly hold edge, and its correlation note (what it does to the candidate's independence from its parent and siblings). Operators compose: a candidate carries 1 to 3.

1. **INVERT_SIGNAL.** Flip every fire to the opposite side. Mechanism: a sleeve that reliably loses after costs is a signal read backwards; a sleeve whose edge decayed may now mark the crowded side. Correlation: perfectly anti-correlated with the parent by construction, counts as a genuinely different hypothesis, not a sibling tweak.
2. **DROP_VARIABLE.** Remove one variable, filter, or constant from the parent's rule (a trend filter, an earnings veto, one indicator leg). Mechanism: overfit parents often carry a variable that was load-bearing in-sample and pure drag out-of-sample; the ablation is the test the original build never ran. Correlation: high with parent; the information is in the difference, so parent and mutant are graded as a pair.
3. **BOLT_RANDOM_VARIABLE.** Attach a variable that has no business mattering (lunar phase bucket, ticker letter-count parity, prime-day flag, Fibonacci-day flag) as a gate, veto, size modulator, or entry condition. Mechanism: mostly a control. If nonsense gates systematically change outcomes, the parent's edge was regime luck wearing a costume; occasionally a nonsense variable is a proxy for a real calendar or microstructure effect nobody modeled. Correlation: the bolted variable is independent of everything, so any result difference is attributable.
4. **CROSSOVER.** Entry logic from parent A, exit and risk logic from parent B (the plan's QD mutation set, made concrete). Mechanism: entries and exits are separable skills; the fleet's best entries and best exits have never met. Correlation: creates a new family cluster (A-entry x B-exit); two crossovers sharing both parents are siblings.
5. **REGIME_GATE_SCRAMBLE.** Replace, invert, or randomize the regime gate (trend, vol percentile, VIX bucket, breadth, coin flip, inverted trend). Mechanism: many sleeves are one regime call away from their mirror performance; the coin-flip gate is the honest control that prices what the parent's gate actually earns. Correlation: medium; gate changes decorrelate returns more than parameter tweaks do.
6. **TIMEFRAME_SHIFT.** Multiply cadence or bar length by a factor (0.25x to 288x). Mechanism: edges live at specific horizons because specific counterparties are forced to trade there; the same rule at a foreign horizon faces a different opponent set. Correlation: medium-low across large shifts, high for adjacent ones.
7. **EXPRESSION_SWAP.** Same signal, different instrument form: shares to call vertical, put vertical, condor, calendar, diagonal, collar, and back. Mechanism: the estate has already proven expression is a separate edge dimension (the overnight Focus-names effect is dead in options form and alive in auction-executed shares). Correlation: the signal is shared, the payoff is not; count as a half-independent draw.
8. **ESTIMATOR_SWAP.** Replace every smoother with a different estimator class (SMA, EMA, Wilder, median, VWAP, Hull, KAMA, trimmed mean, rank percentile). Mechanism: estimator choice is usually an accident of whoever wrote the parent; median and rank forms are robust to the fat tails that break mean-based rules. Correlation: high; treat as sibling unless paired with another operator.
9. **NEARMISS_FLIP.** Trade the parent's near-miss log instead of its fires: the signals that almost qualified. Mechanism: the near-miss set is private data in no training set; if the parent's threshold sits slightly wrong, the near-misses carry the edge the fires dilute, and if the threshold is right, the near-miss arm is the falsifier that proves it. Correlation: disjoint event set from the parent, so outcomes are close to independent draws on the same underlying process.
10. **PARAM_JITTER.** Multiply one numeric constant by a random factor in 0.5x to 1.5x. Mechanism: maps the local curvature of the parent's performance surface; a parent whose edge survives jitter is robust, one that dies was a lucky point. Correlation: highest of all operators; jitter siblings are the main effective-N leak and are discounted hardest in Section 5.
11. **PARAM_EXTREME.** Push one constant to a tail value (0.05x, 0.1x, 5x, 10x). Mechanism: the opposite probe to jitter; extreme settings convert a swing rule into an event rule or a scalper into a position trade, which is a different strategy wearing the parent's clothes. Correlation: medium; tail pushes decorrelate more than their single-parameter change suggests.
12. **GATE_DROP.** Remove a protective gate entirely (earnings veto, BP buffer, session gate, position cap). Mechanism: some gates were added after one bad day and have taxed every day since; the mutant prices the gate. Correlation: high with parent; graded as a pair like DROP_VARIABLE.
13. **SESSION_SHIFT.** Move execution to a different session slice (premarket, overnight only, first hour, last hour, 24h). Mechanism: the estate's one proven decomposition edge is overnight vs intraday; session is a proven axis of difference, not a cosmetic one. Correlation: medium-low; overnight and RTH versions of one rule face different liquidity and different counterparties.
14. **HOLD_INVERT.** Flip flat-by-close, or invert the holding rule (hold winners becomes hold losers, time stop becomes time entry delay). Mechanism: exit timing is where most documented sleeves quietly give back their entry edge. Correlation: medium.
15. **UNIVERSE_SWAP.** Run the parent's rule on a foreign universe class (index only, Focus 30, crypto spot, the inverse of the parent's universe, a random 50 of the S&P 500). Mechanism: a rule that only works on its home universe may be a universe effect mislabeled as a signal; a rule that travels is a mechanism. Correlation: disjoint names, near-independent outcomes.
16. **EXIT_ENTRY_SWAP.** The parent's exit trigger becomes the entry trigger. Mechanism: the purest "do the opposite" operator; exit rules fire at statistically special moments (capitulation, exhaustion, target hits), and nobody in the fleet trades those moments as entries. Correlation: event set overlaps the parent's exits, not its entries; near-independent.

Prototype coverage: the prototype implements all 16. PARAM_JITTER falls back to TIMEFRAME_SHIFT on parents whose source constants could not be extracted (see Section 6), which is recorded in the operator detail when it happens.

## 3. Generator spec

**Parent pools, in priority order.**
- P1: the desk 339. Source of record: `~/workspace/muse-desk-v3/config/sleeves.json` (339 records: id, module, cadence, session, capital slice, flat-by-close, params, costs, rebalance policy), enriched with module-level numeric constants AST-extracted from the strategy sources under `sleeves/`, `desk_sleeves/`, `aster_sleeves/`, `laz_sleeves/`, `desk3/`, `adapters*/` (no imports executed; the house rule against importing terminal modules is honored by parsing, never importing).
- P2: the book's families and live cards (Midna book pods, Ghost Book expressions).
- P3: the candidate backlog as seed records: F1 to F24, G1 to G22, D1 to D9, E1 to E5, the 27-entry innovation queue, the 20 t474u sleeves. These enter as parents too, because a shadow candidate mutated before its first grade is still a countable trial, and its trial index must exist before anyone sees its results.

**Candidate record schema (one JSON object per candidate, append-only).**
- `candidate_id`: content hash of the full record (MUT- plus 12 hex chars in the prototype).
- `trial_index`: sequential integer assigned at generation, before any evaluation. The register is the sequence; a candidate that exists has an index, and an index that exists is never reused or deleted.
- `parents`: one or two parent ids.
- `operators_applied`: list of {op, detail} with before/after values wherever a parameter changed.
- `parameter_set`: the complete mutated configuration (module, cadence, session, capital, flat-by-close, params, constants, plus every operator-written field). A candidate must be fully specified by this field alone; anything left implicit is a spec defect.
- `descriptors`: the behavioral descriptor vector of Section 5's archive (direction, timeframe, expression, regime gate, session, trigger source).
- `family_cluster`: the correlation cluster used for effective-N discounting (parent module family; crossovers get the ordered pair).
- `generation`: seed, generator version, timestamp.

**Sampling policy: directed diversity, not uniform random.**
- Parent selection is weighted: parents in under-occupied novelty cells and parents whose previous mutants produced high descriptor distance get more draws; the adapters3 mass (268 of 339 parents) is capped per campaign so one plumbing family cannot flood the register with near-identical siblings.
- Operator selection is weighted toward the decorrelating operators (INVERT_SIGNAL, NEARMISS_FLIP, EXIT_ENTRY_SWAP, BOLT_RANDOM_VARIABLE, DROP_VARIABLE at weight 3 in the prototype) and away from PARAM_JITTER (weight 1), because jitter buys the least independence per trial.
- Operator count per candidate: 1 to 3, weighted 5/3/2. Single-operator candidates keep attribution clean; three-operator candidates are the deep-chaos tail.
- Campaign sizing comes from Section 5's arithmetic, set before the campaign runs, and the campaign stops at its registered size. Generating more candidates after seeing results is a new campaign with a new register segment, never a silent extension.

**Registration rule.** Generation and registration are the same act. The generator writes the record to the trial register (the estate's master Trial Register from the Consensus Lens program, Phase 0 skeleton, is the file of record; this engine's output appends to it) before any evaluator may read the candidate. An unregistered candidate that gets evaluated anyway is a process defect graded in the nightly audit, because it corrupts the denominator.

## 4. The sealed selection funnel

Five stages. A candidate may only move forward; no stage may be re-run with adjusted parameters on the same candidate (that is a new candidate and a new trial).

- **Stage 0, Register.** Candidate exists with a trial index. Nothing is scored.
- **Stage 1, Mechanical screen (cheap, sealed rules).** Fully specified parameter set; data exists for its universe and timeframe; expression is priceable on the estate's routes; not a byte-duplicate of an existing register row. This screen reads no performance data.
- **Stage 2, Historical screen (the dangerous stage, handled as such).** Replay on stored data with the estate's corrected-cost standard (executable-side pricing, the Wave 4/5 auction-cost corrections where relevant). Results are recorded for the whole cohort, never browsed per-candidate by a human before the cohort closes. Cohort-level Benjamini-Hochberg / FDR framing; per-candidate claims from this stage are forbidden. PBO computed on the cohort as an audit of the selection process itself, target under 0.2.
- **Stage 3, Forward shadow.** Survivors run forward in shadow with pre-registered falsifiers (sample size and checkpoint date written at Stage 3 entry). Scoring is on intermediate outcomes first (signal-day follow-through, next-day move, event outcomes; IC and Brier), because the power math (Section 5) makes per-candidate return verdicts impossible on any useful clock. Cohort verdicts only: a family cluster is promoted or demoted as a cohort against its registered bar.
- **Stage 4, Promotion gate.** A cohort advances to paper-probe only if: deflated Sharpe at least 0.95 computed at the cohort's true effective N (Section 5), HLZ t above 3 on the cohort's primary metric, the cohort's falsifier checkpoint passed, and family-wise arbitration at B5 picks at most one form per family. Promotion size is the standing probe fraction; Amendment 16 stress-loss sizing and L-07 govern from the first paper fill, unchanged.

**No-peeking discipline.** Evaluation windows are sealed at registration. Interim looks are budgeted in advance (the arithmetic: 5 peeks inflate a 5 percent false-positive rate to about 14 percent); any unscheduled look is logged in the register as a peek event and the cohort's alpha is spent accordingly. A peeked cohort that still passes is graded on its peek-adjusted bar, not the nominal one.

## 5. The statistical spine (from the 5.10 generate-and-test arithmetic)

All numbers from `~/memory/2026-11-14.md` section 5.10, the Morillo report's generate-and-test treatment.

- **Base rate.** Plan pi at 0.1 to 2 percent true edge per effectively independent candidate. For a 95 percent chance of at least one real edge: 149 candidates at pi of 2 percent, 2,995 at pi of 0.1 percent; at pi of 1 percent, N of 300 gives P of 95.1 percent and 3.0 expected edges.
- **The luck ceiling.** The False Strategy Theorem: the best of 1,000 worthless trials shows Sharpe about 3.26 by luck; best-of-340 on one year about 2.9. Any single mutant printing a Sharpe under those ceilings for its cohort size has proven nothing yet.
- **Deflated Sharpe bar.** DSR at least 0.95 at true N. The worked example disciplines intuition: a Sharpe 2.5 candidate passes only if it was found within at most 46 trials. In a 1,500-trial campaign, the same Sharpe is expected luck.
- **Effective N, the mutant discount.** Nominal trials are not independent trials. Candidates sharing a parent are correlated draws; candidates sharing a family cluster more so. The register therefore tracks, per cohort: nominal N, cluster count, and effective N computed with a correlation discount (sibling clusters from one parent count as roughly 1 plus (k minus 1) times (1 minus rho) draws, with rho estimated from sibling outcome correlation at Stage 2 and floored at 0.5 for jitter-only siblings until measured). All DSR and t computations use effective N, never nominal. Campaign sizing works backwards: to field 300 effective candidates at an average discount factor of 3 to 5, generate 900 to 1,500 nominal. That is the first campaign's registered size range.
- **Forward power.** About 785 independent resolved bets to detect a 55 percent hit rate; about 31 years to verdict one Sharpe-0.5 sleeve alone. Consequences: no per-candidate forward verdicts, ever; verdicts live at cohort level and on intermediate outcomes (score on earnings surprises and event outcomes at IC near 10 percent, not on returns at IC near 1 percent). The funnel's Stage 3 is built on this, not on patience.
- **Directed diversity premium.** The 5.10 treatment's closing point is this lane's charter: mutation, crossover, inversion, and regime-gating of the existing fleet plus novelty archives beat uniform random by orders of magnitude in pi per draw, and the hidden tax on generated candidates is correlation, which is why the register, the cluster discount, and the archive exist.

## 6. Novelty archive (quality-diversity, MAP-Elites shape)

- **Descriptor axes** (the prototype's six, extensible): direction (as-parent / inverted), timeframe (intraday / swing / daily / weekly), expression (shares / verticals / condor / calendar / diagonal / collar / cash-secured put), regime gate (parent default / named gate / coin flip / inverted), session (rth / 24h / overnight / premarket / first hour / last hour), trigger source (fires / near-misses / exit events).
- **Cells.** The archive is the grid over these axes. Each cell holds the best-scoring registered candidate for that cell (by its stage-appropriate score) plus the count of trials that landed there. Empty cells adjacent to occupied ones are the generator's preferred targets next campaign: novelty search's core move, abandoning the objective gradient for the unexplored cell.
- **Fleet baseline.** The parent fleet itself occupies few cells: the 339 parents, described on these axes, collapse onto a thin slab (overwhelmingly as-parent direction, shares expression, fires trigger, parent-default gate). That thinness is the finding: the fleet explores almost none of its own descriptor space, which is exactly the space this engine exists to fill.
- **Prototype measurement.** Of the prototype's 100 candidates, 59 landed in cells empty in the parent fleet, across 43 distinct cells, from 90 distinct parents. Even weighted, capped, first-pass generation triples the occupied map. The full archive run at campaign scale reports cell occupancy as a headline number beside the trial count.

## 8. Open rows and honest limits

- **Constants coverage.** Only 84 of 339 parents expose module-level constants in their own source file. The adapter families (268 records) need an extractor that follows the adapter to its strategy target before PARAM_JITTER, PARAM_EXTREME, and DROP_VARIABLE can bite on real parameters instead of structural fields. Named, not hidden: until then, adapter-parent mutants are structural mutants.
- **Near-miss data.** NEARMISS_FLIP presumes per-sleeve near-miss logs exist and are addressable. The estate has near-miss material in the dispatcher record and book journals, but a uniform per-sleeve near-miss feed is not yet a file of record; Stage 1's mechanical screen must check it per candidate and fail the candidate closed, not invent the data.
- **Evaluator throughput is the real bottleneck.** This engine can register 1,500 candidates in minutes. The estate's own falsifier, from Saturday's Alpha Hunt, stands: if resolution throughput does not move, generation is theater. The funnel is deliberately cohort-based and intermediate-outcome-based to keep evaluation cost per candidate near zero until Stage 3.
- **No candidate in this prototype has been evaluated.** The 100 records are generation proof only. Any performance claim about any of them today would be exactly the failure this design exists to prevent.

## 9. First campaign, as it would run

1. Register the campaign in the Trial Register: size 1,200 nominal candidates, parent pools P1 plus P3, operator weights as prototyped, seed fixed and recorded.
2. Generate, register, Stage 1 screen (expect a mechanical fail tail; every fail stays in the register with its reason).
3. Stage 2 cohort replay under corrected costs; cohort closes before any human reads per-candidate rows; BH/FDR and PBO printed with the cohort.
4. Stage 3 forward shadow for surviving cohorts with falsifiers and checkpoints written at entry; intermediate-outcome scoring from the first session.
5. First cohort read at a Friday B5 with denominators printed: nominal N, effective N, cells occupied, peeks taken. The campaign's own falsifier: if two consecutive campaigns produce no cohort that clears Stage 3's registered bar, the mutation premise is demoted and the multiplication moves to resolution throughput, per the hunt's standing falsifier.
