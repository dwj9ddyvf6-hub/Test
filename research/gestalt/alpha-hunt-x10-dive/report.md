## 1. Auction theory applied to market opens and closes, and closing auction imbalances

### What the mechanism claims

A batch auction collects orders and clears them at one price that maximises matched volume. Auction theory predicts two tradeable consequences. First, the published imbalance before the clear is public information about latent supply and demand at the indicative price, so the move from the pre auction reference price to the auction price should be partly predictable in the final minutes. Second, flow that is forced and price insensitive, such as index rebalance and benchmark market on close flow, pushes the auction price away from the price that continuous trading would have set, creating either continuation into the auction or reversal after it, depending on whether the flow is informed or purely mechanical.

The structural fact is not in dispute. BMLL, in a Traders Magazine piece dated 24 June 2025, reports that on a typical day in 2024 US exchanges matched an average of 50 billion dollars in notional volume in the closing auction, roughly 9 percent of total daily trading volume, and about 20 percent on index rebalance or options expiration days. On the 28 June 2024 Russell Reconstitution, total volume was 65 percent higher than the June average and over 220 percent higher in the final 30 minutes. A separate Tethys auction report for August 2025 gives six month median closing auction volume shares of 12.43 percent for NYSE listed and 10.12 percent for Nasdaq listed securities, measured against total traded volume on the primary listing basis it defines. The exact percentages differ by definition. The direction does not: the close is now the largest single liquidity event of the day.

### Strongest evidence for

1. Predictability inside the closing auction window. An Imperial College London MSc thesis by Clea Morand (September 2020), using Nasdaq and NYSE imbalance dissemination in the last ten minutes before the close for S and P 500 and Russell 2000 stocks, reports that linear models on imbalance data predict the forward return to the close, that predictability rises as the market on close and limit on close cutoffs approach, and that the model captures about 30 percent of the spread, rising in one reported specification to 34 percent of the spread within 20 seconds. This is a thesis, not a peer reviewed journal article, and venue and index results are uneven, with some cells showing an R squared not significantly different from zero. Flag: single study, student thesis, no live cost proof for a small account.

2. Price moves with the realised imbalance, not with the announced forced trade. Daniel Nathan, in a working paper version dated August 2026 following the S and P 500 Equal Weight quarterly reset and a 92 billion dollar ETF, reports across 62 rebalances from 2010 to mid 2026 that by the closing auction roughly ten times as much offsetting interest gathers around the scheduled trade, that the closing price moves with the net displayed imbalance, and that the scheduled target itself explains only 2.1 percent of the variation in that imbalance and points in the correct direction in only 53.2 percent of stock events. For a trader this is evidence for trading the imbalance feed itself and evidence against trading the headline rebalance list.

3. Opening auction overshoot and reversal, with a clientele mechanism. Berkman, Koch, Tuttle and Zhang (2012) are cited across the overnight literature for the finding that measured overnight returns partly reflect inflated opening prices driven by demand imbalances. Lou, Koch and others (2019 line of work) document a clientele split, retail like trading concentrated near the open and institutional trading concentrated near the close, which gives the open its systematic imbalance character. A 2026 working paper decomposing close to open returns for S and P 500 stocks from October 2008 to December 2023 finds dark period returns positive on average and pre market returns negative, offsetting a substantial share, with the reversal strongest when pre market liquidity is low. That supports a conditional claim, not a blanket fade the gap rule: thin conditions produce noisier opens.

4. Shao and Yao (June 2024 version) using the staggered 2004 introduction of the Nasdaq closing auction in a difference in differences design find the auction raised the informational content of closing prices and reduced noise relative to last trade pricing. That matters for grading: the closing auction price is a better benchmark than the last continuous print, which is the same logic as closing line value in section 5.

### Strongest evidence against

1. The Nathan result cuts both ways. If the scheduled forced trade explains only 2.1 percent of imbalance variation and is directionally right only 53.2 percent of the time, then the popular version of this trade, front running a known rebalance from the announcement list, has almost no signal. Only the late, displayed imbalance carries information, and by then every market maker sees the same feed.

2. Competition and cutoff structure. Nasdaq imbalance information is disseminated every ten seconds before 15:55 and every second after in the Morand description, and NYSE imbalance publication begins well before the close. This is a fully public, fully machine readable signal with a ten minute life. Any edge is a speed and execution edge inside that window, not an information discovery edge. A small book without colocated execution should expect to capture a fraction of the gross spread share the thesis reports, and the report makes no claim about what fraction survives for a retail latency participant. No sourced figure for small participant capture was found.

3. Heavy tails at the close. Derksen, Kleijn and de Vilder (Physica A, 2022), on liquid European stocks, find closing auction return distributions are heavy tailed and, counterintuitively, that large closing moves are typically not caused by large market orders, with tails heavier in a model without market orders because limit orders are placed to counter existing imbalance. Practical meaning: the largest auction dislocations are the least cleanly explained by the imbalance variable a small trader can observe, and tail risk in an auction fade is real.

4. Blog level gap reversal numbers are unverified. A widely shared Substack series reports large down gaps followed by plus 0.28 percent first 30 minute rallies and large up gaps by minus 0.24 percent declines. Flag: single source, no peer review, methodology not auditable from the post. Do not size on those numbers. The peer reviewed and working paper literature supports direction and conditioning on liquidity, not those point estimates.

### Implementable recipe, paper and shadow

Name: CLOSE-IMB-01.
Universe: 30 most liquid US names already followed by the book, plus SPY and QQQ as controls. Data: exchange imbalance feed snapshots at 15:50, 15:55, 15:58 where available from the broker or data vendor already in use. If the imbalance feed is not available point in time, do not substitute end of day imbalance summaries, which are look ahead contaminated for this test.
Signal: signed imbalance divided by average daily volume, recorded at each snapshot. Shadow trade A, continuation: if imbalance at 15:55 is in the top or bottom decile for that name over the trailing 60 sessions, record a hypothetical market on close entry in the direction of the imbalance, graded from the 15:55 reference price to the official closing auction price. Shadow trade B, reversal: same entry held in shadow from the closing auction price to the next day open and to next day 10:00, to test whether the auction move reverses.
Grading: report A and B separately, gross and after a stated cost assumption per share, with hit rate and average basis points per trade. Pre register the decile rule and the cost assumption before the first session. Run at least 60 sessions. Kill criteria are in the breaking point below.
Options expression, second stage only if A survives: no options in stage one. The auction move is minutes long and options spreads will usually exceed it.

### Breaking point

The effect dies in three places. First, when imbalance is small relative to volume, where Morand already finds insignificant cells. Second, at the latency frontier: if fills in shadow cannot be replicated at the recorded reference price in a small live paper account, the gross figure is not yours. Third, on rebalance and expiration days the auction share rises toward the 20 percent BMLL figure and imbalances are dominated by offsetting institutional interest, the Nathan setting, where headline direction is near coin flip at 53.2 percent. Treat those days as a separate regime or exclude them.

Confidence grade: B minus. Mechanism is sound, the venue data are real, and at least two independent sources support predictability of the auction move from displayed imbalance. No source proves net profitability for a sub 1 million dollar, non colocated participant.

## 2. Information cascades and herding measures

### What the mechanism claims

Banerjee (1992) and Bikhchandani, Hirshleifer and Welch (1992) model sequential decisions where each agent sees predecessors actions but not their private signals. After a small number of agreeing actions, rationally, later agents ignore their own signals. Two consequences follow that matter for trading. Cascades form fast, on very little information. A 2024 Journal of Economic Literature paper by the original authors with Omer Tamuz, summarised by UCLA Anderson Review, finds that even when private signals are only slightly better than a coin flip, on average fewer than four people act on their own information before a cascade locks in, and by the twentieth decision the probability of not being in a cascade is below 0.1 percent. And cascades are fragile: because the consensus rests on few real signals, one strong public signal can reverse it abruptly. Anderson and Holt laboratory experiments in 1997 confirmed cascade formation and fragility under controlled conditions.

The Lakonishok, Shleifer and Vishny (LSV) measure operationalises herding from holdings: for a stock in a quarter, the absolute deviation of the buyer share among trading funds from the market wide buyer share, minus an adjustment for what random trading would produce.

### Strongest evidence for

1. Herding exists but is small on average, and concentrated. Grinblatt, Titman and Wermers (American Economic Review, 1995) replicate LSV on mutual funds and find a weak tendency to herd, with the LSV pension fund figure from the original study at 2.7 percent. A French mutual fund study using 1999 to 2005 data finds LSV herding of 6.5 percent, with the Frey, Herbst and Walter measure roughly 2.5 times stronger, and herding stronger in small capitalisation names. Wermers (Journal of Finance, 1999), on 1975 to 1994 data, finds little herding in the average stock but much higher levels in small stocks and growth oriented funds.

2. Herd direction predicted returns at quarterly horizons in the classic sample. Wermers finds stocks bought in herds outperform stocks sold in herds by 4 percent during the following six months, with the difference much more pronounced among small stocks, and interprets herding as speeding price adjustment rather than destabilising at that horizon.

3. Buy and sell herding are asymmetric, which is the most useful modern result. Cai, Han, Li and Li (Federal Reserve FEDS 2016-091), on corporate bonds, find herding much higher than in equities, buy herding associated with permanent price impact consistent with price discovery, and sell herding associated with transitory yet significant price distortion that reverses gradually over following quarters, strongest in high yield, small and illiquid bonds and during the financial crisis. The French equity study likewise finds sell herding has a destabilising impact. Trading translation: herd buying is more often information, herd selling is more often forced or reputational, and therefore fadeable after the fact.

4. Analyst behaviour fits the cascade model directly. Welch (2000), cited in the cascade literature, finds analysts revise recommendations in the direction of recent consensus revisions, the sequential observation structure the theory requires.

### Strongest evidence against

1. Horizon and institution type flip the sign. A later study disentangling investment horizon finds no significant buy minus sell herd difference in the quarter after formation for institutions as a group, a significant reversal over quarters two to four, short term institution herding promoting price discovery, and long term institution herding driving prices away from fundamentals. That directly challenges a naive read of Wermers as a six month continuation signal. The same variable is continuation, then reversal, depending on who is herding and when you measure.

2. LSV is quarterly, stale, and confounded. Holdings are disclosed with a lag, funds can herd because they all read the same public news, which is correlated information, not a cascade, and LSV cannot distinguish the two. The average effect sizes, 2.7 percent to 6.5 percent on the LSV scale, are deviations in buyer proportions, not return forecasts, and are small relative to noise in any single stock quarter.

3. Cascade fragility is a theory of timing failure. The model says reversals are abrupt and triggered by a public signal whose arrival is not predictable from the cascade itself. It tells you a crowded consensus is brittle. It does not tell you which week it breaks. Any strategy that shorts a cascade on fragility alone is short an object that can persist and grow, with no dated catalyst.

4. Practitioner herding content is mostly narrative. Flag: marketing and unverifiable claims are common in this area, including long form research notes that attach precise price targets and percentage moves to herding stories in single names without disclosed data or methods. No such figure is used in this report.

### Implementable recipe, paper and shadow

Name: HERD-STATE-01. This is a state variable, not an entry signal.
Construction from data a small book can actually obtain: quarterly 13F based LSV style herd score per name, delayed and labelled with its disclosure lag, plus a fast proxy basket: five day change in short interest utilisation where available, options put call volume ratio percentile, and news count percentile. Do not pretend the fast proxies are LSV. Keep them as separate columns.
Use in shadow grading: for every existing paper strategy entry in the test window, record herd state at entry (herd buy extreme, herd sell extreme, neither). Grade each strategy separately inside each state. Hypotheses pre registered: (a) momentum entries perform worse when entered into a herd sell extreme that is already two quarters old, the reversal zone in the horizon study; (b) dip buying performs better after herd sell extremes in liquid names, the Cai et al transitory distortion pattern, transposed from bonds to equities and therefore explicitly a cross market hypothesis, not an established equity result.
No standalone trades are generated by HERD-STATE-01. Promotion requires the state split to change a strategy grade by a pre registered margin over at least two quarters of paper data, because the underlying LSV input is quarterly.

### Breaking point

The effect dies at the data lag. By the time 13F herding is public, the quarter it describes has ended and the continuation window in Wermers is already partly spent, while the reversal window in the horizon study may be starting. It also dies in large liquid names, where every study finds herding weakest, which is precisely where a small options book mostly trades. And it dies as a cascade timer entirely: fragility has no clock.

Confidence grade: C plus for trading use, B for the underlying behavioural facts. Herding and cascades are real and well replicated as phenomena. Their conversion into a dated, net positive signal for a small book is unproven in the sources found.

## 3. Market making economics for a small participant

### What the mechanism claims

Glosten and Milgrom (1985) formalise the spread as compensation for adverse selection: a resting quote is a free option the market exercises against you when the counterparty knows more. The modern empirical version is the markout: for a buy filled at price p, the markout at horizon tau is the midprice at fill time plus tau, minus p. If passive fills systematically show negative markouts larger than the half spread plus any rebate, the maker loses on average regardless of how the fill felt at the instant of execution.

### Strongest evidence for the negative claim, that naive passive making loses

1. A live experiment, not a backtest. A 2025 to 2026 working paper, The Market Makers Dilemma, reports a live trading experiment on the Binance Bitcoin perpetual market, with a dataset described in different versions as over 400,000 maker orders in the summary and 232,897 minimum sized maker orders over a one week agnostic submission window in the paper body. Flag: the two counts come from different versions and samples in the same project and should not be blended. The core findings are consistent across versions: maker fill probability is highly predictable from queue variables, with a simple regression reporting an R squared of 0.946; randomly placed orders in balanced queues show about a 50 percent fill probability, against literature estimates below 2 percent for historically observed orders; and the unconditional expected return for maker orders is approximately minus 0.8 basis points, or minus 0.3 basis points net of the 0.5 basis point maker rebate in that venue fee schedule.

2. Queue position is the whole game. The same experiment finds orders at the back of the queue, queue position near 1, almost universally result in negative returns, while orders at the front of the queue, near 0, in favourable conditions can achieve small positive returns before rebates. DeLise (2024), The Negative Drift of a Limit Order Fill, provides the theoretical result behind this: conditional on filling, expected drift is against the maker, and a fill that consumes the whole level is mechanically a fill into a price move against you. Moallemi and Yuan provide a formal queue position valuation model. Practitioner transaction cost analysis reaches the same place: size weighted markouts at stated horizons, commonly read out to one minute and five minutes because adverse selection keeps developing past the millisecond scale, are the standard grading tool, and venues are known to prefer reporting very short horizon markouts because those understate the effect.

3. The fill probability and post fill return trade off against each other. The experiment documents a negative correlation between a maker orders fill likelihood and its post fill return. Conditions that make you likely to fill are conditions that make the fill bad. This is not bad luck and cannot be diversified away inside the same strategy, because it is a property of who chooses to trade with a resting order.

### Strongest evidence for a small participant, honestly stated

1. Front of queue in favourable conditions was not negative in the experiment, before rebates. That is a real, narrow opening: selective, not continuous, making.
2. In equities, maker rebates exist and change arithmetic at the margin, with a representative US equity schedule described in practitioner sources at about 0.30 cents per share maker rebate and about 0.30 cents per share taker fee. Flag: practitioner source, schedules vary by venue and tier, verify against the brokers actual fee schedule before any calculation.
3. Options market making is structurally different because spreads are wider and hedging is in the underlying, but no sourced study was found showing a small, non privileged options market maker earning the spread net of adverse selection and hedge costs. Absence of evidence here is reported as absence, not as proof of impossibility.

### Strongest evidence against, for this books scale

Latency and queue position are not purchasable at small scale. Front of queue requires being early and staying early, which in practice means fast cancellation and replacement when the price moves, and every cancel and replace sends a small participant to the back of the new queue. The experiment population that achieved near front results operated in a single highly liquid crypto perpetual with a stated fee schedule. Transfer to US equities and listed options, with multiple venues, protected quotes, and options hedge slippage, is an assumption, not a finding. No source found demonstrates a sub 1 million dollar book earning spread net in US listed markets without high frequency infrastructure. Vendor content claiming otherwise was found and is flagged: marketing, no auditable fill level data.

### Implementable recipe, paper and shadow

Name: MAKE-GRADE-01. Measurement first, making never until measurement passes.
Step one: for 60 sessions, every paper entry the book already makes is dual recorded, once as the aggressive execution the strategies currently assume, and once as a hypothetical passive limit order at the touch at the same decision time. For the passive version record: filled or not within a stated window, queue proxy at placement (displayed size ahead at that price, from quote data where available), and markouts at 10 seconds, 1 minute, 5 minutes and 30 minutes.
Step two: grade passive minus aggressive, by name and by queue proxy tercile. Pre registered promotion bar: passive must beat aggressive net of fees in the front tercile only, across at least 200 hypothetical fills, before any real passive routing change is even proposed, and back tercile results must be reported, not discarded.
Step three, options specific: record the same markouts on the underlying during every options fill, so hedge timing cost is visible separately from option spread paid.
This recipe costs nothing, risks nothing, and produces the one dataset that decides the whole territory for this specific book: its own fill markout curve.

### Breaking point

Passive economics die at the back of the queue, in fast markets, and at any horizon where the markout curve is still falling at five minutes. They also die on size: a strategy that works for minimum size orders in an experiment says nothing about size that matters to the book, because larger taker flow is exactly the informed flow that sweeps levels. If MAKE-GRADE-01 shows negative five minute markouts exceeding half spread plus rebate in all terciles, the territory is closed for this book, permanently, on its own data.

Confidence grade: A minus for the adverse selection claim and the measurement method. D for the proposition that a small book can earn spread as a maker in US listed markets. That proposition has no supporting sourced evidence in this dive.

## 4. Epidemiology of crowding: how crowded trades unwind

### What the mechanism claims

Crowding is a latent state: many holders, similar reasons, same exit. Its cost is not paid on entry. It is paid on the day a holder who must leave, for reasons unrelated to the thesis, forces prices against everyone holding the same book, whose risk limits then force more selling. Measurable proxies fall in six families in the practitioner and academic literature: positioning concentration, valuation spread between crowded longs and shorts, factor exposure and how far the factor has run, liquidity relative to position size (days to cover, days of average volume), borrow conditions and utilisation, and correlated behaviour on bad days.

### Strongest evidence for

1. The canonical event. Khandani and Lo, What Happened to the Quants in August 2007, hypothesise from TASS data and a simulated long short equity strategy that losses in the week of 6 August 2007 were initiated by the rapid unwinding of one or more sizable quantitative market neutral portfolios, likely forced by margin calls or risk reduction in an unrelated book, and then propagated because portfolios overlapped. Secondary summaries of their simulation describe a three day loss of roughly 7.6 standard deviations for the simulated strategy. Flag: the 7.6 figure is sourced from secondary summaries of the paper, not re verified here from the papers own tables. The qualitative facts are in the primary abstract: unprecedented correlated losses across market neutral funds with no market exposure, and a significant rebound on 10 August 2007, which is the signature of a liquidity event rather than a fundamental repricing. Contemporaneous press surveyed month to date losses ranging from minus 5 percent to minus 30 percent for affected funds, as reported in the paper.

2. Crowded shorts explain momentum crashes, with a usable filter. Yan (2013), Crowded Trades, Short Covering and Momentum Crashes, using January 1980 to September 2012 stock data, segments past losers into crowded losers, highest short interest quintile plus highest institutional exit, and non crowded losers. A summary by CXO Advisory of the papers results reports gross annual returns of 22.1 percent for the non crowded momentum portfolio, 12.6 percent for baseline momentum, and 16.4 percent for the crowded version, with high frequency short sale data showing short covering especially severe in the crowded loser portfolio during momentum crashes. Flag: figures are as summarised by CXO Advisory from the paper, gross, and the sample ends in 2012. Direction is the takeaway: refusing to short the most crowded losers removed much of the crash exposure in sample.

3. Short interest itself is informed on average. Asquith, Pathak and Ritter (2005) are widely cited for the finding that the highest short interest decile underperforms, a result summarised in multiple secondary sources at roughly 1 percent per month over 1988 to 2002. Flag: magnitude here is second hand summary, not re verified from the original tables in this dive. The tension with the squeeze story is the point: high short interest is bearish information until it is extreme plus rising price plus high days to cover, when forced covering dominates. The literature does not give a clean numeric threshold separating the two regimes, and any source claiming one should be treated as unverified.

4. ETF premium and discount stress is the cleanest real time crowding and capacity gauge. In March 2020, a London School of Economics FMG discussion paper (September 2026 version) records LQD trading at a 5 percent discount and HYG at 7 to 8 percent discounts, citing Falato et al and Haddad et al, with the Federal Reserve Secondary Market Corporate Credit Facility closing the discounts within weeks. A separate industry summary reports the average discount for US investment grade corporate bond ETFs reached about 3.4 percent in mid March 2020, with some products above 8 percent. Two lessons: discounts of that size in investment grade wrappers signal arbitrage capacity failure, not a free 5 percent, because authorised participants could not or would not close them; and the discounts closed on a policy backstop, an exogenous event, not on a timetable a trader could have known.

5. Recent factor crowding episodes rhyme. A Substack synthesis of MSCI factor crowding data on the summer 2025 quant wobble reports key alpha linked factors delivering returns near double or more their model forecast volatility from 1 June to 25 July 2025, and a falling pairwise correlation in factor tails on the MSCI Integrated Factor Crowding Model, interpreted as systematic position reduction. Flag: single source synthesis of proprietary MSCI data, not independently verifiable from public data. Use as a hypothesis about signatures, falling tail correlation during unwinds, not as established fact.

### Strongest evidence against timing unwinds

1. Every serious framework found explicitly disclaims timing. A recent practitioner framework building a six indicator crowding score states directly that the score does not forecast when the exit will happen, and that its purpose is to size how much worse a bad day is likely to be. That honesty generalises: crowding measures vulnerability, not a date.
2. Crowded can stay crowded and keep paying. In the factor evaluation literature, crowding compresses prospective premia by narrowing valuation spreads and amplifies drawdowns, but crowded factors also self correct only after drawdowns severe enough to force capital out. Being early against a crowd is indistinguishable from being wrong for position sizing purposes.
3. The best crowding data are lagged or proprietary. Short interest in the US is published twice monthly with a lag of about seven business days after the reporting date, per FINRA descriptions. 13F positioning is quarterly and lagged. Factor crowding models from index providers are proprietary. A small book sees the state late, after faster holders have already adjusted.
4. One striking claim was found in a secondary source, that a value weighted quintile of most crowded stocks delivered a Fama French three factor monthly alpha of 0.54 percent against minus 0.90 percent for the least crowded. It appears without full methodology in the citing piece and the sign is counterintuitive against the rest of the literature. Flag: unverifiable as presented, not used for any recommendation here.

### Implementable recipe, paper and shadow

Name: CROWD-VETO-01, a risk overlay on existing strategies, not a new strategy.
Score per name at entry, from obtainable data only: short interest percent of float percentile, days to cover, borrow fee percentile where the broker exposes it, 20 day return percentile (how far it has run), and ETF or index membership in a currently stressed wrapper where relevant. Composite is a simple average of percentile ranks, versioned and frozen before the test.
Rules in shadow: (a) entries in the top crowding decile are recorded at half size, and short entries in the top decile with days to cover in its top quintile are recorded as vetoed, the Yan filter; (b) every open paper position gets a crowding column so realised drawdowns can be graded by crowding at entry.
Grading after two quarters: compare drawdown distribution and worst ten trades, crowded versus not, inside each existing strategy. Promotion means adopting the veto for real sizing. It does not require crowding to predict returns, only to predict left tail severity, which is what the evidence actually supports.
Unwind timing test, separate and clearly speculative: record days when a top decile crowded name moves more than three percent against the crowd direction on volume above twice its average, as candidate unwind starts, and grade five and twenty day forward returns. Pre register that this sub test is expected to fail, and that a failure closes the timing question rather than prompting threshold tuning.

### Breaking point

Crowding measurement dies when the proxy and the holder base separate: index inclusion changes, borrow recalls, or a single dominant holder whose behaviour is idiosyncratic. The veto dies if, on the books own paper data, top decile names do not show worse left tails, at which point the overlay is cost without benefit. Timing dies, as above, on its pre registered test.

Confidence grade: B for crowding as vulnerability and sizing input, supported by 2007, by the momentum crash literature, and by ETF stress episodes. C for any timing use.

## 5. Professional poker and sports betting transfers, one level further out

### What transfers, and what does not

Three objects transfer cleanly because they are mathematics or measurement, not domain lore. Fractional Kelly under estimation error. Closing line value as a grading standard. Syndicate style information aggregation, meaning many independent estimates combined before staking, with staking separated from estimation. Poker specific bankroll rules of thumb, such as twenty to thirty buy ins for cash games and fifty to one hundred for tournaments in practitioner guides, do not transfer as numbers. They transfer only as the principle that required bankroll is set by variance and edge, not by confidence.

### Strongest evidence for

1. The asymmetry of Kelly errors is a theorem with a clean quantitative form. In the continuous approximation summarised by MacLean, Thorp and Ziemba in The Kelly Capital Growth Investment Criterion and in Thorp (2006), betting a multiple k of the Kelly fraction delivers a share 2k minus k squared of the maximum excess growth rate. Half Kelly therefore keeps 75 percent of maximum growth at half the volatility. Three quarters Kelly keeps about 94 percent. Betting 1.5 times Kelly earns what half Kelly earns, with three times the volatility. Twice Kelly earns zero excess growth. The probability form is equally stark: at full Kelly the probability of ever falling to half of starting capital is 50 percent, and to a tenth is 10 percent; at half Kelly the halving probability falls to 12.5 percent. These are properties of the formula under known probabilities, not empirical claims.

2. Estimation error is the binding constraint, and it hits the mean hardest. The Kelly literature cites Chopra and Ziemba for the result that errors in means are roughly twenty times as costly as errors in variances, summarised in one source as a 20 to 2 to 1 importance ratio across means, variances and covariances. MacLean, Thorp and Ziemba are also cited for the result that a 10 percent error in estimating expected return can produce roughly 50 percent overbetting under full Kelly. Flag: both figures reach this report through secondary summaries of the anthology, not from the original chapters read directly. The direction and the practical rule, size on a pessimistic lower bound of the edge, not on the point estimate, are consistent across every source found, practitioner and academic alike. One synthesis states the rule sharply: when the standard error equals the expected return, the optimal scale is half the naive Kelly fraction.

3. Closing line value is the best validated grading standard in any forecasting market. Joseph Buchdahl tested Pinnacle closing line efficiency on 87,960 pairs of earlier and closing odds across four seasons of European football from 2012 to 2013 onward, treating the ratio of the earlier price to the closing price as expected value and comparing it with realised level stakes yield. The slope was almost exactly 1.00: beating the close by a given margin returned close to that margin, less the bookmaker margin he had deliberately left in. Pinnacle closing prices are the standard benchmark because the book runs low margins, described in practitioner sources at roughly 2 to 3 percent overround on main football markets against 5 to 8 percent at soft books, takes sharp action, and lets informed flow move its price. A 2024 to 2026 evidence review found no publicly documented reproducible method that beats a sharp Pinnacle style closing line on the markets it surveyed, with the two studies that properly benchmark against Pinnacle closing both returning negative results for the challenger models. Flag: that review is a generated research report in a public repository, not peer reviewed. Its negative conclusion is however consistent with Moskowitz (2021, Journal of Finance line of work on US sports markets), summarised as finding opening prices efficient on average and deviations smaller than transaction costs.

4. CLV converges faster than profit. Because every bet, win or lose, produces a CLV observation on the day it is placed, a bettor learns whether their process beats the markets best price long before win loss records can distinguish skill from variance. This is the property that makes the standard valuable for trading, where resolved trade counts are small.

### Strongest evidence against naive transfer

1. Markets have no single closing line unless you define one. A stock has an official closing auction price, which section 1 notes is more informative than the last print, and an option has a settlement and a next day open. The transfer requires an explicit, pre registered benchmark per instrument. Without it, trade CLV becomes a story told after the fact.
2. Poker and sports edges are stationary in a way trading edges are not. A poker win rate over one hundred thousand hands, the sample size practitioner guides require before trusting Kelly inputs, is estimable because the game does not change. A trading strategys edge decays, regimes shift, and the crowd adapts. Kelly inputs from a backtest are therefore systematically more optimistic than poker inputs from live play, which argues for smaller fractions in trading than the quarter to half Kelly range professionals use in betting, not larger.
3. Syndicate aggregation works because syndicate members estimates are genuinely independent and the syndicate can lay off or get down at multiple books. A small trading operation aggregating its own model variants does not have independent estimates, it has correlated ones, and averaging correlated estimates produces false confidence, which Kelly then leverages. No sourced study was found quantifying syndicate aggregation gains in a form transferable to a trading desk. Flag: syndicate claims in popular sources are largely anecdotal and unverifiable, and none are relied on here.
4. Beating the close is not sufficient in thin markets. Buchdahls result is for liquid football markets at a sharp book. In thin markets the close itself can be wrong, a caveat stated in the CLV guides themselves. For options, the closing quote on an illiquid strike is often a wide, stale mark, and CLV measured against it will flatter entries.

### Implementable recipe, paper and shadow

Name: CLV-GRADE-01 plus KELLY-LCB-01.
CLV-GRADE-01: for every paper entry, record at decision time: entry price, the contemporaneous mid or last price of the benchmark, and the benchmark at the pre registered grading moment. For equities the grading benchmark is the same days official closing auction price. For options the grading benchmarks are the underlying close on entry day and the options own mark at next day open, recorded separately because they answer different questions. Define trade CLV as entry advantage versus the grading benchmark, in basis points for equities and in percent of premium for options, with the exact formulas frozen before the test. Grade every strategy monthly on mean CLV and CLV hit rate first, and on profit second. A strategy with positive profit and negative mean CLV over one hundred or more paper trades is classified as lucky, not skilled, and is not promoted. That single rule is the highest value import in this whole dive relative to its cost.
KELLY-LCB-01: no position is sized on a point estimate edge. Size is a fraction, one quarter, of the Kelly fraction computed on the lower confidence bound of the edge, where the bound comes from the paper track record or, before one exists, from assuming the backtest edge is overstated by half, which is the documented direction of selection bias. Cap by the books existing stress loss sizing rules, which always override Kelly. Kelly here is a ceiling check, never a target.
Aggregation rule: when multiple strategies or models signal the same name, record each signal separately and grade the consensus subset separately, rather than averaging confidences into a single larger size. Independence must be demonstrated in the grading, not assumed.

### Breaking point

CLV grading dies if the benchmark is gameable or stale, which is the case for illiquid options strikes and for any benchmark chosen after seeing results. Kelly sizing dies when edge estimates come from fewer than roughly one hundred resolved observations, when edges are correlated across concurrent positions, Kelly assumes independence and simultaneous full Kelly stakes on correlated bets are a classic ruin path, and whenever the lower bound of the edge is at or below zero, in which case the correct Kelly size is zero regardless of the point estimate.

Confidence grade: A minus for CLV as grading and fractional Kelly as a ceiling discipline. C for syndicate aggregation transfer, which is plausible, widely practised, and not quantified in any source found that a small book could rely on.

## 6. Esoteric and heterodox bundle

This territory is five separate claims sharing a section. They get separate verdicts. Bundling them into one grade would be exactly the error this dive exists to prevent.

### 6a. Lunar and calendar micro effects, honest status post 2020

Evidence for: Yuan, Zheng and Zhu (Journal of Empirical Finance, 2006), on 48 country indices, report returns lower around full moons than new moons by 3 to 5 percent per annum in global portfolios, with a cumulative difference of 40.26 basis points per lunar cycle for the 15 day window and 27.48 basis points for the 7 day window in the equal weighted portfolio, significant at the 5 percent level, and not explained in their tests by volatility, volume, macroeconomic announcements, or other calendar effects. Dichev and Janes (2003) report new moon period returns roughly double full moon period returns in long US samples.
Evidence against: the effect does not survive modern samples at tradable size. A skeptical backtest on SPY from January 2000 to September 2026, 6,701 daily observations, reported by its author in a public write up, finds a new minus full difference of 1.59 basis points per day in one window definition with a HAC adjusted t statistic of 0.38 and p value of 0.71, and finds the estimated difference shrinking toward roughly 0.44 basis points per day from 2010 onward. Flag: single author blog backtest, not peer reviewed. But its direction matches the review literature: later re examinations across 62 international indices find at most an enhanced new moon effect and no general full moon effect, and reviews attribute much of the early significance to data mining and multiple testing across the very large space of calendar hypotheses. No mechanism has been established, and the original authors found no volume or volatility channel, which removes the usual forced flow explanation.
Recipe: none recommended. If the desk insists on spending one test slot, the only honest version is a pre registered, zero cost shadow column recording lunar phase at each existing entry, graded after a full year, with no trades generated. Expected value of even this is low.
Breaking point: already broken for trading purposes. An effect whose recent sample estimate is statistically indistinguishable from zero and economically below one basis point per day cannot pay any spread.
Verdict: REFUSE. Confidence in the refusal: B plus. The early literature is real, the decay is the standard fate of calendar anomalies after publication, and post 2020 evidence found is null.

### 6b. Odd lot order flow predictive content from real prints

Evidence for: two distinct odd lot facts must not be merged. First, odd lots are not uninformed retail crumbs. OHara, Yao and Ye (2014), on Nasdaq supplied data for 120 stocks, find odd lot trades were a median 19 percent of trades, up to 66 percent, and contributed 30 percent of price discovery, consistent with informed and algorithmic traders splitting orders into odd lots when odd lots were still excluded from the consolidated tape. Odd lot trades have been reported to the tape since October 2013, which ended that specific invisibility advantage. Second, retail order imbalance, identified by the Boehmer, Jones, Zhang and Zhang algorithm from sub penny price improvement on off exchange prints, predicted cross sectional returns in 2010 to 2015: net retail bought stocks outperformed by approximately 10 basis points over the following week, about 5 percent annualised, with informativeness up to twelve weeks, stronger in smaller, lower priced stocks. Crucially, Boehmer et al themselves find odd lot retail imbalance specifically is much weaker: weekly odd lot imbalance coefficients are positive but not statistically significant, though daily odd lot imbalance can significantly predict next day returns.
Evidence against: a replication and extension reassessing Boehmer et al finds that in 2016 to 2021 the predictive power weakens significantly, weekly retail imbalance no longer predicts large cap returns, the long short strategy is no longer profitable, and conclusions are sensitive to the identification method, with the quote midpoint method giving different answers from the original algorithm in the later period. Against that, Jones, Tan, Zhang and Zhang, in a February 2026 version on the post pandemic period, find retail imbalance still predicts next day returns and more strongly after the pandemic, with an interquartile imbalance increase associated with 3.81 basis points of next day return pre pandemic, 5.91 during, and 6.69 post pandemic. These two later studies conflict on weekly large cap horizons but are compatible on a narrow claim: daily retail imbalance retains small next day predictive content, weekly tradable portfolios may not.
Recipe, TEST, name ODDLOT-01: use the free or already available version of the signal only, which is daily odd lot and retail imbalance proxies from consolidated tape prints, not a paid feed. Shadow a cross sectional tilt on the books existing equity universe: record the imbalance percentile at each close, and grade next day returns by imbalance quintile for 120 sessions, separately for names above and below median market capitalisation, because every source agrees the effect concentrates in smaller names. No trades in stage one. Promotion requires the top minus bottom quintile spread to exceed a pre registered cost assumption in the books actual universe, which is mostly liquid names where the literature says the effect is weakest. That is deliberately a hard bar.
Breaking point: the effect dies in large liquid names, at weekly horizons in post 2016 data, and under identification method changes. It also dies on data cost: if the only version that works requires a paid tick feed whose cost exceeds the basis points available, it is dead for this book regardless of statistical significance.
Verdict: TEST, low priority. Confidence: C.

### 6c. Options charm and vanna hedging flows, with proper controls

Evidence for: the definitions are correct and the hedging logic is real. Vanna is the change in delta when implied volatility changes. Charm is the change in delta as time passes. Dealers hedge delta, so volatility changes and time passage mechanically require hedge adjustments, concentrated near expiry for charm, where 0DTE deltas decay fastest, and around volatility events for vanna, such as the implied volatility collapse after a scheduled announcement. No dispute about the accounting identity.
Evidence against predictive use: no peer reviewed or independently replicated study was found showing that vanna or charm exposure aggregates predict returns net of gamma exposure, volatility changes, and time to expiry, which are the proper controls, because vanna and charm are deterministic functions of the same inputs. The sources making predictive claims are vendors selling exposure dashboards. Flag: marketing. FlashAlpha and HermesGEX content asserts, for example, that vanna is the hidden engine behind US afternoon rallies and that a proprietary card predicts close period hedge flow. No data, sample, or test is published with those claims, and they are unverifiable as presented. The most honest vendor source found, a dealer positioning guide that reports testing its own levels on more than five years of data, states that fading gamma walls on contact and expecting price to be pulled into the pin at expiry did not survive testing and were indistinguishable from randomly placed levels, and that only the broad gamma regime, dampening versus amplifying, showed a pulse. A separate educational source states the correct summary: these terms describe where hedging pressure sits, they do not predict where price goes next, and every level rests on positioning estimates nobody outside the dealing desks can fully observe, because the sign convention, who is long and who is short, must be assumed from open interest.
Recipe: no predictive test is justified on vendor claims. The only defensible use is a context column, name FLOW-CTX-01: record estimated net gamma regime sign and days to expiry concentration at each options entry, and grade realised volatility of existing positions by regime. That tests the one claim with any support, that short gamma regimes amplify moves, as a risk input. Do not build entry signals on vanna or charm levels.
Breaking point: the claim dies at the identification problem. Dealer positioning is not observed, it is inferred under an assumption about customer direction, and flipping that assumption flips every vanna and charm sign. A signal whose sign depends on an unobservable assumption cannot be falsified on public data, and unfalsifiable signals are refused by default in this program.
Verdict: REFUSE as predictive signals, context only. Confidence in the refusal: B. Note the estate has already recorded a GEX failure in prior dives, and nothing found here rehabilitates the family, it extends the same weakness to its second order members.

### 6d. Corporate buyback blackout calendars as forced flow

Evidence for: the flow is large in aggregate. A research summary from BigPic Solutions, drawing on S and P data, puts 2024 corporate buybacks at 942.5 billion dollars and describes buybacks as the largest single source of net equity demand, with an estimated 2 to 5 billion dollars of daily demand removed during blackouts. Flag: the daily removal figure is an estimate in a commercial research summary, not a measured flow. Blackout windows are real, self imposed ahead of earnings to avoid trading on material non public information, typically about four to five weeks and five to seven weeks in December in that summary, and about 76 percent of companies are reported to resume within one to two trading days of earnings.
Evidence against: the return effect is not there in the studies the same summary reports. O Shaughnessy Asset Management analysis reported there finds annualised excess returns of plus 3.2 percent during blackout periods against plus 3.3 percent during open windows, virtually identical, and no statistically significant negative returns during blackouts, in line with a State Street Global Advisors study over 136 months from March 1994 to October 2018. The mechanism for the null is documented: roughly 60 percent of programs are reported to use Rule 10b5-1 plans that keep buying through blackouts on pre set instructions, so the bid never fully leaves. The summary also reports that the raw correlation between buyback dollars and prices, 77 percent, falls to 5 percent when normalised for market capitalisation, a textbook confound, since both respond to the same economic conditions. Rule 10b-18 also caps and shapes the flow that does occur: generally no more than 25 percent of average daily volume, not the opening or closing print, at or below the independent bid, per the safe harbour description in a hedge fund oriented desk note. Flag: that note contains aggressive return claims for announcement signalling trades that are unverifiable and are not used here.
Recipe: REFUSE as a market timer. One narrow, honest residual use: as a single name context note, a company exiting blackout with an authorised, funded program is a known incremental buyer under price and volume constraints, which belongs in a position file, not in an entry model. No shadow strategy is proposed, because the aggregate studies already ran the test at index scale over decades and found nothing.
Breaking point: already at the breaking point. The flow premise is true and the price premise is false, because 10b5-1 continuity and anticipation by other participants absorb the calendar.
Verdict: REFUSE. Confidence: B plus, on the strength of long sample null results from two asset managers reported in one summary. Flag: both null results reach this report through a single commercial summary page, so the confidence is capped until one primary study is read directly.

### 6e. Intraday clock seasonality post 2020

Evidence for: Heston, Korajczyk and Sadka (Journal of Finance, 2010) document that a stocks return in a given half hour predicts its return in the same half hour on subsequent days, with periodicity at multiples of one trading day persisting for up to forty trading days, stronger at the start and end of the day, and a secondary summary of the paper puts the abnormal same bucket return at about 0.06 percent per half hour interval with t statistics above 4 in most buckets. Flag: the 0.06 percent figure is from a secondary hypothesis document summarising the paper, not from the paper read directly here. The mechanism, recurring institutional execution schedules, VWAP and TWAP programs and benchmark timing, is plausible and is supported by a clientele study in three non US markets finding the pattern in the UK and Brazil, weaker in retail dominated China A shares, and strengthening in A shares after MSCI inclusion raised institutional ownership. Separately, Gao, Han, Li and Zhou find the first half hour index return predicts the last half hour, intraday momentum at the market level. And Baltussen, Da and Soebhag document an end of day reversal at the stock level that is distinct from that market momentum: a stocks return up to mid afternoon negatively predicts its last half hour return, with the low minus high quintile portfolio earning 3.78 basis points per day value weighted, 9.5 percent annualised, and 6.86 basis points equal weighted, 17.3 percent annualised, in portfolio sorts with t statistics typically above 10, gross, through December 2019, attributed to retail buy the dip attention trading near the close and short seller risk management.
Evidence against: post 2019 survival is partial and fragile, and this is the best tested claim in the bundle precisely because someone ran a pre registered check. Vidovich and Zerjal (working paper, 6 November 2026), testing Baltussen, Da and Soebhag on point in time S and P 500 members excluding about eighty heavily traded names they had previously examined, find the reversal at 3.13 basis points per day in 2016 to 2019 and 3.05 in 2023 to 2026 measured to the last trade before the closing auction, but indistinguishable from zero in 2020 to 2022, at 1.69 basis points from 2020 onward, resting heavily on 2023 as the strongest year, with 2024 to 2026 alone not separable from the 2020 to 2022 weakness, and on the full index including the heavily traded names the effect falls from 3.40 to 0.74 basis points. No pre registered mechanism test passed, and the authors make no claim net of costs and do not trade it. For the Heston pattern itself, a social media posted futures test claiming every S and P session since 2011, 3,949 sessions and 50,784 trades, reports the same direction strategy losing before costs on ES and NQ futures and all thirteen half hours losing after a 16.50 dollar round turn cost. Flag: single source, social post, unverifiable methodology, and futures are not the cross sectional stock setting of the original paper, so it refutes only a naive index futures version, not the paper. McLean and Pontiff (2016) provide the general prior: published predictor returns fall about 26 percent out of sample and about 58 percent after publication.
Recipe, TEST, name CLOCK-01, two separate sub tests, never blended: (i) End of day reversal shadow: at 15:30, rank the books liquid equity universe by return from prior close to 15:00, record a hypothetical long bottom quintile, short top quintile portfolio held to the close, graded gross and after a stated per share cost, with the largest five names by volume reported separately, since the replication says weakness concentrates there. (ii) Same half hour persistence: for each name, record whether the sign of the 09:30 to 10:00 return matches the sign of the same bucket over the prior five sessions majority, and grade it only as a timing context for entries the book already wants to make in that bucket, not as a standalone trade. Run both for 120 sessions. Costs decide: the gross effect is three to four basis points, so any cost assumption above that ends the test by arithmetic, not by statistics.
Breaking point: costs, first and always, at these magnitudes. Then regime: the reversal vanished for three full years, 2020 to 2022, in the pre registered test, so a live version must include a regime off switch that the literature does not yet specify, which is itself a reason to keep this in shadow. Then crowding: clock effects are the most copyable anomalies in finance, every execution algorithm already knows the schedule.
Verdict: TEST, shadow only, with cost arithmetic as the first gate. Confidence: C plus for net tradability, B minus for the gross statistical existence of the end of day reversal in mid sized names post 2023.

## Cross territory synthesis

Three patterns repeat across all six territories and are worth more than any single signal.

First, measurement imports beat signal imports. The two highest confidence adoptions in this dive are not trades. They are closing line value grading, imported from sports betting, and markout grading, imported from market making. Both answer the question the book most needs answered, whether its entries beat the best available benchmark price, using data the book already generates. Both are cheap, falsifiable, and improve every existing strategy instead of adding a new one competing for the same capital.

Second, every flow story splits into a true flow premise and a usually false price premise. Buybacks really are the largest net demand source and their blackout calendar really is predictable, and returns in blackout and open windows are the same. Index rebalance flow really is enormous, and the scheduled trade predicts the realised imbalance barely better than a coin flip at 53.2 percent. Dealer hedging really is mechanical, and the positioning inputs needed to forecast it are unobservable. The recurring error to refuse is pricing the flow you can see instead of the net imbalance you usually cannot.

Third, fragility and crowding are risk facts, not timing facts. Cascades are fragile, crowded trades unwind violently, and passive fills are adversely selected. None of the three comes with a clock. Used as sizing, veto and grading inputs they are supported by the strongest evidence in this dive, Khandani and Lo, Yan, Cai et al, and the live maker experiment. Used as entry triggers they are the most common way these literatures get misread.

Priority order for test slots, if slots are scarce: CLOSE-IMB-01 first, CLV-GRADE-01 alongside it since it costs no slot, CROWD-VETO-01 as an overlay on existing paper strategies, CLOCK-01 end of day reversal sub test, HERD-STATE-01 grading split, ODDLOT-01 last. MAKE-GRADE-01 runs in the background of all of them because it only requires dual recording of entries already being made.
