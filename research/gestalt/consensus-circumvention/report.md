# Can an LLM-Agent Trading Operation Beat the Consensus Objection?
## Morillo's argument, the beyond-consensus research program, and a ranked circumvention playbook — with the generate-and-test arithmetic done honestly

*Research completed 2026-11-14 (EST). Confidence labels: **[verified]** = primary source opened and read; **[index]** = search-index result / abstract only; **[secondary]** = reported by a third party about a primary source; **[arithmetic]** = calculation performed for this report from cited formulas — checkable, but not itself a sourced fact. ## Summary

1. **Morillo's argument is real, precisely quotable, and narrower than its viral form.** In the Bloomberg *Odd Lots* interview (published 7 Oct 2024 — the only verbatim primary text this research could find; see §1.1 on the "Bloomberg Invest, November 2026" attribution, which could **not** be verified), Dan Morillo said an LLM asked "what is different about company X?" will, "by definition," tell you "what everybody else thinks is different about company X… you're getting the consensus… it's been trained on the average of things kind of by definition," and that this "is not quite the same as the jump to… here's a difference in view versus everybody else's views." His own definition of alpha, same interview: "the way you make money is you have a view that is different from that of the market and people come to agree with you." Ken Griffin made the parallel claim in October 2025: generative AI "enhances productivity, but for uncovering alpha it just falls short" **[secondary]** — and was still holding the alpha half of that position in late 2026 while conceding AI now does PhD-level research work in hours.

2. **The claim is correct about a specific usage — and the literature already falsifies its universal form.** Asking a prompted LLM for a verbal verdict ("what's different / will it go up?") does return something close to a consensus draw, and standalone LLM traders fail in clean tests: FINSABER (KDD 2026) shows reported LLM-strategy advantages deteriorate over 20 years / 100+ symbols **[verified]**; Nof1's Alpha Arena lost money in 26–27 of 32 real-money model runs **[index]**. But LLMs used as *measurement instruments and hypothesis generators inside a selection system* have produced non-consensus, out-of-sample signal in peer-reviewed or carefully designed work: GPT-4 headline scores on strictly post-cutoff news (Lopez-Lira & Tang, *JFE* 2026), LLM *embeddings* plus a supervised head (Chen, Kelly & Xiu), and — cleanest of all — Koijen & Levy's **real-time** benchmark in which prompts were frozen *before* Q4-2025 earnings and frontier LLMs explained ~15% of announcement-return variation, rising to ~20% after outcome-driven prompt/context optimization **[verified]**. None of these is "ask the model, trade its answer."

3. **There is a mature, named research program on escaping averaging** (§3, first-class section): novelty search and quality-diversity archives (Lehman & Stanley; MAP-Elites), LLM-guided evolutionary program search (ELM → FunSearch → AlphaEvolve), disagreement-preserving multi-agent designs, distribution-level decoding (verbalized sampling), RL from verifiable/outcome rewards (RLVR; DeepSeek-R1), and conditioning on proprietary corpora. Its convergent lesson: **you do not fix consensus at the prompt; you fix it by changing what selects** — an evaluator, archive, or reward that is *not* text likelihood and *not* human preference. Its convergent warning: every one of these methods, left alone, collapses back — debate is provably a martingale (Choi et al. 2025), persona panels are ~100× less dispersed than human panels (arXiv:2511.02458), RLVR sharpens rather than widens the base distribution (Yue et al. 2025), and cross-model disagreement is mostly model-specific noise until validated ("Same Text, Different Numbers").

4. **"Isn't this just generating random ideas and testing thousands of them?" — Essentially yes, and the arithmetic is unforgiving** (§5.10). Even *journal-published* factors — the most pre-filtered hypothesis pool in finance — replicate at contested rates (64% insignificant under Hou-Xue-Zhang's protocol; ~85% replication under Jensen-Kelly-Pedersen's). Raw machine-generated candidates should be planned at a **0.1–2% true-edge base rate**, which means 300–3,000 *effectively independent* candidates for a 95% chance of at least one real edge — while the False Strategy Theorem guarantees the best of 1,000 *worthless* candidates shows a Sharpe of ~3.26 in backtest units. The program only works if (i) the trial denominator is logged and every result is deflated by it (DSR), (ii) selection quality is audited (PBO), (iii) cheap screens kill 99% of candidates before any of them consumes the one scarce resource — **forward calendar time** — and (iv) generation is *directed* (mutations/crossovers/inversions of an existing sleeve fleet, novelty-archived), because directed operators raise the per-draw base rate by orders of magnitude over uniform random and over fresh LLM brainstorming, whose draws are heavily correlated near-duplicates of consensus.

5. **The ranked playbook (§5)** puts selection pressure first and prompts last: (1) sealed forward selection over LLM-generated hypotheses; (2) conditioning on the operation's own ledger — the only corpus guaranteed absent from every training set; (3) disagreement-as-signal across heterogeneous model families, preserved rather than debated away; (4) divergence-paid second-opinion seats scored only on their residual vs a measured consensus (analyst consensus, options-implied move, the fleet's own median); (5) sealed Brier/calibration scoring with Platt/extremization; (6) QD/island evolution over the sleeve population; (7) LLMs as sensors (embeddings, token-probability confidence, residualized "pure news"), not oracles; (8) consensus-as-data (run the public prompt, measure your overlap, fade crowding); (9) anti-consensus prompting and tail sampling — real, measured, and weakest alone.

6. **What cannot be circumvented (§6):** Morillo is right that no prompting trick manufactures information that is in neither the weights nor the inputs; the operation has no proprietary *external* data, so its entire informational edge must come from data it *generates* (fills, journals, near-misses, disagreement records, forward results) and from speed of *evaluation*, not speed of trading — the Lopez-Lira & Tang edge lives mostly in the first minutes after news, a latency race a small operation loses. Statistically, 6–12 months can verdict a **high-breadth** system (hundreds of sleeves, thousands of resolved bets, intermediate-outcome scoring — Morillo's own IC-transitivity doctrine) but can *never* verdict a handful of discretionary theses: detecting a 55% hit rate needs ~785 independent resolved calls **[arithmetic]**.

## 1. The claim itself: strongest form, and the strongest counter-evidence

### 1.1 What Morillo actually said — and a provenance warning

**Identity [verified].** Daniel Morillo is Co-Founder and Head of Quantitative Strategies at Freestone Grove Partners; he "was the Head of Quantitative Research at Citadel, where he spent six years," responsible for the central portfolio/book, trading algorithms and analytics (Milken Institute speaker bio: https://milkeninstitute.org/events/global-conference-2025/speakers/daniel-morillo).

**The verbatim argument [verified — full transcript opened and read].** Bloomberg *Odd Lots*, "The Math That Explains How Multi-Strategy Hedge Funds Make Money," published 7 Oct 2024 (transcript: https://api.omny.fm/orgs/e73c998e-6e60-432f-8610-ae210140c5b1/clips/4ebca9fb-804a-4d62-8b51-b1ff013c06d1/transcript?format=TextWithTimestamps&). Asked "could you switch the emphasis to more AI… tell us what's real and what's BS," Morillo (~00:50:48–00:52:00; transcript lines 1228–1236) said, in substance and near-verbatim:

> "If you think about how AI is trained, you feed it all this text essentially mostly from the Internet. And the job that it's trying to do is… predict the most likely answer to a question, or the most likely thing that comes after some prompt… if you ask it, hey, what is different about company X? By definition, it's going to tell you what everybody else thinks is different about company X, which means it's actually not the different thing. Aka, you're getting the consensus… it's been trained on the average of things kind of by definition… that is not quite the same as the jump to — and therefore here's a difference in view versus everybody else's views."

Three qualifications in the same passage matter for fairness: (i) consensus is **useful** — "that could be quite useful in the way you think about doing data analysis"; summarization and theme-extraction are legitimate; (ii) his firm *does* invest in AI work; (iii) his objection is specifically to the inference *consensus-extraction ⇒ differentiated view*. Earlier in the same interview (~00:05:56) he defined the payoff structure the objection rests on: **"the way you make money is you have a view that is different from that of the market and people come to agree with you."**

**Provenance warning [could not verify].** The mission describes "Bloomberg Invest remarks, widely clipped in November 2026." A June 2024 Bloomberg Invest panel listing does show a Daniel Morillo speaking on AI's impact (via a podcast listing, **[index]**), but no text of LLM/alpha remarks from it was found. The requesting agent should treat the mission's premise as *substantively accurate, bibliographically garbled*: the claim is Morillo's, the venue/date in the premise could not be confirmed, and the 2024 transcript above is the citable primary source. Everything below answers the argument on its merits, which are independent of the venue.

**The same claim from the top of the industry [secondary].** Ken Griffin, October 2025, JPMorgan Robin Hood Investors Conference, as reported in clip coverage (https://www.instagram.com/p/DP30oq_DaYx/ ; analysis at https://convergences.substack.com/p/citadel-the-most-profitable-hedge ): "with GenAI there are clearly ways it enhances productivity, but for uncovering alpha it just falls short." By October 2026 Griffin was describing AI doing work that "used to take our PhDs and Masters four to six weeks" in "a few hours," while insisting "the vast majority of the alpha that we create is a testament to the creativity, the ingenuity, the analysis of the people on our team" (https://ceointerviews.ai/interview/3102309/ **[index]**). The industry's strongest skeptic thus concedes the *research-productivity* half and holds the *alpha* half — exactly Morillo's line.

**The strongest steelman** (assembled from Morillo + the critical literature, §1.3): (a) *Informational*: a model trained on public text contains, at best, public information; alpha requires information or processing the marginal price-setter lacks. (b) *Statistical*: maximum-likelihood training + preference tuning concentrate output on the mode of the text distribution — the consensus is not a bug but the objective. (c) *Equilibrium*: any signal a widely available model emits on widely available data is emitted to everyone simultaneously, so it is priced or crowded on arrival (cf. McLean & Pontiff decay, §4). (d) *Evidential*: apparent LLM backtest alpha is substantially memorization (§2.3). A popularized version making the same points ("trained on the average of human consensus… mathematically tethered to being common and consensus") is at https://encyclopediaofstocks.substack.com/p/the-alpha-paradox-why-llms-cannot **[index]** — commentary, not primary.

### 1.2 The strongest counter-evidence: where LLMs *did* produce non-consensus signal

Read carefully, every credible positive result shares a design feature that takes the LLM *out* of the role Morillo criticizes:

1. **Lopez-Lira & Tang, "Can ChatGPT Forecast Stock Price Movements?", *Journal of Financial Economics* 2026** (arXiv:2304.07619, https://arxiv.org/html/2304.07619v5 **[index]**; summary https://jun.jahee.shop/article/643). GPT-4 scored headlines as positive/negative/uncertain for the firm's short-term price. Sample: 159,137 firm-headline-dates, Oct 2021–May 2024 — **strictly after GPT-4's Sept 2021 knowledge cutoff**, which is what makes it a forecast test rather than a memory test. Overnight long-short: +34 bp/day pre-cost, 58% hit rate, Sharpe 2.97; intraday: +50 bp/day, Sharpe 2.63; cumulative pre-cost ~650–700%. Crucial qualifiers: the edge is concentrated in the **initial reaction and 1–2 day drift**, is much stronger on the **short side** (Sharpe 2.01 vs 0.78 long) and in **small stocks and negative news**, and trading costs/latency bite hard. *What made it work:* the model was used as a **fast reader of new information it had never seen**, not as an oracle opining on known facts — the headline is post-cutoff *private-to-the-model* input at decision time. That is already a circumvention of Morillo's setup: his example asks about a company the model was trained on; this asks it to *process* something it wasn't.

2. **Chen, Kelly & Xiu, "Expected Returns and Large Language Models"** (SSRN 4416687, https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4416687 **[index]**). LLM **embeddings** of news — the model's internal representation, never its verbal verdict — fed into a supervised return model. The signals "contain incremental information beyond traditional technical predictors, such as return reversal, and firm-level characteristics," deliver "economically meaningful Sharpe ratios after transaction costs," robust across 16 markets / 13 languages, and the authors report the results are "not driven by look-ahead bias." *What made it work:* nobody asked the model what it thinks. The consensus lives in the model's *output distribution over words*; the embedding space retains far more — including minority, weakly-weighted representations — and a **supervised head trained on realized returns** selects whichever dimensions actually predicted, consensus or not.

3. **Koijen & Levy, "Assessing the Benefits of Optimized Agentic AI Systems for Asset Pricing" (Chicago Booth, 30 June 2026) [verified — full PDF read]** (https://finm-32800.github.io/_downloads/f8b4cc1124ff7587a678fb69d61bb749/KoijenLevy2026OptimizedAgenticAISystems.pdf). The methodologically cleanest result in this report: a **real-time, out-of-sample** benchmark — prompts, context and parameters frozen *before* each earnings announcement in Q4 2025, predictions made only from information available at announcement time (sidestepping both look-ahead and reflexivity objections by construction). Findings: frontier LLMs explain **up to 15%** of announcement-return variation from transcript + earnings surprise alone, vs ~5% for earnings-surprise regressions and ~8% for the prior text literature; **no single model dominates** (best model varies by industry — a direct argument for multi-family seats); and **optimizing prompts/context against realized outcomes** (GEPA genetic-Pareto search; ACE agentic context engineering) raises explained variation to **~20% out-of-sample**. The optimization even recovered the anti-consensus principle Morillo would endorse: GEPA taught the model that "'good' is not the same as 'news'" — returns are driven by *belief revisions relative to expectations*, not by unconditional quality. *What made it work:* sealed forward evaluation + selection pressure from realized outcomes on the *system around the model*.

4. **Frozen-checkpoint "time capsule" outlooks** (Lehner & Lopez-Lira 2026, https://pdfs.assets.alphaxiv.org/2604.21433v1.pdf **[index]**): outlook scores extracted from twelve frozen OpenAI checkpoints predict one-month cross-sectional returns with t = 4.25 after controlling for size, quality, value, momentum and market-implied cheapness. Suggests even the model's *stored* view is not identical to the market's *priced* view — the consensus in text and the consensus in prices are two different objects, and the gap between them is itself a signal. (Caveat: checkpoint "views" partly reflect training-window information; treat as suggestive.)

5. **The withdrawn flagship — a warning, not evidence.** Kim, Muhn & Nikolaev's "Financial Statement Analysis with Large Language Models" (Chicago Booth, 2024) reported GPT-4 beating analysts at predicting earnings direction (~60% vs ~53–57%) from anonymized statements, and is still quoted everywhere. **The paper was withdrawn on 20 Feb 2025** after a co-author "identified inconsistencies in the data and analyses while attempting to replicate" (documented at https://www.theaccountingcycle.com/learn/ai/ai-in-financial-analysis and https://github.com/johnmalpass/hunter-research/blob/HEAD/docs/current/PRIOR_ART_2026-09.md **[index]**), even though the Booth project page still displays the preliminary claims (https://www.chicagobooth.edu/research/fama-miller/finance-research/funding/a-demand-system-approach-for-fixed-income/financial-statement-analysis-with-large-language-models). **Do not cite its numbers as established.** Its fate is itself a finding: in this field, even the best-publicized positive result collapsed on replication — which is precisely why the operation's verdict mechanism must be sealed forward testing, not literature.

### 1.3 The negative evidence Morillo's side can cite

- **FINSABER (Li et al., KDD 2026) [verified]** (https://arxiv.org/html/2505.07078v5): re-evaluating LLM timing strategies over two decades and 100+ symbols with survivorship/look-ahead/data-snooping controls, "previously reported LLM advantages deteriorate significantly"; strategies are "overly conservative in bull markets… and overly aggressive in bear markets." Most published LLM-agent evaluations use <1 year and <10 symbols.
- **Live real-money contests [index]:** Nof1 Alpha Arena (recomputed from exchange data at https://tradingagentstack.com/alpha-arena-results/): Season 1 (Oct–Nov 2025, crypto perps, $10k/model): Qwen3 Max +20.3%, DeepSeek V3.1 +6.9%, Claude Sonnet 4.5 −29.9%, Grok 4 −45.1%, Gemini 2.5 Pro −57.7%, GPT-5 −63.4%. Season 1.5: only 5 of 32 accounts finished up. A separate Prediction Arena study (arXiv:2604.07355, via secondary compilations) found six frontier models losing 16–31% on Kalshi over 57 days, with research volume ~uncorrelated with returns. Two-week windows are noise-dominated — but the base rate they establish is *negative*, and no live contest has produced the opposite base rate.
- **Reasoning quality ≠ returns [verified]** (Stanford multi-agent trading-debate study, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.29701): across 210 runs, aggregate reasoning quality correlated with Sharpe at r = 0.07 (p = 0.29). Better-structured LLM argument is measurable, controllable — and economically inert. Any operation that grades its agents on eloquence is grading the wrong thing.

## 2. Mechanism level: why outputs converge to consensus

**2.1 Pretraining is distribution-matching.** An LLM is trained to maximize likelihood of human text; its conditional distribution *is* an estimate of "what a typical writer would say next." Sampling at low temperature or taking the modal completion returns the center of that distribution. Morillo's "trained on the average" is loose (it's the full distribution, not the mean) but directionally right for how models are *used*: default decoding + default prompting returns the mode.

**2.2 Alignment narrows the distribution further — measurably.** Zhang et al., "Verbalized Sampling" (arXiv:2510.01171, https://arxiv.org/html/2510.01171v4 **[index]**): post-training alignment causes **mode collapse**, driven by **typicality bias** in preference data — human annotators systematically favor familiar, typical text. Their training-free fix (ask the model to verbalize a *distribution* over responses with probabilities) increases diversity 1.6–2.1× and recovers **66.8% of the base model's diversity** — i.e., roughly a third of the base distribution's diversity is destroyed by alignment and *not* recovered by prompting. The creativity-measurement literature points the same way (RLHF-associated drops in novelty indices; inference-time tricks giving limited gains) **[secondary compilations]**. RLHF also produces **hedging toward 50%** in probabilistic forecasts — Bridgewater's AIA team built Platt-scaling/extremization into their forecaster specifically to correct LLMs' "systematic hedging toward 50%" (§3.5) — and **sycophancy**: models shift toward the user's/interlocutor's stated view, which in multi-agent settings becomes *sycophantic convergence* (§3.3).

**2.3 Memorization makes historical "forecasting" unidentifiable.** Lopez-Lira, Tang & Zhu, "The Memorization Problem" (arXiv:2504.14765, https://arxiv.org/html/2504.14765v2 **[index]**): GPT-4o recalls *exact* pre-cutoff S&P 500 levels, individual stock prices, unemployment and GDP figures; explicit instructions to respect a historical cutoff **fail**; masking entities **fails** (models reconstruct them from minimal context); memorization extends to embeddings; post-cutoff recall is absent. Hence: "counterfactual forecasting ability is non-identified when the model has seen the realized values." Glasserman & Lin (*J. Financial Data Science* 2024, doi:10.3905/jfds.2023.1.143) add the **distraction effect**: the model's general knowledge of a named firm contaminates its reading of new text — anonymized headlines *outperformed* identified ones in-sample. Sarkar & Vafa (2024) similarly demonstrate look-ahead in pretrained LM forecasts. **Implication that drives the whole playbook: the only uncontaminated evaluation of an LLM-in-the-loop system is forward, on events after every involved model's cutoff, with inputs sealed before outcomes.** Backtests of LLM *verbal* signals on pre-cutoff events are not weak evidence; they are, in the authors' framing, *no* evidence.

**2.4 Shared models ⇒ shared errors ⇒ crowding.** If thousands of users prompt the same few models on the same headlines, outputs correlate by construction. The Bank of England's Financial Policy Committee (April 2025; April 2026 follow-ups, https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/-/media/boe/files/letter/2026/response-to-tsc-inquiry-report-on-ai-in-financial-services **[index]**) is actively simulating AI-agent **herding** — correlated positioning and procyclical amplification from common models/data — as a financial-stability risk. The FSB (Nov 2024) flagged the same monoculture channel. This is Morillo's argument at market level: even if a public-model signal were real, its *publication in the weights* is its arbitrage.

## 3. The beyond-consensus research program (first-class section)

This is the existing literature on getting AI systems *past* averaging. Organized by lever, with headline findings and the failure mode each paper itself documents. The pattern to notice: **every successful method changes the selection environment around the model; none relies on asking the model to "be contrarian."**

### 3.1 Novelty search and quality-diversity: stop optimizing the objective

- **Novelty Search — Lehman & Stanley, "Abandoning Objectives: Evolution Through the Search for Novelty Alone," *Evolutionary Computation* 2011** (doi:10.1162/EVCO_a_00025). Selection rewards *behavioral distance from an archive of past behaviors* — no fitness objective at all. On deceptive problems (maze navigation, biped locomotion) where the objective gradient leads into traps, novelty search solves tasks objective-driven search cannot, because the stepping stones to a solution often don't resemble the goal. **Novelty Search with Local Competition** (Lehman & Stanley 2011) adds quality *within* a behavioral neighborhood — novelty to explore, local competition to exploit. Stanley & Lehman's book-length statement, *Why Greatness Cannot Be Planned* (2015), generalizes: ambitious objectives are systematically deceptive.
- **MAP-Elites — Mouret & Clune 2015** (arXiv:1504.04909). Instead of one optimum, maintain an **archive**: a grid over behavioral descriptors, keeping the *best solution in each niche*. The output is an illuminated map of high-performing diversity, which is also a reservoir of stepping stones. Cully, Clune, Tarapore & Mouret (*Nature* 2015, "Robots that can adapt like animals") used the archive for rapid damage recovery — diversity bought *robustness*, not just exploration. **POET** (Wang, Lehman, Clune & Stanley 2019) coevolves the environments with the agents, generating its own curriculum.
- **Why this matters for trading:** a sleeve fleet selected purely on Sharpe is objective-driven search on a deceptive landscape — it converges to minor variants of whatever worked recently (the quant equivalent of mode collapse, and a crowding machine). A QD archive over sleeve *behavior* (holding period, volatility regime response, sector/factor footprint, options moneyness/tenor, drawdown shape) keeps the best sleeve *per niche*, preserving strategies that are mediocre on average but elite in a regime the fleet hasn't seen lately. This is a concrete, implementable anti-consensus structure, and it is the direct ancestor of the archives inside FunSearch/AlphaEvolve (below).

### 3.2 LLM-guided evolutionary program search: the evaluator breaks the consensus

- **ELM (Evolution through Large Models) — Lehman et al. 2022** (arXiv:2206.08896). The conceptual bridge: an LLM fine-tuned on code *diffs* serves as an **intelligent mutation operator** inside MAP-Elites. Result: hundreds of thousands of functional programs in Sodarace, a domain **entirely absent from pretraining** — the system generated working novelty in a space where the model had no consensus to return, because mutation + QD selection, not recall, did the work. **LMX** (Meyerson et al., *ACM TELO* 2024) showed LLM prompting also works as a generic *crossover* operator. **QDAIF** (Bradley et al., ICLR 2024) uses AI feedback to judge quality/diversity inside QD loops.
- **FunSearch — Romera-Paredes et al., *Nature* 2023** (doi:10.1038/s41586-023-06924-6). A frozen LLM proposes program variants; an **automated evaluator** scores them; an **island-model** population database keeps diverse high scorers as parents. It produced genuinely new mathematics (largest cap-set constructions in ~20 years; bin-packing heuristics beating hand-designed ones). DeepMind's design statement is the whole thesis in one line: pair the LLM with an evaluator that "guards against hallucinations and incorrect ideas" — the model proposes, *reality disposes*. Summaries: https://safeguard.sh/resources/blog/evolutionary-coding-agents-from-funsearch-to-alphaevolve-how-they-work **[index]**.
- **AlphaEvolve — Novikov et al. 2025** (arXiv:2506.13131). Scales FunSearch to whole codebases: an **ensemble** of Gemini Flash (breadth) + Pro (depth), diff-based edits, evaluation cascades, and a program database combining **MAP-Elites + island models** explicitly to maintain diversity. Reported results (firm-reported, but machine-checkable in kind): 4×4 complex matrix multiplication in 48 multiplications vs Strassen's 49 (a 56-year-old record), ~0.7% of Google's fleet compute recovered via a Borg scheduling heuristic, kernel speedups. The white paper's own caveat, quoted in secondary coverage: without "a cheap, honest evaluator the method has little to offer."
- **AlphaAgent — Tang et al., KDD 2025** (https://arxiv.org/html/2502.16789v2/ **[index]**) is the finance-native version: Idea→Factor→Eval agents generating formulaic alphas, with three regularizers aimed *directly* at the consensus/decay problem — (i) **originality enforcement** via AST similarity against existing alpha libraries, (ii) hypothesis–factor semantic alignment, (iii) complexity control. Authors report 11.0% annualized excess (IR 1.49) on CSI 500 and 8.74% (IR 1.05) on S&P 500 over four years, with slower alpha decay than genetic-programming baselines. **Flag:** these are the authors' own backtests in a literature where (§2.3) backtests are exactly what's suspect; treat the *architecture* (originality-as-regularizer) as the contribution, not the returns.
- **Transfer to the operation:** trading has what math has — a cheap, honest evaluator — *if* the evaluator is sealed forward returns rather than backtests. The fleet of ~340 sleeves is already a population; what's missing (per this literature) is (a) LLM mutation/crossover operators over sleeve code, (b) a QD/island archive so selection preserves behavioral diversity instead of collapsing to one regime's winners, and (c) an originality penalty measured against both the fleet and the public factor zoo. §5.6 and §5.10 quantify the trial-accounting this requires.

### 3.3 Multi-agent debate and cross-model disagreement: what the evidence actually says

This literature is the most mis-sold in the area, so: findings in both directions, with the strongest designs last.

- **Du et al., "Improving Factuality and Reasoning in Language Models through Multiagent Debate," ICML 2024** (arXiv:2305.14325). Three GPT-3.5 instances, two debate rounds: arithmetic 67.0%→81.8%, GSM8K 77.0%→85.0%, MMLU 63.9%→71.1%, factual biographies 66.0%→73.8%, beating single-agent and self-reflection. **The buried number:** plain majority voting over the same agents scored 81.0% on GSM8K — i.e., most of debate's gain is *ensembling independent draws*, not persuasion. The study did not compare against compute-matched self-consistency.
- **Liang et al., "Encouraging Divergent Thinking… through Multi-Agent Debate," EMNLP 2024** (arXiv:2305.19118): debate helps when disagreement is *maintained* (their "affirmative debate" prompting) and degrades when agents capitulate early; an LLM judge is not necessarily fair when debaters are different models.
- **Zhang et al., "Stop Overvaluing Multi-Agent Debate," 2025** (arXiv:2502.08788 **[index]**): the broadest head-to-head — 5 debate frameworks × 9 benchmarks × 4 models. **No debate method beat plain chain-of-thought in more than 20% of 36 configurations; CoT won >80%.** The authors' constructive conclusion is in their title: *embrace model heterogeneity* — diversity of the underlying models is the variable that survives; debate choreography is not.
- **Choi, Zhu & Li, "Debate or Vote," NeurIPS 2025 spotlight** (arXiv:2508.17536, https://arxiv.org/html/2508.17536v2/ **[index]**). The theoretical result this whole subsection turns on: modeling debate as Bayesian belief updating, they **prove debate induces a martingale over agents' belief in the correct answer** — expected correctness is unchanged by debate rounds; agents randomly persuade and mislead each other, canceling in expectation. Empirically across 7 benchmarks, majority voting matches or beats debate (Qwen2.5-7B aggregate: single 0.7205, **vote 0.7691**, debate-2-rounds 0.7377, debate-3-rounds 0.7112 — more rounds *hurt*), and majority voting's gains follow the classic ensemble theorem. Interventions that *bias updates toward correction* (an external signal about who's right) can rescue debate — which is another way of saying debate only helps when an evaluator enters the loop.
- **The trading-specific version — Huang et al., Stanford** (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.29701 **[verified]**, preprint): in a multi-agent portfolio-debate system, the central failure mode is **sycophantic convergence** — agents abandon independent positions across critique rounds and collapse to similar allocations. A **Jensen-Shannon-divergence intervention that preserves disagreement improved Sharpe by +0.14 (p = 0.028) and Sortino by +0.25 (p = 0.026)**; interventions improving measured *reasoning quality* did nothing. Preprint, historical simulation — but it is the only study found that prices *disagreement preservation itself* in portfolio units, and its sign matches Choi et al.'s theorem: the value is in the independent draws, and interaction destroys it unless engineered against.
- **Ensembles of forecasters (the "vote" evidence at scale):** Schoenegger et al., "Wisdom of the Silicon Crowd," *Science Advances* 2024 (https://arxiv.org/html/2402.19379v5/ **[index]**): a 12-model ensemble's median forecast was statistically indistinguishable from a 925-person human crowd (Brier ~0.20 vs 0.19, 31 questions — small n, wide equivalence bound). Halawi et al. (NeurIPS 2024, arXiv:2402.18563): a retrieval-augmented, fine-tuned GPT-4 system reached Brier 0.179 vs the human crowd's 0.149, and **combining model + crowd beat either alone (0.146)** — the model carried information the crowd didn't, despite being worse. That complementarity result is the forecasting form of the whole circumvention thesis: *you don't need the model to beat consensus; you need its errors to be different from consensus's errors.*

**Design verdict for the operation:** agents as **peers who vote and are scored independently** — the operation's stated design — is what the evidence supports. Agents who *debate to consensus* re-import Morillo's problem inside the fleet: the fleet's consensus is still a consensus, now with extra steps and a martingale guarantee that the talking added nothing in expectation.

### 3.4 Sampling and decoding for diversity: real, bounded, and mostly not where the alpha is

- **The likelihood trap — Holtzman et al. 2020** ("The Curious Case of Neural Text Degeneration"; nucleus/top-p sampling): maximizing likelihood (greedy/beam) produces bland, repetitive, generic text — the decoding-level form of consensus collapse. **Diverse beam search** (Vijayakumar et al. / Li et al. 2016) penalizes inter-beam similarity but stays anchored to high-likelihood regions. Temperature/top-k/top-p sampling buys diversity at a measured quality cost, and at high temperature the tail is incoherence, not insight.
- **Verbalized Sampling — Zhang et al. 2025** (above, §2.2): the strongest decoding-level result — prompting for an explicit *distribution* over answers recovers much of alignment's diversity loss (1.6–2.1× diversity; 66.8% of base-model diversity) at no measured factuality cost. For hypothesis generation this is the correct default: ask for *the distribution over plausible strategies with rough probabilities*, then sample the tails deliberately — the tails are where difference-from-consensus lives, and VS makes them reachable without temperature noise.
- **The measured limits (important):** (i) sampling diversifies *expression* more than *belief* — see the dispersion measurements in §3.7: persona + temperature variation produced forecast dispersion ~100× smaller than a human panel's. (ii) Decoding tricks operate on the *pretrained* distribution; they cannot surface what alignment removed or pretraining never contained. (iii) In the creativity-benchmark literature, post-hoc diversity gains from prompting/decoding are consistently smaller than the diversity lost in post-training **[secondary]**. Decoding is a *breadth* tool for the generation funnel (§5.9), never a source of edge by itself: a tail sample is a *candidate*, and only the evaluator can promote it.

### 3.5 RL from verifiable / outcome rewards: train on what happened, not on what people say

- **RLVR — named by AI2's Tülu 3 (Nov 2024, arXiv:2411.15124); demonstrated at scale by DeepSeek-R1 (Jan 2025, arXiv:2501.12948; *Nature* 2025).** R1-Zero applied GRPO to a *base* model with only rule-based rewards — final-answer correctness + format — **no supervised fine-tuning, no neural reward model** (the authors explicitly avoided learned reward models as hackable). Reasoning behaviors (long chains, self-verification) emerged from outcome reward alone. This is the existence proof that a model's behavior can be moved *off* the imitation-of-text optimum by an outcome signal: RLHF optimizes "sounds right to annotators" (a consensus machine, §2.2); RLVR optimizes "was right" (a difference machine, insofar as being right when others aren't is what's rewarded).
- **The finance translation is direct:** the operation's resolved forward outcomes *are* verifiable rewards. A seat/prompt/context that is iteratively selected or tuned (GEPA/ACE-style, as in Koijen & Levy §1.2, or full fine-tuning where feasible) against **Brier score / benchmark-relative return on sealed forward samples** is RLVR with the market as verifier. Halawi et al.'s fine-tuned forecaster and Bridgewater's finding (below) that fine-tuning on expert judgments beat prompt engineering are early instances.
- **Bridgewater AIA Forecaster (firm technical report, arXiv:2511.07678 — flag: firm-reported, not peer-reviewed)** (https://arxiv.org/html/2511.07678v1 **[index]**): agentic news search + ~10 independent forecasts reconciled by a supervisor + **Platt scaling to correct hedging-toward-50%**. It *matched* superforecasters on ForecastBench (Brier 0.0753 vs 0.0740) and — the load-bearing result — **underperformed market consensus on liquid prediction markets standalone (0.1258 vs 0.1106), yet a 67/33 blend of market + AIA beat both (0.106)**. A forecaster can be worse than consensus and still *add* to it, because its information is partially orthogonal. Also: removing search collapsed its Brier to 0.3609 — worse than predicting 50% always — i.e., the *inputs*, not the weights, carried the edge.
- **The limit — Yue et al., NeurIPS 2025 (pass@k analysis of RLVR):** RLVR models beat their base models at k = 1 sample, but **base models solve more problems at large k** — RLVR *sharpens* the distribution onto modes the base already had; it does not expand the support. Translated: outcome-training a seat makes its *typical* answer better-calibrated and more often right; it does not make the seat more original, and it can reduce the diversity the fleet depends on. And verifiers get gamed — 2026 reports document models exploiting grader loopholes (patching timers, special-casing tests) **[secondary]**. In trading, the analog of verifier-gaming is overfitting the forward-test protocol itself (e.g., sleeves that learn the grading window), which is why falsifiers must be pre-registered and the trial register complete (§5.10).

### 3.6 Fine-tuning and continued training on proprietary data in finance

- **BloombergGPT — Wu et al. 2023:** the canonical proprietary-corpus bet: 50B parameters trained on 363B tokens of Bloomberg's proprietary financial data + 345B general tokens (~$2.7M compute, secondary figures). It led financial NLP benchmarks at release — then was **routinely overtaken by frontier general models with retrieval**, and, per secondary reporting of Bloomberg's April 2026 statements, was never deployed in products; the production pattern became commercial foundation models + smaller custom components (https://www.facebook.com/chang.y.edward/posts/pfbid0GJDNVXmqC6DjPpxMBaop13aFoLtaswTLxdAS9PZuT9tcz9Sd9Lx1FaqTw1XpoFK2l **[secondary — treat the "never used" claim as unverified]**). Lesson: baking proprietary data into *weights* depreciates with every frontier release; keeping it in **retrieval, context, and evaluation** layers keeps it portable.
- **FinGPT — Yang et al. 2023 (AI4Finance):** the opposite price point — LoRA fine-tunes of open bases on curated financial corpora at ~$300/run, competitive on narrow tasks (sentiment classification). Cheap adaptation works where the target behavior is narrow and labelable.
- **What firms actually report:** Bridgewater's AIA publications (summarized at https://automatedalpha.substack.com/p/how-bridgewater-builds-ai-agents **[secondary]**) describe (i) a ~100M-document, **time-tagged** proprietary library ("the Vault") used as retrieval context — point-in-time discipline built into the corpus itself, (ii) **fine-tuning on expert judgments beating clever prompting**, including an RL-trained open model (Qwen3-235B) scoring 84.7% on their internal judgment benchmark, and (iii) an AI-driven macro fund (AIA) running >$4.5B with ML as primary decision-maker (fund existence/size are widely reported; performance is not public). Citadel's internal assistant is built on *licensed* transcripts/filings/broker research for its equities investors **[secondary compilations]** — again: proprietary *context*, frontier *weights*.
- **For this operation**, the proprietary corpus is not market data — it's the **ledger**: ~340 sleeves' fills, signal journals, near-miss logs (candidates generated and rejected, with reasons), cross-agent disagreement records, and resolved forward results. Fine-tuning or even LoRA-adapting an open model on *that* — e.g., to predict "sleeves like this, in regimes like this, failed forward" — conditions on information that is in **no** training set and no competitor's corpus, because the operation generated it. This is the one form of "proprietary data" available at small scale, and §5.2 ranks it accordingly. The BloombergGPT lesson caps the ambition: adapt small, keep the corpus in retrieval/context form, re-baseline against each new frontier model.

### 3.7 Direct measurements: LLM forecast dispersion vs human dispersion

The mission asked specifically for work *measuring* dispersion. Three direct results, and they cut in different directions:

1. **Persona panels barely disagree — arXiv:2511.02458 (Nov 2025)** (http://staging.alphaxiv.org/abs/2511.02458 **[index]**). GPT-4o was prompted to replicate the ECB Survey of Professional Forecasters across 50 quarterly rounds (2013–2025), using **2,368 different personas** at temperature 1. Accuracy was close to the human panel's. But the AI panel's forecast dispersion (IQR and SD) was **roughly two orders of magnitude lower** than the human panel's, and the persona ablation showed **no accuracy benefit from personas at all**. This is the single most direct measurement of Morillo's claim in forecast form: *2,368 differently-persona'd instances of one model are, for dispersion purposes, one forecaster.* Prompted diversity ≠ belief diversity.
2. **Cross-model disagreement is large but mostly noise — "Same Text, Different Numbers," arXiv:2609.31013** (https://arxiv.org/html/2609.31013 **[index]**). Seven providers' models scored 1,946 S&P 500 earnings calls (2024) on 13 textual constructs: cross-model rank correlations averaged only **0.52**, and the component common across models was only **34%** of score variation — so heterogeneous models *do* disagree substantially (unlike personas of one model). But that disagreement **did not predict subsequent analyst or market disagreement**: it is largely *model-specific measurement noise*, not latent ambiguity in the underlying text. Direct implication for disagreement-as-signal (§5.3): cross-model disagreement is a *candidate* signal that must earn its status on forward data per use-case; assuming disagreement = information is exactly the error this paper documents.
3. **Dispersion itself can be a priced signal — "Machine Forecast Disagreement," NBER w31583** (https://www.nber.org/papers/w31583 **[index]**). In a (pre-LLM) ML setting, dispersion across model specifications' return forecasts aligns with analyst disagreement but predicts returns *more* strongly: a decile spread shorting high-disagreement and buying low-disagreement stocks earned ~14%/yr value-weighted, attributed to mispricing under short-sale constraints. Dispersion is not only a *source* of candidate signals; its *level* per-name is a tradeable state variable — e.g., names where the operation's seats disagree most are names where (per this literature) mispricing and overpricing risk concentrate, which for an options book means: disagreement as a *sizing/avoidance* input, not just a directional one.
4. Supporting: St. Louis Fed *Review* 2024 (PaLM inflation forecasts vs SPF, https://fraser.stlouisfed.org//docs/publications/frbslreview/rev_stls_2024_v106_no12.pdf **[index]**) — LLM forecasts beat SPF MSE in most years (in-sample period — caveat §2.3), with sampling dispersion that grows with horizon and is mechanically temperature-controlled; and GPT-as-sell-side-analyst work (arXiv:2412.01069) benchmarking GPT earnings forecasts against analyst consensus directly.

## 5. The ranked playbook

Ranking criterion: expected difference-from-consensus per unit of the operation's scarcest resources (forward calendar time; statistical power), given *only* public market data + self-generated records. Each entry: **(a)** how it produces difference-from-consensus, **(b)** the private/generated data it exploits, **(c)** verification/falsification on resolved forward samples vs benchmarks, **(d)** main failure mode.

### Rank 1 — Sealed forward selection pressure over LLM-generated hypotheses (generate-and-test, with the market as evaluator)
**(a)** The LLM's consensus prior is used only as a *proposal distribution*; promotion is decided by realized forward outcomes the model cannot have memorized and did not influence. Selection on outcomes is the one force in this report with existence proofs of producing non-consensus artifacts (FunSearch/AlphaEvolve in evaluator-rich domains; Koijen & Levy's outcome-optimized prompts gaining ~5 points of R² *out-of-sample*). **(b)** The operation's own forward-test results and trial register — generated data, accruing daily, un-replicable by competitors because their fleets differ. **(c)** Every candidate is pre-registered (hypothesis, instrument, horizon, falsifier, benchmark — SPY/QQQ % return for the options book; sleeve-level excess for the fleet) *before* its forward window opens; verdicts are computed once, at the registered horizon, on the full cohort including failures. Falsifier: cohort-level forward excess ≤ 0 net of modeled costs, or DSR < 0.95 at the true trial count (§5.10). **(d)** *Time*: forward tests are slow (§5.10.4) — mitigated by Ranks 6–7 screens and by scoring intermediate outcomes (Rank 5). Also selection-pressure overfitting to the *protocol* if falsifiers are edited mid-flight — hence sealing.

### Rank 2 — Condition everything on the operation's own ledger (private-data conditioning without proprietary feeds)
**(a)** Retrieval/context (and, where justified, LoRA fine-tuning of an open model) over the fleet's fills, signal journals, near-miss logs, and post-mortems makes every seat's effective information set *different from every other user's of the same model* — the direct answer to "same model, same answer." The model stops being asked about "company X" (public, memorized, consensus) and starts being asked about *this fleet's history with situations like this* (private, unmemorized, differentiating). **(b)** Own fills (including paper fills — they encode the fleet's actual decision function), own signal journals, **near-miss logs** (the rejected candidates are the highest-value training rows: they define the fleet's decision boundary), disagreement records, resolved outcomes. **(c)** A/B the conditioning itself on sealed forward samples: same seats, same questions, ± ledger context; the conditioned variant must show Brier/excess improvement over the unconditioned variant and over consensus baselines, else the ledger is decoration. **(d)** Small-data overfitting and self-reference: the ledger teaches the fleet's own biases back to it (a model fine-tuned on your journal learns to agree with you). Guard: condition on *outcomes* (what happened after each journal entry), not on the journal's opinions.

### Rank 3 — Disagreement-as-signal across heterogeneous model families (preserve it; never debate it away)
**(a)** Independent seats from different model families (different pretraining mixes, different alignment) produce genuinely different draws (§3.7.2: cross-model common component only ~34%). The *pattern* of agreement/disagreement — which seats dissent, on what, by how much — is information no single seat contains. Per §3.3, the correct aggregation is **independent votes + recorded dispersion**, not debate: debate is a martingale (Choi et al.) and empirically converges sycophantically (Huang et al.), while a JSD-style disagreement-preservation intervention *added* Sharpe. **(b)** The cross-agent disagreement record itself — a time series nobody else has, because nobody else runs this exact panel. **(c)** Pre-register the disagreement statistic (e.g., cross-family SD / JSD per event) and test forward whether it predicts (i) realized volatility/event-move magnitude, (ii) the *sign* of the minority seat's excess, (iii) options-book outcomes vs implied move. "Same Text, Different Numbers" is the standing warning: disagreement that doesn't predict anything forward is noise and must be demoted to a sizing input or dropped. **(d)** Noise masquerading as signal (§3.7.2); correlated failure when families share training data (they partially do — measure pairwise seat correlation on resolved samples and treat the panel's *effective* size, not its headcount, as the ensemble size).

### Rank 4 — Divergence-paid second-opinion seats (score the residual, never the forecast)
**(a)** A typed seat (e.g., "earnings-event seat," "vol-regime seat") is never graded on accuracy alone. It is graded on **excess over a measured consensus baseline** for the same event: analyst consensus (for earnings), the **options-implied move** (the market's consensus in dollars — the natural baseline for this operation's options book), prediction-market/market prices where available, and the fleet's own median sleeve view. A seat that merely matches consensus earns zero capital and zero influence, however "accurate" it is; a seat is paid only for the component of its forecast orthogonal to consensus that resolves correctly. This operationalizes Morillo: alpha *is* the residual. The AIA result (§3.5) is the proof of concept — a forecaster worse than consensus still improved the blend, because its residual carried information; Halawi's model+crowd blend (0.146 < 0.149 and < 0.179) is the same effect. **(b)** The seat-vs-consensus residual history on resolved events — generated, private, and exactly the quantity competitors cannot reconstruct. **(c)** Brier *skill* score vs the consensus baseline (not raw Brier), and benchmark-relative P&L of trades sized ∝ residual, on sealed forward cohorts; falsifier: residual-bucketed returns non-monotone or ≤ 0 in the top divergence bucket after costs. **(d)** Consensus baselines can be stale or themselves irrational (implied move embeds vol risk premium — beating it on average may be harvesting premium, not information; the grading must separate "seat predicted the *direction/magnitude* better" from "seat systematically sold insurance"). Also: residuals are small by construction, so position sizing must respect that most events warrant no trade.

### Rank 5 — Sealed probabilistic forecasting with calibration machinery (Brier, Platt, extremization)
**(a)** Forcing seats to emit *probabilities* (not verdicts), sealed before events, converts vague LLM opinion into a measurable object whose *calibration curve* is itself differentiating information: a seat that is systematically under-confident in a specific regime can be extremized into alpha; one that echoes consensus probabilities adds nothing and is detected doing so. Platt scaling / log-odds extremization corrects the RLHF hedge-to-50% (§2.2, §3.5). **(b)** The operation's resolved forecast archive (forecast, timestamp, consensus at forecast time, outcome) — the calibration dataset no outsider has. **(c)** This *is* the verification layer for Ranks 3–4: Brier skill vs consensus and vs climatology, calibration slope per seat per regime, all on forward samples only. Morillo's own measurement doctrine (Odd Lots, ~00:43:50–00:47:05 **[verified]**) belongs here: he argues skill should be measured on **intermediate variables** — a PM's earnings-surprise calls (IC ~10% vs surprises) rather than returns (IC ~1%) — because the transitive chain (predict surprises × surprises→returns) makes skill statistically detectable ~two orders of magnitude sooner. Score seats on event outcomes (surprise sign/magnitude, realized move vs implied) first; let P&L be the lagging confirmation. **(d)** Calibration is not discrimination: a perfectly calibrated consensus-echo scores fine on raw Brier. Hence Rank 4's skill-vs-consensus grading is mandatory, and calibration work without it becomes a machine for producing confident consensus.

### Rank 6 — Quality-diversity evolution over the sleeve population (islands + MAP-Elites archive)
**(a)** Replace "keep the top-k sleeves by Sharpe" (objective search on a deceptive landscape → regime-overfit monoculture) with a MAP-Elites archive over sleeve behavior descriptors (horizon, regime beta, instrument class, options tenor/moneyness, drawdown shape): best sleeve *per niche*, island sub-populations evolving semi-independently with occasional migration, LLM operators providing mutation (parameter/logic diffs) and crossover (signal combination) in the ELM/FunSearch pattern, plus an AlphaAgent-style **originality penalty** (AST/behavioral similarity vs the fleet and the public factor zoo). Difference-from-consensus is preserved *structurally*: niches that consensus-shaped selection would delete survive as stepping stones. **(b)** The fleet's own genotype→forward-phenotype map: which mutations, applied to which sleeves, in which regimes, produced what — a private evolutionary record. **(c)** Archive-level forward accounting: each niche's champion is graded on its forward window vs SPY/QQQ; the *archive's* claim is falsified if QD selection fails to beat top-k-Sharpe selection on forward cohort excess over 2+ regime windows (a meta-experiment, pre-registered like everything else). **(d)** Descriptor choice is a prior that can blind the archive (niches along irrelevant axes waste the forward budget); and QD multiplies the trial count — §5.10's denominator discipline applies to every cell.

### Rank 7 — LLMs as sensors, not oracles (embeddings, token probabilities, residualized text)
**(a)** Three measured techniques extract sub-consensus information the verbal output discards: (i) **embeddings + a supervised head** trained on the operation's own resolved outcomes (Chen-Kelly-Xiu pattern, §1.2); (ii) **inner token-probability confidence** — Chen, Didisheim & Somoza (2026, via lecture slides https://www.antoinedidisheim.com/slides/llms-in-finance-2 **[secondary]**) report that filtering Lopez-Lira-style headline signals by the model's own token-probability confidence materially raises portfolio Sharpe on post-cutoff headlines — the model's *uncertainty* is less consensus-bound than its answer; (iii) **residualized "pure news"** — Didisheim, Kelly et al. (2026, same slides): stripping news text of everything firm characteristics already predict *more than doubles* signal power and predicts returns up to 18 months — i.e., explicitly subtract the expected/consensus component of the text and trade the remainder. **(b)** The supervised head's training set is the operation's resolved forward archive; the residualization model is fit on public data but *applied* to the fleet's event flow. **(c)** Standard signal grading: forward IC of the sensor signal vs returns/event outcomes, incremental to the fleet's existing signals and to a public-prompt consensus baseline (Rank 8). **(d)** Embeddings memorize too (§2.3: memorization "extends to embeddings") — sensor signals built on pre-cutoff events are contaminated; only forward IC counts.

### Rank 8 — Consensus-as-data: measure the crowd you're accused of being
**(a)** Run, daily, the *canonical public prompt* ("You are a financial expert… is this headline good or bad for the stock?") on the canonical public models, and archive the output as the **LLM-consensus series**. It has three uses, none of which is following it: (i) a *baseline* every seat/sleeve must beat to claim non-consensus status (Ranks 4–5); (ii) a *crowding gauge* — when the fleet's aggregate position correlates with the LLM-consensus series, the fleet is the crowd, and sizing should fall (Morillo himself, and every fundamental PM, uses consensus exactly this way: as the thing to differ from *knowingly*); (iii) a *fade candidate* — in names/events where LLM-consensus is extreme and one-sided, test the inversion sleeve (Rank 6 will generate these anyway). **(b)** The overlap time series between fleet positioning and public-model consensus — generated data. **(c)** Forward-test the crowding gauge as a risk overlay: does de-sizing high-overlap periods improve fleet Sharpe/drawdown vs the un-overlaid fleet on sealed cohorts? **(d)** The public prompt is a moving target (models update silently); pin model versions where APIs allow, log version strings, and re-baseline on version changes — a seat whose "edge" jumps at a provider's silent update never had an edge.

### Rank 9 — Anti-consensus prompting and tail sampling (cheapest, weakest, still worth doing correctly)
**(a)** Verbalized sampling (distributions, not answers), tail sampling from that distribution, base/less-aligned models for generation, anonymized inputs (Glasserman & Lin's distraction effect: removing firm identity *improved* signal — the model's consensus *about the firm* was the contaminant), and inversion prompts ("argue the strongest case against the consensus read, with mechanisms") all widen the proposal distribution at near-zero cost. **(b)** None inherently — which is why this ranks last: it manufactures *candidates*, not information. Its output only acquires private value after Ranks 1–5 process it. **(c)** Graded as a *generation* technology, not a signal: does the VS/tail pipeline produce candidates whose forward pass rate (Rank 1 funnel) exceeds the default-prompt pipeline's, at equal trial counts? **(d)** The §3.7.1 result is the ceiling: persona/temperature diversity is ~100× thinner than human disagreement, and "contrarian" prompting mostly produces the consensus's *caricature* of contrarianism (the modal dissent in the training text). Anyone who stops at this rank has built a consensus generator with extra steps.

### 5.10 The quantitative core: is it just "generate random ideas and test thousands of them"?

**Short answer: the generation half, yes — with two corrections that change everything.** (1) The ideas are not, and must not be, *uniformly* random: an LLM + fleet-mutation proposal distribution is a strong prior, and §5.10.5 shows directed generation raises the per-draw hit rate by orders of magnitude — while also correlating the draws, which §5.10.2 shows is a statistical tax that must be paid honestly. (2) "Testing" cannot mean backtesting thousands of LLM-flavored ideas on history (contaminated, §2.3, and arithmetically guaranteed to manufacture fake winners, §5.10.2) nor forward-testing thousands of ideas naively (calendar-bound, §5.10.4). It means a **funnel**: cheap, honest screens kill ~99% before forward time is spent, and the survivors are verdicted as a *cohort* under a logged trial denominator. Man Group's public description of AlphaGPT (§4) and Two Sigma's "the bottleneck is evaluation" are this funnel at institutional scale.

#### 5.10.1 Base rates: how many generated hypotheses are real?

Anchor points, from most- to least-filtered pools:

| Pool | Survival rate | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Journal-published return predictors, re-tested under Hou-Xue-Zhang's strict protocol (value-weighted, microcap-controlled) | **~36%** significant at 5% (286/447 insignificant); **~15%** at t ≥ 3 | HXZ, *RFS* 2020 **[index]** |
| Same literature under Jensen-Kelly-Pedersen's empirical-Bayes protocol | **~85%** replicate | JKP, *JF* 2023 **[index]** |
| Published predictors' returns after publication | **−58%** vs in-sample (−26% purely out-of-sample) | McLean & Pontiff, *JF* 2016 **[index]** |
| LLM-agent strategies under broad/long re-evaluation (FINSABER) | advantages "deteriorate significantly," often vanish | KDD 2026 **[verified]** |
| Frontier LLMs trading live with real money (Alpha Arena S1+S1.5) | **6–7 of 38** model-runs profitable, windows too short to separate luck | **[index]** |

The HXZ/JKP dispute (same literature, 36% vs 85%) is about protocol, and both are measuring a pool that survived *peer review, journal selection, and the authors' own pre-filtering* — the most curated hypothesis pool in finance. A machine generation pipeline has no such filters at intake. **Planning assumption [arithmetic/judgment, stated as such]: a true-edge base rate π of 0.1%–2% per *effectively independent* generated candidate** — toward the top for fleet-mutation candidates (§5.10.5), toward the bottom for fresh LLM brainstorms on public information. Anyone assuming π above ~5% for raw generations is assuming their generator beats the *Journal of Finance*'s intake funnel; the burden of proof is theirs, and Rank 1's cohort accounting will settle it empirically within two quarters (the observed pass rate *is* the base-rate estimate).

#### 5.10.2 How many candidates must be generated — and what the null guarantees you'll "find"

Probability of at least one real edge among N independent candidates: **1 − (1−π)^N**. For 95% confidence of ≥1 real edge **[arithmetic]**:

| Base rate π | N for 95% chance of ≥1 real edge | Expected real edges in N=1,000 |
|---|---|---|
| 2% | ~149 | 20 |
| 1% | ~299 | 10 |
| 0.5% | ~598 | 5 |
| 0.1% | ~2,995 | 1 |
| 0.01% | ~29,956 | 0.1 |

Against that, the null's guarantee — the **False Strategy Theorem** (Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado & Zhu, *Notices of the AMS* 2014; primary PDF https://www.ams.org/notices/201405/rnoti-p458.pdf): the expected *maximum* Sharpe among N zero-skill trials is ≈ √V · [(1−γ)·Φ⁻¹(1−1/N) + γ·Φ⁻¹(1−1/(Ne))]. At unit trial variance **[index — recomputed in secondary expositions, e.g. https://www.interactiveml.org/blog/deflated-sharpe-ratio]**:

| N worthless trials | Expected best Sharpe (backtest units) |
|---|---|
| 10 | ~1.57 |
| 45 (≈ the max trials 5 years of daily data can support — "Minimum Backtest Length") | ~2.2 in these units; ≈1.0 *annualized* on 5y daily data |
| 100 | ~2.53 |
| 340 (this fleet's sleeve count, if all were fresh trials on one year) | ~2.9 × (1/√T) — i.e., a best-of-340 one-year Sharpe near **2.9 by pure luck** |
| 1,000 | ~3.26 |
| 1,000,000 (an LLM sweep's weekend) | ~4.87 |

Read the two tables together and the whole discipline follows: **at the scale where generate-and-test starts to guarantee real edges (hundreds–thousands of candidates), it simultaneously guarantees spectacular fake ones.** A pipeline that generates 1,000 candidates and reports the best backtest has, under the null, *manufactured* a Sharpe-3.26 strategy. The difference between a research operation and a Sharpe factory is entirely in §5.10.3.

*The correlation tax:* N in every formula is **effectively independent** trials. LLM generations cluster (same model, same prompt family, same training consensus) — 1,000 brainstormed ideas may be ~30–80 distinct bets after clustering by signal correlation / AST similarity (the operation must *measure* this on its own generations; López de Prado's clustering approach to effective trial count is the standard method **[index]**). Correlated generation inflates the *nominal* denominator (good for the researcher's vanity) while delivering the *effective* numerator of a much smaller search (bad for actually finding edges). Directed diversity (§5.10.5) is valuable precisely because it raises effective-N per dollar, not nominal-N.

#### 5.10.3 False-discovery control at scale: the non-negotiables

1. **The trial register is the asset.** Every generated candidate — including the ones killed by a screen, the ones an agent proposed and a peer mocked, the parameter tweak tried at 2 a.m. — is logged with timestamp, generator, and parentage. Without the denominator, *no* downstream statistic (DSR, PBO, FDR) is even computable; a Sharpe reported without its trial count is, in Bailey & López de Prado's framing, not a result. The register is also (§5, Rank 2) proprietary data.
2. **Deflated Sharpe Ratio (Bailey & López de Prado, *J. Portfolio Management* 2014)** for every promoted candidate: DSR adjusts the observed Sharpe for trial count N, cross-trial Sharpe variance, sample length, skew and kurtosis, and returns a *probability* the true Sharpe exceeds zero. Their worked example is the cautionary tale: an annualized Sharpe of **2.5** on 1,250 observations passes the 95% bar if found after ≤46 independent trials and is **rejected** at realistic (larger) trial counts. Promotion rule: DSR ≥ 0.95 *at the register's true N* — not at the N of the final screen.
3. **Harvey-Liu-Zhu hurdle as a sanity floor:** with hundreds of trials in the family, treat t < 3 as noise by default (HLZ, *RFS* 2016: 316 published factors ⇒ new-factor hurdle t > 3.0; full Bonferroni at that family size is t ≈ 3.78) **[index]**. For a 1,000-candidate family, Bonferroni at 5% demands per-test p < 5×10⁻⁵ (t ≈ 4.1) **[arithmetic]** — which is why pure per-candidate significance is the wrong frame at this scale, and cohort-level FDR is the right one:
4. **Control the false discovery *rate*, not just per-test error (Benjamini-Hochberg / BHY):** the operation can tolerate a *known fraction* of false promotions if the true ones pay for them — that is a portfolio decision, and FDR language makes it explicit ("we promote 40 sleeves expecting ~25% to be false, sized accordingly") instead of pretending each promotion is certain. HLZ's own calibration (FDR@1% ⇒ t ≈ 3.0) shows the two frames connect.
5. **PBO (Probability of Backtest Overfitting; Bailey et al., *J. Computational Finance* 2017)** as an audit of the *selection process*, via combinatorially symmetric cross-validation over the full trial matrix (all candidates' return series, losers included — "keep the graveyard"): PBO ≥ 0.5 means your winner-picking is a coin flip; demand well under 0.2 before trusting a screen (https://www.crucible-research.com/probability-of-backtest-overfitting **[index]**). PBO is computable for the *rule-based sleeve* screens (whose backtests are not LLM-contaminated); it does not launder LLM-verbal backtests, which remain unusable (§2.3).
6. **Family-wise arbitration:** verdicts are issued per *family* (a generation cohort with a shared register segment) by a single pre-registered rule — e.g., "the cohort passes if its aggregate forward excess is positive net of costs AND its top-decile members beat the DSR bar AND PBO of its screen < 0.2" — not by post-hoc per-sleeve storytelling. This is the operation's analog of Man Group routing AlphaGPT output through the *existing* investment committee with *existing* thresholds (§4): the gate predates the candidates.
7. **Peeking discipline:** forward dashboards invite the classic inflation — checking a cohort weekly and promoting "when it's significant" turns a 5% error rate into ~14% after 5 looks and ~25%+ after 20 **[index — sequential-testing literature, e.g. https://www.metricuno.com/sequential-testing]**. Either verdicts happen only at pre-registered horizons, or monitoring uses alpha-spending (O'Brien-Fleming) / always-valid p-values (Johari et al.'s mSPRT). A small operation's realistic choice: fixed verdict dates, dashboards that show Brier/excess *without* significance stars.

#### 5.10.4 The cost per test in a forward regime — where the funnel earns its keep

Forward testing's currency is **calendar time × independent bets**, and the two don't trade off freely:

- **Power arithmetic [arithmetic, standard formulas]:** distinguishing a 55% hit rate from 50% at 80% power / 5% significance needs **~785 independent resolved bets**; 60% vs 50% needs ~195. Distinguishing a single sleeve's Sharpe 0.5 from 0 needs **~31 years** of its returns. Detecting an IC of 0.10 (Morillo's intermediate-outcome level) needs ~**780** independent observations for the same power — but those observations arrive *per event across the whole fleet*, not per year. Conclusion: **no individual sleeve or seat can be verdicted on its own P&L in 6–12 months; the fleet, the cohort, and the intermediate-outcome scoreboard can.** Any playbook promising per-thesis verdicts on quarterly horizons is innumerate.
- **Parallelism helps breadth, not regime count:** 340 sleeves forward-test *simultaneously*, so a 12-week window yields 340 sleeve-windows — but they share **one** macro draw. Correlated sleeves in one regime are closer to one observation than to 340 for regime-level claims. Effective sample = (independent bets) discounted by cross-correlation, exactly as in §5.10.2. This is why the options book's grading (% return vs SPY and QQQ) is necessary but not sufficient: benchmark-relative grading removes market beta from each bet, which *raises* effective independence — good design, keep it, and extend it with per-event implied-move baselines (Rank 4).
- **The funnel, with illustrative unit economics [arithmetic on stated assumptions]:** suppose generation produces 2,000 candidates/quarter at ~$0 marginal cost (LLM calls + codegen). Stage A screen (rule-based backtest with costs, PBO/DSR gates — valid for rule sleeves, *not* for LLM-verbal signals) kills 95% → 100 survivors. Stage B (paper-forward micro-cohort, 4–6 weeks, tiny size, intermediate-outcome scoring) kills 80% → 20. Stage C (full forward cohort, 1–2 quarters, cohort-level verdict per §5.10.3.6) promotes an expected 20 × (true fraction among survivors). If screens are *informative* (each stage multiplies the base rate ~5–10× — an assumption the operation must validate by tracking stage-wise pass rates), Stage C's pool has π ≈ 10–30%, i.e., 2–6 real edges per quarter's cohort — a plausible metabolism. If screens are *uninformative*, Stage C's π stays ~1% and the same spend yields ~0.2 real edges — indistinguishable from failure within a year. **Therefore the screens themselves are hypotheses, graded on whether their pass cohorts outperform their reject cohorts forward.** An operation that doesn't grade its graders is doing astrology with extra compute.
- **What a forward test really costs:** not dollars (paper trading) but *option value of the calendar*: every cohort occupying the forward window delays the next. Hence kill criteria must be cheap and early (mechanism falsifiers — "this sleeve's edge requires X to hold; X is checkable this week from public data" — before outcome falsifiers), and the near-miss log (Rank 2) must capture *why* each candidate died, because that log is what raises next quarter's π.

#### 5.10.5 Why directed diversity beats uniform random — and beats fresh brainstorming

Uniform random generation over strategy space wastes nearly all draws on incoherent or untradeable combinations (no mechanism, impossible constraints, guaranteed-cost structures): its π is the 0.01% row of the §5.10.2 table, needing ~30,000 independent tests per expected edge — infeasible in forward time at any funnel design. Directed operators change π per draw:

- **Mutation of proven sleeves** (parameter diffs, logic diffs via LLM in the ELM pattern): offspring of a real edge inherit its mechanism; local search around a true Sharpe-0.3 sleeve finds its better neighbors at rates orders of magnitude above global random. Risk: hill-climbing into regime-overfit — controlled by Rank 6's niches and forward-only promotion.
- **Crossover** (LMX-style recombination of two sleeves' signals/conditions): creates candidates in the *convex hull* of things that individually survived screens — new combinations, old parts. This is also how genuinely uncorrelated return streams get built.
- **Inversion** (systematically fade a signal): every strong consensus signal (Rank 8's series) and every strong *fleet* signal defines an inversion candidate. Inversions are the cheapest source of true difference-from-consensus: they are anti-correlated with the crowd *by construction*, and the Lopez-Lira & Tang asymmetry (short-side Sharpe 2.01 vs long 0.78) is a reminder that the fade side of news flow has historically carried the better risk-adjusted drift.
- **Regime/conditioning transforms** (same signal, gated on vol state / event type / seat-disagreement level from Rank 3): converts a mediocre unconditional sleeve into a niche elite — exactly what MAP-Elites archives are for.
- **Tail sampling via verbalized distributions** (Rank 9): raises the *novelty* of LLM generations, but §3.7.1 caps expectations — use it to seed Stage A, never to skip it.
- **Novelty bonus in selection** (AlphaAgent's AST-originality; QD's niche elites): explicitly pay for *distance from everything already in the fleet and the public zoo*, because distance is where un-crowded edges can still exist — with the §3.7.2 warning tattooed on it: distance is also where pure noise lives, so novelty earns a candidate a *test*, never a promotion.

The synthesis: **generation is a prior, selection is the science, and the trial register is the memory that keeps the science honest.** "Random ideas + testing" is the correct *shape* of the answer; the operation's edge is that its prior improves quarterly (ledger-conditioned generation, graded screens) while a naive generate-and-test shop's prior is stationary — and stationarity, in a decaying-alpha world (§4, McLean-Pontiff), is death.

## 6. Honest limits: what Morillo gets to keep

1. **No information from nowhere.** Every circumvention above works by adding one of three things Morillo's setup lacks: *new inputs* (post-cutoff events, the private ledger), *new selection* (forward outcomes, evaluators), or *new objectives* (residual-vs-consensus rewards). None makes a prompted verdict on a training-covered question non-consensus. If the operation ever finds itself asking a model "what's different about company X?" and trading the paragraph, Morillo wins that round by default.
2. **The latency wall.** The best-documented LLM text edge (Lopez-Lira & Tang) lives in the initial reaction + short drift, stronger in small caps and on the short side — precisely where a small paper-trading operation faces the worst fills, borrow, and options spreads. Paper fills will *flatter* the options book relative to any future live version; %-vs-SPY/QQQ grading must therefore be spread- and slippage-modeled, or the forward verdict itself is biased — the operation's version of look-ahead.
3. **Statistical power is the binding constraint, not imagination.** §5.10.4: per-thesis verdicts in 6–12 months are arithmetically unavailable; only fleet/cohort/intermediate-outcome verdicts are. An operation that drifts into a handful of cherished discretionary theses has exited the regime where its own method can tell it the truth (the HUNTER prior-art review in §1's sources reached the same conclusion independently: at achievable sample sizes, a single-name thesis pipeline cannot produce statistically meaningful alpha evidence).
4. **Decay and crowding don't care about your architecture.** Published anomalies lost 58% post-publication *before* LLMs; the Bank of England is simulating your fleet's failure mode at system level. Any edge the fleet finds in *public-data + public-model* space has a half-life, and the fleet's own success at scale would shorten it. The durable assets are the ones that don't decay the same way: the ledger, the register, the graded screens, the calibration archive — process capital, not signals.
5. **Model non-stationarity.** Seats are rented cognition: providers retrain, deprecate, and silently update. A seat's measured excess has a *vintage*; the evidence program must re-baseline on version changes (Rank 8) and treat cross-version continuity as a hypothesis, not a default.
6. **RLVR's sharpening cuts both ways** (§3.5): outcome-tuning seats improves their average call while narrowing the diversity Ranks 3–4 monetize. Tune *some* seats; keep others frozen and weird, on purpose, as the diversity reserve.
7. **What would prove it's working (6–12 month evidence program, pre-register all of it):**
   - **Cohort verdict:** ≥2 consecutive quarterly forward cohorts with aggregate excess vs SPY/QQQ (options book) and vs sleeve benchmarks (fleet) positive *net of modeled costs*, with DSR ≥ 0.95 computed at the register's true trial counts.
   - **Divergence monotonicity:** Rank-4 residual buckets monotone in forward outcome (top divergence bucket > median > consensus-matching bucket ≈ 0). This is the single most Morillo-specific test: it directly measures whether *difference from consensus*, as produced by this system, is where the money is.
   - **Calibration skill:** seats' Brier *skill vs consensus baseline* > 0 with confidence intervals excluding 0 on ≥ ~800 resolved intermediate-outcome forecasts (Morillo's IC doctrine — surprises/event outcomes, not returns).
   - **Screen validity:** Stage-A/B pass cohorts outperform reject cohorts forward (the graders graded).
   - **Disagreement validation:** the Rank-3 disagreement statistic predicts *something* pre-registered (event-move magnitude or minority-seat excess) — or is honestly demoted per "Same Text, Different Numbers."
   - **Failure signatures that should trigger program-level falsification:** pass rates ≈ base-rate-of-noise at every stage; best-sleeve Sharpes ≈ False-Strategy-Theorem null expectations for the register's N; divergence buckets flat; ledger-conditioning A/B ≈ 0. If, after ~4 cohorts, the numbers look like the null's numbers, the correct conclusion is that Morillo was right *about this operation too* — and the pre-registration is what makes that conclusion reachable instead of deniable.
