# Chart Reading Mastery & Annotation Craft — Deep Research Report

## Summary

Two encode-ready rule sets follow. **Confidence basis:** Part A pattern statistics are *verified on Bulkowski's own site* (thepatternsite.com, read 2026-10-15) — but note his framing throughout: bull-market samples, "perfect trades" (idealised entries), per-pattern sample sizes in the hundreds–thousands, and statistics last updated at different dates per pattern. They are the best large-sample practitioner statistics available, not peer-reviewed out-of-sample proof. The academic literature is colder: Lo, Mamaysky & Wang (2000) found several patterns carry *incremental information* (not demonstrated profit), and after data-snooping correction (Sullivan, Timmermann & White / White's Reality Check) simple technical rules lost significance for major US indices after the mid-1980s. Encode patterns as **probabilistic context with hard confirmation gates**, never as signals. Part B rules rest on firmer ground where they come from standards (WCAG), experiments (Cleveland & McGill), and platform documentation (TradingView Pine limits, StockCharts/TradingView tool docs); Tufte is a design philosophy, not an experimental result, and is flagged as such.

**How to read the stats:** "Break-even failure rate" = Bulkowski's reported failure metric on each pattern page (see his glossary). "Target met" = % reaching the full measured-move target — hence the rule everywhere below: *scale the measured move by the target-met %, do not project the full height.*

Rules:

- **A1.1 Rank patterns before you draw them.** Prioritise cup-with-handle, rounding bottoms, double bottoms and head-and-shoulders; de-prioritise symmetrical triangles, wedges and diamond tops, which sit at/near the bottom of Bulkowski's performance and failure ranks. — Bulkowski rank tables, https://thepatternsite.com/id75.html and https://thepatternsite.com/id84.html
- **A1.2 Never label a pattern "confirmed" before its confirmation close.** H&S top: a *close* below the neckline (up-sloping) or below the right armpit (down-sloping neckline). Double bottom: a *close* above the peak between the two valleys — Bulkowski's data say that without it, roughly 48% of apparent double bottoms simply continue lower. Cup-with-handle: a close above the right cup rim (or above a down-sloping handle trendline for the early entry). — Bulkowski, pages above.
- **A1.3 Enforce shape tolerances, don't eyeball.** Double bottom: intervening peak ≥ ~10% above the valleys; the two valleys within ~0–4% of each other; valleys typically 2–7 weeks apart (median ~23 days for Eve & Adam). H&S: shoulders near the same price, near-equidistant from the head, similar width — reject lopsided formations. Cup: U-shaped, not V-shaped; 7–65 weeks; handle ≥1 week and confined to the *upper half* of the cup; rims near the same price. — Bulkowski identification guidelines, pages above.
- **A1.4 Require the triangle touch/crossing test.** A valid triangle needs ≥3 touches on one boundary and ≥2 on the other, with price repeatedly *crossing* the interior (filling it, not leaving white space). Wedges need ≥5 touches total (3+2) and ≥3 weeks' duration — shorter than that, classify it as a pennant, not a wedge. — Bulkowski, http://thepatternsite.com/st.html ; rising-wedge guidelines via thepatternsite extraction (see Sources).
- **A1.5 Treat volume as a tendency check, not a hard gate — except at breakout.** Inside patterns, volume *usually* declines (symmetrical triangles 84–86% of the time; wedges ~79%; H&S volume trends down only 61% of the time, highest on left shoulder then head) — so do not invalidate an otherwise clean pattern for imperfect internal volume. Do upgrade confidence when breakout volume is heavy vs. its 30-day average, which Bulkowski associates with better post-breakout performance. — Bulkowski, http://thepatternsite.com/st.html , http://thepatternsite.com/hst.html
- **A1.6 Scale every price target.** Compute target = breakout price ± (pattern height × target-met %). E.g. H&S top ≈ 0.51 × height; symmetrical triangle ≈ 0.58 (up) / 0.36 (down); double bottoms ≈ 0.65–0.72. Never annotate the unscaled measured move as "the target." — Bulkowski measure rules, pages above.
- **A1.7 Expect the retest; don't call it failure.** Throwbacks/pullbacks to the breakout level occur ~62–68% of the time across cup-with-handle, H&S, double bottoms and triangles, and Bulkowski notes they *hurt* ultimate performance — so annotate a "retest zone" at the breakout level by default, and judge the breakout only after the retest resolves. — Bulkowski, pages above.
- **A1.8 Time-box triangles.** Symmetrical-triangle breakouts occur on average ~74% of the way to the apex and upward 60% of the time; a pattern that drifts into its apex unbroken has lost its premise — retire it. Ascending triangles break ~64% of the way to the apex. — Bulkowski, http://thepatternsite.com/st.html
- **A1.9 For flags, demand the pole and use the half-height rule.** No steep flagpole = no flag, just noise. For high-tight flags, Bulkowski's newer guidance is to confirm on a close above the *highest high of the whole pattern including the pole*, not on a flag-trendline break (which fails too often), and to target half the pole height added to the flag low (met ~82% of the time). Prefer tight flags; skip loose, jagged ones whose price pokes outside the boundaries. — Bulkowski high-tight flag page via extraction, https://github.com/traderspost/pinescript-agents/blob/HEAD/thepatternsite/chart-patterns/high-and-tight-flags.md
- **A1.10 Context-filter every pattern.** Bulkowski repeatedly finds patterns perform best near the start of a trend, worse late in it, and that position in the yearly range matters (varies by pattern). Encode: pattern + inbound-trend age + yearly-range position as features of the read, not decoration. — Bulkowski trading tips, pages above.
- **A1.11 Keep the academic ceiling on all claims.** In written reads, phrase pattern conclusions as probabilities with the failure rate attached (e.g. "cup-with-handle, 5% break-even failure in Bulkowski's bull-market sample"), never as predictions. Lo, Mamaysky & Wang's kernel-regression study (US stocks, 1962–1996) supports only "incremental information" for several patterns (head-and-shoulders, double tops/bottoms, triangles, rectangles among them). — Lo, Mamaysky & Wang, *Foundations of Technical Analysis*, J. Finance 2000, https://stuff.mit.edu/people/wangj/pap/LoMamayskyWang00.pdf

### A2. Trendline drawing discipline

- **A2.1 Two points hypothesise, three validate.** Draw a provisional line on two reaction lows (uptrend) / highs (downtrend); promote it to "operative" only on a third touch. More touches = stronger level. If the pivots don't line up, *do not force a line* — StockCharts is explicit that not every chart has a valid trendline. — StockCharts ChartSchool, *Trend Lines*, http://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/chart-analysis/trend-lines
- **A2.2 Use well-spaced, comparable pivots.** Reject lines whose anchor pivots are jammed together or absurdly far apart; prefer relatively evenly spaced reaction points. — StockCharts, *Trend Lines* (spacing rules), same URL.
- **A2.3 Discount steep lines.** The steeper the line, the weaker it is as support/resistance, even with three touches; a near-vertical line is a momentum artefact, not a level. Also remember apparent angle depends on chart aspect/scale — judge steepness on a fixed house scale. — StockCharts, *Trend Lines* (trend angles), same URL.
- **A2.4 Permit internal trendlines — labelled as such.** When spikes/overreactions distort the extremes, draw the line through price *clusters*, ignoring one or two spike wicks "to a reasonable degree," and mark it internal so readers know wicks may pierce it. This is the sanctioned answer to "wicks vs. closes": default to extremes, fall back to clusters/internal when extremes produce a nonsense angle or a false break, and disclose which you used. — StockCharts, *Trend Lines* (internal trend lines), same URL.
- **A2.5 Switch to semi-log above large ranges / long horizons.** On arithmetic scale a $10→$20 move and a $100→$110 move occupy the same space; StockCharts' AMZN example shows arithmetic drawing producing repeated *false* downtrend-line breaks in 2000–01 that never occur on semi-log, and EMC needing three arithmetic lines where one semi-log line fit. Rule of thumb to encode: if the chart's price range spans ≳2× (or multi-year), draw and store trendlines on log scale and say so on the chart. — StockCharts, *Trend Lines* (scale settings), same URL; cf. Investopedia, *Logarithmic vs. Linear Price Scales*, https://www.investopedia.com/ask/answers/05/logvslinear.asp
- **A2.6 A trendline break is a warning, not a reversal.** Require corroboration before calling a trend change: e.g. the prior reaction low/high also breaks, or peak-and-trough structure flips. StockCharts' example: an uptrend line broke while the prior reaction low held and price had just made a new high — the "break" reversed nothing. — StockCharts, *Trend Lines* (bottom line), same URL.
- **A2.7 Apply a penetration filter for pattern/neckline breaks (Edwards & Magee tradition).** The classical confirmation standard in *Technical Analysis of Stock Trends* is a *close* through the level by roughly 3% of price, ideally with a conspicuous expansion of activity for bottom-pattern breakouts; the same tradition warns the filter is asymmetric — downside breaks through a top's neckline are trusted even on light volume, upside breakouts from bottoms are *not* trusted without volume. Encode 3% as the default "decisive" band for daily charts, tunable by volatility (ATR-based equivalent is a reasonable modernisation — practitioner inference, not Edwards & Magee). — Edwards & Magee, *Technical Analysis of Stock Trends*, hosted copy https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/robert-dedwards-john-magee-technical-analysis-of-stock-trendspdf/262666970
- **A2.8 Watch for "hugging."** Repeated bumping/crawling along a line raises break probability (Donchian guideline in ChartSchool): if price stalls against a trendline instead of bouncing cleanly, downgrade the line and pre-write the break scenario. — StockCharts ChartSchool, *Donchian Trading Guidelines*, https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/overview/donchian-trading-guidelines

### A3. RSI divergence

Definitions to encode exactly (practitioner consensus, Wilder/Brown tradition via ChartSchool and standard references):

- **Regular bullish:** price lower low, RSI higher low (reversal candidate). **Regular bearish:** price higher high, RSI lower high.
- **Hidden bullish:** price higher low, RSI lower low (trend-continuation). **Hidden bearish:** price lower high, RSI higher high.

Rules:

- **A3.1 Compare like pivots only.** Build divergence only from *confirmed* swing pivots in both price and RSI (same pivot definition both series), spanning a meaningful interval — Wilder-tradition commentary notes real divergence typically develops over many periods (up to ~20+), not two adjacent bars. Discard micro-divergences between minor wiggles. — Wilder tradition via Barchart/Wilder summary, https://www.barchart.com/media/education/pdf/This%20Secret%20RSI%20Screener%20Spots%20Reversal%20Trades%20Early.pdf (practitioner source — flagged)
- **A3.2 Never act on divergence alone; require a trigger.** Accepted confirmations: (i) RSI *failure swing* completes — bullish: RSI dips above its prior low then breaks its intervening high (bearish mirror); (ii) RSI breaks its own divergence trendline; (iii) price breaks its divergence trendline / prior swing. Until one fires, label divergence "developing / unconfirmed" in the annotation. — StockCharts ChartSchool, *Relative Strength Index (RSI)* (failure swings, Wilder), https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/technical-indicators-and-overlays/technical-indicators/relative-strength-index-rsi?ref=thetanerd.com
- **A3.3 Gate divergence by location.** Practitioner evidence and StockCharts practice agree divergence is materially more credible at a tested horizontal level: require confluence with a prior support/resistance zone, a pattern boundary, or a major moving average before upgrading it in the written read; divergence mid-range, or against a strong trend into uncharted highs/lows, is where false signals cluster. — Practitioner consensus (flagged as such): TradingView education examples, https://www.tradingview.com/chart/XAUUSD/4JRWQvHl-RSI-Indicator-LIES-Untold-Truth-About-Relative-Strength-Index/ ; supported by ChartSchool's level-based RSI usage, RSI page above.
- **A3.4 Read RSI by regime, not by 30/70 alone (Brown).** In bull regimes RSI typically lives ~40–90 and the 40–50 zone acts as support; in bear regimes ~10–60 with 50–60 as resistance. So: in an uptrend, treat RSI dipping to 40–50 and turning as continuation evidence, and do *not* annotate "overbought ⇒ short" at 70+ inside a strong uptrend — RSI can stay extreme while price trends. — Constance Brown, *Technical Analysis for the Trading Professional*, as presented in StockCharts ChartSchool RSI page, same URL as A3.2.
- **A3.5 Prefer hidden divergence for trend trades, regular for range extremes.** Regular divergence is a *reversal warning* with no timing information; hidden divergence aligns with the prevailing trend and is the only kind that should ever support a with-trend entry in your reads. State which kind, every time — retail charts routinely conflate them. — Standard divergence taxonomy, practitioner sources above.
- **A3.6 Disclose the weak standalone record.** No robust peer-reviewed study was found validating RSI divergence as a standalone profitable rule; secondary quantitative write-ups consistently find standalone RSI/divergence underperforms buy-and-hold long-run and degrades badly on noisy intraday timeframes. Encode divergence as a *confluence input with a confirmation gate* (A3.2–A3.3), and keep it off 1-minute-style noise entirely. — Summary of backtest literature via AInvest review, https://www.ainvest.com/news/rsi-divergences-predictive-framework-trend-reversals-continuations-2601/ (secondary — flagged)

### A4. Support/resistance, confluence, Fibonacci, moving averages, volume

- **A4.1 Draw zones, not hairlines — sized by the evidence.** StockCharts: because brief dips through a level don't logically break it, traders establish *zones*; use exact levels only when the trading range is tight and short (< ~2 months), zones when the range is long/wide. Encode zone width from the cluster of wick/body extremes at that level (e.g. min–max of the touching pivots' relevant extremes), not a fixed ±%. — StockCharts ChartSchool, *Support and Resistance*, https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/chart-analysis/support-and-resistance
- **A4.2 Build levels from, in order:** prior reaction highs/lows; prior consolidation shelves (trading-range edges — a range that held for months becomes the level); polarity flips (broken support ⇒ resistance and vice versa — annotate the flip explicitly, it's a first-class event); round numbers only as minor reinforcement. — StockCharts, *Support and Resistance* (highs/lows, support = resistance, trading ranges), same URL.
- **A4.3 Score confluence; annotate only what scores.** A level earns ink when ≥2 independent sources coincide within the zone: e.g. prior shelf + 50/200-day MA + Fibonacci level + volume-by-price node. Volume-by-Price: long bars above price = supply overhang/resistance, below = support; a break through a long bar is itself a strength/weakness signal. — StockCharts ChartSchool, *Volume-by-Price*, https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/technical-indicators-and-overlays/technical-overlays/volume-by-price ; confluence-as-scoring is house synthesis of ChartSchool methods (flagged).
- **A4.4 Use moving averages as dynamic zones with a hierarchy.** 20-day ≈ short-term, 50-day intermediate, 200-day primary trend. In ChartSchool's MA-as-support method, repeated bounces off the 50-day validate it as support; a *close* below the 50-day ends the short/intermediate uptrend claim, and the 200-day then becomes the line that decides whether the long trend survives. Golden cross = 50-day crossing above 200-day (context, not a buy signal by itself). Do not annotate every MA touch — only MAs price has actually respected (≥2 reactions) or that coincide with a horizontal zone (A4.3). — StockCharts ChartSchool, *Finding Support and Resistance in Moving Averages*, https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/trading-strategies-and-models/trading-strategies/moving-average-trading-strategies/finding-support-and-resistance-in-moving-averages ; *Trading Using the Golden Cross*, https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/trading-strategies-and-models/trading-strategies/moving-average-trading-strategies/trading-using-the-golden-cross
- **A4.5 Fibonacci: anchor once, display few, claim little.** Anchor the grid to the single most obvious completed swing (the one a second analyst would pick); if two defensible anchors give different grids, show neither or show the *confluence zone* where they overlap. Display at most 38.2 / 50 / 61.8 (78.6 optional); remember 50% is a Dow-theory convention, not a Fibonacci ratio. Treat a Fib level as valid annotation *only* when it coincides with a horizontal zone/MA/volume node (A4.3). Rationale: the best direct test found Fib zones did **not** bounce price more than equivalent non-Fibonacci zones (see C3). — Levels/anchoring practice: practitioner consensus; evidence: Tsinaslanidis, Guijarro & Voukelatos (2022), as summarised at https://cryptoaimeta.com/investments/fibonacci-retracement/ (secondary summary of the primary study — flagged)
- **A4.6 Read volume at levels with two templates.** (i) *Climax:* after an extended move, a volume surge with a wide range and a close well off the extreme = selling/buying climax — candidate end of move, not continuation. (ii) *Dry-up:* inside bases and on tests/pullbacks, shrinking volume = supply drying up — the healthy base signature (see A5). Trend-direction moves should carry the volume; counter-trend moves should not — and a climax is the exception that ends trends. — StockCharts, *Donchian Trading Guidelines* (volume climax), URL in A2.8; Wyckoff tutorial (A5).

### A5. Wyckoff essentials for a daily chart (StockCharts ChartSchool tutorial)

Source for all A5 rules: StockCharts ChartSchool, *The Wyckoff Method: A Tutorial*, https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/market-analysis/wyckoff-analysis-articles/the-wyckoff-method-a-tutorial.md (paraphrased).

- **A5.1 Label the cycle before the range.** Every base read must state: markdown → accumulation → markup (or the distribution mirror). No accumulation claim without a preceding prolonged down-move.
- **A5.2 Identify accumulation events in sequence:** PS (preliminary support: volume and spread expand as the decline first stalls) → SC (selling climax: climactic wide-spread selling absorbed, close well off the low — this sets the range bottom) → AR (automatic rally — its high sets the range top) → ST (secondary test(s) of the SC area, which must show *significantly diminished* volume and spread if the bottom is real).
- **A5.3 Phases as a checklist:** A = PS/SC/AR/ST (decline stopped, range defined); B = range-building / cause-building (duration matters — cause ∝ eventual effect); C = spring/shakeout *or its absence* (Schematic 2 has no spring — never require one); D = SOS + LPS (see A5.5); E = markup out of the range.
- **A5.4 Grade springs strictly.** A spring = a brief move below range support that *closes back inside* the range. It is only validated by its test: a retest making a higher low on *lesser* volume. A break below support that stays below, or a test on heavy volume, is not a spring — it's a breakdown / "supply still present, not ready for markup."
- **A5.5 Require SOS → LPS for the markup call.** Sign of strength = advance on widening spread and relatively higher volume, often right after a spring/test. Last point of support = the subsequent low-volume pullback holding above/at the old range top (the "back-up" to the creek). Annotate the pair together; SOS without a successful LPS is unconfirmed.
- **A5.6 Apply effort-vs-result on every climax and stall.** High volume (effort) with narrow range / no new high after a long advance (no result) = large interests unloading — early warning of change. Conversely wide result on modest effort early in a range = supply exhausted (AR logic). This single law is also your divergence cross-check for A3.
- **A5.7 Markdown mirror:** after distribution, expect markdown to be faster than the preceding markup; in markdown, annotate failed rallies to old support-turned-resistance and expanding volume on down-legs vs. shrinking volume on rallies as the "trend intact" evidence — and a selling climax/spring at the end as the first (only the first) sign it may be ending.

## B. Annotation / visual rules we can encode

### B1. Professional platforms

- **B1.1 Put the price in the right margin, the words on leader lines.** TradingView's canonical annotation set separates the jobs: *Price Label / Price Note* pins the exact price (label sits on the price axis side, point marks the spot), *Signpost / Callout / Balloon* carries prose at an offset connected to its anchor point. Encode exactly that split: every level gets a right-margin price tag; every explanation gets an offset callout with a leader — never prose sitting on top of candles. — TradingView, *Meet the new Highlighter and Signpost tools*, https://www.tradingview.com/chart/USDJPY/q3YyU43S-Meet-the-new-Highlighter-and-Signpost-drawing-tools/ ; *Price Label* support doc, https://in.tradingview.com/support/solutions/43000518083-price-label/
- **B1.2 Budget drawing objects like a platform does.** TradingView Pine enforces it mechanically: drawing objects default to ~50 per script, hard max 500 lines / 500 boxes / 500 labels / 100 polylines, and when the cap is hit the *oldest are silently deleted*. Adopt a stricter editorial cap for a static annotated chart (house rule, informed by that ceiling): ≤ ~12–15 visible text labels on the main pane, ≤ 5 on any sub-pane; when the budget is exceeded, aggregate or drop lowest-ranked items first (platform logic: newest/most-relevant survives). — TradingView Pine documentation (limits), via mirror https://github.com/mrojas-mx/pine-script-v6-kb/blob/HEAD/examples/writing/limitations.md
- **B1.3 Aggregate near-identical levels into one zone label.** If two levels fall within a small band (encode: within ~0.5× ATR or overlapping zones per A4.1), merge them into a single shaded zone with one label ("$182–185 resistance: Mar high + 200-day") rather than two lines + two labels. This is both the StockCharts zones doctrine (A4.1) and standard S/R-tool behaviour (auto-merging levels within an ATR band to prevent clutter). — StockCharts zones page (A4.1); merging behaviour documented in TradingView S/R tooling, https://www.tradingview.com/scripts/supportandresistance/page-12/?script_access=open&script_type=indicators (practitioner tool — flagged)
- **B1.4 Keep a fixed visual grammar across every chart.** Same geometry = same meaning, always: horizontal band = zone, dashed = unconfirmed/developing, solid = confirmed, arrow markers reserved for events (breakout, spring, climax), text only in callouts. Bloomberg Terminal's public materials do not publish an annotation style guide (see Could-not-verify) — its de-facto lesson, echoed in UX literature on professional density, is *consistency under density*, not minimalism for its own sake. — TradingView annotation tool taxonomy, B1.1 sources; consistency principle: Shneiderman's golden rules (secondary summary in Sources).

### B2. Data-visualisation research

- **B2.1 Maximise data-ink; delete before you add (Tufte — philosophy, not experiment).** Remove/shrink: heavy gridlines (keep faint horizontal only), chart borders, background gradients, 3D, redundant legends (label directly instead), duplicate encodings of the same number. Every surviving non-price element must answer "what decision does this ink support?" Note honestly: controlled studies (Bateman et al. 2010, via secondary summaries) found tasteful embellishment did *not* hurt comprehension and sometimes aided recall — so enforce Tufte as a *clutter* rule (competing ink), not as a ban on all non-data ink. — Tufte, *The Visual Display of Quantitative Information* (1983), principles summarised at https://github.com/dmarticus/dotfiles/blob/HEAD/ai/skills/tufte-viz/references/tufte-principles.md (secondary summary — flagged)
- **B2.2 Encode key quantities by position on a common scale — that means the right axis.** Cleveland & McGill's experiments rank perceptual accuracy: position on a common scale > position on unaligned scales > length > angle/slope > area > volume > colour. Practical encodings: levels are read off the shared price axis (never encode a level's importance by label *size* or zone *area* alone); slope judgements (trendlines) are intrinsically mid-accuracy — always pair a trendline with its price labels at touch points. — Cleveland & McGill (1984, JASA), ranking summarised at https://github.com/snds/workspace/blob/HEAD/03-skills/infod-encoding-theory/SKILL.md (secondary summary — flagged)
- **B2.3 Run a real collision pass; "nudge or drop," never overlap.** Legible-label research defines the requirement plainly: labels must not overlap other labels *or* data marks. The state-of-the-art Vega-Lite approach rasterises marks into an occupancy bitmap and places labels only in free space. Encode a priority-ordered greedy pass: sort labels by importance, place each in the nearest free candidate position (above/below/left/right of anchor, then leader-line offset), and if no free slot exists within a bounded nudge distance, *drop or aggregate the label* — an overlapped label is worse than a missing one. Force-directed placement is the classic alternative but is documented as computationally expensive relative to the rest of rendering; for static renders, bitmap/greedy is sufficient. — Wang et al., *Legible Label Layout for Data Visualization* (arXiv:2405.10953), https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.10953v1 ; greedy "render-or-nudge" practitioner account, https://towardsdatascience.com/minimizing-overlapping-labels-in-interactive-visualizations-b0eabd62ef0/
- **B2.4 Meet WCAG contrast on dark themes, numerically.** Normal annotation text ≥ 4.5:1 against its background, large text and essential graphical objects ≥ 3:1 (WCAG AA). On dark chart themes this means *lightening* label text and de-saturating candle colours that fail against the background — test the rendered chart, not the palette in isolation. — WCAG AA summary, Ulster University accessibility standards, https://www.ulster.ac.uk/accessibility-standards/colour-accessibility
- **B2.5 Never let colour carry meaning alone; fix the red/green problem.** ~8% of men have red-green colour-vision deficiency — precisely finance's up/down pair. Rules: (i) keep red/green candles if house style demands, but make every *annotation* meaning redundant — buy zones also labelled "BUY ZONE," divergence also drawn with lines + "−DIV" text, support/resistance distinguished by position + label, not hue alone; (ii) for categorical annotation colours use the Okabe–Ito palette (orange #E69F00, sky blue #56B4E9, bluish green #009E73, yellow #F0E442, blue #0072B2, vermillion #D55E00, reddish purple #CC79A7, black #000000) — designed to stay distinguishable across CVD types; (iii) verify with a CVD simulator / greyscale pass before shipping a template. — WCAG "not colour alone," same URL as B2.4; Okabe & Ito (2008) / Wong (2011) palette via https://github.com/dgilford/ai-science-toolkit/blob/HEAD/skills/figure-review/COLORBLIND.md (secondary compilation — flagged)
- **B2.6 Typography floor for annotations.** House rule synthesising WCAG + platform practice (flagged as synthesis, no single source): annotation text ≥ 11–12px equivalent at final render size, one sans-serif family, two weights max (regular + semibold for the headline call), sentence case (faster to read than all-caps for multi-word labels), and a solid or ≥85%-opacity halo/background behind any text that must sit over price action so contrast (B2.4) is computed against a known surface.

### B3. Retail-investor UX, mobile, progressive disclosure

- **B3.1 Summary first, detail on demand — in two levels, not five.** Nielsen's progressive-disclosure research: show the core first, defer advanced/rare material to *one* secondary level; designs work best with about two levels, more disorients. Chart implementation: Level 1 (default render) = trend verdict + 1 pattern + nearest support/resistance zones + buy zone. Level 2 (tap/expand) = RSI panel detail, Fib grid, secondary patterns, full evidence list. Shneiderman's mantra is the same rule for data: overview first, zoom and filter, then details on demand. — Nielsen (NN/g, 2006), *Progressive Disclosure*, described at https://www.nngroup.com/articles/nielsen-norman-group-20-years/?lm=40-years&pt=youtubevideo ; Shneiderman mantra summary in Sources.
- **B3.2 On mobile, cut data before you shrink it.** Small-screen visualisation studies find: limiting the displayed range/series and labelling only min/max/key points preserves comprehension; segmenting ("cutting") a large chart into panels reduces task time and mental workload, while cramming the full desktop chart scaled-down destroys it. Encode mobile renders as a *different chart*, not a scaled one: fewer bars, ≤ ~5 annotations, no RSI sub-pane by default (divergence becomes one marker + one line of text), zones kept, Fib off. — Mobile chart usability studies (MDPI *Applied Sciences* 2025; MTU responsive-viz review), https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/15/19/10832 and https://www.csl.mtu.edu/classes/cs4760/www/projects/s19/grad4/www/docs/Topic_Paper.pdf
- **B3.3 Use small multiples / sparklines for comparison, on identical scales.** Tufte's small-multiples rule: same design, same scale, repeated — the reader compares data, not layouts. For a watchlist/summary view: one sparkline per name, identical time window and (within a panel) consistent scaling convention, with only the current price and one zone annotated. Never mix log and linear, or different windows, inside one multiples row. — Tufte, small multiples / sparklines, secondary summary in B2.1.
- **B3.4 Direct-label everything; no legends on a single-name chart.** A legend forces eye-shuttling between key and data; direct labels at the line/zone they name remove it (Tufte; also standard in the label-placement literature, B2.3). If an element can't be direct-labelled within the label budget (B1.2), it probably shouldn't be on the chart. — Tufte via B2.1; Vega-Lite label paper, B2.3.

## C. Evidence-backed findings that contradict common retail-charting habits — do NOT do these

1. **Do NOT feature the symmetrical triangle as a premium setup.** It's retail's favourite pattern and Bulkowski's near-worst: performance rank 36/39 (up) and 34/36 (down), break-even failure 25%/37%, downside targets met only 36% of the time. — http://thepatternsite.com/st.html
2. **Do NOT trade pennants/flags expecting the pole to repeat ("half-mast" as a promise).** Pennants: 54% break-even failure both directions, average swing only ~6–7%, full-height target met ~32–35% — the post-pennant leg matches/exceeds the pole only ~30% of the time. Scale targets (A1.9) or skip. — Bulkowski pennant data via extraction, Sources.
3. **Do NOT present Fibonacci levels as inherently meaningful prices.** Tsinaslanidis, Guijarro & Voukelatos (2022), testing Fib retracements algorithmically on major US/German indices, found bounces at Fib zones statistically indistinguishable from bounces at equivalent non-Fibonacci zones; wider zones "worked" only because they're wider targets. Fib = attention map / confluence input only (A4.5). — via https://cryptoaimeta.com/investments/fibonacci-retracement/ (secondary summary of the primary study)
4. **Do NOT call a double top/bottom at the second peak/trough.** Unconfirmed double bottoms continue in the original direction ~48% of the time on Bulkowski's count; the pattern *does not exist* until the confirmation close (A1.2). Same for H&S before the neckline close. — http://thepatternsite.com/aedb.html
5. **Do NOT project the full measured move as "the target."** Full targets are met only ~51% (H&S top), ~58/36% (symmetrical triangle), ~61% (cup-with-handle). Retail charts that draw the full height as a target line overstate the objective in roughly half of all cases. — Bulkowski pages, A1 table.
6. **Do NOT fade a breakout because price "came back" (throwback).** Retests are the *norm* (62–68%), not invalidation — and conversely, do not chase the first break intraday: confirmation is a close (A1.2, A2.7), and StockCharts' own trendline example shows wick-breaks on the wrong scale manufacturing false signals wholesale. — Bulkowski pages; StockCharts *Trend Lines*.
7. **Do NOT short "overbought" RSI / buy "oversold" RSI in a trend.** Brown's regime ranges (bull ~40–90, bear ~10–60) mean RSI 70+ is normal trend behaviour; the tradable RSI events are failure swings, regime-zone support/resistance, and *confirmed* divergence at levels (A3.2–A3.4). — StockCharts ChartSchool RSI (Brown).
8. **Do NOT act on RSI divergence the moment lines diverge.** Divergence carries no timing; in strong trends into new highs it can persist and re-diverge repeatedly while price continues — the classic retail counter-trend loss. Gate it (A3.2, A3.3). — Practitioner/academic summaries in A3.6 (flagged secondary).
9. **Do NOT draw trendlines on a linear chart across a multi-year, multi-fold move, and do NOT force a line through every pivot.** Both manufacture false breaks (AMZN/EMC cases) and false levels ("best not to force the issue"). — StockCharts *Trend Lines*.
10. **Do NOT require textbook volume inside patterns, and do NOT ignore it at breakouts/climaxes.** Internal volume tendencies are only 61–86% tendencies (A1.5) — hard volume filters throw away most valid patterns; but climax volume at extremes and dry-ups on tests are genuinely diagnostic (A4.6, A5). — Bulkowski; StockCharts.
11. **Do NOT expect a spring in every accumulation base.** Wyckoff Schematic 2 has none; demanding one makes you miss bases and mislabel ordinary breakdowns as springs. A spring is defined by its *test* (higher low, lesser volume), not by the dip alone. — StockCharts Wyckoff tutorial.
12. **Do NOT encode up/down or good/bad by red vs. green alone** — invisible as a distinction to ~8% of male readers; and do NOT solve clutter by shrinking type below legibility or letting labels overlap candles/other labels: drop, merge, or disclose-on-demand instead (B1.2, B2.3, B3.1). — WCAG / Okabe–Ito sources, B2.4–B2.5.
13. **Do NOT cite pattern statistics as if patterns were proven profitable.** After data-snooping correction across thousands of rules, technical-rule performance for major US indices lost significance after the mid-1980s (Sullivan, Timmermann & White); Lo et al. claim only incremental information. Bulkowski's samples are bull-market, perfect-trade, and partly decades old. Written reads must carry the base rate and the regime, or they overclaim. — Hsu & Kuan review summary, https://www.nottingham.ac.uk/research/groups/grangercentre/documents/confs/paper-kuan-july-2009.pdf ; Lo et al., A1.11.
