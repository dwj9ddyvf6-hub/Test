# Technical Analysis Study and Practice Atlas

An educational study of technical analysis, visual chart reading and reproducible chart-reading practice. Start with the illustrated PDF. The editable Word document contains the same study; the Markdown file preserves the chapter text and linked references.

## Files

- [Illustrated atlas PDF](Technical_Analysis_Study_and_Practice_Atlas.pdf)
- [Editable atlas DOCX](Technical_Analysis_Study_and_Practice_Atlas.docx)
- [Chapter text and references](Technical_Analysis_Study_and_Practice_Atlas.md)
- [Research and practice archive](Technical_Analysis_Study_and_Practice_Archive.zip)
- [SHA-256 integrity manifest](SHA256SUMS.txt)

The atlas covers price structure, support and resistance, candlesticks, classical patterns, breakout/retest sequences, volume, indicators, relative strength, Elliott wave, Wyckoff, market profiles, multi-timeframe analysis, trader-specific methods, chart engines and deliberate practice. It includes original teaching figures and four preserved before-and-after exercises with future bars hidden at the initial reading.

## Public edition and privacy

This edition omits private mailbox links, message identifiers, subscriber details, personal correspondence, personal attribution and personal-reference inventory entries. No raw newsletter bodies, harvested post corpus or third-party chart images are included. Original source authors retain their rights; links and educational summaries are provided for attribution and further study. Repository citations are source references, not a representation that every linked repository or file is accessible to every reader.

The inventory counts describe the captured research snapshot before privacy filtering. The public inventory omits personally identifying metadata entries, so its rows need not reproduce the unredacted snapshot totals. Some module references identify underlying research receipts or source files outside this compact archive.

## Educational use

This is educational material, not individualized investment advice, an order recommendation or a promise of returns. The four masked exercises are descriptive learning examples, not four executed trades or evidence of a measured strategy win rate. No trading engine, portfolio, deployment or schedule was changed by the study.
